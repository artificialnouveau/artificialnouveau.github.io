Tech and Infrastructure Grants in the UK

Open calls

  1. Radical Sabbatical 2027: six-month residencies on touch at the University of Birmingham (GBP 3,500 fee + GBP 500 production, UK-based artists)

    Eastside Projects with University Collections, University of Birmingham · University of Birmingham, Birmingham, UK (in person, research residency). Open to artists based in the UK only. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget per artist; two artists selected.

    Radical Sabbatical is a collaboration between Eastside Projects and University Collections at the University of Birmingham that gives two artists a six-month residency centred on research and development. The 2027 edition is themed around touch: artists are matched with one or more academics and asked to interrogate and interpret academic research, with areas including digital touch and technology, haptic and sensory experience, embodied knowledge, disability and inclusive design, medical perspectives, materiality and craft, memory and emotion, and human and non-human sensory connection. Academic partners include the Active Touch Lab and the Department of Creative Industries. Artists have regular one-to-one conversations with staff and present at a final sharing event. ELIGIBILITY: artists based in the UK. SUPPORT: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget. TIMELINE: deadline Friday 20 November 2026, 11:59pm; interviews for shortlisted artists 10 December 2026; residencies begin January 2027; final sharing event and evaluation June/July 2027. APPLY using the artists' callout pack linked from the call page; questions to info@eastsideprojects.org.

  2. Arts Council England: Responsible AI Sector Champions Programme (GBP 9,450 fee for 27 days, creative freelancers)

    Arts Council England · England, UK · Deadline: 23 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 9,450 fee covering 27 days of engagement with the programme

    A paid leadership programme from Arts Council England for creative freelancers who want to shape how artificial intelligence is used in the arts and culture sector. Sector Champions influence policy and practice, share their own work and ideas, and take part in conversations about art, culture and technology in England and beyond. It sits within the Arts Council's wider Responsible AI work, alongside its sector guidance and toolkit on AI. WHO: creative freelancers; check the call page for the full eligibility criteria, including residency and experience requirements. WHAT YOU GET: a fee of GBP 9,450 covering 27 days of engagement with the programme. APPLICATION: via the Arts Council England programme page, which carries the guidance, programme dates and application route. DEADLINE: 23 November 2026; confirm the closing time on the call page. Details here are taken from a sector newsletter listing of the call, so check the official page before applying.

  3. Arts Council of Northern Ireland: Individual Artists Digital Evolution Awards 2026/27 (up to GBP 10,000 for digital art)

    Arts Council of Northern Ireland (National Lottery funded) · Northern Ireland. Applicants must live in Northern Ireland. · Deadline: 26 Nov 2026 · Award: Up to GBP 10,000 per award. The applicant's own artist fees can be budgeted at up to GBP 125 a day or GBP 500 a week. No application fee stated.

    A National Lottery award from the Arts Council of Northern Ireland for individual artists to make digital art and build skills in new media and immersive practice. WHAT COUNTS: art created exclusively in the digital space, or translated into physical form through technology, including AR and VR, 3D and digital creation, data-driven work and app-based art. Simple digitisation of existing work and filmed performances or recordings are excluded. WHO: individual artists of any discipline who live in Northern Ireland, are 18 or over, and have made a significant contribution to the arts there for at least one year in the last five. Partnership projects are possible with the artist as lead. University staff and students can apply only if the project is not part of their job or studies. Not eligible: artists who have had two of these awards in the past five years, and social media content creators. AMOUNT: up to GBP 10,000, for projects running between 1 March 2027 and 29 February 2028. Decisions are expected in February 2027. HOW TO APPLY: online only through the Arts Council's grants portal, linked from the programme page, with the budget template and all mandatory enclosures. DEADLINE: Thursday 26 November 2026 at 12 noon; the system shuts at noon precisely and uploads must be complete by then.

  4. COMPUTALA 2027 open call: Superstition and the Machine (LCB Depot x New Media Art Club, Leicester)

    LCB Depot with New Media Art Club · Leicester, UK (LCB Depot). Open to UK and international applicants; international work can only be shown digitally. · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 50 participation fee for each selected artist (funded by Arts Council England Project Grants). Free to enter. Work can be sold; the retail partner takes a 20 percent admin fee on sales.

    COMPUTALA is LCB Depot's annual January season on technology in contemporary art practice, made this year with New Media Art Club. The 2027 exhibition, Superstition and the Machine, invites artists, technologists and creative practitioners to explore the intersections of technology, magic, ritual and digital folklore, from any perspective. Work in all media is welcome: digital art, interactive installations, creative coding, video, sound, performance, net art, games, physical computing and experimental forms. OFFER: GBP 50 participation fee for selected applicants, exhibition at LCB Depot (and possibly other venues) plus online promotion, the option to sell work, and free use of the space as a studio during the season. The season also hosts events, workshops, talks and screenings (family day 9 January, closing 29 January); send proposals to hi@newmediaart.club. ELIGIBILITY: open to all, whether based in Leicester/shire, the East Midlands or elsewhere; international work is shown digitally only. Existing work is recommended given the tight turnaround; selected work must be delivered by 18 December 2026. APPLY via the Google Form; free to enter, alternative formats available on request. DEADLINE: 4 December 2026, 23:59.

  5. n-Space Fellowship, second cohort (Prediction): 18-month research fellowship at Somerset House Studios, London (GBP 10,000 fee plus shared GBP 30,000 R&D fund)

    Somerset House Studios, with UAL Creative Computing Institute, Abandon Normal Devices, Lancaster University and the National Arts Council Singapore; supported by the Rothschild Foundation · Somerset House Studios, London, UK. London-track fellows must be based in London and use the n-Space workspace at least two days a week. A separate track brings one Singapore citizen or permanent resident to London for three months. · Deadline: 07 Dec 2026 · Award: London track: GBP 10,000 fee and access to a GBP 30,000 shared R&D fund, with 24/7 workspace. No travel, relocation or accommodation support. Singapore track: GBP 5,000 fee plus return flights, accommodation, per diems and ground transport, and the same shared fund. Up to GBP 200 towards an access support worker for the application. No application fee mentioned.

    n-Space at Somerset House Studios is a space for experimental, collaborative research across art, technology and society, aimed at outcomes that do not fit traditional institutional platforms. Its second fellowship cohort is convened around PREDICTION. THEME: once the domain of divination and prophecy, prediction now runs through sensing infrastructures, statistical forecasting and AI systems that shape markets, governance and public life, and often produce the futures they anticipate. Research can treat prediction as a subject, a method or a medium. WHO: artists, designers, engineers, lawyers, scientists and technologists who want to work in a collaborative research cohort, across fields from economics and law to computation and the arts. Applicants need five or more years of practice in their current field and may not be in full-time education, PhD programmes included, during the fellowship. At most three of the fellows will be artists. Duos and collectives can apply but split the fee and name up to two representatives. Nationality and visa rules are not stated. COHORT: six fellows, five based in London and one from Singapore. COMMITMENT: London fellows must be based in London, use n-Space as their main base at least two days a week, attend about 80% of sessions, and join fortnightly Friday sessions at UAL's Creative Computing Institute in person (12 April to 18 June and 4 October to 10 December 2027). SUPPORT: GBP 10,000 fee, a GBP 30,000 R&D fund shared by the cohort, 24/7 workspace, access to rehearsal, recording and project spaces and the Makerversity workshops, expert sessions, curatorial support from curator Linda Rocco, and mentorship from Constant Dullaart, Gary Zhexi Zhang, Stephanie Dinkins and Xin Liu. OUTPUT: at least one research output for the final presentation at Somerset House in September 2028. It need not be a finished artwork; a prototype, software, model, writing or proposal all count. SINGAPORE TRACK: with the National Arts Council Singapore, one Singapore citizen or permanent resident with five or more years in art and technology gets a nine-month fellowship, with three months in London (April to June 2027), a GBP 5,000 fee and travel, accommodation and per diems covered; it has its own call page and application form, listed here as a separate entry. APPLICATION: online form linked from the call page. DEADLINE: Monday 7 December 2026, 10:00 UK time, for both tracks.

    Somerset House Studios with the National Arts Council Singapore; research partner UAL Creative Computing Institute; supported by the Rothschild Foundation · Hybrid. In person at Somerset House Studios, London, UK from April to June 2027 and for the final presentation in March 2028; online from July 2027 to February 2028. Open to Singapore citizens and permanent residents. · Deadline: 07 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 5,000 fellowship fee, plus return flights to London, accommodation, per diems and ground transport for both London visits, and access to a GBP 30,000 R&D fund shared by the six-person cohort. No application fee mentioned.

    The Singapore place in the second n-Space fellowship cohort at Somerset House Studios, run with the National Arts Council Singapore. n-Space is a space for experimental, collaborative research across art, technology and society; this cohort of six (five London-based fellows plus the Singapore fellow) is convened around PREDICTION as a line of enquiry, from forecasting and sensing infrastructures to AI systems. WHO: Singapore citizens and permanent residents with five or more years of practice in art and technology, artists and technologists alike. Applicants may not be in full-time education, PhD programmes included, during the fellowship. Collectives can apply but appoint one representative. COMMITMENT: be in London from April to June 2027, attend all fellowship sessions and one-to-one meetings, including the Friday sessions at UAL's Creative Computing Institute, then continue online (on UK time, in English) until an in-person public presentation at Somerset House in March 2028. OUTPUT: not necessarily a finished artwork; a prototype, software, writing or research proposal all count. APPLICATION: FormSG form linked from the call page; audio or video applications are accepted as access support. Queries to harry.leek@somersethouse.org.uk or chrystal_ho@nac.gov.sg. DEADLINE: Monday 7 December 2026, 10:00 UK time; applicants hear back in February 2027. London-based applicants should use the separate London track, listed on its own.

    CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre, supported by UKRI · UK (lead applicant must be a UK-registered company; project activity UK-based).     A new co-commission from CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre to take a creative technology idea that is ready to be completed, honed or technically advanced through focused R&D, then present it to audiences at Southbank Centre's Creative Intelligence festival in autumn 2027. Team and funded activity must be UK-based. Webinar Q&A on 16 November 2026, 14:00 GMT. DEADLINE: 17 December 2026, 14:00 GMT.

    EVA London (BCS Computer Arts Society) · BCS London offices, Moorgate, London, UK, and online via Zoom (hybrid).   10. Awesome Foundation Local Chapters: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants (US, UK, Canada, Australia, NL, Asia)

    Awesome Foundation (local chapters) · City and regional chapters; each funds projects in or benefiting its own area. US, Canada, Australia, UK (Glasgow), Netherlands (Utrecht), Malaysia, Singapore, Armenia, Uzbekistan. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Usually USD 1,000 (or local equivalent) per grant, no strings attached, typically one grant per chapter per month. No application fee.

    The Awesome Foundation is a network of volunteer giving circles: in each chapter about ten trustees pool their own money to give a USD 1,000 no-strings micro-grant, usually monthly, to a project that adds "awesome" to their area: art, public interventions, community tech, events, science and civic ideas. One application on the main site can be sent to the chapter for your location. ACTIVE CHAPTERS (made grants in 2026, checked 9 Nov 2026): US: Ann Arbor, Austin, Baltimore, Cass Clay, Chicago, Cleveland, Detroit, Gainesville, Georgetown MA, Gloucester MA, Hamilton-Wenham MA, Ipswich MA, Los Angeles, Newburgh NY, Newburyport MA, New York City, Oahu, Orlando, Peekskill NY, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Portland OR, Poughkeepsie NY, Rhode Island, Rockport MA, San Antonio, San Francisco, San Jose, Santa Cruz, South Bend, St. Paul, State College PA, Washington DC, Westminster MD (Durham, Miami and Seattle each made one grant early in 2026). Canada: Kingston, Kitchener-Waterloo, Newmarket, Ottawa, Toronto. Australia: Adelaide, Fleurieu, Lake Mac, Lower Hunter, Newcastle, Sydney. UK: Glasgow. Netherlands: Utrecht (one grant in early 2026; Leiden and Rotterdam have been quiet since 2025 and 2024). Asia: Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Tashkent, Yerevan. ELIGIBILITY: set per chapter, generally individuals or groups doing the project in that chapter's area. APPLY: free, short online form. DEADLINE: rolling. Worldwide themed chapters are listed separately (Disability, Conservation and Climate, On the Water).

  11. British Council Travel Grant Fund (film and XR, rolling)

    British Council, supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund · Open to UK-based filmmakers and XR creatives (UK residents or citizens, aged 18+). Grants fund travel to international festivals, showcases, labs and co-production forums worldwide. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Varies by strand: short and feature film festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants capped at GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants capped at GBP 2,000.     The British Council's standing Travel Grant Fund helps early to mid-career UK filmmakers and XR creatives represent their work abroad, build networks, reach new audiences and raise the international profile of new and emerging UK film and immersive talent. Supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund. WHAT IT FUNDS: travel to attend international film festivals, showcases, training and development labs, and co-production forums, across five strands: short film festival grants, feature film festival grants, interactive/XR grants (including VR, immersive storytelling experiences and installations), lab grants and co-production forum grants. AMOUNTS: short and feature festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants up to GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants up to GBP 2,000. DEADLINE: rolling, no fixed deadlines; submit at least two weeks before the event starts (retrospective applications for past events are not accepted). Free to apply.

  12. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; the page does not list specific upcoming cohort dates, so state your preferred dates and locations in the application. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.
