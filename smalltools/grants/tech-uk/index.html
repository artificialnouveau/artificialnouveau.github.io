Tech and Infrastructure Grants in the UK

Open calls

  1. Digital Democracy Edinburgh 2027: Call for Proposals (TICTeC, mySociety, AI4CI, Scottish Government)

    mySociety (TICTeC) with the AI for Collective Intelligence (AI4CI) Hub, the Scottish Government and the Edinburgh Futures Institute, University of Edinburgh · Edinburgh Futures Institute, Edinburgh, Scotland, UK. Primarily in-person, with a limited number of remote presentation slots. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: NO GUARANTEED CASH, listed as an exception at the editor's request: accepted speakers get complimentary conference registration, and a limited number of travel scholarships are available for speakers who could otherwise not attend (priority to applicants from ODA-eligible countries and under-29s; amount not published). Free to submit.

    Digital Democracy Edinburgh is a two-day conference from mySociety's TICTeC (The Impacts of Civic Technology) series, run with the AI for Collective Intelligence (AI4CI) Research Hub, the Scottish Government and the Edinburgh Futures Institute, for researchers, policy makers and practitioners reimagining technology for more resilient democracies. CORE QUESTION: how can technology reimagine democratic decision-making and build a more resilient democracy? STRANDS: building democratic technology (digital public infrastructure, participatory platforms, AI-assisted and augmented deliberation, open-source civic tech, tech for infrastructure-poor settings); democracy in a digital age (quality of participation, hybrid online and offline participation, transparency and accountability, digital exclusion); and embedding digital democracy around the world (open government, scaling and transferring innovations, lessons from implementation, lower-income democracies). Topics also include collective intelligence, prioritising public values in AI and the limits of digital democracy. FORMATS: 15-minute presentations and 5-minute lightning talks (individuals or groups), and 75-minute collaborative workshops or roundtables for about 20 to 30 participants (groups of contributors only); session proposals up to 300 words. The organisers want real-world experience, evaluation and implementation learning, and warn that generic AI-generated proposals are unlikely to score well. SUPPORT: complimentary registration for accepted speakers; limited travel scholarships (flag the need on the form; priority to ODA-eligible countries and under-29s); visa invitation letters on registration. Speakers must be available for both full days. TIMELINE: submit via the Google Form linked from the event page by 23:59 GMT on 15 November 2026; notification by 7 December 2026; presenters must register by 18 December 2026 to confirm their slot. Contact: tictec@mysociety.org.

    National Gallery (National Gallery X), London · National Gallery, London, UK. Open to UK-based emerging creatives. · Deadline: 17 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 15,000 commission. Up to 10 shortlisted applicants receive GBP 500 each to develop proposals.

  3. Cryptic Nights 2027/28 Open Call (sound, performance and technology, Scotland)

    Cryptic, Glasgow · Glasgow, Scotland. Open to emerging artists based in Scotland. · Deadline: 19 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 800 fee for solo artists, up to GBP 2,000 for groups, plus residency days, mentoring, travel and access budgets.

    Cryptic Nights supports emerging Scotland-based artists making work that combines original sound or music with performance, screen or technology, to develop an existing piece and present it in Cryptic's programme. Selected artists receive a fee, residency time, mentoring and travel and access support. No application fee.

  4. Radical Sabbatical 2027: six-month residencies on touch at the University of Birmingham (GBP 3,500 fee + GBP 500 production, UK-based artists)

    Eastside Projects with University Collections, University of Birmingham · University of Birmingham, Birmingham, UK (in person, research residency). Open to artists based in the UK only. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget per artist; two artists selected.

    Radical Sabbatical is a collaboration between Eastside Projects and University Collections at the University of Birmingham that gives two artists a six-month residency centred on research and development. The 2027 edition is themed around touch: artists are matched with one or more academics and asked to interrogate and interpret academic research, with areas including digital touch and technology, haptic and sensory experience, embodied knowledge, disability and inclusive design, medical perspectives, materiality and craft, memory and emotion, and human and non-human sensory connection. Academic partners include the Active Touch Lab and the Department of Creative Industries. Artists have regular one-to-one conversations with staff and present at a final sharing event. ELIGIBILITY: artists based in the UK. SUPPORT: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget. TIMELINE: deadline Friday 20 November 2026, 11:59pm; interviews for shortlisted artists 10 December 2026; residencies begin January 2027; final sharing event and evaluation June/July 2027. APPLY using the artists' callout pack linked from the call page; questions to info@eastsideprojects.org.

  5. COMPUTALA 2027 open call: Superstition and the Machine (LCB Depot x New Media Art Club, Leicester)

    LCB Depot with New Media Art Club · Leicester, UK (LCB Depot). Open to UK and international applicants; international work can only be shown digitally. · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 50 participation fee for each selected artist (funded by Arts Council England Project Grants). Free to enter. Work can be sold; the retail partner takes a 20 percent admin fee on sales.

    COMPUTALA is LCB Depot's annual January season on technology in contemporary art practice, made this year with New Media Art Club. The 2027 exhibition, Superstition and the Machine, invites artists, technologists and creative practitioners to explore the intersections of technology, magic, ritual and digital folklore, from any perspective. Work in all media is welcome: digital art, interactive installations, creative coding, video, sound, performance, net art, games, physical computing and experimental forms. OFFER: GBP 50 participation fee for selected applicants, exhibition at LCB Depot (and possibly other venues) plus online promotion, the option to sell work, and free use of the space as a studio during the season. The season also hosts events, workshops, talks and screenings (family day 9 January, closing 29 January); send proposals to hi@newmediaart.club. ELIGIBILITY: open to all, whether based in Leicester/shire, the East Midlands or elsewhere; international work is shown digitally only. Existing work is recommended given the tight turnaround; selected work must be delivered by 18 December 2026. APPLY via the Google Form; free to enter, alternative formats available on request. DEADLINE: 4 December 2026, 23:59.

  6. The Creative Intelligence Award 2027 (CoSTAR National Lab x Southbank Centre)

    CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre, supported by UKRI · UK (lead applicant must be a UK-registered company; project activity UK-based). Prototyping at Pinewood Studios; showcase at Southbank Centre, London. · Deadline: 17 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 50,000 grant (one award), plus up to GBP 25,000 in AI compute and cloud credits, 2 x 5 days studio access at Pinewood Studios, a six-week CoSTAR prototyping team sprint, 3 days pre-show testing, Southbank curatorial and producing support, and 20 hours of mentorship. No application fee.

    A new co-commission from CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre to take a creative technology idea that is ready to be completed, honed or technically advanced through focused R&D, then present it to audiences at Southbank Centre's Creative Intelligence festival in autumn 2027. They want original concepts where AI, immersive, real-time, virtual production or other emerging technology is integral rather than an add-on, across any artform (music, theatre, dance, literature, visual and live art, design, architecture, food, film, games), with cross-disciplinary work encouraged and touring potential valued. ELIGIBILITY: lead applicant must be a UK-registered, trading company or other eligible UK legal entity; consortia welcome, and artists, technologists, researchers and sole traders can take part as collaborators. Team and funded activity must be UK-based. Webinar Q&A on 16 November 2026, 14:00 GMT. DEADLINE: 17 December 2026, 14:00 GMT.

  7. EVA London 2027: Call for Proposals (Electronic Visualisation and the Arts, 12-16 July 2027)

    EVA London (BCS Computer Arts Society) · BCS London offices, Moorgate, London, UK, and online via Zoom (hybrid). Open internationally. · Deadline: 11 Jan 2027 · Award: NO CASH, listed as an exception at the editor's request (conference presentation slot and publication in the proceedings). Free to submit, but at least one author of each paper and all panel and workshop presenters must pay conference registration (rates not yet published; to be set in spring 2027, with a special rate for Research Workshop presenters).

    EVA London is a long-running hybrid conference on electronic visualisation and the arts, covering digital art, creative technology, computing and visual culture, held at BCS in Moorgate, London, and online. STRANDS: long papers (8 pages, draft PDF required) and short papers (2 pages, abstract-only proposal), both in 20-minute slots; papers with demonstration (8 pages); demonstrations (2 pages, 10 or 20 minutes, online demos possible); panel sessions (30 minutes); workshops and tutorials (usually 90 or 120 minutes); and a Research Workshop for postgraduate students and unaffiliated digital artists (15-minute talks, optional two-page written submission). TO APPLY: a summary proposal of up to 400 words via EasyChair (https://easychair.org/conferences/?conf=evalondon2027). DEADLINES: main proposals Monday 11 January 2027; Research Workshop applications by email to Graham Diprose (grahamdiprose@gmail.com) by Monday 22 February 2027, only while places remain. Notification 22 February 2027; final papers 12 March 2027; fees published 12 April 2027; registration opens 11 May 2027. Presenters must register; no honorarium, prize or current bursary scheme is stated.

  9. Awesome Foundation Local Chapters: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants (US, UK, Canada, Australia, NL, Asia)

    Awesome Foundation (local chapters) · City and regional chapters; each funds projects in or benefiting its own area. US, Canada, Australia, UK (Glasgow), Netherlands (Utrecht), Malaysia, Singapore, Armenia, Uzbekistan. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Usually USD 1,000 (or local equivalent) per grant, no strings attached, typically one grant per chapter per month. No application fee.

    The Awesome Foundation is a network of volunteer giving circles: in each chapter about ten trustees pool their own money to give a USD 1,000 no-strings micro-grant, usually monthly, to a project that adds "awesome" to their area: art, public interventions, community tech, events, science and civic ideas. One application on the main site can be sent to the chapter for your location. ACTIVE CHAPTERS (made grants in 2026, checked 9 Nov 2026): US: Ann Arbor, Austin, Baltimore, Cass Clay, Chicago, Cleveland, Detroit, Gainesville, Georgetown MA, Gloucester MA, Hamilton-Wenham MA, Ipswich MA, Los Angeles, Newburgh NY, Newburyport MA, New York City, Oahu, Orlando, Peekskill NY, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Portland OR, Poughkeepsie NY, Rhode Island, Rockport MA, San Antonio, San Francisco, San Jose, Santa Cruz, South Bend, St. Paul, State College PA, Washington DC, Westminster MD (Durham, Miami and Seattle each made one grant early in 2026). Canada: Kingston, Kitchener-Waterloo, Newmarket, Ottawa, Toronto. Australia: Adelaide, Fleurieu, Lake Mac, Lower Hunter, Newcastle, Sydney. UK: Glasgow. Netherlands: Utrecht (one grant in early 2026; Leiden and Rotterdam have been quiet since 2025 and 2024). Asia: Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Tashkent, Yerevan. ELIGIBILITY: set per chapter, generally individuals or groups doing the project in that chapter's area. APPLY: free, short online form. DEADLINE: rolling. Worldwide themed chapters are listed separately (Disability, Conservation and Climate, On the Water).

  10. British Council Travel Grant Fund (film and XR, rolling)

    British Council, supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund · Open to UK-based filmmakers and XR creatives (UK residents or citizens, aged 18+). Grants fund travel to international festivals, showcases, labs and co-production forums worldwide. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Varies by strand: short and feature film festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants capped at GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants capped at GBP 2,000. Free to apply.

    The British Council's standing Travel Grant Fund helps early to mid-career UK filmmakers and XR creatives represent their work abroad, build networks, reach new audiences and raise the international profile of new and emerging UK film and immersive talent. Supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund. WHAT IT FUNDS: travel to attend international film festivals, showcases, training and development labs, and co-production forums, across five strands: short film festival grants, feature film festival grants, interactive/XR grants (including VR, immersive storytelling experiences and installations), lab grants and co-production forum grants. AMOUNTS: short and feature festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants up to GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants up to GBP 2,000. WHO CAN APPLY: UK residents or citizens aged 18+; applicants can be directors, producers or writers attached to the selected project. KEY CONDITIONS: the festival, lab or forum must appear on the British Council's Key Lists of eligible events, and the project must be registered on the British Council's UK Project Database. DEADLINE: rolling, no fixed deadlines; submit at least two weeks before the event starts (retrospective applications for past events are not accepted). Free to apply.

  11. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.
