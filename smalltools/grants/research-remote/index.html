Open calls

  1. Bellingcat Contractor: AI Engineer (Open Source Agentic Systems)

    Bellingcat · Remote. Requires a time zone with sufficient overlap with Bellingcat's Europe-based team. · Deadline: 08 Nov 2026 · Award: Annual contractor fee of USD 80,000 to 93,000 for full-time (40 hours/week), depending on experience and track record; a part-time (20 hours/week) option is also offered, rate not stated.

    Bellingcat, the open source investigations collective, is hiring a contractor AI engineer to build agentic systems that support its investigators. (Listed on Grant Desk as an exception to the no-jobs rule: a remote, paid, public-interest AI contract.) THE ROLE: build, test, deploy and monitor multi-agent workflows for open source investigations; develop agentic pipelines with human oversight, feedback loops and evaluation metrics; architect and maintain cloud infrastructure for hosting LLM harnesses and monitoring agents using token-as-a-service providers; develop MCP servers and data bridges connecting agents to open source research tools and databases; and create evaluation frameworks for the performance and reasoning of agentic pipelines. WHAT YOU GET: an annual fee of USD 80,000 to 93,000 for full-time work (part-time 20 hours/week also possible), on an initial 12-month contract, working remotely with some flexibility to work from other countries for a limited number of weeks per year. ELIGIBILITY: hands-on full-stack development experience including infrastructure maintenance and server-side data workflows; experience with agentic AI workflows and open-source harnesses; experience with human-in-the-loop design (approval checks, feedback loops); a collaborative record of working with non-technical colleagues on public interest technology; English proficiency; and a time zone overlapping with Europe. Experience with mission-driven organisations or open source / public interest tech is a plus. APPLICATION: via Google Form; Bellingcat asks that applications not be AI-generated (AI for grammar checks is fine). Review is rolling from 21 October and interviews may start before the close. DEADLINE: 8 November 2026.

  2. PRISM 2027: Call for AI Safety Research Mentors

    PRISM (Peer-Driven Research Initiative for Safety Methodologies) · Fully remote, all timezones welcome · Deadline: 25 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 1,000 honorarium per mentor (about 20 mentors), plus co-authorship on the team's conference submission (venues like NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR). Research manager and support staff included.

    PRISM (Peer-Driven Research Initiative for Safety Methodologies) is recruiting about 20 mentors to guide teams of 4-5 emerging AI safety researchers from research question to a conference submission, with a focus on supporting women and underrepresented groups. The 2026 cohort ran 12 teams and produced 9 papers accepted to NeurIPS workshops. ROLE: you propose the project and set the research direction; help fellows build research taste (choosing good questions, designing sound experiments, knowing when a result is worth trusting); run weekly milestone reviews of work the team has already refined among themselves; own the quality bar for the final paper. PRISM provides a clear structure and support staff, which you are free to adjust. TEAM: PRISM hands you 15-20 pre-filtered candidates matched to your criteria (advanced undergraduates to experienced professionals, many with graduate degrees); you pick your team, no cold outreach. ELIGIBILITY: research experience at roughly late-PhD or MATS-scholar level and the ability to guide a team on AI safety research. Fully remote, all timezones. TIMELINE: mentor applications 29 October to 25 November 2026, reviewed on a rolling basis and may close early if spots fill; shortlisted applicants are interviewed and record a 5-10 minute pitch of their research question for fellow applicants; mentors announced end of November; fellow recruitment 14 December to 8 January (one Q&A session); fellow selection 11 to 20 January; program 31 January to 23 May 2027.

  3. RJI Professional Innovation Fellowships 2027 (Individual, Institutional and Emerging Technology tracks)

    Reynolds Journalism Institute (University of Missouri) · Remote, with a few paid visits to the Reynolds Journalism Institute in Columbia, Missouri, United States. · Deadline: 08 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 75,000 for the Individual and Institutional tracks, USD 100,000 for the Emerging Technology track, paid in three instalments tied to deliverables; RJI also pays for the fellow's visits to its Columbia, Missouri campus. No application fee.

    The Reynolds Journalism Institute at the University of Missouri funds people and teams to build and launch a tool, workflow or product that helps newsrooms, in three tracks: Individual (USD 75,000), Institutional (USD 75,000, paid to a host organisation or the fellow) and Emerging Technology (USD 100,000, where AI or another new technology must be essential to the solution). The output must be a free, open-source resource usable by US journalists; tools limited to one city or state are unlikely to be funded. The fellowship is remote and runs for eight months from 28 June 2027 with an in-person kickoff at RJI and a couple of paid visits. ELIGIBILITY: anyone, anywhere; no age, background or journalism credential required. Applications open 2 November 2026 on Submittable; a shortlist is invited in early to mid February 2027 to submit a fuller application, with finalist interviews 5 to 23 April 2027. DEADLINE: 8 January 2027 (first round; no time zone stated, RJI is on US Central time).

  4. Pantheon: Research Fellowship in robot learning (remote, USD 6,000/month)

    Pantheon · Remote · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 6,000 per month stipend (about USD 12,000 to 18,000 over the fellowship) plus enterprise-level compute for experiments. No application fee.

    Pantheon, which builds general-purpose robot models, runs a remote research fellowship in which each fellow leads a project on an open problem the team meets while training and evaluating its models; strong results are folded into the next generation of those models. WHO: graduate students and exceptional undergraduates with backgrounds in pretraining large models, reinforcement learning or adjacent fields who want to work on general-purpose robotics. CURRENT RESEARCH QUESTIONS: value learning for general-purpose tasks; model drift and test-time adaptation in world models; multimodal representation (vision, touch, force, proprioception at different rates or missing); scaling laws for forward dynamics models versus video pretraining; learning from deliberately induced failure. OFFER: USD 6,000/month stipend, enterprise-level compute, weekly 1:1 mentorship (team includes ETH Zurich alumni, ICML authors, NeurIPS award winners, and researchers from xAI and Jane Street), work toward a paper or other public research output; outstanding fellows may be offered full-time roles. Past fellows came from ETH Zurich, IIT Bombay, Yale, Stanford and Cambridge. APPLY: email your CV, a short note on the research questions that excite you, and any relevant work to fellowship@pantheon.inc. No deadline is published (rolling).

  5. Digital Impact Alliance (DIAL) Research Fellowship

    Digital Impact Alliance (DIAL) · Remote; international organisation. Experience working in or with global majority countries encouraged. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 5,000 stipend for a 6-8 month fellowship. No application fee.

    The Digital Impact Alliance (DIAL) Research Fellowship supports curious, passionate mid-career researchers and practitioners to explore frontier questions on digital public infrastructure (DPI) and data governance. Fellows bring their own knowledge and research agenda while contributing to DIAL's core thematic priorities: creating more value for more people from data; understanding how digital systems and services impact people, including and especially the most marginalised; and accelerating the adoption of data-sharing and governance practices that fuel innovation and empower people. SUPPORT: a USD 5,000 stipend for research completed over 6-8 months, plus mentorship from DIAL staff and industry experts, access to DIAL's network and resources, collaboration with partner organisations, and support amplifying the work. Each fellow produces two preliminary pieces and one final, more extensive output (research papers or reports, policy briefs, expert-comment series, etc.). ELIGIBILITY: professionals from academia, the private sector, and civil society, typically with 8-15 years of experience in digital development, policy, or research; demonstrated work at the intersection of technology and society (including DPI and data governance); a track record of high-quality writing, research, or policy briefs; experience working in or with global majority countries; and strong analytical and communication skills. TO APPLY (rolling basis): submit a CV or resume, a writing sample or portfolio, and a research proposal. Questions: fellows@dial.global. Apply via https://dial.global/apply-to-our-fellows-program/.

  6. Luminos.AI Legal Engineering Fellowship (Fall 2026)

    Luminos.AI · Remote · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 35 per hour, part-time. No application fee.

    Luminos.AI, an embedded AI-governance and legal-risk infrastructure company (built by former Fortune 500 AI governance leads and company-builders), runs a part-time fellowship for a technically inclined law student or recent graduate who wants to work at the frontier of AI agent oversight and risk evaluation. Fellows work directly with senior lawyers and engineers on the practical problem of how to test, evaluate and govern generative and agentic AI systems that change every quarter. WHAT YOU DO: research and help design rigorous testing and evaluation approaches for generative and agentic AI, translating fast-moving legal and regulatory requirements into concrete, testable standards; track the global AI regulatory landscape in real time and turn it into practical guardrails; draft frameworks, workflows and risk assessments that hold up under real deployment conditions; and partner closely with the technical team, where comfort around code, models or data is an asset. Fellows with technical skills who want to spend part of their time working directly with engineering are encouraged to do so. WHO YOU ARE: currently pursuing a JD (or a recent graduate) from a top law program; an excellent researcher and writer comfortable applying existing law to genuinely new situations; genuinely enthusiastic about AI and willing to learn the technical details. Bonus: coding ability, ML/GenAI exposure, or a background in privacy, security or data governance law. TERMS: USD 35 per hour, remote, part-time, flexible 3 to 6 month term, with direct mentorship from senior legal and technical leadership. TO APPLY: send a cover letter, resume, transcript and writing sample to careers@luminos.ai with the subject line 'Application for Luminos.AI Fall 2026 Fellowship'. Rolling review with remote interviews.

  7. Outrider AI + Nuclear Reporting Fund (rolling)

    Outrider Foundation · Remote. US-based funder; no nationality restriction stated. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to USD 10,000 per reporting project.

    Outrider funds journalists and outlets reporting on the intersection of artificial intelligence and nuclear weapons: how AI is changing nuclear risk, command and control, and policy. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis through the Google Form linked from the fund page. No application fee.

  8. Kapor Foundation Research Fellowship

    Kapor Foundation · Remote (US-focused tech policy/research) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Cash award (amount per project; first awards announced by 30 June 2026)

    Supports journalists producing long-form investigative reports and tech-policy researchers conducting research, analysis or evaluation that informs policy related to the Kapor Foundation's three priority areas, with an emphasis on responsible AI and tech ethics. Priority areas: CS/AI Education, Innovation, Governance. Aims to dismantle systemic inequities in the tech sector by funding researchers and investigative journalists exploring barriers and driving actionable solutions. Applications accepted on a rolling basis; first awards to be announced by 30 June 2026.
