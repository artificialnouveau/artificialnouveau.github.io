Funded Open Calls in the UK

  1. 2027 KCCUK Open Call for Emerging Korean Artists (Korean Cultural Centre UK)

    Korean Cultural Centre UK (KCCUK) · London, UK (group exhibition, February to April 2027, dates TBC). Korean nationality or Korean heritage required, plus a UK art school degree (BA, MA or PhD) or current UK residence. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 250 participant support fee per selected artist (number of artists not stated), plus exhibition space, invigilation, opening event and KCCUK press/social promotion. Artists cover production, transport and installation costs. No application fee.

    The Korean Cultural Centre UK's annual open call for emerging Korean or Korean-heritage artists, particularly UK art school graduates, leading to a group exhibition from February to April 2027 (exact dates to be confirmed). Open theme; all fine art genres welcome, including video, sound and performance (submit a video link of max. 5 minutes plus stills). Selection by a panel of external curators and art professionals; results announced on the KCCUK website in early December 2026. ELIGIBILITY (all required): Korean nationality or Korean heritage; graduated from a UK-based art school (BA, MA or PhD) or currently living and working in the UK; aged 18+; at least one previous solo or group exhibition; works made within the past 5 years and not previously shown at KCCUK. SUPPORT: exhibition space and invigilation, opening event, certificate of participation, promotion via KCCUK channels (Korean and English press release, social media) and a GBP 250 participant support fee per artist; all other costs (production, transport, equipment, install) are borne by the artist, and support may change depending on KCCUK's circumstances. APPLY: one PDF (max. 7MB, in English) with artist bio (max. 200 words), portfolio of up to 5 works (max. 3 images each, with title, materials, dimensions, year, technical requirements) and artwork statements (150 words per work, max. 700 total), emailed to kccuk.opencall@gmail.com. Enquiries: Jaemin Cha, Curator, jaemin.cha@kccuk.org.uk. DEADLINE: Sunday 15 November 2026, midnight GMT.

  3. Refugee Festival Scotland 2027 x Opening Shot: Open Call for First-Time Filmmakers (GBP 1,500 fee plus GBP 3,000 production budget)

    Refugee Festival Scotland (Scottish Refugee Council) with Opening Shot · Glasgow, Scotland, UK (six in-person workshop days in Glasgow). RESTRICTED: applicants must live in Scotland and consider themselves a refugee or asylum seeker, or have family lived experience of seeking safety; not in full-time formal education. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 1,500 participation fee plus a GBP 3,000 production budget per filmmaker, with mentoring and a premiere screening at Refugee Festival Scotland in June 2027. Four filmmakers selected. No application fee stated.

    Refugee Festival Scotland (run by the Scottish Refugee Council, festival 11 to 20 June 2027) and Opening Shot invite four first-time filmmakers with lived experience of seeking safety to make a short film of about five minutes. WHAT YOU GET: a GBP 1,500 participation fee, a GBP 3,000 production budget, six in-person workshop days in Glasgow (December 2026 to March 2027), a 14-week supported production period with mentoring, and a premiere screening at the festival in June 2027. ELIGIBILITY: aged 18 and over; you consider yourself a refugee or asylum seeker, or your family has lived experience of seeking safety; you live in Scotland; you are not currently enrolled in full-time formal education; you can travel to Glasgow for all six workshop days. Beginners welcome, no previous experience or script needed. DEADLINE: Sunday 22 November 2026 at midnight. OTHER 2027 FESTIVAL STRANDS: the Artists Professional Development Programme (GBP 1,000 grants) closed on 8 October 2026; the Small Grants round for refugee-led and refugee-focused groups in Scotland (up to GBP 750 in 2026) runs from early December 2026 to mid-February 2027; event registration for organisers opens early December 2026 and closes late March 2027. Contact: festival@scottishrefugeecouncil.org.uk.

  4. Open City Documentary Festival 2027 (Call for Entries)

    Open City Documentary Festival / UCL · London, UK · Deadline: 27 Nov 2026 · Award: Screening fees for all selected works: GBP 275 for works over 60 min, GBP 140 for works over 40 min, GBP 70 for works under 40 min, GBP 100 for cross-platform projects (all incl. VAT)

    The 17th Open City Documentary Festival takes place at venues across London in April 2027, presenting international contemporary and retrospective non-fiction film, audio and cross-media work alongside filmmaker Q&As, panels, talks and workshops. The festival nurtures experimentation within the expanded field of non-fiction cinema, from artists' moving image to documentary, the essay film, audio and cross-media works; recent artists screened include Sky Hopinka, Onyeka Igwe, Luke Fowler, Ana Vaz, Jessica Sarah Rinland and Morgan Quaintance. ELIGIBILITY: non-fiction film and video, cross-platform projects and audio works completed after 1 January 2026; no strict premiere requirement, but works not yet publicly screened in London are prioritized. All selected filmmakers receive screening fees: GBP 275 for works over 60 minutes, GBP 140 for works over 40 minutes, GBP 70 for works under 40 minutes, GBP 100 for cross-platform projects (all incl. VAT). Submissions via FilmFreeway with tiered entry fees (lower before the early deadline). EARLY DEADLINE: 25 September 2026. FINAL DEADLINE: 27 November 2026; all entrants notified by 22 February 2027.

  5. Hidden Door 2027: Visual Art Open Call (Edinburgh, up to GBP 1,500 per artwork, Scotland-based artists)

    Hidden Door Festival · Edinburgh, Scotland (in person; venue to be announced, festival anticipated early June 2027). Open to artists based wholly or partly in Scotland, or graduates of a Scottish art school within the last three years. · Deadline: 03 Dec 2026 · Award: Up to GBP 1,500 per artwork/application, covering all artist fees and expenses, budgeted against Scottish Artists' Union rates (most awards will be lower). No application fee.

    Hidden Door is a volunteer-run, multi-arts festival that reopens disused and overlooked buildings in Edinburgh. Its 2027 edition (venue still secret, anticipated early June 2027) runs on the theme "Afterlives of Use", and this open call invites visual artists to propose existing work or work in progress that can be shown in the festival's site-specific spaces. The call does not fund the production of entirely new work. ELIGIBILITY: practising artists who either hold an art-related degree or are self-taught with at least five years of sustained practice, and who are based wholly or partly in Scotland or graduated from a Scottish art school in the last three years. Current undergraduates are not eligible; postgraduates are. SUPPORT: up to GBP 1,500 per artwork/application including all artist fees and expenses, in line with Scottish Artists' Union rates; most awards will be lower. Parallel open calls for dance and spoken word close on the same date, and calls for film, theatre and music are to follow. APPLY via the online form linked from the call page. Notification by 11 January 2027. DEADLINE: Thursday 3 December 2026.

    Daiwa Anglo-Japanese Foundation · For individuals, societies, associations and other bodies in the UK or Japan running projects that connect the two countries. · Deadline: 31 Mar 2027 · Award: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (typical awards GBP 3,000-4,000); flights up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day. A contribution, not full funding. No application fee.

    The Daiwa Foundation Small Grants support projects that promote interaction between the UK and Japan in any field, including arts exchanges, exhibitions, conferences, grassroots and educational exchanges and research travel, with travel between the two countries as the main focus. New initiatives are encouraged, and gender equality and SDG themes are welcome; continuing or regular events (such as an annual festival edition) are unlikely to be funded without a strong reason or special occasion. APPLICATION: read the Application Notes, then apply via the Foundation's online portal (separate forms for UK- and Japan-based applicants); advice from grants@dajf.org.uk. Decisions by late May or early June 2027. DEADLINE: 31 March 2027 (the following round closes 30 September).

  10. British Council Travel Grant Fund (film and XR, rolling)

    British Council, supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund · Open to UK-based filmmakers and XR creatives (UK residents or citizens, aged 18+). Grants fund travel to international festivals, showcases, labs and co-production forums worldwide. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Varies by strand: short and feature film festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants capped at GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants capped at GBP 2,000. Free to apply.

    The British Council's standing Travel Grant Fund helps early to mid-career UK filmmakers and XR creatives represent their work abroad, build networks, reach new audiences and raise the international profile of new and emerging UK film and immersive talent. Supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund. WHAT IT FUNDS: travel to attend international film festivals, showcases, training and development labs, and co-production forums, across five strands: short film festival grants, feature film festival grants, interactive/XR grants (including VR, immersive storytelling experiences and installations), lab grants and co-production forum grants. AMOUNTS: short and feature festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants up to GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants up to GBP 2,000. WHO CAN APPLY: UK residents or citizens aged 18+; applicants can be directors, producers or writers attached to the selected project. KEY CONDITIONS: the festival, lab or forum must appear on the British Council's Key Lists of eligible events, and the project must be registered on the British Council's UK Project Database. DEADLINE: rolling, no fixed deadlines; submit at least two weeks before the event starts (retrospective applications for past events are not accepted). Free to apply.

  11. BFI National Lottery Audience Projects Fund

    British Film Institute (BFI National Lottery) · United Kingdom (legally constituted organisations centrally managed in the UK, not more than 50% foreign-owned) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Pilot projects GBP 10,000 to GBP 20,000. Standard projects GBP 20,000 to GBP 500,000 per year. Immersive projects may include up to GBP 150,000 of production costs within the GBP 500,000 cap. Total fund GBP 19.7M over three years. No application fee.

    The Audience Projects Fund distributes GBP 19.7 million of National Lottery money over three years to widen UK audiences for film and immersive work, covering exhibition, distribution, festivals, touring programmes and sector-support initiatives. It reopened in November 2025 and runs continuously with no closing date, which makes it one of the more accessible large BFI pots for organisations working on the audience-facing rather than production side. AWARDS are tiered: small pilot projects receive GBP 10,000 to GBP 20,000, while full projects can receive GBP 20,000 to GBP 500,000 per year, with immersive projects permitted to include up to GBP 150,000 of production cost inside that envelope. ELIGIBILITY is strictly institutional. Applicants must be legally constituted organisations centrally managed in the UK and not more than 50% foreign-owned. Eligible types include limited companies (not classed as 'large' under the Companies Act 2006), community interest companies, LLPs, UK local or statutory authorities, and registered UK charities or trusts. Individual practitioners cannot apply. Applicants must evidence experience in marketing, festivals, distribution, immersive installations, cultural programming, touring or sector support. TIMING: there are no deadlines but lead times are enforced. Single-film distribution projects must apply at least 10 weeks before release; all other projects at least 16 weeks before the project starts.
