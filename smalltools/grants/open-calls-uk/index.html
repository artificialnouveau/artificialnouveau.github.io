Funded Open Calls in the UK

Currently 10 active open calls open to applicants in the UK, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. GMAC Film: Little Pictures 2026/27 (Scotland)

    GMAC Film (Glasgow Media Access Centre) · Scotland, UK · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: Up to GBP 6,000 production funding per film, plus training, workshops, industry mentorship and free equipment and resource access

    GMAC Film's Little Pictures scheme funds Scotland-based filmmakers making their first funded short film, with up to GBP 6,000 production funding per film plus a year of training, workshops, industry mentorship and free access to equipment and resources. Fiction, documentary, animation and experimental work are all eligible, and the scheme has produced over 50 films across Scotland in its six years, prioritizing underrepresented voices in the screen sector. ELIGIBILITY: 18+, based anywhere in Scotland, not in full-time education, and not previously commissioned to make a film (Opening Shot alumni excepted); no prior filmmaking experience required, and separate tracks exist for filmmakers and producers. Access support provisions are available for applicants facing additional barriers. Deadline: 9 September 2026, 10am.

  2. Scottish Documentary Institute: Bridging the Gap 2026/27

    Scottish Documentary Institute, with Screen Scotland and Northern Ireland Screen · Scotland and Northern Ireland, UK · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: Up to 4 films receive a full commission with a cash production budget (GBP 8,500 per film in the previous round) plus production and distribution support

    Bridging the Gap is the Scottish Documentary Institute's flagship short documentary talent scheme for new and emerging filmmakers based in Scotland and Northern Ireland. From an initial shortlist of 12 projects, up to 4 films receive full commissions with cash budgets (GBP 8,500 per film in the previous round) plus production and distribution support, with finished films distributed to cinemas and international festivals; BtG shorts have a long track record of festival play and awards. The programme combines professional development, production funding and distribution: shortlisted filmmakers take part in intensive workshops and pitch to a panel of experts. The scheme looks for surprising, challenging and moving submissions that embrace the possibilities of the documentary genre and subvert expectations in form and content. Access support is available for disabled applicants, and information is available in alternative formats on request. Deadline: 9 September 2026, midnight.

  3. Paul Mellon Centre: Exhibition Publication Grants (Autumn 2026 round)

    Paul Mellon Centre for Studies in British Art · London-based funder. Applications open internationally; the research must concern British art, visual culture and/or architectural history. · Deadline: 14 Sep 2026 · Award: Up to GBP 6,000. No application fee.

    The Paul Mellon Centre funds scholarship and dissemination in British art, visual culture and architectural history, from the medieval period to the present and across the relevant geographical and cultural contexts. This is the Autumn 2026 round, the first under the Centre's restructured funding scheme announced earlier in 2026, and all grants in the round close together on Monday 14 September 2026 at 23:59 BST. ELIGIBILITY: the Centre's funding is open to INTERNATIONAL applicants, not just UK-based ones (the exceptions are the MA/MPhil Studentship and Doctoral Scholarship, which are not part of this round). Every applicant must show how the award advances their research on British art, visual culture and/or architecture. SUPPORT: general application guidance, an updated FAQ and the Our Funding Focus page set out what the Centre looks for, and there are writeups of previously successful projects. The grants team will take specific questions or arrange a call at grants@paul-mellon-centre.ac.uk. No application fee. THIS GRANT: up to GBP 6,000 for organisations producing publications tied to their exhibitions and displays, covering practical publishing costs such as image and production costs.

  4. Trust me, I'm an AI/rtist: Open Call for Creative Interventions

    Waag Futurelab (Horizon Europe project HAMLET) · Europe. Engaging with a specific AI Enabler includes an on-site visit to its technology partner: CERTH (Greece), The Audience Agency (Warrington, UK), the Centre of Excellence for Research & Innovation (Cyprus), Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (Greece), or CAUSA Creation (Austria). Final presentations September 2027. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 20,000 commission fee excluding VAT, inclusive of artist fee, travel, accommodation and production costs. Applicants must have a VAT number in Europe. No application fee.

    Waag Futurelab invites artists, theatre makers, choreographers, dancers, game designers and other creative practitioners to engage directly with seven experimental generative AI Enablers being co-created with technologists and dance, theatre and gaming professionals. The call is an invitation into the development process: selected artists get access to the technologies, datasets, interfaces and assumptions that shape these systems while they are still being built. Rather than testing whether the systems work, artists are asked to interrogate why they work the way they do, who they are designed for, what forms of creativity they enable or constrain, and what assumptions about culture, intelligence, authorship, labour, bodies, audiences and society are embedded in them. Waag is looking for individuals and collectives willing to move beyond adoption into investigation, and welcomes proposals that are speculative, critical, poetic, provocative, humorous, performative, constructive or disruptive, including work that challenges, misuses, reimagines, hacks, subverts or plays with the tools, exposes hidden biases, questions environmental costs, or imagines alternative technological futures. THE SEVEN AI ENABLERS, each requiring an on-site visit to its technology partner: the Generic Procedural Content Generation Enabler (text-to-3D assets and avatars, CERTH, Greece); the Data-Driven Cultural Content Adaptation Enabler (audience-aware creative content ideation with ranked candidate ideas, CERTH, Greece); the Cross-Domain Audience Engagement Analytics Enabler (turning heterogeneous cultural participation data into interpretable engagement signals, The Audience Agency, Warrington, UK); the Dance Style Analysis and Generation Enabler (dance video into virtual 3D choreography, dissected into body, effort, shape and space components, Centre of Excellence for Research & Innovation, Cyprus); the Immersive Design Integration Enabler for Dance & Theatre (NLP-driven XR environments with speech-to-3D scene editing, Cyprus); the Music-to-Movement Translation Enabler (audio into a time-aligned feature set spanning genre classification, emotional valence-arousal analysis, rhythm extraction and latent-space projection, feeding motion generation, spatial audio and visual choreography, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, Greece); and the Adaptive Narrative Enabler for Games & Theatre (an evolutionary AI trained on creator-written story elements that generates infinite story variations, explicitly rejecting LLMs because of their dependence on copyrighted or public-domain corpora that encode 19th and early 20th century sexism, racism and imperialism, CAUSA Creation, Austria). ELIGIBILITY: artists, designers, creative technologists, theatre makers, dancers, choreographers and game designers, as individuals or collectives; applicants must hold a European VAT number. An info session with live demonstrations of the AI Enablers runs 31 July 2026, 10:00 to 11:30 CEST (meeting ID 995 7016 2341). Applications go through Homerun using the official Application Template, uploaded as a PDF; read the Guide for Applicants, which contains the full Enabler descriptions, before applying. Questions: zoenie at waag dot org. The AI Enablers and this call are part of the Horizon Europe project HAMLET, which aims to make generative AI accessible to cultural organisations of all sizes. Closes 30 September 2026, 23:59 CEST. Free to apply.

  5. Nordic Currents 2027-2028 Open Call (Nordic Council of Ministers)

    Nordic Council of Ministers · Projects take place primarily in the applicant organisation's home country, in European countries neighbouring the Nordic region: primarily Belgium, Estonia, France, Germany, Ireland, Lithuania, Latvia, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: EUR 100,000 to EUR 800,000 per project. The total pool for the initiative is EUR 800,000, so between one and eight proposals will be funded. Co-financing from local, national or other sources is a prerequisite for eligibility. No application fee.

    Nordic Currents is a new international culture initiative from the Nordic Council of Ministers, running 2027-2028, that invites cultural organisations in European countries neighbouring the Nordic region to join forces with Nordic partners on projects that move audiences, deepen relationships and create lasting connections. Trust, openness, artistic freedom and democratic values are named as its core. The stated aims are to showcase the richness and quality of contemporary Nordic culture and art in neighbouring European countries and to build long-term collaborations between Nordic and European actors. WHO CAN APPLY: established organisations based in European countries neighbouring the Nordic region, primarily Belgium, Estonia, France, Germany, Ireland, Lithuania, Latvia, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom. Eligible applicant types are cultural organisations, institutions, promoters, festivals and producers, as well as embassies and diplomatic missions working in cooperation with arts and culture stakeholders. Every application must have at least two partners from different Nordic countries who stay active throughout the project. WHERE: in a single country or as a cooperation across several countries, but activities must primarily take place in the country where the applying organisation is based; if several European partners outside the Nordic region are involved, activities may also be organised in their countries. Emphasis goes to projects in regions and countries with existing local interest in Nordic art and culture, where Nordic and local partners can build long-term, mutually beneficial collaborations. WHAT THE PROJECT MUST INCLUDE: both artistic programmes and events accessible to local audiences, and platforms for professional exchange and sector development. Public cultural events can be festivals, programmes, series or other curated formats, standalone or attached to existing structures. Projects must also include network-building, knowledge sharing and other forms of sector development. Programming must include representation from all the Nordic countries, with artists and cultural actors from Denmark, the Faroe Islands, Finland, Greenland (Kalaallit Nunaat), Iceland, Norway, Sweden and Aland. Projects must take place between 1 June 2027 and 1 June 2028. FUNDING: applications may request EUR 100,000 to EUR 800,000; the total pool is EUR 800,000, so between one and eight proposals will be successful. Co-financing through local, national or other funding sources is a prerequisite for eligibility. HOW TO APPLY: proposals are submitted in English through an online portal open only between 1 October and 1 November 2026. ASSESSMENT: all eligible proposals go to a professional committee and expert reviewers in the corresponding art fields, judged on artistic quality, connection to the Nordic region, relevance to the artistic fields covered, how the concept incorporates democratic values such as openness, trust and artistic freedom, capacity to implement the collaboration, public appeal for the intended audiences, public accessibility, potential for long-term impact and future collaboration, and organisational and financial capacity. Selected projects are publicly announced in May 2027.

  6. Jerwood Foundation: Arts Grants (2027 round)

    Jerwood Foundation · United Kingdom. Applicants must be UK registered charities or UK limited companies. · Deadline: 17 Mar 2027 · Award: No published minimum or maximum. Previous awards have reached up to GBP 200,000, but most grants are considerably smaller. Funds project and activity costs. No formal match-funding requirement, but a fundraising strategy must be submitted. No application fee.

    The Jerwood Foundation makes grants to UK organisations for public-facing arts activity across theatre, music, dance, visual arts and literature, including artist development programmes, commissions, residencies and projects involving the Jerwood Collection. Awards have historically gone as high as GBP 200,000, though the Foundation states most grants are much smaller. Applications are single-stage: a written proposal of no more than 10 sides of A4 excluding financial statements, sent by email to info@jerwood.org or by post, and it must include a summary sheet giving location, amount requested, timeline and audience figures. Applications missing that summary sheet are rejected automatically. The March 2026 deadline has passed; the next round opens 3 February 2027 at 9am and closes 17 March 2027 at 2pm, with decisions on 28 April 2027 and all applicants notified by 11 May 2027. TIMING RULE: activity funded in that round must start or open to the public between 1 July 2027 and 30 June 2028, so there is a long lead time and retrospective funding is impossible. Grants to individuals are not made, so artists must be supported through a host organisation. Core costs and capital work are explicitly out of scope, which makes this a good fit for discrete festivals, exhibitions and public programmes.

  7. British Council Travel Grant Fund (film and XR, rolling)

    British Council, supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund · Open to UK-based filmmakers and XR creatives (UK residents or citizens, aged 18+). Grants fund travel to international festivals, showcases, labs and co-production forums worldwide. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Varies by strand: short and feature film festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants capped at GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants capped at GBP 2,000. Free to apply.

    The British Council's standing Travel Grant Fund helps early to mid-career UK filmmakers and XR creatives represent their work abroad, build networks, reach new audiences and raise the international profile of new and emerging UK film and immersive talent. Supported by BFI National Lottery funding in partnership with BFI NETWORK and the BFI International Fund. WHAT IT FUNDS: travel to attend international film festivals, showcases, training and development labs, and co-production forums, across five strands: short film festival grants, feature film festival grants, interactive/XR grants (including VR, immersive storytelling experiences and installations), lab grants and co-production forum grants. AMOUNTS: short and feature festival grants GBP 150 to 1,500 (typically up to GBP 750); XR travel-only grants GBP 150 to 1,500; large XR grants GBP 1,500 to 5,000; lab grants up to GBP 6,000; co-production forum grants up to GBP 2,000. WHO CAN APPLY: UK residents or citizens aged 18+; applicants can be directors, producers or writers attached to the selected project. KEY CONDITIONS: the festival, lab or forum must appear on the British Council's Key Lists of eligible events, and the project must be registered on the British Council's UK Project Database. DEADLINE: rolling, no fixed deadlines; submit at least two weeks before the event starts (retrospective applications for past events are not accepted). Free to apply.

  8. Creative Scotland: National Lottery Open Fund for Organisations (rolling)

    Creative Scotland · Activity must take place in Scotland and benefit people in Scotland. Applicant organisations must be constituted and based in Scotland; organisations elsewhere in the UK with a UK-wide remit may apply for activity in Scotland; organisations outside the UK must have a UK-based partner lead the application. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 1,000 to 100,000. Covers a one-off project or production, creative research and development, a longer-term programme of work, or business and organisational development. No stated match-funding requirement and no application fee. Applicants must hold a UK bank account in the same name as the application.

    The National Lottery Open Fund for Organisations is Creative Scotland's main open-access grant for constituted arts, creative and cultural organisations, and it is genuinely rolling with no deadlines. It supports a specific one-off project or production, creative research and development, or a longer-term programme of work, and can also fund business sustainability and organisational development costs. Eligible organisations can apply for GBP 1,000 to 100,000 for projects lasting up to 18 months. TIMING RULES: decision lag is banded by request size, with GBP 1,000 to 20,000 decided within 10 weeks and GBP 20,000 to 100,000 within 14 weeks. The start date must be after the decision date, and the guidance recommends allowing a further 2 to 4 weeks for funding agreements and first payment. Critically for festivals, applicants are barred from starting activity, including advertising events or issuing calls for collaborators, before funding is confirmed, so a public programme should be planned to start roughly 12 to 18 weeks after submission. Only one application may be in assessment at any time. Non-UK organisations can be involved but cannot lead. Current Regularly Funded and Multi-Year Funded Organisations are excluded.

  9. BFI National Lottery Audience Projects Fund

    British Film Institute (BFI National Lottery) · United Kingdom (legally constituted organisations centrally managed in the UK, not more than 50% foreign-owned) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Pilot projects GBP 10,000 to GBP 20,000. Standard projects GBP 20,000 to GBP 500,000 per year. Immersive projects may include up to GBP 150,000 of production costs within the GBP 500,000 cap. Total fund GBP 19.7M over three years. No application fee.

    The Audience Projects Fund distributes GBP 19.7 million of National Lottery money over three years to widen UK audiences for film and immersive work, covering exhibition, distribution, festivals, touring programmes and sector-support initiatives. It reopened in November 2025 and runs continuously with no closing date, which makes it one of the more accessible large BFI pots for organisations working on the audience-facing rather than production side. AWARDS are tiered: small pilot projects receive GBP 10,000 to GBP 20,000, while full projects can receive GBP 20,000 to GBP 500,000 per year, with immersive projects permitted to include up to GBP 150,000 of production cost inside that envelope. ELIGIBILITY is strictly institutional. Applicants must be legally constituted organisations centrally managed in the UK and not more than 50% foreign-owned. Eligible types include limited companies (not classed as 'large' under the Companies Act 2006), community interest companies, LLPs, UK local or statutory authorities, and registered UK charities or trusts. Individual practitioners cannot apply. Applicants must evidence experience in marketing, festivals, distribution, immersive installations, cultural programming, touring or sector support. TIMING: there are no deadlines but lead times are enforced. Single-film distribution projects must apply at least 10 weeks before release; all other projects at least 16 weeks before the project starts.

  10. Forty-Four Words: Reimagining Ghana Visual Artist Commission

    Forty-Four Words, in collaboration with Ekow Barnes · London, UK and Accra, Ghana · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 2,500 artist commission fee per artist (three artists selected), plus a materials budget, curatorial support and research support

    Reimagining Ghana is a UK-Ghana arts project by Forty-Four Words in collaboration with writer Ekow Barnes, supported by the UK Government and Arts Council England, exploring how contemporary creative practice can respond to ideas of Ghanaian identity, memory and representation. Having begun with commissioned non-fiction essays on the question What is Ghana?, the project now commissions three visual artists to create new work informed by research, dialogue and public engagement, running until Summer 2027 with public events in London and Accra. COMMISSION: GBP 2,500 artist fee per artist, a materials budget, curatorial and research support, and dialogue with peers and project collaborators; the commission fee, timeline and core deliverables are guaranteed even as the research evolves. ELIGIBILITY: artists aged 18+ of Ghanaian heritage, available throughout the project period, working across visual arts; the team looks for curiosity, thoughtful engagement with identity, memory, history, place and representation, and openness to collaboration. APPLY via the Google Form with an artist statement, motivation, outline of interests, and one PDF with CV and portfolio (minimum four works). The deadline is rolling; reviewing began 17 August 2026, so early application is advised.
