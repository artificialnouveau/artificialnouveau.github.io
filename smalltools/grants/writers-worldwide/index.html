Open calls

  1. Nieman Fellowships at Harvard, Class of 2028

    Nieman Foundation for Journalism at Harvard · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (residential academic year at Harvard) · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus additional allowances for housing, childcare and health insurance. Fellows audit courses across Harvard and participate in Nieman seminars, master classes and workshops. No application fee.

    The Nieman Foundation brings around two dozen journalists to Harvard each year for two semesters of study, reflection and community, one of the oldest and best-known midcareer journalism fellowships in the world. Fellows design their own course of study, audit classes across Harvard's schools and take part in Nieman's own seminars and master classes; many use the year to deepen a beat such as technology, AI accountability, science or press freedom. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus allowances toward housing, childcare and health insurance. WHO IS ELIGIBLE: working journalists, staff or freelance, in any medium, with at least five years of full-time media experience; open to applicants from any country. Freelancers may use a recent supervising editor for one of the three required recommendation letters. Special fellowships within the program include the Nieman-Berkman Klein Fellowship in Journalism Innovation. TO APPLY: applications for the class of 2028 open in the first week of October 2026 and require two essays (personal statement and study proposal), work samples and three recommendation letters. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026 for international applicants; 31 January 2027 for US citizens.

  2. National Geographic Society: The Human Thread RFP

    National Geographic Society (Center for Human Histories and Cultures, with support from Lilly Endowment Inc.) · Worldwide · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Level I: up to US$20,000 (for applicants new to National Geographic funding, one year to implement). Level II: up to US$100,000 (for more established applicants, two years to implement).

    Request for proposals from the National Geographic Society's Center for Human Histories and Cultures, funded by Lilly Endowment Inc. It seeks storytelling projects that document and illuminate the origins and contemporary expressions of global cultures and religions, and the wider spectrum of human experience, through strong creative and research-based approaches. Open to photographers, filmmakers and authors worldwide. TWO LEVELS: Level I, up to US$20,000 over one year, for applicants new to National Geographic Society funding; Level II, up to US$100,000 over two years, for more established storytellers. ELIGIBILITY: 18 or older; apply in English; submit a portfolio of prior media work. Priority goes to applicants with lived experience in, or established relationships with, the communities they document. You may lead only one proposal at a time, and must have filed final reports for any earlier National Geographic grants you led. Project start dates must be at least six months after the deadline. A grant does not guarantee publication on National Geographic platforms. HOW TO APPLY: only through the National Geographic Funding Portal (register or log in, open the RFP, submit before 1 December 2026); email and mail submissions are not accepted.

  3. In Development x Asterisk Essay Contest: How Will AI Affect Developing Countries? (USD 10,000 top prize)

    In Development and Asterisk Magazine, with a special prize from the African Tech Futures Lab · Worldwide, online submission. Authors based in low- and middle-income countries particularly encouraged. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: Winner USD 10,000 plus publication in Asterisk (print and online) and In Development; two runners-up USD 5,000 each plus publication in Asterisk (online) and In Development; two honourable mentions USD 2,000 each. Separate African Tech Futures Lab prize of USD 2,500 for the best essay by an African national, combinable with the main prizes. No entry fee stated.

    An essay contest run by the development-economics publication In Development and Asterisk Magazine on how transformative AI will affect low- and middle-income countries, and how those countries might respond. Essays can focus on one country, a region or a group of countries; the topic is open, with suggested angles including whether AI drives convergence or divergence between rich and developing countries, labour-market impacts, political economy, the diffusion of health technology, and philanthropic funding from AI companies. Essays should be deeply researched with original analysis; reporting is encouraged but not required. All selected essays are fact-checked and edited before publication. FORMAT: 2,000 to 5,000 words in English, previously unpublished (including on personal blogs or newsletters). Co-authored entries are allowed, but each person may appear on only one entry. AI POLICY: AI may be used for research, for translating an essay written in another language, and for feedback, but submissions containing AI-generated content beyond translation are not accepted. PRIZES: one winner USD 10,000, two runners-up USD 5,000 each, two honourable mentions USD 2,000 each, plus a USD 2,500 African Tech Futures Lab prize for the best essay by an African national. Judges include Clara Collier (Asterisk), Lauren Gilbert (In Development) and Rose Mutiso (African Tech Futures Lab). DEADLINE: 31 December 2026, 11:59 PM Anywhere on Earth, via the Google Form linked from the announcement. Questions: essaycontest@indevelopmentmag.com.

  4. Knight Science Journalism Academic-Year Fellowship 2027-28 (MIT)

    Knight Science Journalism Program at MIT · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (in residence for the academic year). International applicants welcome; MIT sponsors a J-1 visa. · Deadline: 09 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend over 9.5 months, plus USD 5,000 for travel and housing, health insurance and a childcare subsidy. No application fee.

    An academic-year fellowship at MIT for journalists covering science, health, technology or the environment, with seminars, MIT and Harvard courses and a self-directed study project. ELIGIBILITY: full-time staff or freelance journalists with at least 3 consecutive years of science, health, technology or environment coverage; international applicants welcome. Applications open 15 November 2026; deadline 9 January 2027.

  5. C Magazine: Call for Pitches (Art Writing & Criticism)

    C Magazine · Toronto, Canada (open to writers internationally) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: 35-45 cents/word; print reviews flat CAD $430; online reviews CAD $210. No fee.

    Contemporary art magazine commissioning paid art writing and criticism, with pitches accepted on an ongoing basis (online reviews/pitches continuous; themed print issues have set deadlines, e.g. the Spring issue around 15 September 2026). A direct paid outlet for criticism on digital, new-media and AI art. ELIGIBILITY: writers at all experience levels are invited; not restricted to Canada. Mostly pitch-based, with short online reviews accepted unsolicited.

  6. Rest of World: Labor x Tech Reporting Commissions (Year 3)

    Rest of World, underwritten by the Ford Foundation · Remote. Reporting from anywhere in the world except India; writers based in India are not eligible for this series. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per commissioned story of 800 to 1,000 words. No application fee.

    Rest of World is in the third year of its Labor x Tech program, underwritten by the Ford Foundation, commissioning reported stories on the changing nature of work at a moment of upheaval: AI tools and robotics reshaping professions at every rung of society, from self-driving cars replacing drivers to humanoid robots working alongside nurses; lay-offs across the tech industry; and the gig work model continuing to reshape traditional employment. The programme's premise is that this moment needs documenting now, in stories that help prepare society for what comes next. WHAT THEY COMMISSION: reported stories of 800 to 1,000 words from around the world on this beat, at a fee of USD 1,000 per story. WHAT THEY ARE LOOKING FOR, based on previous years: how new technology is transforming existing industries in unique ways and creating unexpected new pockets of employment; how technology is leading to significant job gain, job loss, and government responses to those trends; whether large demographic shifts are following the adoption of new technology or the arrival of new industries; how workplace technology is changing workplace dynamics; how new industries and tools are changing workers' incomes; and the expansion of the gig work model alongside the threats of automation. EXAMPLES of the kind of story they hope to commission, all previously published by Rest of World: workers in Chinese data factories teaching humanoid robots boring tasks; entry-level tech workers describing the AI-fuelled jobpocalypse; Microsoft and Google claiming their data centres create thousands of jobs while their permit filings say otherwise; AI helping judges close cases quickly and lawyers open them quickly; tech workers fuelling a mini baby boom in Taiwan's Silicon Valley; Dubai's robotaxi ambitions coming for human drivers; and the job brokers who run the lives of the migrant workers staffing Taiwan's chip plants. ELIGIBILITY NOTE: the programme is unable to commission reporting in India or from writers based in India for this series. HOW TO PITCH: send 2 to 3 paragraphs laying out the nutgraf of the proposal and explaining how the pitch fits the editorial mission of the programme. Indicate what sources will be used, whether any pre-reporting has been completed, what reporting has already been done on the topic at other outlets, and what the story contributes to the discourse. Pitches go to laborxtech@restofworld.org. Pitching is open on a rolling basis; no closing date is stated.
