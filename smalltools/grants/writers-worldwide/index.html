Open calls

  1. MPI Short Film Lab 2027: narrative short film lab with fellowship grants and a USD 10,000 script grant (theme: For the Greater Good?)

    Moving Picture Institute (MPI) · Mostly virtual, with an in-person summit in Los Angeles, USA, for the directing stage. Applicants may be based anywhere in the world. · Deadline: 29 Nov 2026 · Award: Filmmakers accepted into the lab receive fellowship grants (amount not published). One official selection receives a USD 10,000 grant, which includes MPI acquiring the script for further development. Travel support for the Los Angeles summit is not mentioned. No application fee mentioned.

    The Moving Picture Institute is a production company and talent incubator that makes films about human freedom. Its Short Film Lab takes writer-directors from a concept to a finished short script, and can lead to production as an MPI Original film. THEME 2027: For the Greater Good? Stories exploring the tension between collective goals and individual freedom: when, if ever, should the freedom of one be sacrificed for the benefit of many? The setting can be a family, workplace, school, neighbourhood, political movement, government or an imagined society, and any genre is welcome. Documentary shorts are not considered. PRIORITY goes to projects with fewer than five principal actors and few or no extras, limited locations (ideally one), and stories about characters within the US. WHO: applicants may be based anywhere in the world. Up to three concepts per applicant, each as a separate application. STRUCTURE: (1) a ten-week virtual screenwriting workshop with masterclasses, February to April 2027, taking a treatment to a first draft; (2) a directing workshop for an estimated six to eight filmmakers, June to September 2027, including an in-person summit in Los Angeles to rehearse scenes with actors, ending in a final script and pitch deck; (3) in late autumn 2027 MPI picks at least one official selection. MONEY: accepted filmmakers receive fellowship grants (figure not stated). The official selection receives USD 10,000. RIGHTS: that grant includes MPI's acquisition of the script for further development; the page does not spell out ownership of the script or film beyond that, so read the terms before accepting. TIMELINE: first-round results 18 December 2026; advancing applicants submit a treatment by 4 January 2027; workshop places decided in late January 2027. APPLICATION: a pitch through the online form linked from the lab page. DEADLINE: Sunday 29 November 2026, 11:59 pm Pacific time.

  2. Nieman Fellowships at Harvard, Class of 2028

    Nieman Foundation for Journalism at Harvard · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (residential academic year at Harvard) · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus additional allowances for housing, childcare and health insurance. Fellows audit courses across Harvard and participate in Nieman seminars, master classes and workshops. No application fee.

    The Nieman Foundation brings around two dozen journalists to Harvard each year for two semesters of study, reflection and community, one of the oldest and best-known midcareer journalism fellowships in the world. Fellows design their own course of study, audit classes across Harvard's schools and take part in Nieman's own seminars and master classes; many use the year to deepen a beat such as technology, AI accountability, science or press freedom. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus allowances toward housing, childcare and health insurance. WHO IS ELIGIBLE: working journalists, staff or freelance, in any medium, with at least five years of full-time media experience; open to applicants from any country. Freelancers may use a recent supervising editor for one of the three required recommendation letters. Special fellowships within the program include the Nieman-Berkman Klein Fellowship in Journalism Innovation. TO APPLY: applications for the class of 2028 open in the first week of October 2026 and require two essays (personal statement and study proposal), work samples and three recommendation letters. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026 for international applicants; 31 January 2027 for US citizens.

  3. National Geographic Society: The Human Thread RFP

    National Geographic Society (Center for Human Histories and Cultures, with support from Lilly Endowment Inc.) · Worldwide · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Level I: up to US$20,000 (for applicants new to National Geographic funding, one year to implement). Level II: up to US$100,000 (for more established applicants, two years to implement).

    Request for proposals from the National Geographic Society's Center for Human Histories and Cultures, funded by Lilly Endowment Inc. It seeks storytelling projects that document and illuminate the origins and contemporary expressions of global cultures and religions, and the wider spectrum of human experience, through strong creative and research-based approaches. Open to photographers, filmmakers and authors worldwide. TWO LEVELS: Level I, up to US$20,000 over one year, for applicants new to National Geographic Society funding; Level II, up to US$100,000 over two years, for more established storytellers. ELIGIBILITY: 18 or older; apply in English; submit a portfolio of prior media work. Priority goes to applicants with lived experience in, or established relationships with, the communities they document. You may lead only one proposal at a time, and must have filed final reports for any earlier National Geographic grants you led. Project start dates must be at least six months after the deadline. A grant does not guarantee publication on National Geographic platforms. HOW TO APPLY: only through the National Geographic Funding Portal (register or log in, open the RFP, submit before 1 December 2026); email and mail submissions are not accepted.

  4. Ocean Culture Life: Storytelling Grants 2027 (GBP 1,000, 3,000 or 5,000 for ocean stories in any medium)

    Ocean Culture Life · Worldwide · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Grants of GBP 1,000, GBP 3,000 and GBP 5,000; applicants choose the level that matches their production costs

    Annual grants, now in their fourth year, for creators telling ocean stories in any medium: film, photography, writing, audio, illustration and art. Projects fall under three pillars: stories that connect people to the ocean through wonder or personal encounter, stories that explain something true about the ocean (science, culture, history, a species or a place), and stories that document a threat or a solution in motion and point toward action. Every grant level is open to solo creators and teams, and to single-format or multi-format work. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be members of the Ocean Culture community, which is free to join. Applications are submitted through the Ocean Culture platform and close on 1 December 2026 at midnight GMT. Winners are announced by 31 January 2027.

  5. Bibliothek Andreas Züst: Studio Residency for Duos and Collectives, Summer 2027 (six weeks in the Swiss Alps; room, board and travel covered, living subsidy up to CHF 750)

    Bibliothek Andreas Züst · Alpenhof panorama lodge, St. Anton (Oberegg), Switzerland, at 1,110 m above the Rhine Valley and Lake Constance. Open to national and international cultural workers; no nationality or age rule is stated. · Deadline: 15 Dec 2026 · Award: Accommodation, food and use of the studio are free, and travel costs are covered. An additional subsidy for living costs of up to CHF 750 can be requested separately. No application fee stated.

    The Bibliothek Andreas Züst, the 12,000-volume private library of the Swiss photographer, collector and polymath Andreas Züst, is housed at the Alpenhof lodge above the Rhine Valley and offers studio residency stipends for work in and around the collection. The summer 2027 round is for duos and collectives of up to five people. WHO: national and international cultural workers across disciplines, including visual arts, literature, new media, music, theatre, design, architecture, film, photography and art-related fields. The call is framed for duos and collectives, and the application assumes collaborators. WHAT YOU GET: a private bedroom for each member and a shared studio at the Alpenhof, with accommodation, food and studio use free of charge and travel costs covered. A further subsidy towards living costs of up to CHF 750 can be requested separately. WHEN: 4 July to 15 August 2027. APPLICATION: by email to residency@bibliothekandreaszuest.net as one PDF of at most 7 MB, containing the completed application form (a PDF linked from the residency page), a project description of at most one A4 page, documentation of previous work including CV (five pages in total), a one-page CV for each collaborator, and a one-page statement on your motivation for working together as a collective. Results are announced by the end of January 2027. DEADLINE: 15 December 2026 (no time stated).

  6. The Stringer Awards 2027: multi-year unrestricted cash awards for journalists and creative practitioners exposing abuses of power (aiming for USD 9,000-100,000)

    The Stringer Foundation · Worldwide. Applications accepted in any language. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: Multi-year, unrestricted cash awards; the foundation aims for grants between USD 9,000 and USD 100,000, depending on funds raised. No application fee mentioned.

    The Stringer Foundation gives multi-year, unrestricted cash awards to people who use public media to expose abuses of power and injustices that harm the public, while upholding the truth. WHO: journalism is defined broadly, so the call is open to journalists and creative practitioners across all public media forms, including writing, photography, film, public poetry, video, visual art, music, social media and theatre. WHAT YOU GET: an unrestricted, multi-year cash award. The foundation says it will hand out as much as it raises, with running costs covered by a donor so that every dollar raised outside its endowment goes to recipients; its stated aim is grants of USD 9,000 to 100,000, so the final amounts are not guaranteed. Vetted applicants also get access to security, mental health, publication and legal support. APPLICATION: a Google Form taking an estimated 15 to 20 minutes; applicants may answer in their own language and receive a copy of their submission as confirmation. DEADLINE: 31 December 2026 (time not stated). Details are taken from the application form itself.

  7. Knight Science Journalism Academic-Year Fellowship 2027-28 (MIT)

    Knight Science Journalism Program at MIT · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (in residence for the academic year). International applicants welcome; MIT sponsors a J-1 visa. · Deadline: 09 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend over 9.5 months, plus USD 5,000 for travel and housing, health insurance and a childcare subsidy. No application fee.

    An academic-year fellowship at MIT for journalists covering science, health, technology or the environment, with seminars, MIT and Harvard courses and a self-directed study project. ELIGIBILITY: full-time staff or freelance journalists with at least 3 consecutive years of science, health, technology or environment coverage; international applicants welcome. Applications open 15 November 2026; deadline 9 January 2027.

  8. Newberry Library Artists-in-Residence Fellowships 2027-28, Chicago (USD 3,000 for a one-month research residency)

    Newberry Library · Newberry Library, Chicago, USA. No nationality limit stated. · Deadline: 15 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 3,000 stipend for a one-month residency. No application fee mentioned.

    One-month residencies for artists and writers whose projects need time with the Newberry's collections: maps, prints, manuscripts, printing history, Indigenous studies, genealogy and local history, music and dance archives. The library has hosted creative fellows alongside its scholars since the 1940s. WHO: artists and writers in any medium with a project that depends on research in the collection; no degree requirement and no nationality limit is stated. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 3,000 stipend and a month as a fellow in the reading rooms. APPLICATION: through the library's SlideRoom portal, with forms, a project description and work samples. DEADLINE: Friday 15 January 2027.

  9. New America Fellows Program, Class of 2028 (USD 15,000 to 30,000 unrestricted for nonfiction books, films, podcasts and multimedia in progress)

    New America · Nonresidential: Fellows stay in their jobs and home city. Two required in-person gatherings in Washington, DC, USA (opening, week of 4 October 2027; closing, May 2028), with travel, lodging and most meals covered, plus monthly virtual gatherings. Non-US citizens and applicants based abroad may apply. · Deadline: 01 Feb 2027 · Award: Unrestricted stipend of USD 15,000 to 30,000, typically paid in three instalments directly to the Fellow (not to an institution). The level varies by class and by the funding secured that year. Travel, lodging and most meals for the two Washington gatherings are covered. Team projects receive the same amount as an individual. No application fee stated.

    New America, the Washington think tank, runs a one-year fellowship for writers, journalists, filmmakers and other storytellers finishing an ambitious nonfiction reporting project. WHAT IT SUPPORTS: a book, film, podcast, longform article or multimedia work that is already in progress; the stated purpose is to get projects over the finish line, and concept-stage projects are told to wait for a later cycle. Applicants must have done substantial reporting on the project or have significant experience in its field. Most Fellows have a book contract, a production partner or distributor, or a commissioning publication, but none of these is required, and neither is an agent or a formal proposal. Most supported projects are books, though the programme says it increasingly welcomes other narrative formats. WHAT YOU GET: unrestricted funding of USD 15,000 to 30,000, usually in three instalments, to spend as the Fellow chooses (reporting, travel, fact-checkers, research assistance, multimedia production, or income to free up time); a cohort with monthly virtual sessions; and launch events and newsletter coverage for the finished work. FORMAT: nonresidential, so Fellows can keep a full-time job. The in-person opening gathering (three days in the week of 4 October 2027) and closing gathering (May 2028) in Washington require full attendance, with travel, lodging and most meals paid; Fellows are asked to attend at least 75 percent of the virtual sessions. ELIGIBILITY: non-US citizens and people based abroad for all or part of the year may apply; teams of two or more may apply through one applicant. SELECTIVITY: 10 to 15 Fellows a year; the Class of 2027 had 538 applications for 14 places. Selection looks for a novel perspective on an important issue, a link to New America's mission or a newsworthy or culturally significant topic, a clear plan, and evidence the applicant can finish. APPLICATION: online, with a statement of interest, plan of approach, up to three work samples in total (filmmakers must include a clip, trailer or sizzle reel of the proposed film) and two reference letters requested through the portal. AI POLICY: the application narrative must be written entirely by the applicant; using generative AI to draft or rewrite it is prohibited and can disqualify. TIMELINE: finalists (40 to 50) are told in late April and interviewed; final decisions in early July 2027; the class is announced in September 2027. DEADLINE: Monday 1 February 2027, 11:59 PM EST; reference letters by 8 February 2027, 11:59 PM EST. Apply at https://newamerica.smapply.org/prog/classof2028/.

  10. Rest of World: Labor x Tech Reporting Commissions (Year 3)

    Rest of World, underwritten by the Ford Foundation · Remote. Reporting from anywhere in the world except India; writers based in India are not eligible for this series. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per commissioned story of 800 to 1,000 words. No application fee.

    Rest of World is in the third year of its Labor x Tech program, underwritten by the Ford Foundation, commissioning reported stories on the changing nature of work at a moment of upheaval: AI tools and robotics reshaping professions at every rung of society, from self-driving cars replacing drivers to humanoid robots working alongside nurses; lay-offs across the tech industry; and the gig work model continuing to reshape traditional employment. The programme's premise is that this moment needs documenting now, in stories that help prepare society for what comes next. WHAT THEY COMMISSION: reported stories of 800 to 1,000 words from around the world on this beat, at a fee of USD 1,000 per story. WHAT THEY ARE LOOKING FOR, based on previous years: how new technology is transforming existing industries in unique ways and creating unexpected new pockets of employment; how technology is leading to significant job gain, job loss, and government responses to those trends; whether large demographic shifts are following the adoption of new technology or the arrival of new industries; how workplace technology is changing workplace dynamics; how new industries and tools are changing workers' incomes; and the expansion of the gig work model alongside the threats of automation. EXAMPLES of the kind of story they hope to commission, all previously published by Rest of World: workers in Chinese data factories teaching humanoid robots boring tasks; entry-level tech workers describing the AI-fuelled jobpocalypse; Microsoft and Google claiming their data centres create thousands of jobs while their permit filings say otherwise; AI helping judges close cases quickly and lawyers open them quickly; tech workers fuelling a mini baby boom in Taiwan's Silicon Valley; Dubai's robotaxi ambitions coming for human drivers; and the job brokers who run the lives of the migrant workers staffing Taiwan's chip plants. ELIGIBILITY NOTE: the programme is unable to commission reporting in India or from writers based in India for this series. HOW TO PITCH: send 2 to 3 paragraphs laying out the nutgraf of the proposal and explaining how the pitch fits the editorial mission of the programme. Indicate what sources will be used, whether any pre-reporting has been completed, what reporting has already been done on the topic at other outlets, and what the story contributes to the discourse. Pitches go to laborxtech@restofworld.org. Pitching is open on a rolling basis; no closing date is stated.
