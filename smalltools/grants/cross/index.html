  1. Pulitzer Center: Global South Civil Society Microgrants 2026

    Pulitzer Center · Global South, with the call naming Africa, Latin America and Southeast Asia. Groups based in the United States apply to the separate U.S. Civil Society Microgrant instead. · Deadline: 06 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 2,000 to USD 4,000 per project. Applicants name the amount requested and submit a draft budget. Funds are transferred to the partner after the agreement is signed; partners file a financial narrative and outcomes report at the end but are NOT required to collect detailed receipts. No application fee.

    The Pulitzer Center funds civil society in the Global South to build civic engagement projects around Pulitzer Center-supported journalism: grassroots collectives, community-led movements, ARTISTS and civic coalitions taking published reporting to the communities it concerns. This grant cycle is restricted to the Center's Climate and Environment topics: RAINFOREST (drivers of rainforest degradation, the role of global markets, finance and policy, centring the rights and roles of Indigenous peoples), OCEAN (degradation of marine environments, ocean health and the climate crisis, ocean governance, science-backed and community-led solutions), and CLIMATE AND LABOR (the intersection of climate and labour, including gender, livelihoods, adaptation and community resilience). WHAT COUNTS AS A PROJECT: community engagement and organising that connects reporting with audiences; creative expressions, explicitly including digital campaigns, public performances and creative publication formats; public dialogues bringing together affected communities, journalists, decision-makers and academia; and other innovative uses of the reporting that build on local cultural practices. Every project must use at least one published Pulitzer Center-supported reporting project meaningfully, or involve a Pulitzer Center-supported journalist centrally, and must name a clear target audience. OUT OF SCOPE: journalism and reporting projects themselves (those go to the Center's reporting grants), anything not built on Pulitzer Center-supported reporting, direct advocacy or lobbying, campaigns endorsing political candidates or parties, and for-profit projects. WHO CAN APPLY: grassroots organisations, community educators, civil society organisations and coalitions, youth movements and other groups working at the intersection of journalism, civic rights and active citizenship, with past experience running civic engagement activities in the Global South, proven experience on the named topics, and the operational capacity to manage a microgrant from an international organisation. APPLICATION: a project description of no more than 400 words, the target audience and desired impact, your methodology, a timeline and budget, the lead applicant's CV with a letter of recommendation, and, where activities involve Indigenous peoples or traditional communities, a signed or recorded statement of consent from a community member. Applications are accepted in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French and Indonesian through Submittable. TIMELINE: applications 15 August to 6 September 2026, selection announced mid-September 2026, onboarding October 2026.

In-person attendance required. · Deadline: 07 Sep 2026 · Award: Oral presentations: €100 honorarium (invoice required). Performances: €400 honorarium (invoice required). No submission fee.

    Híbrides is a biennial of artistic research co-organised by the Axolot.cat collective and the DARTS (Design, Art, Technology and Society) research group at the UOC in Barcelona. The biennial brings classification into dialogue with its previous themes of Small Data (community-defined, intentional data collection and analysis) and Embodiment (bodily experience as a foundation of situated, sensory knowledge). Both academic and non-academic contributions are welcome. FORMATS ACCEPTED: Oral Presentations (20 minutes; artistic practice, artistic research, or academic research projects; may be video-recorded for a public archive and developed into short papers); Performances (20-30 minutes; must engage with the physical body, stage, and technology; complete works, including previously presented work; takes place in a multipurpose space with technical support). SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS: online form with abstract (max 1,000 characters), professional biography (max 1,000 characters), and link to representative materials (3-5 images; video and technical rider required for performances). HONORARIA: oral presentations €100; performances €400; invoice required in both cases. Participants must follow the event's code of conduct. Event runs 26-28 November 2026 at UOC Poblenou campus and Convent de Sant Agustí, Barcelona. Deadline: 7 September 2026. Submit at https://hibrides.axolot.cat/opencall.html.

  5. Futures of Listening x KNMI: Earth Immersion, Trembling Planet Residency

    Futures of Listening (FIBER, 4DSOUND, Robin Koek, Art of Sound) in collaboration with KNMI, the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute · Partially self-hosted, partially at the 4DSOUND studio in Amsterdam, and partially at KNMI in De Bilt near Utrecht, Netherlands. · Deadline: 13 Sep 2026 · Award: Total residency fee of EUR 7,125, calculated as 25 commissioned days at a day rate of EUR 285. Six of those 25 days can be spent at the 4DSOUND studio working with their team. Some additional financial support is available if needed, including material and travel costs, but these must be clearly defined and agreed before the residency starts. No application fee.

    One residency for an artist, designer or maker working with sound, exploring spatial sound in relation to seismology, climate and discharge-related acoustics, run with the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute. THE PREMISE: climate change is making major landslides, ice-breakoff events from marine-terminating glaciers, and sound-producing ice movements more common. These generate infrasonic vibrations inaudible to humans that sometimes spread across the entire planet and into the atmosphere. The residency asks how immersive sound techniques can make these events tangible and build meaningful sonic narratives from them. WHAT YOU GET ACCESS TO: scientific data on seismological events, vibrations, infrasound and the movement of land and ice masses, with scientific expertise from KNMI (lead researcher Jelle Assink) and artistic guidance from the Futures of Listening team, relating local events to global impact. RESEARCH QUESTIONS to position your proposal against include how immersive sound can cultivate situated listening to ecological processes and more-than-human encounters, how artistic and scientific methods of working with environmental data can inform each other, and what is gained, lost or ethically at stake when environmental sounds are translated, interpreted or reconstructed through spatial and synthetic means. DELIVERABLE: residents are expected to create a final spatial sound production using the 4DSOUND system. Other outcomes may include immersive installations, data-driven sonic works, audiovisual pieces, software prototypes or reusable methodologies. Experience in art-science environments is welcome but not required; you may instead explain why working in one would benefit your practice. HARD ELIGIBILITY GATE: applicants must be based in the Netherlands AND registered as an artist with the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (KvK). This is not negotiable and rules out non-NL applicants. Futures of Listening is a Netherlands-based platform for spatial sound, immersive experience, performance and installation art, founded by FIBER Festival, 4DSOUND, Robin Koek and Art of Sound at the Royal Conservatoire The Hague, supported by CIIIC and Stimuleringsfonds.

    The Democracy Stories Lab, an initiative of People Powered (a global hub for participatory democracy) in partnership with the Democracy Narratives Alliance, invites creators worldwide to submit visual content -- short videos, illustrations, or graphics -- that tells a compelling story about democracy, voice, fairness, belonging, and community power. Tone is open; serious and comedic approaches are both welcome. SUBMISSIONS: up to two finished visual content pieces per applicant, answering the prompt of what the future would look like if people had a real say in the decisions that shape their lives. Each submission should build on one of five narrative types from the DNA research brief 'How to Talk About Democracy': outcome-oriented (how democracy shows results), intrinsic-value (why democracy is the best choice), collectivist (the importance of diverse voices), individual (how democracy is empowering) and process-oriented (showing democratic institutions work); a short creative brief gives further guidance. Winners announced October-November 2026. Note: the initiative was itself voted into existence through People Powered's participatory budgeting process (PP Decides).
