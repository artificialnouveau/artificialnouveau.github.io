Funded Prizes and Awards in the US

Currently 8 active prizes and awards open to applicants in the US, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Kennedy Center: Access/VSA Emerging Young Artists 2027, Thrive (15 x USD 3,000)

    John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, Office of Accessibility and VSA · USA (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico and territories; right to work in the US required). Career week and exhibition in Washington DC. · Deadline: 19 Nov 2026 · Award: 15 winners each receive a USD 3,000 stipend, an all-expenses-paid career development week in Washington DC (14 to 18 June 2027), and exhibition of one work in DC. No entry fee.

    The Kennedy Center's Access/VSA Emerging Young Artists Program (2027 theme: Thrive) awards 15 young disabled visual artists a USD 3,000 stipend, an all-expenses-paid career development week in Washington DC (14 to 18 June 2027) and exhibition of one work. ELIGIBILITY: aged 16 to 25 on the deadline date; US-based with the right to work in the US; have a disability as defined by the ADA and be willing to disclose it publicly; submitted work made in the last three years and after the onset of disability; no generative-AI work; previous winners ineligible. OBLIGATIONS: attend three virtual workshops (April to June 2027) and the DC week to receive the stipend. APPLICATION: free, via CaFE (artist.callforentry.org); accommodation and alternative-format requests by 30 October 2026. Winners notified by 13 January 2027. Questions: vsainfo@kennedy-center.org. DEADLINE: 19 November 2026, 23:59 ET.

  2. Van Alen Institute: Re:Paris Prize, USD 35,000 travel research fellowship on design in Africa and its diaspora (US-based applicants)

    Van Alen Institute · US-based applicants; international field research travel, with presentations at Van Alen Institute (New York) in Fall 2027 and the second Pan-African Biennale in 2028. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 35,000 travel fellowship; one US-based winner selected through this open call (two Africa-based winners were selected via the 2026 Pan-African Biennale).

    The Re:Paris Prize, run by Van Alen Institute, funds research into how design plays a transformative role in Africa and its diaspora. Each winner receives a USD 35,000 travel fellowship for field research that requires international travel to connect with experts and opportunities. Two Africa-based winners were chosen from the 2026 Pan-African Biennale; a third, US-based winner is selected through this open call. Proposals address one of five themes, including 'Data, algorithms, and the public realm', on how digital infrastructures reshape public space, civic life and spatial perception. Winners present their learnings at Van Alen Institute in Fall 2027 and at the second Pan-African Biennale in 2028. ELIGIBILITY: US-based applicants; further rules on the call page. APPLY via the Airtable form linked from the project page. DEADLINE: Sunday 22 November 2026.

  3. The David Prize 2026/27: USD 200,000 each, no strings attached, for five New Yorkers with ideas for a better New York City

    The David Prize (Walentas Family Foundation) · New York City, USA. For individuals who live and work in one of the five boroughs. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 200,000 each to five individuals, unrestricted, with no metrics or reporting asked of winners. No application fee mentioned.

    The David Prize gives five New Yorkers USD 200,000 each, no strings attached, to pursue an idea that would make New York City better. It is not an arts prize: no field or sector is named, and ideas are judged on being urgent and additive to the city. Artists, technologists, organisers, researchers and anyone else can enter, provided the idea is rooted in New York. WHO: individuals, not organisations, who live and work in one of the five boroughs and can show a track record of extraordinary work. PROCESS: Step 1 is a short open submission with three questions about you and your idea, reviewed on a rolling basis. Selected applicants are invited to Step 2, which takes two to three hours and includes up to four references. Finalists are announced in summer 2027 and interviewed in person. Nominations of other people are accepted all year and roll into the next cycle after the open call closes. MONEY: USD 200,000 per winner, unrestricted; winners are not asked for metrics or reports. APPLICATION: via Submittable. DEADLINE: 30 November 2026 (time not stated on the prize's own pages; one listing gives 6 pm Eastern). Late submissions are not accepted, even for technical problems. Step 1 results towards the end of autumn.

  4. Tag! Queer Shorts Festival 2027: Call for Submissions (Portland)

    Tag! Queer Shorts Festival · Portland, Oregon, USA (Clinton Street Theater). International. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Award categories with cash prizes (amounts not stated): Hispanic/Latine Creativity, Power of Community, Best Erotic Short and Radical Futures Student Film, plus a Festival Director's Award. Modest submission fees on FilmFreeway; fee waivers for historically excluded communities and filmmakers from countries that criminalize queer identities, and discount codes for Pacific Northwest filmmakers.

    Tag! invites LGBTQIA+ filmmakers to submit finished shorts of 15 minutes or under, including credits, in any genre (theme for 2027: "You're It!"). About 40 to 45 shorts are selected. English captions are required; films made with generative AI are not programmed; no works in progress. Submissions open 1 July to 1 December 2026, exclusively via FilmFreeway.

  5. J. Anthony Lukas Work-in-Progress Awards 2027

    Columbia Journalism School and the Nieman Foundation · USA. Requires a fully executed contract with a US-based publisher. · Deadline: 03 Dec 2026 · Award: Two awards of USD 25,000 each. No entry fee for the Work-in-Progress awards.

    The J. Anthony Lukas Work-in-Progress Awards, given jointly by Columbia Journalism School and the Nieman Foundation at Harvard, are open for 2027 submissions. Two annual awards of USD 25,000 each help writers complete a book-length work of narrative nonfiction on a topic of American political or social concern; books on science, technology and media accountability have won in past years. Applicants need a fully executed contract with a US-based publisher for the book in progress. There is no entry fee for the Work-in-Progress awards; note that the Lukas Book Prize and the Mark Lynton History Prize run on the same portal with the same deadline but carry a USD 75 entry fee. TO APPLY: submit via the Columbia Journalism Awards Submittable portal. DEADLINE: 3 December 2026, 11:59 PM ET.

  6. American Documentary Film Fund 2027: AmDocs pitch competition for US documentary projects (up to USD 50,000)

    American Documentary and Animation Film Festival (AmDocs) · Palm Springs, California, USA. In person: finalists must attend the festival to pitch. US citizens and legal residents only. · Deadline: 08 Jan 2027 · Award: Up to USD 50,000 in cash prizes may be distributed each year among the winning project(s); the number of winners and the split are not published. Entry fee USD 60 until 11 December 2026, then USD 70 until 8 January 2027.

    The American Documentary Film Fund is the pitch competition of AmDocs, the American Documentary and Animation Film Festival in Palm Springs. A select group of documentary projects is invited to pitch to an industry panel during the festival, and the fund distributes upwards of USD 50,000 a year to the winning project or projects. WHO: documentary filmmakers who are US citizens or legal residents. New projects and works in progress are both accepted. On the Festhome listing the fund sits under short films of 40 minutes or less. Note that the fund is for documentary projects; animation and music videos are separate festival competition categories with trophies, not fund money. PITCH: finalists show a preview of exactly three minutes and take up to ten minutes of questions from the panel. Attendance at the festival for the pitches and the awards ceremony is mandatory, and winners are announced at the closing ceremony. OBLIGATIONS: winners screen the finished film at AmDocs within a year of completion and add a full-screen credit for the American Documentary Film Fund. APPLICATION: via Festhome, with (1) an outline of the story, (2) a three-minute excerpt or promo, required even if shooting has not started, (3) a budget showing what has been paid and what is needed for completion, and (4) a previous completed work where applicable. DEADLINES: late deadline 11 December 2026 (USD 60), extended deadline 8 January 2027 (USD 70). Notification 5 February 2027. Festival 17 to 21 March 2027.

  7. AT&T Untold Stories 2027 (Tribeca Festival): USD 1.2 million film production prize

    Tribeca Festival and AT&T · USA. Applicants must be 18+ and live in the US or Puerto Rico. Finalists pitch in person at the Tribeca Festival, New York, June 2027. · Deadline: 18 Jan 2027 · Award: One winner receives USD 1,200,000 to produce the film; the four other finalists receive USD 15,000 grants each. No entry fee.

    AT&T Untold Stories, now in its 10th year, is a production prize run by the Tribeca Festival and AT&T that funds a feature film from an underrepresented filmmaker. Industry judges select five projects; their filmmakers pitch to a jury of media professionals at the 2027 Tribeca Festival in June. One project wins USD 1.2 million and the other four finalists receive USD 15,000 each. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be at least 18 and reside in the US or Puerto Rico; each project needs an attached producer who is not the director; a complete English-language screenplay is required, registered with the WGA or the US Copyright Office. Submissions are reviewed on a rolling basis, so applying early helps. Virtual info session: 17 November 2026, 11am ET. Applicants are notified by 3 May 2027. TO APPLY: submit online via the Tribeca Untold Stories page; read the official rules first. DEADLINE: 18 January 2027, 11:59 PM ET.

  8. Hillman Prizes 2027 for Journalism (incl. Broadcast and Documentary)

    The Sidney Hillman Foundation · Open to journalists globally; work must have been widely accessible to a US audience. Ceremony in New York City, May 2027. · Deadline: 30 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 5,000 honorarium and a certificate for each Hillman Prize winner. No entry fee.

    The Hillman Prizes honour journalism in service of the common good, with priority for work with US impact. Five categories: Book (nonfiction); Newspaper Journalism (story, series or multimedia, print or online); Magazine Journalism (longform); Broadcast Journalism (local news, television, documentary, radio or podcast, at least 20 minutes total package length); and Opinion and Analysis (any medium). All entries are also considered for the SEIU Award for Reporting on Racial and Economic Justice. Winners receive a USD 5,000 honorarium and a certificate at a ceremony in New York City in May 2027; winners announced April 2027. ELIGIBILITY: work published in 2026; open to journalists globally, but the work must have been widely accessible to a US audience. Self-nomination allowed, or entry by an editor or outlet; no limit per outlet; enter each work in one category only. No entry fee. TO APPLY: the call for entries opens 1 December 2026 via the foundation's Submittable portal (fill in the form on the nominations page). DEADLINE: 30 January 2027, 11:59 PM.
