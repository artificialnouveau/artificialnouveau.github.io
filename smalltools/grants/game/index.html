Game Design Grants, Fellowships and Residencies

Currently 21 active paid game design grants, fellowships and residencies. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. The Strong National Museum of Play: Research Fellowships, January to May 2027 cycle (video game history and play; USD 750/week)

    The Strong National Museum of Play (incl. ICHEG, International Center for the History of Electronic Games) · Rochester, New York, USA. In person, full time. Applicants must live more than 50 miles from The Strong. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 750 per week for up to 4 weeks (Strong and Valentine/Cosman fellowships); G. Rollie Adams USD 750/week for up to 3 weeks; Frances M. Williams USD 750 flat. No application fee.

    Short research residencies for work with The Strong's collections: the Brian Sutton-Smith Library and Archives of Play, the museum's artifacts, and the ICHEG electronic and video game holdings. The Strong and Valentine/Cosman fellowships explicitly cover video and electronic games, making this a digital-heritage and games-as-culture fit. ELIGIBILITY: academics, independent scholars, museum scholars, and Master's or PhD students living more than 50 miles away. OFFER: USD 750 per week. APPLY by email to researchfellowships@museumofplay.org with a cover letter (dates and weeks requested), a project description of up to 1,200 words, a 500-word summary of the Strong materials needed, a CV, and two referee letters sent directly. Fellows research full time on site and write a blog post afterwards. Three cycles a year (15 April, 15 August, 15 November). DEADLINE: 15 November 2026; awards announced by mid-December.

  2. Zoud GameLab 2026: Global Game Jam financial-literacy jam (prize and funding pool up to USD 140,000)

    Zoud (UAE National Financial Wellbeing and Sustainability Initiative) with Global Game Jam · Online, worldwide. Open to participants aged 18 and over in any country; solo developers, teams of any size and studios. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: Shared prize and development-support funding pool of up to USD 140,000 across best overall game, runner-up, third place, financial-literacy impact, regional perspective, compelling gameplay and a Best Game from the UAE award; individual prize amounts are not published. No entry fee.

    Zoud GameLab is a month-long virtual game jam run by Global Game Jam and Zoud, the UAE's national financial wellbeing initiative, under the theme Make FinLit Fun. Participants build a game that integrates financial literacy in a meaningful way; this is part of the judging criteria, and a scoring rubric is published on the jam page. WHO: anyone aged 18 or over by 15 November 2026, anywhere in the world; solo developers, teams of any size and studios, each member registering individually. Prior Global Game Jam participation is not required. WHAT YOU GET: a share of up to USD 140,000 in prizes and development-support funding; entrants who want to be considered for development funding submit the game, a concept or design document, a two-minute gameplay video and a game budget. A pre-jam learning programme of webinars, resources and expert sessions ran from September. APPLICATION: register and submit on itch.io (and the Global Game Jam Discord). DEADLINE: 15 November 2026 at 23:59 anywhere on earth (the itch.io counter may show 16 November in some time zones).

  4. Creative Saskatchewan: Digital Game Development Grant (Pre-Production and Production)

    Creative Saskatchewan · Saskatchewan, Canada. Projects must be at least 51% Saskatchewan-owned. · Deadline: 18 Nov 2026 · Award: Up to CAD 50,000 per fiscal year or 50% of the cash budget, whichever is lower (lifetime cap CAD 100,000 or 50%). Non-repayable. No application fee.

    Creative Saskatchewan funds Saskatchewan-owned video game projects with commercial intent, through a Pre-Production stream (prototype or vertical slice) and a Production stream (a market-ready game). Individuals or corporations may apply; the applicant must own the copyright and share in revenue, the project must be at least 51% Saskatchewan-owned, and it must be fully financed (the grant covers up to 50% of the cash budget) before funds are released. Apply by 18 November 2026, 4:00 pm Saskatchewan time.

  5. Rhizome Microgrants 2026 (USD 500-1,500 for digital art and networked culture projects)

    Rhizome · Online. Open call for web-based and networked projects; no location requirement is stated in the announcement. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 500 to 1,500 per project. Additional annual grants through the Terrell Davis fund (with Joygill Moriah and LVLZ Healing Hub) for work speaking to Black and Afrodiasporic experiences; projects in any category can opt in.

    Rhizome's annual microgrants, running since 2014, fund new creative projects related to digital art and networked culture, especially web-based works. Past awardees include a multilingual archive of printed "Internet directory" books, a browser-based adventure game about caring for "small but alive" creatures, and a free pop-up school in Portland. THIS YEAR'S CATEGORIES: PERMANENCE, with Larva Labs (digital projects built to endure); MISUSE (projects that misuse the technologies they depend on); HANDMADE (non-AI works and handmade digital practices); and CO-OP (cultural artifacts that result from collective online behaviour). TERRELL DAVIS FUND: with Joygill Moriah and Liz Mputu (LVLZ Healing Hub), Rhizome offers additional grants honouring the legacy of digital artist and curator Terrell Davis, for work speaking to Black and Afrodiasporic experiences; projects in any category may opt in. WHO CAN APPLY: artists, hackers, researchers, game masters, designers, storytellers, tinkerers, community builders, scientists, archivists and culture workers, regardless of technical or demographic background. JURY: Rhizome staff with guest jurors Larva Labs and Natalie Stone, Joygill Moriah, Herdimas Anggara and Annie Rau. DEADLINE: 20 November 2026.

  6. Indigenous Screen Office: Interactive and Immersive Program 2026-27 (XR, games, apps; with the CMF)

    Indigenous Screen Office (ISO-BEA), in partnership with the Canada Media Fund · Canada. Indigenous creators who are Canadian citizens or permanent residents. · Deadline: 02 Dec 2026 · Award: Up to CAD 50,000 for development and CAD 100,000 for production; CMF-eligible companies can get up to CAD 50,000 / 100,000 more. No application fee stated.

    The Indigenous Screen Office's Interactive and Immersive Program, delivered with the Canada Media Fund, funds development and production of Indigenous-led interactive and immersive screen content, including games, apps and XR. AMOUNTS: up to CAD 50,000 for development and up to CAD 100,000 for production; individuals and collectives are capped at CAD 100,000. CMF-eligible projects (for-profit, Canadian-controlled companies with a head office in Canada) approved by the ISO can receive up to a further CAD 50,000 for development and CAD 100,000 for production. Individuals and sole proprietors must reside in Canada, work in screen-based content and keep financial and creative control, with two of three key creatives Indigenous. Companies must be at least 51% Indigenous-owned and registered in Canada; collectives must be Indigenous-led with a signed collective agreement. Any career level; emerging creators need at least one prior credit in their key role. One application per applicant per fiscal year. TIMELINE: opened 3 November 2026; closes 2 December 2026, 5:00 pm PST; notifications 3 to 4 months after closing. Extension requests at least 10 business days before the deadline.

  8. NOVA Games Foundation Fellows, inaugural round (USD 100,000 to build an original game prototype)

    NOVA Games Foundation · Not specified. Open only to students and recent graduates of 59 programmes at 13 partner universities worldwide. · Deadline: 10 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 100,000 non-repayable grant, no equity taken; press reports some fellows can receive up to USD 500,000 more after milestone reviews. Expected window 7 December 2026 to 10 January 2027.

  9. Creative Europe MEDIA: Innovative Tools and Business Models 2027 (EUR 14 million call, AI, data and virtual worlds for audiovisual)

    Creative Europe MEDIA (European Commission / EACEA) · EU Member States, EEA and countries associated to Creative Europe MEDIA. For organisations (legal entities) established in a MEDIA country; natural persons cannot apply. · Deadline: 14 Jan 2027 · Award: Total call budget EUR 14,000,000; no fixed cap per project; 80 percent funding rate. No application fee.

    The MEDIA strand's Innovative Tools and Business Models call (CREA-MEDIA-2027-INNOVBUSMOD) funds tools and business models that strengthen the competitiveness of the European audiovisual and video games industries. WHAT IT FUNDS: tools for subtitling, accessibility, discoverability and recommendation; automated rights management and data analytics; business models linking festivals, cinemas and VOD; new modes of production, financing or distribution using AI, big data, blockchain, virtual worlds or NFTs; and greening the audiovisual and video games industry. WHO: a single organisation or a consortium of two or more, established in a MEDIA country; individuals cannot apply. WHAT YOU GET: grants at an 80 percent funding rate from a total budget of EUR 14,000,000. APPLICATION: via the EU Funding and Tenders portal. DEADLINE: 14 January 2027, 17:00 CET (Brussels).

WHAT IT FUNDS: concept and prototype development up to a first playable, vertical slice, alpha or demo; production-phase work is excluded, as are puzzle, sports, racing, quiz and party genres. WHO: European video game production companies, XR studios and audiovisual production companies whose main activity is game or software development or audiovisual production, established in a country fully participating in MEDIA and majority-owned by nationals of such countries. The coordinator must have produced or developed a game or immersive experience commercially distributed between 1 January 2024 and the deadline, evidenced by a sales report (work-for-hire and Early Access titles do not count), and must hold the majority of rights. WHAT YOU GET: a lump sum of up to EUR 200,000 (60 percent of the estimated budget) from a EUR 13.5 million pot. APPLICATION: single stage via the EU Funding and Tenders Portal. DEADLINE: 2 February 2027 at 17:00 Brussels time.

  20. Game Design Emerging Talents: She Got Game

    Pro Helvetia (Swiss Arts Council) · Remote/international mentoring programme; applicants must be based in one of Pro Helvetia's liaison-office regions (Arab region, East Asia, South America, South Asia and Vietnam, and West, Central, East and Southern Africa). · Deadline: 01 Sep 2027 · Award: CHF 3,000 toward the production of a prototype or demo version, plus individual coaching and community access; potential access to international trade fairs. When attending trade fairs, applicants with childcare responsibilities can request up to CHF 250 per week for childcare or children's travel expenses. No application fee.

    She Got Game is a Pro Helvetia mentoring programme promoting equal opportunities in the Swiss and international game industry, supporting the representation of women in management positions through dialogue with experienced international experts from the game industry. The call is aimed at professional game designers who identify as women from the regions of Pro Helvetia's liaison offices: the Arab region, East Asia, South America, South Asia and Vietnam, and West, Central, East and Southern Africa. ELIGIBILITY: Emerging Talents are game developers or game studios whose relevant training or registration (self-employed, sole proprietorship, or company) dates back less than seven years. Selection criteria include digital content for devices such as computers, consoles, mobile devices, or mixed-reality devices requiring a high level of interaction; clearly defined objectives in terms of participation and career; originality, feasibility, degree of innovation, and marketability of the project; and project leaders holding the copyright and rights of use. Excluded: commissioned work and projects that are part of an advertising or promotional campaign, and purely technological projects without obvious narrative and/or aesthetic content. WHAT IS OFFERED: individual coaching and input talks on project development, business strategy and finances, marketing, PR, and pitch training; involvement in a growing community of female game developers; potential access to international trade fairs; CHF 3,000 toward the production of a prototype or demo version; and up to CHF 250 per week toward childcare or children's travel expenses when attending international trade fairs (a budget must be presented in advance). All modules are conducted in English; the programme is a time-consuming commitment of roughly 30 hours over six months, plus potential additional time attending events or trade fairs. APPLICATION: submit a short CV (including education/training, nationality, and residency status in Switzerland), a short description of the current project (type and genre, gameplay, platform, development status, target audience, with relevant visual material, max. 10 A4 pages), a project portfolio, and expectations regarding the programme's objectives. All documents should be submitted in English; if not possible, applications may be submitted in German, French, or Italian. KEY DATES: applications open 1 July 2026; submission deadline 1 September 2026, 23:59 CEST; notification of decision in November 2026; earliest project start 1 January 2027. Apply via the Pro Helvetia application portal.

  21. UK Games Fund: Content Fund (rolling expressions of interest)

    UK Games Fund (UK Games Talent and Finance CIC, backed by DCMS) · United Kingdom. Applicant studios must be UK-registered SMEs with PAYE employees in games development. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 100,000 to 250,000 per project as a non-repayable grant for commercial games-for-entertainment content. Paid 70/30: the final 30 percent is released once third-party leverage funding is secured within the same financial year. No application fee.

    The Content Fund is the UK Games Fund's larger grant strand, awarding GBP 100,000 to 250,000 in non-repayable grants to develop commercial games-for-entertainment content with mass-market potential. ELIGIBILITY: applicant companies must be UK-registered SMEs with PAYE employees working in games development, owned by UK-based founder-directors as significant shareholders, not facing acute working capital problems, and founded with a rationale beyond securing this grant. The project must already have a prototype, and the IP must be owned by the applicant or appropriately licensed. Eligible costs cover staff and contractor expenses for hands-on development only; gambling content is excluded. FINANCES: grants are paid on a 70/30 split, with 30 percent withheld until the studio secures additional third-party (leverage) funding within the same financial year, so applicants should have a co-financing route in mind. HOW TO APPLY: expressions of interest are submitted via the Content Fund site and are open on a rolling basis, nominally until 31 March 2029, with early closure possible if demand is high. The UK Games Fund also runs the Prototype Fund (up to GBP 100,000, periodic rounds) and Starter Fund (up to GBP 20,000 for solo developers) for earlier-stage work.
