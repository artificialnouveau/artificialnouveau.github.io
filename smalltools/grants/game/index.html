Game Design Grants, Fellowships and Residencies

Open calls

  1. Processing Foundation Fellowship 2027

    Processing Foundation · USA only. The Collective Intelligence track is remote (US-based); Gaming the System is hybrid in Los Angeles; DIY Computing is hybrid in New York City. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 10,000 stipend over the 8-month fellowship, plus open-source software mentorship, cohort collaboration, professional development and public presentation opportunities. No application fee.

    The Processing Foundation's 2027 fellowship cycle supports artists, educators and creative technologists working on open-source projects, with a USD 10,000 stipend over an 8-month fellowship period. THREE TRACKS: Collective Intelligence (remote, US-based), co-designing AI education tools with youth, aimed at educators with software development or AI/ML experience; Gaming the System (hybrid, Los Angeles), building an open-source toolkit for video game design with underserved communities, for game makers and creative coders based in LA; and DIY Computing (hybrid, New York City), developing open-source hardware and software environments for custom computers, for early-career hardware artists in NYC (applicants currently enrolled in degree programs are ineligible for this track). ELIGIBILITY: US applicants only; hybrid tracks require residency in LA or NYC respectively. International applicants are pointed to the Foundation's Microgrants and Processing Community Day instead. APPLICATION: via nomination form, selecting one track; self-nominations are allowed. Selection by a panel of the Processing Foundation team and external reviewers, judging strength of the project, fit with the Foundation's mission and potential impact. TIMELINE: nominations open 7 October 2026, close 1 November 2026 at 11:59 PM PST; fellows notified 14 December 2026; fellowship runs 25 January to 26 September 2027.

  2. Playful Obscura 2027: Arthouse Games Residency Africa (Addis Ababa, Goethe-Institut and A MAZE.)

    Goethe-Institut Addis Abeba, with Creative HUB Ethiopia, A MAZE., British Council and Alliance Ethio-Francaise · Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. For artists and game developers based in Africa working in playful media and arthouse games. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Three-week funded residency hosting two artists at a time. 2027 financial terms were not published in the call excerpt we could verify; the 2026 Accra edition provided accommodation, a production budget of up to EUR 1,000 and a EUR 300 stipend. No application fee.

    Playful Obscura is the Goethe-Institut's arthouse games residency for Africa, co-run with the Berlin playful-media festival A MAZE. After editions in Ho (2025) and Accra (2026), the third edition moves to Addis Ababa with Creative HUB Ethiopia, the British Council and Alliance Ethio-Francaise. WHAT IT IS: a three-week residency hosting two artists simultaneously to develop experimental, artistic games and playful media, with mentoring from the A MAZE. network and a public showcase. WHO: artists, game developers and creative technologists based in Africa who can work independently on a project; the 2026 call drew 77 applications from 16 African countries. WHAT YOU GET: travel and accommodation plus production support; the 2026 edition offered a production budget of up to EUR 1,000 and a EUR 300 stipend, and the 2027 figures should be confirmed in the full call. APPLICATION: via the Goethe-Institut South Africa Playful Obscura page. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026.

  3. Screen Australia Future Leaders Delegation: BitSummit 2027 (Kyoto)

    Screen Australia · Delegation to BitSummit 2027 in Kyoto, Japan (21-23 May 2027), with a pre-departure workshop in Melbourne. Open to Australian citizens or permanent residents, or incorporated Australian companies. · Deadline: 05 Nov 2026 · Award: AUD 6,000 per delegate towards flights, accommodation, event tickets, ground transportation and meals, plus covered travel costs for the pre-departure workshop and the opportunity to exhibit a game on the Australia stand. Up to eight delegates.

    Screen Australia's Future Leaders Delegation sends up to eight early-to-mid career Australian gamemakers to BitSummit 2027 in Kyoto, Japan (21-23 May 2027), one of Asia's leading independent games festivals. SUPPORT: AUD 6,000 per delegate towards flights, accommodation, event tickets, ground transportation and meals; travel costs for a mandatory pre-departure workshop in Melbourne (mid-April 2027) are covered separately; delegates can exhibit their game as part of an Australia stand. ELIGIBILITY: Australian citizens or permanent residents (individuals) or incorporated Australian companies with an active ABN and GST registration; must have released and/or published at least one game on a public platform; must be actively developing a game ready for public presentation by May 2027; must be available for both the workshop and event dates; must not have previously participated in a Screen Australia Future Leaders Delegation. Assessment weighs project quality, career relevance, anticipated impact, and commitment to diversity and inclusion. HOW TO APPLY: via Screen Australia's funding portal. Decisions announced by 11 December 2026. DEADLINE: 5 November 2026 at 5pm AEST.

  4. Realities in Transition RiT2 Open Call #3: Uncharted Grounds, a VRChat world residency with L.E.V. Festival (EUR 10,000 fee plus up to EUR 15,000 production)

    Realities in Transition (RiT2, Creative Europe), with L.E.V. Festival · Online residency, with a Test Lab at L.E.V. Gijon and a premiere at L.E.V. Matadero (Madrid), Spain. Check the RiT residencies page for country eligibility. · Deadline: 06 Nov 2026 · Award: EUR 10,000 gross artist fee per selected artist (paid in three instalments) plus up to EUR 15,000 gross production budget shared among the team. No travel or accommodation included. No application fee mentioned.

    Realities in Transition (RiT2), a Creative Europe XR cooperation project, and host partner L.E.V. Festival invite artists with VR development experience to co-create a walkable VRChat world conceived as a voyage, with 5 to 7 environments, 3 to 5 avatars and optional NPC players; events, social worlds and theatre pieces are not wanted. Three individual artists are selected and work together with a creative technologist, each indicating the skills they bring (for example Unity development, 3D art, animation, sound art or narrative writing) with portfolio examples. WHAT YOU GET: an artist fee of EUR 10,000 gross per artist (EUR 6,000 on signing, EUR 2,000 after the Test Lab, EUR 2,000 at the end) and a production budget of up to EUR 15,000 gross for working hours or external assistance. The residency is fully online from January to June 2027, with a Test Lab at L.E.V. Gijon in May 2027 and a premiere at L.E.V. Matadero in September 2027; no travel or accommodation is included. APPLICATION: online form linked from the call page. DEADLINE (extended): 6 November 2026, 23:59 CEST.

  5. Zoud GameLab 2026: Global Game Jam financial-literacy jam (prize and funding pool up to USD 140,000)

    Zoud (UAE National Financial Wellbeing and Sustainability Initiative) with Global Game Jam · Online, worldwide. Open to participants aged 18 and over in any country; solo developers, teams of any size and studios. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: Shared prize and development-support funding pool of up to USD 140,000 across best overall game, runner-up, third place, financial-literacy impact, regional perspective, compelling gameplay and a Best Game from the UAE award; individual prize amounts are not published. No entry fee.

    Zoud GameLab is a month-long virtual game jam run by Global Game Jam and Zoud, the UAE's national financial wellbeing initiative, under the theme Make FinLit Fun. Participants build a game that integrates financial literacy in a meaningful way; this is part of the judging criteria, and a scoring rubric is published on the jam page. WHO: anyone aged 18 or over by 15 November 2026, anywhere in the world; solo developers, teams of any size and studios, each member registering individually. Prior Global Game Jam participation is not required. WHAT YOU GET: a share of up to USD 140,000 in prizes and development-support funding; entrants who want to be considered for development funding submit the game, a concept or design document, a two-minute gameplay video and a game budget. A pre-jam learning programme of webinars, resources and expert sessions ran from September. APPLICATION: register and submit on itch.io (and the Global Game Jam Discord). DEADLINE: 15 November 2026 at 23:59 anywhere on earth (the itch.io counter may show 16 November in some time zones).

  6. BIG Festival at gamescom latam 2027: free indie game submissions (Sao Paulo, awards with cash prizes)

    gamescom latam / BIG Festival · Sao Paulo, Brazil (Distrito Anhembi). Open to independent developers anywhere in the world. · Deadline: 15 Jan 2027 · Award: Free submission and a no-cost exhibition slot for selected games, plus eligibility for the BIG Festival awards. 2027 prize amounts are not yet published; in 2025 Best Game carried BRL 5,000 and in 2026 the WINGS Award carried EUR 2,000 in cash.

    BIG Festival, the indie games competition inside gamescom latam in Sao Paulo, has opened free submissions for its 2027 edition. The 2026 edition drew a record 966 submissions from 75 countries. WHO: independent developers and studios anywhere in the world, with a playable game or demo; see the official rules for category requirements (BIG Starter, BIG Highlight and the WINGS Award for teams where women and marginalised groups hold prominent roles). WHAT YOU GET: selected titles are exhibited at no cost on the show floor, playtested by visitors, and compete for the BIG Festival awards; past editions paid cash to winners (BRL 5,000 for Best Game 2025, EUR 2,000 for the 2026 WINGS Award). A separate free call for conference speakers closes 18 January 2027. APPLICATION: via the gamescom latam website. DEADLINE: 15 January 2027 at 21:59 Eastern time.

  7. Creative Europe MEDIA: European Video Games and Immersive Content Development 2027 (up to EUR 200,000)

    Creative Europe MEDIA (European Commission / EACEA) · EU Member States, EEA and countries associated to Creative Europe MEDIA. For game and XR production companies established and majority-owned in a MEDIA country. · Deadline: 02 Feb 2027 · Award: Lump-sum grant of up to EUR 200,000 per project, set at 60 percent of the estimated budget. Total call budget EUR 13,500,000. No application fee.

    The MEDIA strand's annual development scheme for video games and immersive content (call CREA-MEDIA-2027-DEVVGIM) funds the pre-production of interactive narrative works with original content and quality gameplay intended for commercial release on PC, console, mobile or XR platforms. WHAT IT FUNDS: concept and prototype development up to a first playable, vertical slice, alpha or demo; production-phase work is excluded, as are puzzle, sports, racing, quiz and party genres. WHO: European video game production companies, XR studios and audiovisual production companies whose main activity is game or software development or audiovisual production, established in a country fully participating in MEDIA and majority-owned by nationals of such countries. Publishers and natural persons cannot apply. The coordinator must have produced or developed a game or immersive experience commercially distributed between 1 January 2024 and the deadline, evidenced by a sales report (work-for-hire and Early Access titles do not count), and must hold the majority of rights. WHAT YOU GET: a lump sum of up to EUR 200,000 (60 percent of the estimated budget) from a EUR 13.5 million pot. APPLICATION: single stage via the EU Funding and Tenders Portal. DEADLINE: 2 February 2027 at 17:00 Brussels time.

  10. Game Design Emerging Talents: Pre-Production

    Pro Helvetia (Swiss Arts Council) · Switzerland-based emerging game designers and studios. · Deadline: 01 Mar 2027 · Award: Up to CHF 20,000, plus possible additional coaching. No application fee.

    Pro Helvetia's Game Design Emerging Talents pre-production grant supports early-career game designers in developing playable prototypes for digital games. Emerging Talents are game developers or studios whose game-design education or company foundation dates back less than seven years. ELIGIBILITY: requires a connection to Switzerland (Swiss citizenship, or permanent residency with regular artistic activity in Switzerland); not open to practitioners without a Swiss connection. At least 50 percent of participants must be Swiss citizens or permanent residents whose place of work is in Switzerland. AMOUNT: up to CHF 20,000, plus possible additional coaching. Deadlines twice a year: 1 March (23:59 CET) and 1 September (23:59 CEST). Apply via the Pro Helvetia portal.

  13. Game Design Emerging Talents: She Got Game

    Pro Helvetia (Swiss Arts Council) · Remote/international mentoring programme; applicants must be based in one of Pro Helvetia's liaison-office regions (Arab region, East Asia, South America, South Asia and Vietnam, and West, Central, East and Southern Africa). · Deadline: 01 Sep 2027 · Award: CHF 3,000 toward the production of a prototype or demo version, plus individual coaching and community access; potential access to international trade fairs. When attending trade fairs, applicants with childcare responsibilities can request up to CHF 250 per week for childcare or children's travel expenses. No application fee.

    She Got Game is a Pro Helvetia mentoring programme promoting equal opportunities in the Swiss and international game industry, supporting the representation of women in management positions through dialogue with experienced international experts from the game industry. The call is aimed at professional game designers who identify as women from the regions of Pro Helvetia's liaison offices: the Arab region, East Asia, South America, South Asia and Vietnam, and West, Central, East and Southern Africa. ELIGIBILITY: Emerging Talents are game developers or game studios whose relevant training or registration (self-employed, sole proprietorship, or company) dates back less than seven years. Selection criteria include digital content for devices such as computers, consoles, mobile devices, or mixed-reality devices requiring a high level of interaction; clearly defined objectives in terms of participation and career; originality, feasibility, degree of innovation, and marketability of the project; and project leaders holding the copyright and rights of use. Excluded: commissioned work and projects that are part of an advertising or promotional campaign, and purely technological projects without obvious narrative and/or aesthetic content. WHAT IS OFFERED: individual coaching and input talks on project development, business strategy and finances, marketing, PR, and pitch training; involvement in a growing community of female game developers; potential access to international trade fairs; CHF 3,000 toward the production of a prototype or demo version; and up to CHF 250 per week toward childcare or children's travel expenses when attending international trade fairs (a budget must be presented in advance). All modules are conducted in English; the programme is a time-consuming commitment of roughly 30 hours over six months, plus potential additional time attending events or trade fairs. APPLICATION: submit a short CV (including education/training, nationality, and residency status in Switzerland), a short description of the current project (type and genre, gameplay, platform, development status, target audience, with relevant visual material, max. 10 A4 pages), a project portfolio, and expectations regarding the programme's objectives. All documents should be submitted in English; if not possible, applications may be submitted in German, French, or Italian. KEY DATES: applications open 1 July 2026; submission deadline 1 September 2026, 23:59 CEST; notification of decision in November 2026; earliest project start 1 January 2027. Apply via the Pro Helvetia application portal.

  15. UK Games Fund: Content Fund (rolling expressions of interest)

    UK Games Fund (UK Games Talent and Finance CIC, backed by DCMS) · United Kingdom. Applicant studios must be UK-registered SMEs with PAYE employees in games development. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 100,000 to 250,000 per project as a non-repayable grant for commercial games-for-entertainment content. Paid 70/30: the final 30 percent is released once third-party leverage funding is secured within the same financial year. No application fee.

    The Content Fund is the UK Games Fund's larger grant strand, awarding GBP 100,000 to 250,000 in non-repayable grants to develop commercial games-for-entertainment content with mass-market potential. ELIGIBILITY: applicant companies must be UK-registered SMEs with PAYE employees working in games development, owned by UK-based founder-directors as significant shareholders, not facing acute working capital problems, and founded with a rationale beyond securing this grant. The project must already have a prototype, and the IP must be owned by the applicant or appropriately licensed. Eligible costs cover staff and contractor expenses for hands-on development only; gambling content is excluded. FINANCES: grants are paid on a 70/30 split, with 30 percent withheld until the studio secures additional third-party (leverage) funding within the same financial year, so applicants should have a co-financing route in mind. HOW TO APPLY: expressions of interest are submitted via the Content Fund site and are open on a rolling basis, nominally until 31 March 2029, with early closure possible if demand is high. The UK Games Fund also runs the Prototype Fund (up to GBP 100,000, periodic rounds) and Starter Fund (up to GBP 20,000 for solo developers) for earlier-stage work.
