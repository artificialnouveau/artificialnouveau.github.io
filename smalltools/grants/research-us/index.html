Research and Journalism Grants in the US

Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions.     Federation of American Scientists (Day One Project) · Primarily remote, US-policy focused, with two mandatory in-person events in the US (California retreat 4-7 November 2026; Washington DC capstone the week of 22 February 2027). · Deadline: 07 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend for the part-time fellowship (about 5 hours per week), plus a USD 1,000 merit award for the most actionable policy memo and advocacy plan. Runs 30 September 2026 to 28 February 2027 at about 5 hours per week. DEADLINE: 7 September 2026, 11:59PM ET.

  2. New_ Public Entrepreneur in Residence, Pilot Cohort

    New_ Public · Remote, with an in-person kickoff retreat in Philadelphia, USA (19 to 22 October 2026). Open to applicants based in the United States or Canada. · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 22,500, paid in three monthly installments across the residency, each contingent on continued active participation. Plus USD 1,500 toward an AI tool of the resident's choice for coding, prototyping or design. Two residents are selected in the pilot cohort. Also provided: hands-on product and strategy support from senior New_ Public staff and outside experts; access to New_ Public's five years of original research on moderation, governance, community stewardship and platform design, plus direct access to the researchers behind it; and introductions across New_ Public's network of major platforms, startups, university researchers and funders for hiring, fundraising, early user testing and community partnerships. Residents must submit a Form W-9 or W-8BEN to receive the stipend. No application fee.

    New_ Public is piloting a virtual Entrepreneur in Residence programme for solo founders building alternatives to the mainstream social platforms. Its framing: people are turning away from algorithmic social media, AI bots are taking over the big platforms, and users are retreating into private closed spaces, which leaves an opening for builders working on a new information ecosystem rather than the status quo. The residency supplies research, experts, connections and tools; the founder brings the vision and experience. TWO FOCUS AREAS: humans connecting to humans, meaning products that bring people into meaningful contact online (forums, messaging apps, groups, microblogging, in-person meetups), including AI-powered platforms and tools as long as they connect people to people rather than people to bots; and local and civic information environments, meaning tools for distributing local news, building community knowledge and facilitating collective sensemaking, so people can get trustworthy information about their communities. WHAT RESIDENTS DO: use self-directed time to prototype ideas, test assumptions and gain clarity; participate in regular check-ins, engage with the other resident and the wider team, and take candid feedback on product and strategy; engage seriously with go-to-market questions about who the user is, how they find you, and the path to sustainability; and help shape the programme itself, since the pilot cohort's feedback directly informs how the EIR programme runs going forward. WHO THEY WANT: entrepreneurial founders ready to build their next thing within the focus areas, with a track record of or clear drive toward work that builds social trust, connection and belonging; strong product thinkers with good judgment, a relentless focus on user needs, and openness to coaching on mission-aligned business models. An early idea is enough, no working product required. The pilot is open to founders with something else going on alongside, though the full programme will most likely be full time. APPLICATION PROCESS: submit the written application online; shortlisted applicants submit a short video by 18 September 2026 covering background, motivation and a quick pitch; full applications and videos go to the advisory board, with offers made in early October ahead of the kickoff. FUTURE COHORTS: New_ Public plans to open the next cohort to the UK and EU from spring 2027. Deadline 11:59pm Pacific on Wednesday 9 September 2026.

    Fund for Investigative Journalism · USA-primary (foreign-based stories require strong U.S. angle) · Deadline: 14 Sep 2026 · Award: Up to $10,000 (regular) or $1,000 to $2,500 (seed)

    Grants for in-depth investigative reporting that exposes corruption, malfeasance or misuse of power across public and private sectors. Covers print, online, broadcast, books, documentaries and podcasts. Surveillance, abuse-of-power and accountability investigations all fit. Letter of Commitment from a news outlet required for full proposals (not for seed). Seed deadline ~10 May 2026; regular deadline 14 September 2026, 23:59 ET. Reviewed three to four times per year. Stories must be published in English with a U.S. media outlet. Ethnic media and journalists of colour particularly encouraged.

  4. Kluge Fellowships at the Library of Congress 2027

    John W. Kluge Center, Library of Congress · Washington, DC, United States (in residence at the Thomas Jefferson Building, 101 Independence Ave SE). Open to applicants of any nationality: U.S. citizens and foreign nationals alike, with J-1 exchange visitor visa sponsorship provided for those who need it. · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 5,000 per month for a residency of four to eleven months, so USD 20,000 to USD 55,000 depending on the tenure awarded. Twelve Kluge Fellowships are awarded each year. Stipends are paid monthly by electronic transfer to a U.S. bank account. No application fee, no participation fee.

    The John W. Kluge Center awards twelve residential fellowships a year for humanistic and social science research that draws on the Library of Congress collections, with interdisciplinary and cross-cultural projects particularly welcome. Fellows get four to eleven months in residence at a stipend of USD 5,000 per month, work and discussion space in the Jefferson Building, and access to the Library's specialist staff, the world's largest law library, extensive multilingual books and periodicals, and deep special collections of manuscripts, maps, music, films, recorded sound, prints and photographs, alongside the wider scholarly community in Washington. The Center was established in 2000 through a USD 60 million endowment from John W. Kluge. ELIGIBILITY: scholars who received a terminal advanced degree (the highest degree available in the discipline, usually a PhD, JD or international equivalent) within the past seven years, in the humanities, social sciences, or a professional field such as architecture or law. The degree must be in hand at the time of submission, and full-time students are therefore out of scope. Open to U.S. citizens and foreign nationals; selected foreign nationals are assisted in obtaining a Library-sponsored J-1 visa, though holders of H-1B visas are not eligible. APPLICATION: through the Library's online portal, opening 15 April 2026 and closing 11:59pm on 15 September 2026. Materials required are an application form in English, a CV (max 2 pages), a 500-word research abstract, a one-page statement on how the Library's collections will serve the work, a project proposal with work plan (max 3 pages), a bibliography (max 3 pages), and two reference letters from referees who have read the proposal. No fee.

  6. American Academy in Berlin: Berlin Prize Fellowship 2027-28

    American Academy in Berlin · Berlin, Germany, at the Hans Arnhold Center. RESTRICTED: fellowships are open only to citizens and permanent residents of the United States. · Deadline: 25 Sep 2026 · Award: Monthly stipend of USD 5,000, plus roundtrip airfare, accommodation at or near the Hans Arnhold Center, partial board, in-house library services and professional networking opportunities. Roughly two dozen fellowships awarded each year. No application fee.

    The American Academy in Berlin is an independent, nonprofit institute for advanced study in the arts, humanities, social sciences, law, journalism and public policy, whose primary mission is to strengthen the cultural and intellectual ties between the United States and Germany. Each year, after a rigorous selection process, the Academy awards roughly two dozen semester-long Berlin Prize fellowships to individuals who demonstrate excellence in their fields. DISCIPLINES: applications are welcome across the humanities, social sciences, law, journalism and various areas of public policy. The Academy generally encourages projects addressing pressing issues of the day, specifically naming democracy, security, technology and society (especially AI), energy including climate, and global order. Dedicated fellowships also support projects in Jewish studies, German studies, and public health and biotechnology. Project proposals need not focus on Germany, but candidates should explain how a residency in Berlin would enhance their work. NOT ACCEPTED: proposals in mathematics or the natural sciences. NOTE FOR ARTISTS: visual artists and composers are invited through separate, nomination-based competitions, so there is no open application route for them here. ELIGIBILITY: fellowships are restricted to citizens and permanent residents of the United States. Both emerging and established figures are eligible. PhD holders qualify as emerging scholars with a strong record of peer-reviewed work beyond the dissertation; candidates in non-academic fields such as journalism, filmmaking and policy need a significant record of publication or production; fiction and nonfiction writers must have published at least one book with a reputable press. WHAT FELLOWS DO: fellows present their work to audiences in and around Berlin through lectures, readings, public discussions, performances and film screenings, and should be willing to engage with relevant German institutions, organizations and the media; these activities are integral to the Academy's mission of fostering transatlantic dialogue. APPLICATION MATERIALS: a project proposal of 1,500 to 1,750 words plus a one-page bibliography; a curriculum vitae of up to 10 pages; two writing samples totalling no more than 60 pages; and three letters of recommendation, which are due 2 October 2026. DEADLINE: Friday 25 September 2026 at 12:00 noon Eastern Time (18:00 Central European Time). TIMELINE: all candidates are notified of the competition outcome by email in late March 2027; the 2027-28 Berlin Prize recipients are announced in May 2027.

  9. Science & Technology Policy Fellowships, 2027-28 Class

    American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS) · Washington DC, USA. Fellows are placed in US federal executive, legislative and judicial branch offices and must reside in the DC area. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Stipend depending on experience and placement, roughly USD 85,000 to 140,000; the dedicated AI legislative branch cohort is paid at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale.     The AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships place scientists and engineers inside US federal agencies, congressional offices and the judicial branch for a year of hands-on policymaking, contributing technical knowledge and analytical skills to the federal policy process. AI, data and computing policy placements are among the largest tracks, and AAAS runs a dedicated AI legislative branch cohort that places AI experts in congressional offices at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. ELIGIBILITY: US citizens holding a PhD or equivalent doctoral degree in any science or engineering field, or a master's degree in engineering plus three years of post-degree professional experience; some tracks accept a bachelor's plus five years. Relevant expertise includes AI, machine learning, computer science, robotics and data science. Fellows must reside in DC, Maryland or northern Virginia during the fellowship year. TIMELINE: the application portal opens 1 August 2026 and closes 1 November 2026 at 11:59pm ET, with eligibility notification in late November, interview notification January to February 2027, semi-finalist interviews in March, placement offers by early May, and the fellowship year running 1 September 2027 to 31 August 2028. Apply through fellowshipapp.aaas.org.

    The American-Scandinavian Foundation's award program for study and research abroad is its longest-standing commitment to educational exchange: over the past 100 years, more than 4,000 fellowships and grants have gone to Americans working in the Nordic region and Scandinavians in the US engaged in study, research, or creative arts projects. REPORTING: fellows abroad more than six months submit an interim report mid-project, and all fellows submit a final narrative report within 60 days of the fellowship period's end.   11. ACLS Digital Justice Grants 2026-27 (Seed and Development)

    American Council of Learned Societies (ACLS), funded by the Mellon Foundation · Grant funds must be administered by a US institution of higher education. No citizenship or residency restriction is stated for the principal investigator. · Deadline: 03 Nov 2026 · Award: Seed Grants USD 10,000 to 25,000 for start-up and prototyping work; Development Grants USD 50,000 to 100,000 for projects past proof of concept. Institutional indirect costs are not covered. No application fee.

    ACLS Digital Justice Grants fund digital projects in the humanities and interpretive social sciences that critically engage with the interests and histories of people of colour and other historically marginalised communities, including Black, Latinx, Indigenous, disabled and LGBTQ+ communities. The programme's stated aims are to diversify the digital domain, advance justice and equity in digital scholarly practice, and contribute to public understanding of racial and social justice issues, with an explicit interest in scholars working with under-utilised or understudied source materials and those at institutions with less infrastructure for digital work. TWO TRACKS, one deadline: SEED GRANTS of USD 10,000 to 25,000 for projects in the start-up or prototyping phase, covering planning, prototyping and methodology testing; and DEVELOPMENT GRANTS of USD 50,000 to 100,000 for projects that have moved past proof of concept and can show preliminary work and evidence of engagement. ELIGIBILITY: at least one principal investigator must be a scholar in the humanities or interpretive social sciences, and awarded funds must be administered by a US institution of higher education. WHAT IS NOT FUNDED: creative works such as novels or films, textbooks, straightforward translations, purely pedagogical projects, and institutional indirect costs. Note that outputs are expected to be made as widely available as intellectual property allows, ideally under the most liberal open-source or Creative Commons licence. TIMING: applications go through the ACLS online fellowship and grant administration system (ofa.acls.org) by 3 November 2026 at 17:00 ET; grant terms begin between 1 July and 31 December 2027 and run 12 to 18 months. The programme is funded by the Mellon Foundation.

  14. Physical AI Safety Institute: Research Fellowship (expression of interest)

    Physical AI Safety Institute (PAISI) · Not stated in the call. PAISI is a US 501(c)(3) nonprofit and its team is affiliated with UC Berkeley and the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago; because fellows are given access to physical robot platforms, expect an in-person or hybrid component. No nationality restriction is stated for expressions of interest. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Stipend amount is not published. Fellows receive a stipend, access to physical robot platforms, compute for simulation and training, and structured mentorship. PAISI was seeded by a grant from BlueDot Impact. Free to submit an expression of interest.

    Robot foundation models (pi-zero, OpenVLA, GR00T and others) are approaching widespread deployment on the back of more than USD 18 billion in funding, and almost nobody is working on their safety. The Physical AI Safety Institute, a new nonprofit founded by Bear Haon and Kaylene Stocking and seeded by a grant from BlueDot Impact, describes itself as a capacity-building organisation growing the community that develops techniques to interpret, align and control robot foundation models. Its research fellowship runs in 12-week cohorts: each fellow works within a stream led by a mentor on a concrete problem in physical AI safety, with mentors drawn from researchers with track records in robot foundation models, mechanistic interpretability, safe control or adversarial robustness for embodied systems. Each cohort ends with a research symposium where fellows present their work. STREAMS: mechanistic interpretability (sparse autoencoders and activation patching for VLA and WAM architectures); adversarial robustness (jailbreaking and prompt injection in the embodied action space); runtime monitoring (detecting when a policy operates outside its competence envelope); sim-to-real safety transfer (do safety behaviours survive deployment on physical hardware); and robot security (cyberattack vectors, data poisoning and supply chain for deployed robot models). WHO SHOULD APPLY: researchers with strong technical foundations in robotics or machine learning and a demonstrated interest in safety; prior safety experience is not required. PROCESS: this stage is an expression of interest, not a full application. The form takes about two minutes and asks you to pick your stream and preferred cohort dates; concrete details including stipend figures and application deadlines are shared through that channel, and neither is published on the site as of listing. PAISI also publishes seven free curriculum modules running from classical robot safety through VLA interpretability, adversarial robustness and runtime monitoring to deployment evaluation, plus a white paper, The Case for Physical AI Safety, at paisi.ai/the-case-for-physical-ai-safety. Contact: bear@paisi.ai.

  17. Good Life Residents Program: AI and Finance

    Roosevelt Institute, in partnership with Data & Society · Remote. Travel may be requested for select convenings and for the culminating public event. US economic policy focus. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,000 stipend, plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event if needed. No application fee.

    The Good Life Residents Program is a six-month Roosevelt Institute initiative that develops new leaders and elevates bold ideas across six economic policy priorities under its Good Life Agenda. Each topic area hosts a cohort of 3 to 4 residents plus 1 to 2 senior Topic Advisors (academics, policy leaders, organizers or government practitioners) who provide strategic and intellectual mentorship and moderate the closing event. Residents write a roughly 1,500-word original policy essay advancing a people-centered policy vision against a topic-specific prompt, take part in structured dialogue with peers, Topic Advisors and Roosevelt Think Tank directors, help prepare and appear at a culminating public event, contribute to press opportunities such as op-eds and reporter calls where applicable, and act as ambassadors for the Good Life Agenda in their professional and public networks. The current round, the AI and Finance Residents Program, is run in partnership with Data & Society and asks for bold ideas on how to shape, redirect, interrupt or make more visible and contestable the flows of capital into generative AI. Framing: breakneck investment in generative AI technology and infrastructure is distorting the stock market, profit measures, bond issuance, the pace and composition of growth, job creation and trade policy, with seven tech companies accounting for more than a third of S&P 500 market capitalisation and complex, sometimes circular financial arrangements obscuring risk. Suggested lines of inquiry include what government interventions could improve public oversight, risk allocation and direction-setting in private markets; how other investments could be coordinated and scaled if AI spending is crowding them out; what role public spending or new institutions could play in catalysing technology R&D designed to support workers, sustainability and supply chains; what democratic input mechanisms would keep state influence over capital flows less vulnerable to corruption; and how federal AI policy should address public hostility to data centre investments alongside national security and competitiveness. Compensation is USD 3,000 plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event. FLAG: Roosevelt does not publish an application deadline for the AI and Finance round on the programme page, and earlier rounds (Social Security financing, Federal Reserve powers, housing) each ran their own short application window. Check the Formstack application form linked from the programme page for the current closing date before applying.

  18. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.
