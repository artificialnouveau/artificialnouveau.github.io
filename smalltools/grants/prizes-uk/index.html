Funded Prizes and Awards in the UK

Currently 8 active prizes and awards open to applicants in the UK, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. BFI NETWORK England: Early Development Fund (GBP 3,000-5,000 treatment awards for debut long-form writers)

    BFI NETWORK (BFI, National Lottery funding) · England. Writers must be based in England. · Deadline: 11 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 3,000 to 5,000 per project to develop a treatment (stage one). Projects completed to schedule can then apply for second-stage funding to write a full first draft. National Lottery funding; no application fee stated.

    BFI NETWORK's Early Development Fund supports writers at the very start of a debut long-form project: those who do not yet have a first draft of a script (or the equivalent format for immersive work). Stage one awards GBP 3,000 to 5,000 to produce an initial treatment setting out story, characters, themes and structure, plus related materials such as a mood board or other visual content. It covers long-form live-action, immersive and animation fiction. Projects supported at treatment stage and completed to schedule can apply for a second stage of funding to complete a first draft of the feature-length screenplay or immersive equivalent. ELIGIBILITY: writers based in England who have not yet written a feature film or immersive work that has been produced and secured UK distribution; apply with or without a producer. TIMELINE: opened 7 October 2026, closes 11 November 2026; decisions by 10 February 2027 (do not chase before then). APPLY online via the link in the funding guidelines; access support, easy read and BSL introductions are available.

  2. Golden Fleece Award 2027 (Ireland, two EUR 10,000 awards)

    The Golden Fleece Award (Lillias Mitchell Trust) · Island of Ireland (artists living and working in Ireland or Northern Ireland), plus Irish-born artists working overseas. · Deadline: 11 Dec 2026 · Award: EUR 10,000 each for two category winners (Visual Art, and Craft + Applied Art); total fund usually around EUR 30,000, with shortlisted artists also receiving a smaller financial award (amount to be confirmed). No application fee.

    Annual award from the Lillias Mitchell Trust recognising craft and visual artists connected to Ireland. AWARDS: EUR 10,000 each to two winners, one in Visual Art and one in Craft + Applied Art (total fund usually around EUR 30,000); shortlisted artists also receive a smaller financial award, amount to be confirmed. ELIGIBILITY: craft and visual artists living and working on the island of Ireland, and Irish-born artists working overseas; any career stage with demonstrated artistic merit; full-time students are normally not considered; previous main-award winners cannot reapply. APPLICATION: online, no fee. TIMELINE: applications open 12:00 noon on 16 October 2026. DEADLINE: 11 December 2026, 17:00.

  3. The Creative Intelligence Award 2027 (CoSTAR National Lab x Southbank Centre)

    CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre, supported by UKRI · UK (lead applicant must be a UK-registered company; project activity UK-based). Prototyping at Pinewood Studios; showcase at Southbank Centre, London. · Deadline: 17 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 50,000 grant (one award), plus up to GBP 25,000 in AI compute and cloud credits, 2 x 5 days studio access at Pinewood Studios, a six-week CoSTAR prototyping team sprint, 3 days pre-show testing, Southbank curatorial and producing support, and 20 hours of mentorship. No application fee.

    A new co-commission from CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre to take a creative technology idea that is ready to be completed, honed or technically advanced through focused R&D, then present it to audiences at Southbank Centre's Creative Intelligence festival in autumn 2027. They want original concepts where AI, immersive, real-time, virtual production or other emerging technology is integral rather than an add-on, across any artform (music, theatre, dance, literature, visual and live art, design, architecture, food, film, games), with cross-disciplinary work encouraged and touring potential valued. ELIGIBILITY: lead applicant must be a UK-registered, trading company or other eligible UK legal entity; consortia welcome, and artists, technologists, researchers and sole traders can take part as collaborators. Team and funded activity must be UK-based. Webinar Q&A on 16 November 2026, 14:00 GMT. DEADLINE: 17 December 2026, 14:00 GMT.

  4. Royal Institute of Philosophy Essay Prize 2026: Humans and AI

    Royal Institute of Philosophy · Remote submission by email. Open internationally. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 1,000 cash plus GBP 500 of Cambridge University Press books; the winning essay is published in the journal Philosophy.

    The Royal Institute of Philosophy's annual essay prize asks for an original philosophical essay on the 2026 topic, Humans and AI. Essays of up to 8,000 words, anonymised for review, are submitted by email. No eligibility restrictions are stated. Note: the Cambridge Core prize page still shows the older 2024 call at a different amount; the institute's own announcement is current. No application fee.

  5. Royal Society of Sculptors: First Plinth Public Art Award 2027 (GBP 20,000 commission)

    Royal Society of Sculptors · London (Dora House), then a second site. · Deadline: 12 Jan 2027 · Award: GBP 20,000 commission for the winner; up to 5 shortlisted artists receive GBP 1,000 each for maquettes. Entry fee about GBP 34 (GBP 17 early bird until 17 November).

    The First Plinth award commissions a large-scale sculpture for the plinth outside the Royal Society of Sculptors' Dora House in London, later moving to a second site. ELIGIBILITY: any artist working in three dimensions. OFFER: GBP 20,000 commission with fabrication support and mentoring from engineers and consultants; up to five shortlisted artists get GBP 1,000 each to make maquettes. APPLY via CuratorSpace; entry fee about GBP 34, or GBP 17 before 17 November. DEADLINE: 12 January 2027.

  6. The Whickers Film & TV Funding Award 2027 (GBP 120,000 for a first feature documentary)

    The Whickers · Open worldwide. Shortlisted directors pitch in person at Sheffield DocFest, UK, June 2027 (travel and accommodation covered). · Deadline: 22 Jan 2027 · Award: GBP 120,000 main award; GBP 25,000 development award for the runner-up. Free to enter.

    The Whickers Film & TV Funding Award backs a director-led documentary by a first-time feature director. The winner receives GBP 120,000 and the runner-up a GBP 25,000 development award. ELIGIBILITY: open worldwide to directors or co-directors aged 18+ making their first documentary of 50+ minutes (co-directors must also be first-time feature directors); project at late development or early production (not a finishing fund), budget at or under GBP 600,000 (animation may exceed); an executive producer must be attached at application; film in English or English-subtitled; up to 6 minutes of taster footage; one proposal per applicant per year. Award money is paid to a company, fiscal sponsor or production account, not to an individual (shortlisted applicants may agree to form a limited company). TIMELINE: applications open 24 November 2026 via the online form; deadline 22 January 2027, 12:00 midday GMT; shortlist late April 2027; finalists pitch at Sheffield DocFest in June 2027, where the winner is announced. Free to enter.

  7. Bradford Textile Society: Design Competition 2026-27 (UK)

    Bradford Textile Society · UK. Open to all UK-based students and independent designers. · Deadline: 07 May 2027 · Award: A collective GBP 17,000 in awards across a wide spectrum of textile specialities. No entry fee is stated on the competition page.

    The Bradford Textile Society, which has run its design competition since 1893, awards a collective GBP 17,000 across textile categories in the 2027 cycle. CATEGORIES: weave, knit, print, embroidery, combined textile processes, mixed media and new technologies, the last being a natural home for e-textile and smart-textile work; a new Coldharbour Mill Award for Tartan is added for 2027. ELIGIBILITY: all UK-based students and independent designers are welcome to enter. KEY DATES: entries open 22 March 2027 and close at midnight on 7 May 2027; the entry portal is currently closed between cycles, but the deadline and categories are published now. No entry fee is stated on the competition page.

  8. Iris Prize 2027: International LGBTQ+ Short Film Prize (GBP 40,000)

    Iris Prize · Cardiff, Wales, UK. International strand open worldwide; other strands UK only. · Deadline: 02 Jun 2027 · Award: GBP 40,000 for the winner to make their next short film in the UK; smaller UK-only awards (Community and Education GBP 250, Micro Short GBP 100). Submission fee not stated on the official page; Iris alumni get a full waiver.

    Billed as the world's largest LGBTQ+ short film prize, the Iris Prize international shorts strand is open worldwide; the Best British, student, community and education strands are UK only. Submissions open 11 January 2027; deadlines: early bird 31 March, regular 24 May, late 2 June 2027.
