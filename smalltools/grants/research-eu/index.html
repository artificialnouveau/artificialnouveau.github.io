  1. European Cultural Foundation: Artistic Research Fellowship (ECF Historical Archives)

    European Cultural Foundation · Archives held at the Historical Archives of the European Union, Florence, Italy. Open to artists able to work with the archives. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 10,000 artistic-research grant covering travel, accommodation, and living costs; output delivered within 2 years. No application fee.

    The European Cultural Foundation invites artists to research its historical archives, held at the Historical Archives of the European Union in Florence, through a new Artistic Research Fellowship with a EUR 10,000 grant covering travel, accommodation, and living costs; the artistic output is delivered within two years. A strong fit for archive-driven, research-based, and critical data practices. Deadline 30 September 2026, 17:00 CET. ELIGIBILITY: professional creatives in visual arts, performing arts, film and audiovisual practices, podcasting and audio storytelling, documentary, journalism or literary practices, who can demonstrate a research-based practice and a clear dissemination strategy. Applications and all supporting documentation must be in English, submitted via the online form. Fellows work at the HAEU inside the scholarly community of the European University Institute, with the archives housed at Villa Salviati. A parallel Academic Research Fellowship of EUR 5,000, for PhD and postdoctoral researchers, early-career researchers within ten years of their master's or doctorate, and independent scholars, is listed separately on the desk and shares this deadline.

  2. European Cultural Foundation: Academic Research Fellowship (ECF Historical Archives)

    European Cultural Foundation, hosted at the Historical Archives of the European Union · Historical Archives of the European Union, Villa Salviati, Florence, Italy, within the European University Institute. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 5,000 grant to support a research stay at the HAEU, covering travel, accommodation and living expenses. The parallel Artistic Research Fellowship carries EUR 10,000 and additionally covers artistic production. No application fee.

    The academic track of the ECF Fellowship Programme, which fosters new research engaging with the history of the European Cultural Foundation and the broader role of culture in European integration. The ECF was established in 1954 by figures including Denis de Rougemont, Robert Schuman, Hendrik Brugmans and Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands, on the belief that Europe will not be built by treaties alone. Over seventy years it has produced programmes, institutes, campaigns and collaborations, including a contribution to shaping and implementing the ERASMUS student mobility scheme. That legacy is now preserved at the Historical Archives of the European Union in Florence alongside the records of the EU's main institutions, a placement that itself recognises the Foundation's role in European integration. ELIGIBILITY for the Academic Research Fellowship: researchers in European studies, cultural studies, history and related fields across the humanities and social sciences, specifically PhD and postdoctoral researchers, early-career researchers within ten years of completing a master's or doctoral degree, and independent scholars with an active research profile. Fellows work at the HAEU embedded in the international scholarly community of the European University Institute, with the archives housed at Villa Salviati. Applications and all supporting documentation must be submitted in English through the online form. NOTE: this is the EUR 5,000 academic track. The EUR 10,000 Artistic Research Fellowship, open to professional creatives in visual arts, performing arts, film and audiovisual practices, podcasting and audio storytelling, documentary, journalism and literary practices, is listed separately on the desk and shares the same deadline.

  3. Camargo Fellowship 2027-2028 for Artists, Scholars and Thinkers (Cassis, France)

    Camargo Foundation · Camargo Foundation campus, Cassis, France. Open worldwide. · Deadline: 01 Oct 2026 · Award: EUR 350 per week (EUR 3,500 total for the ten-week residency), plus basic travel funding and a furnished apartment with equipped kitchen on the Camargo campus. 14 fellowships per year: 7 for artists and 7 for scholars and thinkers. No application fee.

    The Camargo Foundation invites artists, scholars and thinkers to its flagship Core Fellowship: a ten-week self-directed residency on its clifftop campus overlooking the Mediterranean in Cassis, France, dedicated to research, experimentation and interdisciplinary exchange. Curators apply under the Thinkers category, which covers accomplished professionals and practitioners in cultural and creative fields who are professionally engaged in critical thought, including curators, critics and journalists; projects should engage theoretical, artistic or societal questions. WHAT YOU GET: a stipend of EUR 350 per week (EUR 3,500 total), basic transportation funding, and a furnished apartment with an equipped kitchen on campus. Fourteen fellowships are awarded per year: seven for artists and seven for scholars and thinkers. RESIDENCY PERIODS: Fall 2027 (8 September to 17 November 2027) or Spring 2028 (23 February to 4 May 2028). ELIGIBILITY: artists in visual arts, performance, writing, film, music or multidisciplinary work with at least 5 years of professional practice; scholars holding or pursuing a PhD focused on French and Francophone cultures or cross-cultural Mediterranean studies; and thinkers as described above. APPLICATION: via Submittable with a 100-word proposal summary, a 1,200-word proposal narrative, a 300-word rationale for Camargo, a 300-word career-stage rationale, a CV (max 3 pages), work samples and two references. NOTE: the foundation has announced that this is the final Fellowship selected through a public open call until further notice; a new selection process begins in July 2027, making this the last chance to apply directly.

  4. Institute for Law & AI: Winter Research Fellowship EU Law 2027 (Cambridge, UK)

    Institute for Law & AI (LawAI) · Cambridge, UK (in-office; remote candidates considered in exceptional circumstances). GBP 7,000 relocation and housing payment provided. · Deadline: 12 Oct 2026 · Award: GBP 1,000 weekly stipend for the 10-week fellowship (GBP 10,000 total), plus a GBP 7,000 relocation and housing payment. No application fee.

    The Institute for Law & AI's Winter Research Fellowship (EU Law) is a 10-week full-time paid research fellowship for lawyers who want to deepen their understanding of AI and law and contribute to the field, with close support and mentorship from LawAI staff. Previous Seasonal Research Fellows have gone on to legal and policy roles in the European AI Office, leading AI companies, academia and think tanks, including LawAI itself. FUNDING: a GBP 1,000 weekly stipend plus a GBP 7,000 relocation and housing payment; fellows work from LawAI's Cambridge, UK office, with remote candidates considered in exceptional circumstances. ELIGIBILITY: open to graduate law students, professionals and academics with diverse skill sets, experience levels and degrees of knowledge in AI law and policy. Fellowship runs 1 February to 9 April 2027. Applications are considered on a rolling basis as received, so apply early. DEADLINE: 12 October 2026.

  5. Stroom Den Haag: PRO Research

    Stroom Den Haag · The Hague, Netherlands. Open to Hague artists registered with Stroom and to curators, mediators, critics, publicists and others professionally active in the contemporary art world; applicants from outside The Hague apply with a CV. · Deadline: 25 Oct 2026 · Award: Maximum EUR 5,000 per research project, up to EUR 20,000 per applicant per calendar year. Amounts exclude VAT and grants are only awarded on demonstrable financial need. No application fee.

    Stroom Den Haag's PRO Research scheme contributes to the costs of a research project or artist-in-residency that feeds the development of new work and benefits The Hague's art climate. WHO CAN APPLY: The Hague artists registered in the Stroom database with an updated profile (Mijn Stroom), and also curators, mediators, critics, publicists and others professionally active in the contemporary art world; applicants from outside The Hague submit a CV for consideration. APPLICATION: via the Stroom portal using Stroom's budget model, with amounts excluding VAT. NOT ELIGIBLE: applicants enrolled in (art) educational programmes (Bachelor, Master, postdoc, PhD) or art academy students, and post-academic work periods such as the Rijksakademie, De Ateliers, the Jan van Eyck Academie and the EKWC fall outside the funding scheme system. DEADLINE: the last 2026 round closes 25 October 2026 (rounds recur roughly every six weeks). The grant is valid for up to two years after the decision, with 80 percent paid upfront.

  6. Art Explora x Cité internationale des arts Residency, 7th Call (Paris)

    Art Explora Foundation and Cité internationale des arts · Paris, France. Open worldwide to artists with at least 5 years of professional practice. · Deadline: 30 Oct 2026 · Award: EUR 1,000 per month living stipend plus up to EUR 3,000 production grant, a furnished live-work studio of around 50 m2 at the Cité internationale des arts, round-trip travel to Paris and curatorial mentoring. No application fee.

    The 7th call of the Art Explora residency programme at the Cité internationale des arts opened 21 September 2026. The programme supports projects exploring the crossovers of art with science, technology and ecology, which makes it one of the stronger art-science fits in Europe. Residents receive a furnished live-work studio of around 50 m2 in Paris, EUR 1,000 per month living stipend, up to EUR 3,000 production budget, round-trip travel and mentoring, with residencies of 3 or 6 months for solo artists and 3 months for duos of an artist with a researcher, curator or writer. Open to visual, performing and digital artists of all nationalities and ages with at least 5 years of professional experience and an international exhibition record. Programme details at artexplora.org. TO APPLY: submit via the Art Explora application platform. DEADLINE: 30 October 2026, 11:59 PM Paris time.

  7. Royal Academy of Fine Arts Antwerp: Call for Two-Year Research Proposals (2027 start)

    Royal Academy of Fine Arts Antwerp (AP University of Applied Sciences and Arts) · Antwerp, Belgium. Salaried research position; applicant must be legally employable in Belgium. · Deadline: 03 Nov 2026 · Award: Two-year research projects: 55% employment for the research assignment over 24 months (contractual project staff member) plus EUR 2,000 in operating funds (EUR 1,000 per project year). No application fee.

    The Royal Academy of Fine Arts Antwerp (AP University of Applied Sciences and Arts) invites applications for two-year artistic research projects starting 20 September 2027. Two-year research projects provide 55% employment for the research assignment over 24 months (as a contractual project staff member) plus EUR 2,000 in operating funds (EUR 1,000 per project year). Proposals are developed in collaboration with the Academy's research groups and must align with, endorse, and further develop the mission and vision of the Royal Academy of Fine Arts Antwerp. PRECONDITIONS: the proposal must be in line with the Academy's mission/vision; the applicant must be legally employable by the Royal Academy of Fine Arts Antwerp/AP Hogeschool on a contractual basis; and, at the time of application, the applicant must hold a Master's degree in Visual Arts awarded by a Belgian higher education institution (a non-Belgian degree is possible only if validated or recognised through the NARIC procedure, with proof included or a declared willingness to undergo it). PROCEDURE (two steps): STEP 1, apply via the Expression of Interest form sent to research_royalacademyantwerp@ap.be before 3 November 2026, 12:00 noon (applicants informed no later than end November 2026 whether they may develop a full dossier); STEP 2, submit the full dossier via the two-year project application form before 1 February 2027, 12:00 noon. Evaluation by an independent reading committee aims to conclude in May 2027. Contact: research_royalacademyantwerp@ap.be.

  8. KHK CURE Fellowships 2027/2028, Saarland University (theme: Things)

    Kaete Hamburger Centre for Cultural Practices of Reparation (CURE), Saarland University · Saarbruecken, Germany. Fellows must live in Saarbruecken during the fellowship and participate actively in centre events; extended research absences are not permitted. Open internationally; retired persons and Saarland University staff are not eligible. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: Up to twelve fellowships. Fellows on unpaid leave from their institution receive approximately EUR 6,100 to 7,200 per month before taxes, depending on qualifications; alternatively, fellows retaining their salary can have their home institution reimbursed for replacement teaching. One round trip to Saarbruecken is reimbursed, a fully equipped individual workspace is provided, and an apartment can be arranged for around EUR 1,250 per month. Eligible accompanying family members may receive additional support. No application fee.

    The Kaete Hamburger Centre for Cultural Practices of Reparation (KHK CURE) at Saarland University invites applications for up to twelve fellowships for its fourth year, October 2027 to September 2028. CURE welcomes scholars from cultural studies, the humanities and the social sciences whose work examines questions of reparation and irreparability; artists holding a doctorate and experienced in academic collaboration may also apply. ANNUAL THEME: 'Things', foregrounding the material dimension of reparation: objects, artefacts and material environments through which practices such as repair, mending and reuse acquire cultural and political significance. Proposals must align with at least one of the Centre's three thematic fields (history, experience or nature) and address the annual theme. CONDITIONS: fellowships run six to twelve months from 1 October 2027; fellows on unpaid leave receive approximately EUR 6,100 to 7,200 per month before taxes depending on qualifications, or the home institution is reimbursed for replacement teaching; one round trip to Saarbruecken is reimbursed under the Saarland Travel Expense Act; a fully equipped individual workspace is provided and an apartment can be arranged for around EUR 1,250 per month; fellows must live in Saarbruecken and take part in the Centre's international, interdisciplinary research community. ELIGIBILITY: a completed PhD (submitted and defended) by the deadline; retired persons and Saarland University staff are not eligible. HOW TO APPLY: via the online form with a one-page motivation letter, CV (max 3 pages), list of publications, and a five-page project description with bibliography, in English, German or French. Notification by 30 April 2027. Deadline 30 November 2026.

  9. ESA Open Space Innovation Platform (OSIP): rolling open call for ideas

    European Space Agency (ESA) · Ideas can be submitted from anywhere in the world via ideas.esa.int. To implement a funded idea yourself, you or your affiliated entity must be eligible to do business with ESA under its procurement regulations, which in practice means being based in an ESA member state (the Netherlands qualifies). · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Varies by route and campaign. Successful ideas are supported through PhD or postdoctoral research co-funded by ESA and universities, early technology development activities, or system studies. Free to submit.

    OSIP is the European Space Agency's standing front door for unsolicited novel ideas, launched in 2019 and now the main entry point for new concepts into ESA, both in response to specific problems and through open calls. HOW IT WORKS: ideas are submitted through two mechanisms. Campaigns are targeted calls responding to specific questions with defined timelines (recent examples include Future Navigation Demonstrators and elements for the EOCognitiveLab mission); Channels are permanently open, open-ended calls on broader topics with no deadline. Submissions can go through an optional community discussion phase, with voting and comments, before expert evaluation by ESA. WHO CAN SUBMIT: individuals, businesses, universities and organisations anywhere in the world. The catch for funding: to implement a funded idea yourself, you must be eligible to do business with ESA under Article 18 of its procurement regulations, which in practice means affiliation with an entity in an ESA member state. WHAT FUNDING LOOKS LIKE: successful ideas are typically supported as co-funded PhD or postdoctoral research (ESA plus a university), early technology development activities, or system studies; amounts vary by route and campaign. WHY IT IS ON THE DESK: this is the one genuinely public, always-open route into ESA for independent researchers, technologists and artists working with space data or space technology concepts; new campaigns appear regularly, so checking the platform periodically is worthwhile. Submit via ideas.esa.int.
