  1. Andy Warhol Foundation: Exhibition Support (Fall 2026 round)

    The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts · Applicant's own venue. US-based 501(c)(3) required; international organisations must first submit a letter of inquiry before any proposal is accepted. · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 60,000 to 100,000. Direct costs only (artist payments, catalogue and publication, artist-designed public programmes); staff salaries excluded. Request must not exceed 25 percent of total direct project costs, which functions as a de facto 75 percent co-financing requirement. No application fee.

    Supports solo, two-person and thematic group exhibitions of contemporary visual art. The Foundation explicitly prioritises artists whose work is under-recognised and whose practices are experimental or hard to categorise, which makes it a genuine route for media arts, digital culture and art-and-science exhibitions rather than a blue-chip-only fund. Grants of USD 60,000 to 100,000 cover direct exhibition costs including artist fees, new commissions, catalogue production and artist-designed public programming, but not staff salaries. Applicants may request no more than 25 percent of total direct costs. The proposal is a roughly three-page curatorial letter plus detailed direct-cost budget, organisational budget and IRS documentation. Deadlines are 1 March and 1 September annually, with notifications 1 July and 1 January. IMPORTANT: this is a postmark or email-timestamp deadline, not a portal deadline. Fiscal sponsors are explicitly not accepted; the applicant must hold its own IRS ruling letter. The six-month-to-two-year timing rule is strict, so a September 2026 application suits exhibitions programmed from mid-2027 onward.

  2. Andy Warhol Foundation: Project Grants for Small-Scale Organizations (Fall 2026 round)

    The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts · Applicant's own venue or programme, with a mandatory public component. US-based only. · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 20,000 to 30,000 toward direct costs such as artist payments and production. No application fee.

    Launched in February 2026 as an expansion of the Foundation's programme to reach organisations too small for the Exhibition Support and Multi-year strands, and it is the single most relevant new US scheme for small media-arts festivals. It funds visual arts projects with a public component, explicitly naming film festivals, exhibitions, installations, residencies and interdisciplinary collaborations. Grants are USD 20,000 to 30,000 toward direct costs. The hard gate is the USD 200,000 annual operating budget ceiling plus US 501(c)(3) status held directly by the applicant, not via a fiscal sponsor. The Foundation states it assesses creative risk-taking and the growth of participating artists rather than community impact metrics, which suits experimental sound and digital culture programming. Proposals are a maximum of three pages plus a one-year operating budget, a project budget and IRS documentation. Deadlines are 1 March (projects begin 1 July or later) and 1 September (projects begin 1 January or later). Youth-focused projects are ineligible. One project per round, and grantees must wait a year after project completion and final reporting before reapplying.

  3. Creative Residency at Bloedel Reserve: Class of 2027

    Bloedel Reserve · Bainbridge Island, Washington, USA, between Seattle and the Kitsap Peninsula, reachable by ferry from Seattle or by car via the Kitsap Peninsula. Residents must be 21 or older. International applicants are eligible but are funded differently from domestic ones (see amount). · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 22 application fee. Furnished private house on the grounds with linens, stocked kitchen, wi-fi, laundry and use of a smart car; an electric piano is available. Domestic residents receive a USD 1,000 stipend. International residents receive no stipend and may instead submit up to USD 1,000 of expenses for reimbursement. Residents pay for their own meals and their own travel to Bainbridge Island. There is no dedicated studio; residents work in the house.

    Bloedel Reserve's residency programme exists to foster creative thinking inspired by nature and to explore the connection between humans and the environment. It is open to professional and emerging artists, composers, writers, botanists, landscape architects, researchers and others working on themes of nature and human connection to it. Residents get a three-week stay at a 140-acre internationally recognised public garden and forest preserve, housed in a small home hidden in the woods off the main paths. Note for non-US applicants: the funding is structured as expense reimbursement rather than a stipend, capped at USD 1,000, which for transatlantic applicants is unlikely to cover airfare plus meals over three weeks.

  4. City of Moorhead: 2026 Call for Public Art (Movable Interactive Piece)

    City of Moorhead, Minnesota · Moorhead, Minnesota, USA. The piece will be sited at different locations throughout the city. No geographic eligibility restriction is stated in the call, so non-US artists appear eligible, but this is not confirmed in writing. · Deadline: 04 Sep 2026 · Award: Not published. The call announcement states no budget, commission amount or artist fee, and the linked Request for Art document could not be retrieved. Confirm the budget with art@moorheadmn.gov before investing time in a proposal. No application fee is mentioned.

    The City of Moorhead is seeking proposals for an inspirational, movable public artwork to be installed at rotating locations across the city. Interactive elements are explicitly encouraged, with kinetic sculpture, digital installation and tactile elements named as examples. Strong proposals invite public participation, promote creativity and youth engagement, are safe for all members of the public, are durable enough for year-round outdoor conditions, and draw people to gather. Submissions are reviewed and ranked by the Artist Review Team, whose recommendation passes to the Art and Culture Commission and then to City Council for the final decision. Questions go to art@moorheadmn.gov. Proposals are due by 10:00am on Friday 4 September 2026.

  5. Erie Canal Museum: Erie Canal Artist-in-Residence 2027

    Erie Canal Museum (with the New York State Canal Corporation) · New York State, USA. Non-residential: there is no live-in accommodation, and the work is fieldwork along the New York State Canal System (524 miles of navigable waterways and 365 miles of multi-use trails) and its communities, with engagement with Canal Corporation and Museum staff and facilities. The Museum is in the 1850 Syracuse Weighlock Building, Syracuse, NY. Finalist interviews are conducted virtually. · Deadline: 06 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 10,000 artist stipend per residency, for two Artists-in-Residence. Collectives applying as a group receive one USD 10,000 stipend between them. No application fee. The final project feeds into an exhibit and catalogue, with an exhibit opening in the first half of 2028.

    The Erie Canal Museum, in partnership with the New York State Canal Corporation, is appointing two Erie Canal Artists-in-Residence for a one-year, year-long creative inquiry into the New York State Canal System, including the historic Erie Canal: reflecting on its past, contemplating its current condition, and considering its future. The 2027 residencies are dedicated to projects that reflect upon the Canal's physical, environmental and social condition and history, its materiality and its context, and the project must directly address the Canal. Applicants propose a specific creative visual project that must be exhibitable in a gallery setting, identify their target audiences and explain how the work will be shared with them and with the public at large. Studies of Canal communities are encouraged, but the Canal and its context must be central. Projects may focus on key areas or on the entire system, and applicants are told to weigh that scale: 524 miles of navigable waterways and 365 miles of multi-use trails. ELIGIBILITY: all artists are encouraged to apply, and special consideration goes to artists with ancestral ties to the Haudenosaunee Confederacy and/or the Mohican Nation and to artists who have lived or worked in Canal communities. Only individual artists or artist collectives may apply; artists representing nonprofit or for-profit organisations may not. Artists must be 18 or older and may not be currently enrolled in school full time. For collectives, only one USD 10,000 stipend is awarded to the group. APPLICATION: a statement of interest and proposal (up to 500 words) covering why the residency interests you, your proposed project and how it meets the three residency goals (engage with the Canal, adjacent Canalway Trails, Canal Corp. and Museum staff, Canal facilities, communities and visitors; reflect directly on the Canal's physical, environmental and social condition and history; consider a fuller, more complete account of the Canal's past, present and future), which geographic section you will focus on, how you would share work publicly during the residency, your proposed final project, and how your previous work and public engagement relate; an artist's statement (up to 300 words); a resume or CV (max three pages); two professional references with phone and email; and ten examples of recent work (written work limited to three pages each) with a list giving title, date, materials and size plus short descriptions. EVALUATION covers clarity and relevance of the proposal, the scope, feasibility and clarity of the plan to reach the intended audience, the plan for public engagement including at least one public-facing event during the residency, a final project usable in an exhibit and catalogue, and the quality and relevance of prior work. Applications are reviewed by a panel of Erie Canal Museum and Canal Corporation representatives plus invited arts professionals (the 2026 panel included Elizabeth Dubben of Collar Works, artist and educator Dennis RedMoon Darkeem, and Garth Johnson, Curator of Ceramics at the Everson Museum of Art); finalists interview with the panel. SUBMIT application packages as PDFs by email to Natalie Stetson, Executive Director, at employment@eriecanalmuseum.org with 'Erie Canal Artist-in-Residence' in the subject line, by 11:59 pm on Sunday 6 September 2026. The RFP is at eriecanalmuseum.org/about/careers/.

  6. Teiger Foundation: Hosting Grant (September 2026 quarterly deadline)

    Teiger Foundation · United States. The presenting organisation must be US-based, though the originating project may have come from anywhere in the world. · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 50,000 or USD 75,000 (fixed request tiers, applicant chooses). Up to 30 percent may cover indirect and overhead costs. Organisations with annual budgets under USD 3.5 million receive special consideration for infrastructure costs. No matching requirement. No application fee.

    Funds US organisations to present curator-led contemporary visual art exhibitions and projects that originated elsewhere, on condition the presenting institution meaningfully adapts the project to its own context. Teiger's remit covers group exhibitions, single-artist surveys, participatory and community-engaged projects, digital exhibitions, and live and virtual performance, making it one of very few US funders that will directly finance media-art and performance programming at organisational level. Requests are fixed at USD 50,000 or USD 75,000 with up to 30 percent for indirect costs, and there is no minimum organisation budget size, with smaller organisations explicitly favoured for infrastructure support. Hosting deadlines are quarterly: 9 September 2026 and 9 December 2026, both 17:00 ET, each mapped to a defined project-opening window roughly a year out. IMPORTANT: re-presenting a touring project unchanged is explicitly not competitive; the curatorial adaptation is the substance of the application. US 501(c)(3) status is required and the fund is not open to individuals or non-US organisations. Teiger's larger biennial Call for Proposals is not running in 2026 and next opens in autumn 2026 with an early-2027 deadline.

  7. MAD Artist Studios Residency, Cycles 46 and 47

    Museum of Arts and Design (MAD) · Sixth floor of the Museum of Arts and Design, New York City, USA. Open to residents of the New York City metro area only. · Deadline: 11 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend based on the on-site public schedule: USD 15,000 for three public-facing days per week, or USD 10,000 for two days. Also provided: a dedicated studio of roughly 200 square feet in the Museum with worktable, sink, storage and Wi-Fi; a separate stipend for materials and mentorship; professional development and studio visits with museum staff; additional paid public programming opportunities; admission to paid public programmes; and access to MAD's galleries, public programmes and exhibition openings. Shared equipment includes a Skutt 1027 kiln, a Brent pottery wheel and a 22 inch floor loom. MAD does not pay travel costs or provide housing. Residents are one-time independent contractors and are responsible for their own taxes. Collectives may apply but are paid the individual stipend. APPLICATION FEE: USD 5 per application, with fee waivers available on request by emailing education@madmuseum.org before submitting.

    Established in 2008 and the first of its kind, the Artist Studios at MAD is a public-facing community engagement and professional development programme on the sixth floor of the Museum, where visitors meet artists-in-residence at work. Two artists are selected per six-month session (the application text elsewhere says three, so confirm with MAD). More than 200 artists and designers have come through the programme, which MAD describes as an innovative model for museum education, with a sustained focus on artists from historically underrepresented communities whose work pushes the boundaries of contemporary craft; alumni have exhibited at the Queens Museum, Whitney, Brooklyn Museum and Studio Museum. WHAT MAD MEANS BY CRAFT: both a discipline and a way of thinking, covering traditional studio craft materials such as clay, fiber, wood, glass, metal and stone, and craft-based methods used in new ways, including handwork combined with digital tools, performance or experimental materials. Craft here is defined by deep engagement with process, materiality and the hand rather than by medium. RESIDENCY EXPECTATIONS: this is a substantial public commitment, not quiet studio time. Residents commit to 14 or 21 public open studio hours a week on Thursdays, Fridays and/or Saturdays 10:30am to 5:30pm and/or Wednesdays 11:00am to 7:00pm, on a schedule fixed with MAD's Education Department for the whole six months. During those hours residents host K-12 school groups (in person and virtual) and public tour groups. Because of the contact with schoolchildren, all selected artists must pass a background check. ELIGIBILITY: an artist or designer with an active studio practice in the applied arts; a resident of the New York City metro area, defined as the five boroughs plus nearby New York, New Jersey and Connecticut counties within reasonable commuting distance, typically on MTA, PATH or NJ Transit; at least 21 at the time of application; and a US citizen or legal US resident with a valid SSN or EIN. The Museum does not sponsor visas and states this is not subject to exception. Programme alumni may not reapply, though unsuccessful past applicants may. SELECTION: the committee looks for a mature body of work engaging critically with the transdisciplinary fields of contemporary craft, design, art and technology, a strong conceptual framework, deep engagement with process and materials, a clear direction or question for the residency, and genuine readiness for the open studios format. Top applications go to interview with the Deputy Director of Education and Manager of Public Programs and Studios, and finalists' references are contacted. Applicants are strongly encouraged to visit the studios in person first. NOT AN EXHIBITION PROGRAMME: residents may show work in progress in their studio and finished work in the sixth-floor vitrines, but the residency does not include a gallery exhibition or a proposal for MAD's galleries. STUDIO RESTRICTIONS: no open flames, all hot work approved by the Director of Facilities, no large or unsafe power tools such as saws, and no toxic materials including turpentine, certain resins, glazes, lusters and lead-based paints. Applicants submit one application indicating their preferred schedule and which session or sessions they want, and are selected for one. Initial notifications October 2026, finalist interviews November 2026, offers December 2026. Questions: education@madmuseum.org. Deadline 11 September 2026.

  8. GLEAN 2027 Call for Artists

    GLEAN (Recology Oregon Recovery, Metro and ReClaim It), with Rainmaker Craft Initiative · Portland, Oregon, USA. Open to artists from Portland; work takes place at the Metro Central transfer station. · Deadline: 13 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 3,000 per artist, increased from previous years. Five local artists are selected. Artists also receive five months of access to the Metro Central transfer station to gather discarded materials, and the programme culminates in a group exhibition at a local gallery. This round includes another collaboration with Rainmaker Craft Initiative. No application fee is mentioned.

    GLEAN, meaning to collect gradually bit by bit, is a juried art programme that taps the creativity of artists to make people think about their consumption habits, the waste they generate and the resources they throw away. Each year five local artists, chosen by a jury of arts and environmental professionals, get access to the Metro Central transfer station for five months to glean discarded materials and make work from them. The programme ends with a group exhibition of the results at a local gallery in the summer. The 2027 round carries an increased stipend of USD 3,000 per artist and another collaboration with Rainmaker Craft Initiative. Launched in 2010, GLEAN is a partnership between Recology Oregon Recovery Inc., an employee-owned company managing resource recovery facilities, Metro, the regional government running the Portland area's garbage and recycling system, and ReClaim It, a nonprofit environmental arts organization. ELIGIBILITY: artists from Portland, Oregon. Applications are open 1 August to 13 September 2026.

  9. Queens Museum: QM-Jerome Foundation Fellowship for Emerging Artists 2027-2028

    Queens Museum (with the Jerome Foundation) · Queens, New York City, USA · Deadline: 28 Sep 2026 · Award: $20,000 fellowship award per artist (two artists selected), plus professional development consultations, mentorship from Queens Museum staff and an established New York-based artist, and a solo exhibition at the Queens Museum in 2028. No application fee.

    The QM-Jerome Foundation Fellowship for Emerging Artists selects two emerging visual artists living in New York City for its 2027-2028 cohort. Each fellow receives a $20,000 award, professional development consultations, mentorship from Queens Museum staff and an established New York-based artist, and a solo exhibition at the Queens Museum in 2028, with curatorial support across a full year of project development. Aimed at early-career visual artists (roughly two to ten years of professional practice) who have a consistent practice and developing public profile but have not yet reached major institutional recognition. ELIGIBILITY: must currently reside in one of NYC's five boroughs and maintain residency through the fellowship; artists enrolled in degree-granting programs are not eligible; review the detailed criteria on exhibition history, previous grants and years of practice. Supported by the Jerome Foundation. Apply via the Queens Museum fellowship program page. Deadline: 28 September 2026, 11:59 PM EST.

  10. Literary Arts Project Grants 2027

    South Arts · USA, nine-state South Arts region: Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina and Tennessee. · Deadline: 07 Oct 2026 · Award: Up to USD 5,000 per project. Eligible expenses include personnel, artist fees, editorial services, travel, marketing, facility rental and insurance. No application fee.

    South Arts' Literary Arts Project Grants fund up to USD 5,000 for the creation and publishing of creative literary work (journals, books, anthologies) and for community engagement projects such as reading series, festivals and podcasts. Eligible expenses include personnel, artist fees, editorial services, travel, marketing, facility rental and insurance. ELIGIBILITY: independent nonprofit literary arts organizations, independent publishers, and small presses including literary journals, headquartered in the nine-state South Arts region (Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina and Tennessee) with an established literary arts mission. This is an organizational grant, not an individual one; individual writers should look at the Southern Prize for Literature instead. Applications opened 12 August 2026; award notifications in November 2026, with a final report due 30 June 2027, and all expenses must occur between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027. Deadline 7 October 2026.

  11. Kala Fellowship Program

    Kala Art Institute · Berkeley, California, USA · Deadline: 01 Mar 2027 · Award: USD 3,000 stipend, 24/7 facility access for 1 to 9 months, 1 month of housing for artists outside the 9 Bay Area counties, a Kala class or workshop, and a group exhibition USD 25 application fee (free if applying December through January 15)

    The Kala Fellowship Program supports international, national, and local artists producing innovative work in and across multiple media, with an emphasis on Kala's specialized printmaking, photography, and digital media equipment. The institute supports up to 6 artists per cycle with a USD 3,000 stipend, 24/7 access to its Berkeley facilities for 1 to 9 months, one month of housing for artists based outside the 9 Bay Area counties, a Kala class or workshop, and a culminating group show in the Kala Gallery. Applications are submitted through kala.slideroom.com and require a statement of interest, an artist CV, documentation of 5 to 10 recent works, and two references. Artists enrolled in a degree-granting program during the fellowship period are not eligible. The application opens in December with no fee through January 15, after which a USD 25 application fee applies through the March 1 deadline.

  12. Marfa Open: Residency and Exhibition Proposals (rolling)

    Marfa Open Arts · Marfa, Texas, United States. Open to international artists; intake by direct contact. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: 3-4 week live-in residency in the Marfa Open gallery space ending in an exhibition; annual art festival participation; historically no fees.

    Marfa Open runs a gallery, an annual art festival (6th edition), and a 3-4 week live-in residency in its gallery space that ends in an exhibition; it has also mounted international shows. Historically free to propose and open to everyone including international artists. NOTE: there is no formal application portal; intake is by direct contact via the website (the old marfaopen.org application site is dead; the live site is marfaopen.art).
