Funded Open Calls in the US

Currently 15 active open calls open to applicants in the US, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions.   1. Netherland-America Foundation: Cultural Grant Program (1 November 2026 Cut-Off)

    The Netherland-America Foundation (NAF) · Netherlands and United States. For professional artists and cultural organisations running projects with a clear Dutch-American exchange. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Typically up to USD 5,000 per project, depending on scope and impact; higher amounts possible depending on available budget. No application fee.

    The Netherland-America Foundation funds professional artistic and cultural projects that show a clear exchange between Dutch and American artists, institutions or audiences, such as exhibitions, performances and other cultural initiatives that promote dialogue and collaboration. Both emerging artists and established organisations may apply. NOT FUNDED: applicants who are not working cultural professionals, seminar attendance, degree tuition, residencies, research, projects without a Dutch-American connection (these are not forwarded to the committee), and projects already underway or finished at the time of review. TIMING: apply at least 6 months before the money is needed; a letter of intent is optional and encouraged for projects 9 or more months out, after which the committee may invite a full application. Recent grantees must wait three years before reapplying. Letter and budget in English, addressed to the NAF Cultural Committee; include visuals where relevant. QUARTERLY CUT-OFFS: 1 February (window 2 Nov-1 Feb, results mid-March), 1 May (results mid-June), 1 August (results mid-September) and 1 November (window 2 Aug-1 Nov, results mid-December). Submit via Submittable.

  2. Sanford Underground Research Facility: Artist-in-Residence Program 2027 (Lead, South Dakota)

    Sanford Underground Research Facility (SURF) · Sanford Underground Research Facility, Lead, South Dakota, USA, in the Black Hills. The call does not state a residency or nationality restriction; travel, housing and per diem for both visits are covered. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend paid in two installments, plus up to USD 5,000 in additional funds the artist may apply for to create the artwork. Transportation (airfare, rental car or mileage), housing and per diem are covered for both visits; a studio space and guided surface and underground tours are provided; return shipping of the exhibition is covered up to USD 7,000.

    The SURF Artist-in-Residence program invites artists to create work inspired by the Sanford Underground Research Facility in Lead, South Dakota. At 4,850 feet deep, SURF is the deepest underground laboratory in the United States, housed in the former Homestake Gold Mine, and hosts experiments in physics, biology, geology and engineering. The residency is modeled after programs run by the National Park Service, CERN and Fermilab, and aims to create awareness of the science and encourage interdisciplinary work. STRUCTURE: a four-week on-site visit in summer 2027 (between 22 May and 31 July, timed to the artist's schedule) with studio time, guided underground visits, tours of Lead and the surrounding Black Hills sites, and dinners with scientists; then a one-week visit in October 2027 to install an art exhibition, give up to two lectures based on the proposal, and run promotional or outreach events in the local community (which may include schools, universities, interviews or documentation of the residency). FUNDING: a USD 5,000 stipend paid in two installments; the artist may apply for up to USD 5,000 in additional funds for creating the artwork; transportation, housing and per diem for both visits; and return shipping covered up to USD 7,000. CONDITIONS: each artist donates at least one artwork, selected by a SURF representative, to SURF's permanent art collection, and is responsible for installation and de-installation in coordination with SURF and the exhibition space. A timeline and expectations are agreed in an MOU before the residency begins. PHYSICAL REQUIREMENTS: working underground requires being comfortable in dark spaces, following all safety protocols, walking on uneven surfaces for at least a mile at a time, and negotiating wet, slippery conditions and an elevator ride in the dark; safety training and loaned personal protective equipment are mandatory, and a SURF guide accompanies the artist at all times. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026, via the SURF AiR application portal.

  3. MIT Museum: BRAIN Digital Content Open Call

    MIT Museum · Cambridge, Massachusetts, US (content shown on-site; remote creation possible) · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Flat honorarium of USD 2,500 to 5,000 per selected proposal, at the Museum's discretion

    The MIT Museum seeks proposals for compelling digital content exploring humanity's relationship with the BRAIN, the museum's thematic focus from April to September 2027 bringing together art, science, technology and design on cognition, neurotechnology, brain-computer interfaces, mental health and the boundaries of ability. Selected proposals run as ambient content on the two-story digital screen (3840x3024, 80:63 aspect ratio, 20'3" x 16'2") in the museum's central Exchange amphitheater. The museum wants immersive, non-linear, experimental, non-didactic work, such as artful visualizations of data or scientific phenomena; interactivity (touchscreens, QR codes) is out of scope and content-critical audio is discouraged. Content must run on the museum's existing hardware (Windows PC with RTX 3090, NEC LED wall with Analog Way 4K system). WHO CAN APPLY: artists, scientists, engineers, designers and other creative practitioners, from MIT or the broader community. SUPPORT: flat honorarium of USD 2,500-5,000 per selected proposal; no additional development funding. Applications reviewed from 2 November, accepted list finalized January 2027. Deadline: 1 November 2026.

  4. MIT Museum: BRAIN Programmatic Open Call (public programs)

    MIT Museum · Cambridge, Massachusetts, US (programs take place at the MIT Museum in Kendall Square) · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Up to USD 1,000 total per selected program, covering all costs including speaker and performer fees, travel, materials and equipment rental; proposals using external funding or smaller budgets welcome. The Museum sets ticket prices and keeps ticket revenue. No application fee.

    Companion call to the BRAIN Digital Content Open Call: the MIT Museum seeks public programs exploring humanity's relationship with the BRAIN, its thematic focus from April to September 2027 spanning cognition, neurotechnology, brain-computer interfaces, mental health and the boundaries of ability. FORMATS: talks, demonstrations, performances, workshops, films, master classes, music, VR/XR, LARP and maker programs; art-and-science projects especially encouraged. NOT eligible: installations and exhibitions, including temporary ones (screen-based work goes through the separate BRAIN Digital Content call). VENUES: the Exchange amphitheater (capacity 100), Phillip Sharp Room (80), two Learning Labs classrooms (30 each), open areas and roving carts; target audience is curious adults and teens. FUNDING: up to USD 1,000 total per program, covering all costs including speaker/performer fees, travel, materials and equipment rental. WHO CAN APPLY: artists, scientists, engineers, designers and other creative practitioners aged 18+, from MIT or the broader community; proposals need not relate to MIT research; multiple proposals allowed. APPLY via the Fillout form with event name, program description, intended audience, practical considerations, budget, and marketing plans. TIMELINE: review from 2 November 2026, accepted programs finalized by 15 December 2026, programs run April to September 2027. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026, 11:59 PM EST. Questions: mitmuseum-opencall@mit.edu.

  5. Inciter Art (Fractured Atlas): quarterly open call for essays, 'tending to the hearth' (USD 750 stipend)

    Fractured Atlas (Inciter Art) · Remote (online publication); payment via W-9, so US-based writers in practice. · Deadline: 02 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 750 stipend per selected writer, paid by ACH deposit or check once the first draft is finished (a W-9 form is required). No submission fee.

    Inciter Art, the Fractured Atlas blog, runs themed quarterly open calls for short essays by artists and arts workers, and this season's theme is 'tending to the hearth': generosity, solidarity and collective investment in the invisible infrastructure behind creative work. Pitch an idea through the form; selected writers are notified one week after the deadline, get four weeks to write a first draft, then one round of edits with the final due a week later, with the Fractured Atlas editorial specialist available for support. Published pieces run 750 to 900 words. Editors look for pitches grounded in your own experience as an artist or arts worker that say something new or a familiar thing in a new way. AI may be used only in a supporting role for writing (research, structure, grammar) and AI-generated images are not accepted; every pitch is read by a human. Writers keep all rights and copyright; Fractured Atlas may use the final piece in promotion with credit. Everyone who submits hears back. ELIGIBILITY: artists and arts workers; payment requires a W-9, so it is set up for US taxpayers. Pitches outside the themed call are also accepted and reviewed on a rolling basis. DEADLINE: Monday 2 November 2026, 11:59PM ET; later submissions are reviewed on a rolling basis.

  6. Data Through Design 2027 Open Call: 10th Anniversary Exhibition (New York City)

    Data Through Design (DxD) · New York City, USA. Open call; applicants must be approved to work in the US to be paid the stipend. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 900 stipend per selected project, paid half in the weeks leading up to the exhibition and half after. No application fee.

    Data Through Design, the annual exhibition of artworks that creatively analyze, interpret and interrogate data from New York City's Open Data portal, opens its 10th anniversary call, held in conjunction with NYC Open Data Week. THEME: reflections, reverberations and the arc of data over time; the evidence of time (footprints, traces, scars, markers of change), temporal mirrors and kaleidoscopes, messages from the past and future, fractals of time and visuals of scale. Artists may also respond to a previous DxD theme in light of time, and alumni artists are particularly encouraged to apply, including by revisiting past projects. FORMATS: digital, analog, physical, performance, sound works, walks, installations, creative coding, participatory mapping, drawings and more; the organizers highlight participatory data collection, radical and cognitive mapping, and data experienced through time, sound and the senses. REQUIREMENT: projects must reference or use at least one NYC Open Data dataset; other data, including novel and experimental collection systems, is welcome alongside it. WHAT YOU GET: USD 900 stipend, half before and half after the exhibition, plus inclusion in the multi-media group show in New York City, tentatively 26 March to 11 April 2027. Work must be installation-ready by 19 March 2027 and delivered to the venue 22 to 24 March. ELIGIBILITY: open to artists, civic technologists, data enthusiasts, city dwellers and creative practitioners; applicants must be approved to work in the US to be paid the stipend. The submission process has been revamped for this edition, with a new rubric and submission materials to review. APPLICATION: free, via the submission form on the DxD website. DEADLINE: 15 November 2026 at 23:59 EST.

  9. Kennedy Center: Access/VSA Emerging Young Artists 2027, Thrive (15 x USD 3,000)

    John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, Office of Accessibility and VSA · USA (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico and territories; right to work in the US required). Career week and exhibition in Washington DC. · Deadline: 19 Nov 2026 · Award: 15 winners each receive a USD 3,000 stipend, an all-expenses-paid career development week in Washington DC (14 to 18 June 2027), and exhibition of one work in DC. No entry fee.

    The Kennedy Center's Access/VSA Emerging Young Artists Program (2027 theme: Thrive) awards 15 young disabled visual artists a USD 3,000 stipend, an all-expenses-paid career development week in Washington DC (14 to 18 June 2027) and exhibition of one work. ELIGIBILITY: aged 16 to 25 on the deadline date; US-based with the right to work in the US; have a disability as defined by the ADA and be willing to disclose it publicly; submitted work made in the last three years and after the onset of disability; no generative-AI work; previous winners ineligible. OBLIGATIONS: attend three virtual workshops (April to June 2027) and the DC week to receive the stipend. APPLICATION: free, via CaFE (artist.callforentry.org); accommodation and alternative-format requests by 30 October 2026. Winners notified by 13 January 2027. Questions: vsainfo@kennedy-center.org. DEADLINE: 19 November 2026, 23:59 ET.

  11. CURRENTS 2027 Art & Technology Festival: Call for Submissions (Santa Fe)

    CURRENTS New Media / Parallel Studios · Santa Fe, New Mexico, USA. Festival 11-20 June 2027. Open to established and emerging new media artists from the USA and around the world. · Deadline: 08 Dec 2026 · Award: NO GUARANTEED CASH, listed as a deliberate exception to the desk's paid-only rule: selected artists may be eligible for travel, lodging and shipping support, decided case by case, and the festival may be able to supply needed equipment. International shipping support is explicitly uncertain due to cost. SUBMISSION FEE: USD 30 for a single work, USD 25 each for multiple works.

    CURRENTS presents one of the leading art and technology festivals in the United States, held every summer in Santa Fe, New Mexico since 2010, bringing together established and emerging new media artists from the USA and worldwide. The 2027 edition (11-20 June 2027) accepts submissions in: installations (interactive and non-interactive); audio experiences; virtual, augmented and mixed reality environments; robotics, kinetic and electro-mechanical objects and installations; multimedia performance; fabrication, 2D/3D printing and wearables; and experimental single channel video, documentary and animation. The festival is a collaboration between the non-profit Parallel Studios, partners throughout the city, and interns and volunteers. SUPPORT: selected artists may be eligible for travel, lodging and shipping support, though the festival warns it may not be able to cover international shipping given costs and risks, and may instead be able to supply needed equipment; no artist fee is published. SUBMISSION FEE: USD 30 single, USD 25 each for multiple. DEADLINE: 8 December 2026; participating artists announced March 2027. Questions to connect@currentsnewmedia.org.

Delgado for a year-long program culminating in an exhibition at the Kaplan Gallery. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 10,000 budget covering exhibition costs and curatorial fees, year-long mentorship, and staff support for printing, promotions and exhibition programming.   13. apexart Open Calls: Curatorial Proposals for NYC and International Exhibitions

    apexart · Exhibitions in apexart's New York City space (October call) or anywhere in the world except NYC (February call). Anyone, from anywhere, can submit. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 10,000 per selected exhibition: USD 8,000 for expenses (curator travel, artwork shipping, artist and participant honorariums, equipment, installation) plus an honorarium of up to USD 2,000 for the curator/organizer. For exhibitions outside NYC, apexart matches up to USD 1,000 for venue rental if needed. apexart additionally covers insurance, advertising, publicity, brochure design/printing/mailing, the opening reception and (for NYC) documentation, and handles most logistics. No application fee.

    apexart's Open Calls turn a curatorial idea into a funded apexart exhibition, selected by a crowdsourced international jury of more than 600 people in 70 countries. WHO CAN SUBMIT: anyone, from anywhere, with no fees at any level: trained and student curators, artists, writers and creative people regardless of experience, background or location. HOW: a 500-word proposal for a focused, idea-driven, original group exhibition, submitted through the online form during the proposal window. TWO ANNUAL WINDOWS: every October for exhibitions in apexart's NYC space, and every February (to early March) for exhibitions taking place anywhere in the world except NYC; for the international call you must propose a location you have actually visited, with the specific venue settled after selection. SELECTION: jurors vote anonymously online (each proposal gets an equal number of views, submitter names hidden); proposals are judged on the strength of the idea and how well it is communicated, not on connections or CV. WHAT WINNERS GET: a USD 10,000 budget (USD 8,000 for expenses, up to USD 2,000 curator honorarium, plus up to USD 1,000 venue-rental match outside NYC), with apexart covering insurance, publicity, the printed brochure, the opening reception and most logistics including loan agreements and shipping, and staff working closely with the guest curator. Winning proposals become part of apexart's next exhibition season. Check the site for the current window's exact dates; submission forms only go live during the proposal periods.

    Roger Tory Peterson Institute (RTPI) · Jamestown, New York, USA. Out-of-town artists receive housing, per diem, and travel reimbursement. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 honorarium per artist; out-of-town artists also receive housing, per diem, and reimbursement for travel.

    The Roger Tory Peterson Institute's artist-in-residence program nurtures nature artists with a week in Jamestown, New York, and facilitated access to the Peterson Collection, inclusive of Roger Tory Peterson's original field guide art, fine art, films, slides, and personal papers. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 1,000 honorarium, with housing, per diem, and travel reimbursement for out-of-town artists.     Northwest Film Forum (Warhol Foundation Regional Regranting Program) · King County, Washington, USA (incorporated and unincorporated areas). Proof of residency required at finalist stage. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 60,000 distributed in total: one USD 10,000 grant for an artist team or artist-run collective, and ten USD 5,000 grants for new works or projects by individual artists.

    Run by Seattle's Northwest Film Forum as part of the Warhol Foundation's Regional Regranting Program, the Collective Power Fund supports visionary new arts projects that incite public dialogue, push boundaries, explore genre fluidity, and are often non-traditional, anti-institutional, socially responsible or disruptive of existing structures. APPLICATION: via Submittable (nwfilmforum.submittable.com). DEADLINE: the 2026 application is open but the closing date was not published at the time of listing; the 2025 cycle closed mid-November, so apply early and confirm the deadline with grants manager jade@nwfilmforum.org.
