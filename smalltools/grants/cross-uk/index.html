Cross-Disciplinary and Social Impact Grants in the UK

Currently 8 active cross-disciplinary and social impact grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the UK. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Youth Music: Trailblazer Fund (Round 14)

    Youth Music · England only. Projects for children and young people aged 25 or under. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 2,000 to GBP 30,000 for organisations in England. No application fee.

    Youth Music's Trailblazer Fund gives GBP 2,000 to GBP 30,000 to organisations in England running projects that let children and young people aged 25 or under make, learn and earn in music. A project should do at least one of three things: trial work or test a new way of working, sustain a grassroots programme, or disrupt the status quo. Doing all three is welcome. THEMES: your work must meet one of these: early years; Disabled, d/Deaf and neurodivergent young people; the youth justice system; young people facing barriers; young adults; or organisations and the workforce. WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE IN PRACTICE, using Youth Music's own examples: a new CIC testing ways to help nurseries embed music-making into the day; a small grassroots music organisation sustaining a programme that already works; a Music Education Hub setting up a Youth Board with real budget and decision-making power over how the hub is run; or an established youth centre trying a music programme for the first time. ROUND 14 (this entry): opened 23 October 2026, deadline 20 November 2026 at 5pm, notification 19 March 2027, for projects starting between April and June 2027. All deadlines are 5pm. NOTE: this is a grant to ORGANISATIONS, not to individual musicians or composers. Apply via the Youth Music funding portal (youthmusic.my.site.com); access support of up to GBP 900 is available for d/Deaf, Disabled or neurodivergent applicants.

  2. Futures of Listening x Immersive Audio Network: Residency Open Call (Bristol, IAN Festival 2027)

    Futures of Listening (FIBER, 4DSOUND, Robin Koek, Art of Sound) and the Immersive Audio Network (IAN) · Bristol, UK (Arnolfini and IAN's mobile Immersive Audio Lab). Open only to practitioners registered with the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (KvK). · Deadline: 21 Nov 2026 · Award: EUR 1,750 residency fee (excl. VAT, paid on invoice), plus return travel from the Netherlands to Bristol and private accommodation for 22 to 30 January 2027. No application fee.

    One residency for a freelance audio practitioner (composer, sound designer, audio maker, artist or researcher) registered in the Netherlands, to develop an existing project into a spatial, story-led sound work. STRUCTURE: a two-day introductory course in immersive audio, then three days of studio work in IAN's mobile Immersive Audio Lab (a dome with 25 Genelec loudspeakers and 3 subwoofers, Dolby Atmos 9.1.6 and Ambisonics up to fourth order; own computer and software welcome), ending with a public work-in-progress at the IAN Festival at Arnolfini, Bristol. RESEARCH FOCUS: sound-first storytelling, sonic stories for and by multiple audiences including the non-human, and the whole body in space as the site of immersive experience. Mentoring by Prof. Kate Pullinger (NET Lab, Bath Spa University) and Dr Angela McArthur (UCL). The resident also writes an editorial piece for The Writing Platform, published March 2027. ELIGIBILITY: KvK registration required (the fee is paid on invoice); working DAW skills (editing, montage, mixing); no prior spatial audio experience needed and emerging artists are encouraged; available to travel 22 to 30 January 2027. Applications in English. Deadline 21 November 2026, 23:59 CET.

  3. Refugee Festival Scotland 2027 x Opening Shot: Open Call for First-Time Filmmakers (GBP 1,500 fee plus GBP 3,000 production budget)

    Refugee Festival Scotland (Scottish Refugee Council) with Opening Shot · Glasgow, Scotland, UK (six in-person workshop days in Glasgow). RESTRICTED: applicants must live in Scotland and consider themselves a refugee or asylum seeker, or have family lived experience of seeking safety; not in full-time formal education. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 1,500 participation fee plus a GBP 3,000 production budget per filmmaker, with mentoring and a premiere screening at Refugee Festival Scotland in June 2027. Four filmmakers selected. No application fee stated.

    Refugee Festival Scotland (run by the Scottish Refugee Council, festival 11 to 20 June 2027) and Opening Shot invite four first-time filmmakers with lived experience of seeking safety to make a short film of about five minutes. WHAT YOU GET: a GBP 1,500 participation fee, a GBP 3,000 production budget, six in-person workshop days in Glasgow (December 2026 to March 2027), a 14-week supported production period with mentoring, and a premiere screening at the festival in June 2027. ELIGIBILITY: aged 18 and over; you consider yourself a refugee or asylum seeker, or your family has lived experience of seeking safety; you live in Scotland; you are not currently enrolled in full-time formal education; you can travel to Glasgow for all six workshop days. Beginners welcome, no previous experience or script needed. DEADLINE: Sunday 22 November 2026 at midnight. OTHER 2027 FESTIVAL STRANDS: the Artists Professional Development Programme (GBP 1,000 grants) closed on 8 October 2026; the Small Grants round for refugee-led and refugee-focused groups in Scotland (up to GBP 750 in 2026) runs from early December 2026 to mid-February 2027; event registration for organisers opens early December 2026 and closes late March 2027. Contact: festival@scottishrefugeecouncil.org.uk.

  4. Youth Music: Recharge Fund, Racial Equity (up to GBP 120,000 core funding over four years)

    Youth Music · England. For creative organisations led by communities who experience racial injustice and working with children and/or young people. · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: Core funding of up to GBP 120,000 over four years per organisation, from a total pot of GBP 2,400,000. No application fee.

    Youth Music's Recharge Fund: Racial Equity gives multi-year core funding to creative organisations across England that are led by communities who experience racial injustice and that work with children and/or young people of different ages and backgrounds. It is not music-only: organisations can work across a variety of art forms. MONEY: up to GBP 120,000 of core (unrestricted) funding over four years, from a total investment of GBP 2,400,000. TIMELINE: applications opened 16 October 2026 and close 4 December 2026 at 5pm; decisions at the end of March 2027; funded programmes start between 1 June and 30 September 2027. HOW TO APPLY: through the Youth Music funding portal (youthmusic.my.site.com); 1:1 calls with the team can be booked. Access support of up to GBP 900 is available for d/Deaf, Disabled or neurodivergent applicants. NOTE: this is a grant to ORGANISATIONS, not to individual artists.

  5. Cove Park Awarded Residencies 2027-2028 (Scotland, UK, Central and South America and diasporas)

    Cove Park · Cove Park, Peaton Hill, Argyll and Bute, Scotland (in person, private self-catering accommodation and studio). Open to people based in the UK, Central or South America, Central and South American diaspora artists based in Europe or North America, and Scotland-born artists living anywhere. Students in formal education are not eligible. · Deadline: 11 Dec 2026 · Award: Fully funded: GBP 775 per week fee (VAT inclusive, freelance), travel allowance (Scotland GBP 65 to 200, rest of UK up to GBP 250, Europe up to GBP 400, other international up to GBP 1,250), materials up to GBP 150 for studio-based practitioners, private accommodation and studio, visa and access support. Application Access Bursary available (request by 30 November 2026). No application fee.

    Cove Park's biennial call for fully funded Awarded Residencies at its 50-acre rural residency site in Argyll, on the west coast of Scotland, programmed between May 2027 and December 2028. Residencies run from 2 weeks up to 12 weeks (average four weeks) and can be split across the two years around caring or work commitments; international residents can count slow-travel time as part of the funded period. Open to artists, creative practitioners, writers, translators and researchers in all art forms and at every career stage, including collectives and collaborative groups; no formal qualification needed. Priorities include work responding to climate breakdown, sound artists and musicians (new Sound Studio), and work on home, place, language (Scots, Gaelic, BSL), displacement and belonging. No final presentation is required. ELIGIBILITY: based in the UK, Central America or South America; Central and South American diaspora artists based in Europe or North America; or born in Scotland and living elsewhere. Anyone in full- or part-time formal education on 11 December 2026 is not eligible. SUPPORT: GBP 775 per week fee; travel allowance (Scotland GBP 65 to 100 Central Belt, up to GBP 200 Borders/Highlands/Islands; rest of UK up to GBP 250; Europe up to GBP 400; other international up to GBP 1,250); materials up to GBP 150; visa support; private self-catering accommodation (suitable for collaborators and those attending with children) and private studios. An Application Access Bursary (e.g. to pay a support worker) is available for applicants facing barriers due to disability or neurodivergence; request it from Alex Marrs by 30 November 2026. Video or audio applications accepted. Applicants are asked not to use generative AI in their application. APPLY via the online form: 500-word statement, 100-word bio, CV, up to 8 images or video/audio links or writing samples. SELECTION: shortlisting by the programme team and former residents; shortlist notified by 12 February 2027; Zoom interviews the week of 22 February 2027. Contact: Alex Marrs, alex@covepark.org. DEADLINE: Friday 11 December 2026, 5pm GMT.

  6. Daiwa Foundation Small Grants, March 2027 round (GBP 2,000-9,000 for UK-Japan projects)

    Daiwa Anglo-Japanese Foundation · For individuals, societies, associations and other bodies in the UK or Japan running projects that connect the two countries. · Deadline: 31 Mar 2027 · Award: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (typical awards GBP 3,000-4,000); flights up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day. A contribution, not full funding. No application fee.

    The Daiwa Foundation Small Grants support projects that promote interaction between the UK and Japan in any field, including arts exchanges, exhibitions, conferences, grassroots and educational exchanges and research travel, with travel between the two countries as the main focus. New initiatives are encouraged, and gender equality and SDG themes are welcome; continuing or regular events (such as an annual festival edition) are unlikely to be funded without a strong reason or special occasion. NOT ELIGIBLE: commissions of artworks, capital costs, consumables, salaries or professional fees, tuition and commercial activity. WHAT YOU GET: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (most awards GBP 3,000-4,000), with flights funded up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day, plus GBP 50 extra per flight for environmental projects in Japan. APPLICATION: read the Application Notes, then apply via the Foundation's online portal (separate forms for UK- and Japan-based applicants); advice from grants@dajf.org.uk. Decisions by late May or early June 2027. DEADLINE: 31 March 2027 (the following round closes 30 September).

  7. Awesome Foundation Local Chapters: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants (US, UK, Canada, Australia, NL, Asia)

    Awesome Foundation (local chapters) · City and regional chapters; each funds projects in or benefiting its own area. US, Canada, Australia, UK (Glasgow), Netherlands (Utrecht), Malaysia, Singapore, Armenia, Uzbekistan. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Usually USD 1,000 (or local equivalent) per grant, no strings attached, typically one grant per chapter per month. No application fee.

    The Awesome Foundation is a network of volunteer giving circles: in each chapter about ten trustees pool their own money to give a USD 1,000 no-strings micro-grant, usually monthly, to a project that adds "awesome" to their area: art, public interventions, community tech, events, science and civic ideas. One application on the main site can be sent to the chapter for your location. ACTIVE CHAPTERS (made grants in 2026, checked 9 Nov 2026): US: Ann Arbor, Austin, Baltimore, Cass Clay, Chicago, Cleveland, Detroit, Gainesville, Georgetown MA, Gloucester MA, Hamilton-Wenham MA, Ipswich MA, Los Angeles, Newburgh NY, Newburyport MA, New York City, Oahu, Orlando, Peekskill NY, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Portland OR, Poughkeepsie NY, Rhode Island, Rockport MA, San Antonio, San Francisco, San Jose, Santa Cruz, South Bend, St. Paul, State College PA, Washington DC, Westminster MD (Durham, Miami and Seattle each made one grant early in 2026). Canada: Kingston, Kitchener-Waterloo, Newmarket, Ottawa, Toronto. Australia: Adelaide, Fleurieu, Lake Mac, Lower Hunter, Newcastle, Sydney. UK: Glasgow. Netherlands: Utrecht (one grant in early 2026; Leiden and Rotterdam have been quiet since 2025 and 2024). Asia: Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Tashkent, Yerevan. ELIGIBILITY: set per chapter, generally individuals or groups doing the project in that chapter's area. APPLY: free, short online form. DEADLINE: rolling. Worldwide themed chapters are listed separately (Disability, Conservation and Climate, On the Water).

  8. Wales Arts International: International Opportunities Fund (rolling, up to GBP 7,500 for presenting work and collaborating abroad)

    Wales Arts International (Arts Council of Wales) · For arts organisations, individual creative professionals and collectives based in Wales; activity takes place in person outside Wales and the UK with at least one international partner. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to GBP 7,500 for international collaboration, presentation of work, and networks and events; up to GBP 5,000 for international residencies (up to GBP 7,500 outside Europe with prior approval). Covers up to 90 percent of eligible costs. No application fee.

    Wales Arts International's International Opportunities Fund supports Wales-based creative professionals and arts organisations to build relationships, collaborations and networks abroad, share skills internationally and raise Wales's profile. WHO: arts organisations, individual creative professionals and formal or informal collectives based in Wales (unconstituted groups need one lead applicant). WHAT IS FUNDED: in-person, arts-focused activity outside Wales and the UK with at least one international partner, such as presenting work at an international partner's invitation (where the partner pays at least an appropriate fee), attending international networks and events, collaboration and partnership development, and residencies organised by an international partner. WHAT YOU GET: up to GBP 7,500 for collaboration, presentation, and networks and events, or up to GBP 5,000 for residencies (GBP 7,500 outside Europe with prior approval), covering up to 90 percent of eligible costs. Artists, bands, songwriters or producers invited to international showcase festivals should use the PRS Foundation International Showcase Fund instead. APPLICATION: rolling via the Arts Council of Wales portal; projects must start at least 10 working weeks after submission. DEADLINE: rolling.
