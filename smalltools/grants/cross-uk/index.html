Cross-Disciplinary and Social Impact Grants in the UK

Currently 12 active cross-disciplinary and social impact grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the UK. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions.   1. Liveline Fund: Round 1 (UK grassroots live music)

    Liveline, delivered by Music Venue Trust with Save Our Scene and the Association of Independent Promoters · United Kingdom. Guarantorship shows must be at Music Venue Alliance member venues; for a full tour at least 70 percent of dates must be at subscribed MVT venues. Regional focus includes the Highlands and Islands, West Wales and Northern Ireland. · Deadline: 18 Aug 2026 · Award: No fixed grant sizes published; every application is assessed case by case against a real budget rather than first come first served. The Guarantorship Scheme covers up to 80 percent of losses on a show or tour: if it breaks even nothing is paid, and if it turns a profit the promoter or venue keeps it. The other three funds cover venue hire, travel, accommodation, ferry and remote-access costs, or genre-specific safety, licensing and access costs. No application fee.

    Liveline is a national risk-sharing fund rebuilding grassroots touring, programming and local music scenes across the UK, delivered by Music Venue Trust with Save Our Scene and the Association of Independent Promoters, alongside the LIVE Trust and the industry's voluntary levy on stadium and arena tickets. The premise is that good shows are not happening because the downside risk is too high, so rather than a traditional subsidy it is an incentive-led model that only pays out when things go wrong. FOUR FUNDS: (1) GUARANTORSHIP SCHEME covers up to 80 percent of losses on an individual show or tour, for promoters, in-house bookers, artists or their representatives; break even and nothing is paid, turn a profit and you keep it. (2) LOCAL SCENE DEVELOPMENT FUND covers venue hire costs and local promoter training to build regular programming, for independent promoters or venues developing an in-house programme. (3) TOUR EXTENSION AND REGIONAL ACCESS FUND covers the long travel, fuel, accommodation and ferry costs that stop tours reaching remote parts of the UK, for promoters, agents, management and artists. (4) GENRE SPECIFIC SAFETY AND ACCESS FUND covers extra security, licensing and structural costs for genres and scenes that face additional barriers, for venues, promoters, artists or collectives. ELIGIBILITY: grassroots venues (Music Venue Alliance member venues), independent promoters including AIP members, in-house venue programmers, and artists, agents and teams building sustainable tours. Guarantorship shows must be at MVA subscribed venues, and for a full tour at least 70 percent of dates must be at subscribed MVT venues. TWO CATCHES WORTH KNOWING: tours must NOT already be announced, and Liveline funds a headline artist only once. Applications for Round 1 close Tuesday 18 August 2026. Start with the eligibility checker at livelinefund.uk, or contact info@livelinefund.uk.

  2. Youth Music: Trailblazer Fund (Round 13)

    Youth Music · England only. Projects for children and young people aged 25 or under. · Deadline: 28 Aug 2026 · Award: GBP 2,000 to GBP 30,000 for organisations in England. No application fee.

    Youth Music's Trailblazer Fund gives GBP 2,000 to GBP 30,000 to organisations in England running projects that let children and young people aged 25 or under make, learn and earn in music. A project should do at least one of three things: trial work or test a new way of working, sustain a grassroots programme, or disrupt the status quo. Doing all three is welcome. THEMES: your work must meet one of these: early years; Disabled, d/Deaf and neurodivergent young people; the youth justice system; young people facing barriers; young adults; or organisations and the workforce. WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE IN PRACTICE, using Youth Music's own examples: a new CIC testing ways to help nurseries embed music-making into the day; a small grassroots music organisation sustaining a programme that already works; a Music Education Hub setting up a Youth Board with real budget and decision-making power over how the hub is run; or an established youth centre trying a music programme for the first time. ROUND 13: opened 31 July 2026, deadline 28 August 2026 at 5pm, notification 11 December 2026, for projects starting between January and March 2027. ROUND 14: opens 23 October 2026, deadline 20 November 2026 at 5pm, notification 19 March 2027, for projects starting between April and June 2027. All deadlines are 5pm. NOTE: this is a grant to ORGANISATIONS, not to individual musicians or composers.

  3. University of East Anglia: Deputy Dean, CUCN-UEA Digital Creativity Institute

    University of East Anglia (UEA), Faculty of Arts and Humanities · Norwich, United Kingdom · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: GBP 69,488 to 73,709 per annum depending on qualifications and experience, plus the standard UEA pension and leave package. No application fee.

    Senior leadership post at the Digital Creativity Institute, the joint venture between the Communication University of China Nanjing (CUCN) and UEA, sitting in UEA's Faculty of Arts and Humanities. The Deputy Dean leads UEA's academic contribution to the Institute, covering curriculum and programme development, staffing and quality assurance across digital media, creative technology and related disciplines, and works with the CUCN partner leadership on strategy, recruitment and partnership growth. This is a rare senior role for someone who works across creative practice and digital technology rather than in a single discipline, and it involves regular engagement with the partner institution in China. Salary GBP 69,488 to 73,709. Advertised 3 August 2026 through jobs.ac.uk and the UEA vacancy portal; closes 31 August 2026. Check the UEA posting for the full person specification and whether the role requires periods based in Nanjing.

  4. ITER 2026: International Touring and Environmental Responsibility Programme

    Julie's Bicycle and in futurum, funded by Arts Council England, Arts Council Norway, Creative Scotland and the Danish Arts Foundation · England, Scotland, Norway and Denmark. Seminars run online. · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: The seminar programme is free to join. Groups of three or more participants can then apply for up to GBP 30,000 for collaborative projects, with funding applications open 6 November 2026 to 11 January 2027 and projects starting spring 2027. No application fee.

    ITER is now in its fifth year and supports people in the performing arts to build environmentally responsible, inclusive and equitable ways of touring and collaborating internationally. It is initiated and financed by Arts Council England, Arts Council Norway, Creative Scotland and the Danish Arts Foundation, and designed and delivered by Julie's Bicycle and in futurum. Creative Scotland joined as a partner for 2026. TWO TRACKS, up to 60 places in total: PATHFINDERS, ten sessions introducing the principles of sustainable touring for people new to the subject; and CHANGEMAKERS, nine sessions for those further along, on leadership and systemic change. Seminars run weekly from 1 October to 10 December 2026, Pathfinders on Tuesdays and Changemakers on Thursdays, 10:00 to 12:00 UK time, alongside networking and a new alumni network. THE MONEY COMES IN PHASE TWO: after the seminars, groups of three or more participants can apply for up to GBP 30,000 to implement collaborative projects that cut environmental impacts and advance climate justice. Funding applications open 6 November 2026 and close 11 January 2027; awarded projects begin in spring 2027. ELIGIBILITY: based in England, Scotland, Norway or Denmark, working in dance, music or theatre. That covers music and theatre venues receiving touring work, festivals, orchestras, theatre companies, solo artists, performers and musicians, performing arts producers, and multidisciplinary arts organisations. Applications are made through the relevant national funder (Arts Council England open funds, Creative Scotland targeted funding, Arts Council Norway, Danish Arts Foundation). Questions go to environmentaltouring@artscouncil.org.uk.

  5. Arts Council England: Music Growth Fund, GBP 1,000-40,000 strand

    Arts Council England · England. Supports new music by living, UK-based artists and music creators. · Deadline: 03 Sep 2026 · Award: GBP 1,000 to GBP 40,000, from a total annual budget of GBP 3,000,000 for this strand in 2026/27. You must find at least 25 percent of the total project budget as CASH match funding. No application fee.

    The Music Growth Fund is Arts Council England's new investment programme for growth in new music, meaning original live performance, sound recording or musical work recently or newly created or performed by living UK-based artists and music creators. It sits inside the wider GBP 45 million Music Growth Package. WHO IT IS FOR: music festivals, music managers, music promoters, music publishers, record labels, recording studios, rehearsal spaces and grassroots music venues, plus established sector bodies and other sector support organisations that support them. Each applicant type has its own Applicant Specific Guidance on the Music Growth Package pages. IMPORTANT: this is business and organisational investment, not a grant for individual artists to make work. Individual artists, bands, producers and artist collectives should instead look at the separate Music Creators Growth Fund (GBP 10,000-20,000), whose guidance lands 1 September 2026 with the portal opening 1 October 2026. WHAT TO PROPOSE: how investment will be used to grow music businesses and/or the wider music industry, and enhance opportunities for artists and music creators, industry professionals, music fans and new audiences. Growth can mean increased reach, stronger business models, improved sustainability, skills development or expanded capacity, and is judged in the context of each applicant's own scale and ambitions. DECISION CRITERIA: potential for growth, effective use of funds, contribution to a fairer and more inclusive music industry, and contribution to a climate resilient music industry. BEFORE YOU START: you need an approved profile on the Arts Council grants management system before you can submit, so allow time for that. Arts Council reserves the right to change the programme, so check you are on the latest guidance. THIS STRAND: GBP 1,000 to GBP 40,000, with a GBP 3,000,000 annual budget for 2026/27 and a 25 percent cash match requirement. ROUNDS FOR 2026/27 (GBP 1,000-40,000 strand): Round 1 opens 6 August and closes 3 September 2026, decision 28 October. Round 2 opens 4 September and closes 15 October 2026, decision 28 October. Round 3 opens 16 October and closes 26 November 2026, decision 3 February 2027. Round 4 opens 27 November 2026 and closes 7 January 2027, decision 9 March 2027. ROUNDS FOR THE GBP 40,001-100,000 strand: Round 1 opens 7 September and closes 1 October 2026, decision 30 November. Round 2 opens 23 November 2026 and closes 15 January 2027, decision 8 March 2027.

  6. UAL AKO Storytelling Institute: Storytelling Research Fellows 2026 (six posts)

    University of the Arts London (UAL AKO Storytelling Institute), with the Power of Pop Fund · High Holborn, London, United Kingdom (hybrid) · Deadline: 11 Sep 2026 · Award: GBP 48,563 per annum pro rata (around GBP 19,425 for the part-time fellowship hours), plus UAL benefits. No application fee.

    The UAL AKO Storytelling Institute, with the Power of Pop Fund, is recruiting six Storytelling Research Fellows to run independent research projects on how storytelling in popular culture shapes attitudes, behaviour and public discourse. Fellows work across media including television, film, gaming, podcasts and digital culture, are supported by the Institute's Head of Research and a peer cohort, and produce actionable findings for creative practitioners, funders and social-impact organisations, presented at a closing public symposium. ELIGIBILITY: experienced researchers with a PhD or equivalent research expertise gained academically, independently or through professional practice, with a track record of delivering high-quality research and working with external partners. Practice-based and non-academic researchers are explicitly in scope. APPLICATION: CV, record of publications, a research proposal of 1,000 to 1,200 words, and a supporting statement including at least one example of a research project you designed and delivered. Applications via the UAL jobs portal; closes 11 September 2026.

  7. Trust me, I'm an AI/rtist: Open Call for Creative Interventions

    Waag Futurelab (Horizon Europe project HAMLET) · Europe. Engaging with a specific AI Enabler includes an on-site visit to its technology partner: CERTH (Greece), The Audience Agency (Warrington, UK), the Centre of Excellence for Research & Innovation (Cyprus), Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (Greece), or CAUSA Creation (Austria). Final presentations September 2027. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 20,000 commission fee excluding VAT, inclusive of artist fee, travel, accommodation and production costs. Applicants must have a VAT number in Europe. No application fee.

    Waag Futurelab invites artists, theatre makers, choreographers, dancers, game designers and other creative practitioners to engage directly with seven experimental generative AI Enablers being co-created with technologists and dance, theatre and gaming professionals. The call is an invitation into the development process: selected artists get access to the technologies, datasets, interfaces and assumptions that shape these systems while they are still being built. Rather than testing whether the systems work, artists are asked to interrogate why they work the way they do, who they are designed for, what forms of creativity they enable or constrain, and what assumptions about culture, intelligence, authorship, labour, bodies, audiences and society are embedded in them. Waag is looking for individuals and collectives willing to move beyond adoption into investigation, and welcomes proposals that are speculative, critical, poetic, provocative, humorous, performative, constructive or disruptive, including work that challenges, misuses, reimagines, hacks, subverts or plays with the tools, exposes hidden biases, questions environmental costs, or imagines alternative technological futures. THE SEVEN AI ENABLERS, each requiring an on-site visit to its technology partner: the Generic Procedural Content Generation Enabler (text-to-3D assets and avatars, CERTH, Greece); the Data-Driven Cultural Content Adaptation Enabler (audience-aware creative content ideation with ranked candidate ideas, CERTH, Greece); the Cross-Domain Audience Engagement Analytics Enabler (turning heterogeneous cultural participation data into interpretable engagement signals, The Audience Agency, Warrington, UK); the Dance Style Analysis and Generation Enabler (dance video into virtual 3D choreography, dissected into body, effort, shape and space components, Centre of Excellence for Research & Innovation, Cyprus); the Immersive Design Integration Enabler for Dance & Theatre (NLP-driven XR environments with speech-to-3D scene editing, Cyprus); the Music-to-Movement Translation Enabler (audio into a time-aligned feature set spanning genre classification, emotional valence-arousal analysis, rhythm extraction and latent-space projection, feeding motion generation, spatial audio and visual choreography, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, Greece); and the Adaptive Narrative Enabler for Games & Theatre (an evolutionary AI trained on creator-written story elements that generates infinite story variations, explicitly rejecting LLMs because of their dependence on copyrighted or public-domain corpora that encode 19th and early 20th century sexism, racism and imperialism, CAUSA Creation, Austria). ELIGIBILITY: artists, designers, creative technologists, theatre makers, dancers, choreographers and game designers, as individuals or collectives; applicants must hold a European VAT number. An info session with live demonstrations of the AI Enablers runs 31 July 2026, 10:00 to 11:30 CEST (meeting ID 995 7016 2341). Applications go through Homerun using the official Application Template, uploaded as a PDF; read the Guide for Applicants, which contains the full Enabler descriptions, before applying. Questions: zoenie at waag dot org. The AI Enablers and this call are part of the Horizon Europe project HAMLET, which aims to make generative AI accessible to cultural organisations of all sizes. Closes 30 September 2026, 23:59 CEST. Free to apply.

  9. Nordic Currents 2027-2028 Open Call (Nordic Council of Ministers)

    Nordic Council of Ministers · Projects take place primarily in the applicant organisation's home country, in European countries neighbouring the Nordic region: primarily Belgium, Estonia, France, Germany, Ireland, Lithuania, Latvia, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: EUR 100,000 to EUR 800,000 per project. The total pool for the initiative is EUR 800,000, so between one and eight proposals will be funded. Co-financing from local, national or other sources is a prerequisite for eligibility. No application fee.

    Nordic Currents is a new international culture initiative from the Nordic Council of Ministers, running 2027-2028, that invites cultural organisations in European countries neighbouring the Nordic region to join forces with Nordic partners on projects that move audiences, deepen relationships and create lasting connections. Trust, openness, artistic freedom and democratic values are named as its core. The stated aims are to showcase the richness and quality of contemporary Nordic culture and art in neighbouring European countries and to build long-term collaborations between Nordic and European actors. WHO CAN APPLY: established organisations based in European countries neighbouring the Nordic region, primarily Belgium, Estonia, France, Germany, Ireland, Lithuania, Latvia, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom. Eligible applicant types are cultural organisations, institutions, promoters, festivals and producers, as well as embassies and diplomatic missions working in cooperation with arts and culture stakeholders. Every application must have at least two partners from different Nordic countries who stay active throughout the project. WHERE: in a single country or as a cooperation across several countries, but activities must primarily take place in the country where the applying organisation is based; if several European partners outside the Nordic region are involved, activities may also be organised in their countries. Emphasis goes to projects in regions and countries with existing local interest in Nordic art and culture, where Nordic and local partners can build long-term, mutually beneficial collaborations. WHAT THE PROJECT MUST INCLUDE: both artistic programmes and events accessible to local audiences, and platforms for professional exchange and sector development. Public cultural events can be festivals, programmes, series or other curated formats, standalone or attached to existing structures. Projects must also include network-building, knowledge sharing and other forms of sector development. Programming must include representation from all the Nordic countries, with artists and cultural actors from Denmark, the Faroe Islands, Finland, Greenland (Kalaallit Nunaat), Iceland, Norway, Sweden and Aland. Projects must take place between 1 June 2027 and 1 June 2028. FUNDING: applications may request EUR 100,000 to EUR 800,000; the total pool is EUR 800,000, so between one and eight proposals will be successful. Co-financing through local, national or other funding sources is a prerequisite for eligibility. HOW TO APPLY: proposals are submitted in English through an online portal open only between 1 October and 1 November 2026. ASSESSMENT: all eligible proposals go to a professional committee and expert reviewers in the corresponding art fields, judged on artistic quality, connection to the Nordic region, relevance to the artistic fields covered, how the concept incorporates democratic values such as openness, trust and artistic freedom, capacity to implement the collaboration, public appeal for the intended audiences, public accessibility, potential for long-term impact and future collaboration, and organisational and financial capacity. Selected projects are publicly announced in May 2027.

  10. Creative Scotland Open Fund for Individuals

    Creative Scotland · Scotland, United Kingdom. Applicants must be living in Scotland and hold a UK bank account in their own name. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Between GBP 500 and GBP 50,000. Overall annual budget for the fund is GBP 5 million.

    The Open Fund for Individuals is one of Creative Scotland's key funding programmes, supporting the wide range of activity initiated by artists, writers, producers and other creative practitioners in Scotland. The overall annual budget for this fund is GBP 5 million. WHAT IT SUPPORTS: periods of research, development and/or delivery of creative activity, such as specific projects and productions, for up to 24 months; it can support an individual's time where this is related to specific creative outcomes. Applicants are asked to give a start and end date for the activity and to describe the outcomes, benefits and impacts they wish to achieve. ELIGIBILITY: freelance and self-employed artists and creative practitioners living in Scotland who are at least 18 years old; applicants must have a UK bank account in their own name. Those who are not self-employed individuals or sole traders may instead be eligible for the National Lottery Open Fund for Organisations. FUNDING AMOUNTS: between GBP 500 and GBP 50,000. TIMESCALES: requests between GBP 500 and GBP 20,000 receive an outcome in about 10 weeks; requests between GBP 20,001 and GBP 50,000 in about 14 weeks. This is a rolling open fund with no fixed deadline. Download the guidance for applicants and apply via the online portal.

  11. Creative Debuts: Black Artists Grant (BAG)

    Creative Debuts · United Kingdom (UK-based artists) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 500 unrestricted, no-strings grant to one recipient per month, usable for work, materials, equipment, travel, research or living costs. No application fee.

    Creative Debuts' Black Artists Grant (BAG) is a small, fast, recurring and unconditional cash grant for UK-based Black artists, deliberately barrier-free with no outcomes or reporting required. ELIGIBILITY: UK-based artists of Black heritage who self-identify as Black, across all creative disciplines (visual art, music, film, jewellery, sculpture, choreography and more); no age limit. One recipient is selected each month; apply once and the application is retained for future months. Apply via the Creative Debuts BAG page.

  12. The White Pube Creatives Grant

    The White Pube · United Kingdom · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: £500 (no strings attached)

    Monthly £500 grant for a different working-class creative practitioner based in the UK. Open to anyone making stuff: art, writing, performance, sound, music, craft, comedy, games. Money can be used for time, materials, equipment, research, subscriptions, development, travel, or rent and bills. Apply by emailing funding@thewhitepube.com with a brief intro, contact and a work sample. Rolling, no deadlines, no reporting expected. Non-recipients stay in consideration for future months without re-applying.
