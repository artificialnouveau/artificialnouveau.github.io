Writing and Translation Grants in the US

Currently 19 active writing and translation grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the US. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Browse the list below, or use the interactive desk for filtering and shortlisting.

  1. MPI Short Film Lab 2027: narrative short film lab with fellowship grants and a USD 10,000 script grant (theme: For the Greater Good?)

    Moving Picture Institute (MPI) · Mostly virtual, with an in-person summit in Los Angeles, USA, for the directing stage. Applicants may be based anywhere in the world. · Deadline: 29 Nov 2026 · Award: Filmmakers accepted into the lab receive fellowship grants (amount not published). One official selection receives a USD 10,000 grant, which includes MPI acquiring the script for further development. Travel support for the Los Angeles summit is not mentioned. No application fee mentioned.

    The Moving Picture Institute is a production company and talent incubator that makes films about human freedom. Its Short Film Lab takes writer-directors from a concept to a finished short script, and can lead to production as an MPI Original film. THEME 2027: For the Greater Good? Stories exploring the tension between collective goals and individual freedom: when, if ever, should the freedom of one be sacrificed for the benefit of many? The setting can be a family, workplace, school, neighbourhood, political movement, government or an imagined society, and any genre is welcome. Documentary shorts are not considered. PRIORITY goes to projects with fewer than five principal actors and few or no extras, limited locations (ideally one), and stories about characters within the US. WHO: applicants may be based anywhere in the world. Up to three concepts per applicant, each as a separate application. STRUCTURE: (1) a ten-week virtual screenwriting workshop with masterclasses, February to April 2027, taking a treatment to a first draft; (2) a directing workshop for an estimated six to eight filmmakers, June to September 2027, including an in-person summit in Los Angeles to rehearse scenes with actors, ending in a final script and pitch deck; (3) in late autumn 2027 MPI picks at least one official selection. MONEY: accepted filmmakers receive fellowship grants (figure not stated). The official selection receives USD 10,000. RIGHTS: that grant includes MPI's acquisition of the script for further development; the page does not spell out ownership of the script or film beyond that, so read the terms before accepting. TIMELINE: first-round results 18 December 2026; advancing applicants submit a treatment by 4 January 2027; workshop places decided in late January 2027. APPLICATION: a pitch through the online form linked from the lab page. DEADLINE: Sunday 29 November 2026, 11:59 pm Pacific time.

  2. Nieman Fellowships at Harvard, Class of 2028

    Nieman Foundation for Journalism at Harvard · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (residential academic year at Harvard) · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus additional allowances for housing, childcare and health insurance. Fellows audit courses across Harvard and participate in Nieman seminars, master classes and workshops. No application fee.

    The Nieman Foundation brings around two dozen journalists to Harvard each year for two semesters of study, reflection and community, one of the oldest and best-known midcareer journalism fellowships in the world. Fellows design their own course of study, audit classes across Harvard's schools and take part in Nieman's own seminars and master classes; many use the year to deepen a beat such as technology, AI accountability, science or press freedom. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus allowances toward housing, childcare and health insurance. WHO IS ELIGIBLE: working journalists, staff or freelance, in any medium, with at least five years of full-time media experience; open to applicants from any country. Freelancers may use a recent supervising editor for one of the three required recommendation letters. Special fellowships within the program include the Nieman-Berkman Klein Fellowship in Journalism Innovation. TO APPLY: applications for the class of 2028 open in the first week of October 2026 and require two essays (personal statement and study proposal), work samples and three recommendation letters. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026 for international applicants; 31 January 2027 for US citizens.

  3. Kent Cultural Alliance SFW Residency, Spring 2027 "SERVICE": six-week live-in residency in Chestertown, Maryland (USD 7,500 stipend)

    Kent Cultural Alliance · Raimond Cultural Center, Chestertown, Maryland, USA. Live-in. Open to artists across the US; international artists only with a visa that allows work in the US. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 7,500 stipend for the six-week session, travel reimbursed up to USD 1,000, studio and gallery expenses reimbursed up to USD 1,500, and on-site housing with a private en-suite bedroom. No application fee.

    A community-engaged residency on Maryland's Eastern Shore. Three artists live and work for six weeks in the Raimond Cultural Center, a restored historic house, around the theme SERVICE, working with local partners For All Seasons, the Kent County Food Pantry and Sumner Hall. Each artist leads one or two workshops with a social service agency or school, and the session ends with a six-week show in a 1,000 sq ft gallery (artists may sell work; no commission is taken). WHO: post-degree career artists in any discipline (visual art, creative writing, music, fashion, dance and more) with a real commitment to community engagement; no current degree candidates; artists must live on site for the whole session. US-based artists are the main audience; international artists need a current visa that permits work in the US (no tourist visas). Children, family members and pets cannot normally be accommodated. The building is accessible, with an ADA-compliant bedroom, lift and ramp. SUPPORT: USD 7,500 stipend, up to USD 1,000 travel, up to USD 1,500 studio and gallery expenses, private en-suite bedroom, shared kitchen and small studios. APPLICATION: online form with two narratives of up to 500 words (community engagement; the theme of service), up to 4 images, and website or social links. DEADLINE: Tuesday 1 December 2026, 5:00 pm EST. A separate call for a Fall 2027 session is expected.

    Residential fellowships for work on the history, politics, literature and culture of peoples of African descent that needs the Schomburg Center's collections. A graduate degree is not required, but the fellowship cannot support work toward a degree. WHAT YOU GET: USD 35,000 for a long-term fellowship of one semester, or USD 3,000 a month for a short-term fellowship of one to three months. Fellows are in full-time residence at the Center and may not hold other employment during that time, except on sabbatical from a home institution. APPLICATION: through the library's Submittable portal, with the application form, a 1,500-word description of the proposed study, a CV of up to three pages, and referees (three letters for long-term, at least two for short-term); letters must also arrive by the deadline and no extensions are given.   6. NRDC Climate Storytelling Fellowship 2027 (via The Black List)

    Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) in partnership with The Black List · United States (verify international eligibility on Black List program page) · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: $20,000 to each of three writers ($60,000 total) to support revision of a feature screenplay or pilot.

    Annual screenwriting fellowship from the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) administered through The Black List, awarding $20,000 each to three writers to revise a feature screenplay or pilot that engages with climate change in a compelling way. Submission and selection happen on The Black List platform; applicants should review program eligibility and any associated hosting/submission fees on blcklst.com before applying. Strong fit for narrative writers using fiction to dramatise climate, ecology, energy, or environmental-justice themes (rather than documentary). 2027 CYCLE: submissions are open until 4 December 2026 and are capped at a maximum of 500, so early entry is advised. All accepted submissions receive a free script evaluation and one free month of script hosting on the platform. Fellows also receive consultation with NRDC's Rewrite the Future team, which works to transform the onscreen portrayal of climate change beyond dystopia and apocalypse toward solutions, plus six months of mentorship from an established screenwriter; past mentors include Lucia Aniello, Dustin Lance Black, Roxane Gay, Nick Kroll and Daniel Scheinert.

  7. Craigardan Trillium Fellowship 2027 (residency for artists, activists and scholars of color, USD 4,600 + USD 500 honorarium)

    Craigardan · Elizabethtown, New York, USA (Adirondack Park). In person; rural, car recommended. · Deadline: 15 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 4,600 fellowship toward a minimum two-week residency (covers the residency fee), plus a USD 500 honorarium. Travel, materials and personal expenses are not covered. USD 25 application fee (waivers available on request).

    The Trillium Fellowship supports artists, activists and scholars of color working in any discipline who would benefit from the time and space to pursue their work or find creative retreat at Craigardan, a residency and farm in the Adirondacks. One Trillium fellowship is awarded each year, need-based, by invitation or application (nominations accepted). The fellow offers a public talk in their discipline. OFFER: USD 4,600 toward a minimum two-week residency plus a USD 500 honorarium; all residents get a private room, a farm share and pantry, shared kitchen and living spaces, and studio space suited to their discipline. Transportation, materials and personal expenses are not covered (bus or train pickup can be arranged for a USD 40 flat fee). ELIGIBILITY: no citizenship, age or degree limits are published; applicants should be able to work independently in their field. APPLY through the general 2027 summer residency application (residencies 1 May to 1 November 2027): select the fellowship option and add a supporting statement covering your financial position and creative accomplishments, along with preferred dates, a short bio, CV, Statement of Intent and work samples. USD 25 application fee, waivers via program@craigardan.org. DEADLINE: 15 December 2026; notifications by 15 January 2027.

  10. Museum of Fine Arts, Houston: Core Residency Program 2027-29 (USD 100,000 over 23 months, artists and critical writers)

    Core Residency Program, Glassell School of Art, Museum of Fine Arts, Houston (MFAH) · Houston, Texas, USA (in-person residency with private studio). International applicants welcome; limited J-1 visa support through International Arts & Artists. Houston-based applicants get priority when qualifications are comparable. · Deadline: 08 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 100,000 (USD 50,000 per year) plus a health and wellness stipend, private studio, and free or discounted Glassell School classes. Application fee USD 12.

    Two-year residency for exceptional, highly motivated emerging artists and critical writers committed to developing a sustainable practice. Over the 23-month term, Core Fellows receive USD 100,000 (USD 50,000 a year) plus a health and wellness stipend, a private studio and regular studio visits from regional, national and international artists and curators. They get free and discounted classes at the Glassell School of Art and can request access to its studios (painting, printmaking, digital and film photography, sculpture, jewelry, ceramics), and borrowing privileges at the MFAH Hirsch Library and Rice University's Fondren Library. PROGRAMME: a group exhibition in the spring of year one and a solo exhibition or project in the spring of year two (critics-in-residence propose their own second-year outcome); a public artist lecture and open studio in year one; a second open studio and gallery talk in year two. SELECTION: a jury of arts professionals selects fellows on the quality of submitted work and the potential impact of the Houston residency on their practice; when qualifications are comparable, priority goes to a Houston-based artist or critic. VISAS: limited administrative support for international applicants seeking J-1 visas via International Arts & Artists; no support for other visa types. FEE: USD 12 application fee. Apply via SlideRoom by 8 January 2027. Accommodation requests: coreprogram@mfah.org.

  13. NYSCA/NYFA Artist Fellowship 2027 (USD 8,000 unrestricted; Fiction, Folk/Traditional Arts, Interdisciplinary Work, Painting, Video/Film)

    New York Foundation for the Arts (NYFA), with the New York State Council on the Arts (NYSCA) · New York State, USA. Residents of New York State and/or one of the Tribal Nations located there, for at least the last two consecutive years (2025 and 2026). · Deadline: 12 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 8,000 unrestricted cash grant per fellow; three finalists per category receive USD 1,000. In 2026 NYFA awarded 126 fellowships. The application is free.

    The NYSCA/NYFA Artist Fellowship is an unrestricted cash grant for artists living in New York State. It is not a project grant: it funds an artist's practice and is awarded on the strength of the work. Fifteen disciplines rotate over three years, five a year. 2027 DISCIPLINES: Fiction (including graphic fiction and writing for story-driven video games), Folk/Traditional Arts, Interdisciplinary Work, Painting, and Video/Film. Digital/Electronic Arts returns in the 2029 cycle and Music/Sound in 2028. WHO: artists aged 25 or over by the deadline who have lived in New York State, or one of the Tribal Nations located in it, for at least the last two consecutive years (2025 and 2026), are not enrolled in a degree-seeking programme, and are the originators of the work. Not eligible: anyone who received this fellowship in the past five years (2022 to 2026). Collaborations of up to three artists may apply, and you may apply in at most two categories. WHAT YOU GET: USD 8,000 unrestricted. APPLICATION: free, through Submittable, with work samples (the most important part; work from the past five years is recommended) and short support statements including a 250-word artist statement. If an AI-powered tool was used in the creative process, disclose which tool and how. Applications are reviewed by a panel over three elimination rounds. The form closes automatically at the deadline. DEADLINE: Tuesday 12 January 2027, 5:00 PM ET.

    Villa Albertine (Embassy of France in the United States) · Cities across the United States, usually one city per residency, with a few roving residencies. Open to any nationality and to French and non-French speakers, but every application needs a letter of support from a partner organisation based in France. Minimum age 21. · Deadline: 21 Jan 2027 · Award: Residency stipend of about USD 110 per day (roughly USD 3,300 per month, adjusted to the city), paid as a lump sum. International travel (and domestic travel for roving residencies), accommodation, insurance and, where needed, a rental car are covered separately. No application fee stated.

    Villa Albertine, the French Embassy's residency institution in the United States, will host around fifty exploratory residencies between January and December 2028 for artists, thinkers and cultural professionals whose projects can only take shape through a stay in the US. DISCIPLINES: visual arts, architecture, performing and street arts, music, film and television series, literature, nonfiction, graphic novels and comics, children's literature, museum studies and heritage, art history, new media, podcasts, culinary arts and more; interdisciplinary projects are welcome. WHAT YOU GET: Villa Albertine pays a residency stipend as a lump sum (about USD 110 per day in Washington, D.C., roughly USD 3,300 per month, adjusted to each city's cost of living) and arranges and pays for international travel, accommodation, health, liability and repatriation insurance, and a rental car where the city or project requires one. WHO: individuals, or duos with a genuinely shared practice (no larger groups), aged at least 21 before departure, of any nationality, living in or outside the US. DEDICATED CALLS in the same season, each with its own page and for individuals only: Arts in the Age of AI (listed separately on Grant Desk), Craft and Design (two months, New York residencies with Industry City and WantedDesign), Classical Music (with the Philharmonie de Paris), and the Recanati-Kaplan Prize for creators from the Arab world (USD 15,000 plus a two-month residency in autumn 2027 or in 2028).   17. New America Fellows Program, Class of 2028 (USD 15,000 to 30,000 unrestricted for nonfiction books, films, podcasts and multimedia in progress)

    New America · Nonresidential: Fellows stay in their jobs and home city. Two required in-person gatherings in Washington, DC, USA (opening, week of 4 October 2027; closing, May 2028), with travel, lodging and most meals covered, plus monthly virtual gatherings. Non-US citizens and applicants based abroad may apply. · Deadline: 01 Feb 2027 · Award: Unrestricted stipend of USD 15,000 to 30,000, typically paid in three instalments directly to the Fellow (not to an institution). The level varies by class and by the funding secured that year. Travel, lodging and most meals for the two Washington gatherings are covered. Team projects receive the same amount as an individual. No application fee stated.

    New America, the Washington think tank, runs a one-year fellowship for writers, journalists, filmmakers and other storytellers finishing an ambitious nonfiction reporting project. WHAT IT SUPPORTS: a book, film, podcast, longform article or multimedia work that is already in progress; the stated purpose is to get projects over the finish line, and concept-stage projects are told to wait for a later cycle. Applicants must have done substantial reporting on the project or have significant experience in its field. Most Fellows have a book contract, a production partner or distributor, or a commissioning publication, but none of these is required, and neither is an agent or a formal proposal. Most supported projects are books, though the programme says it increasingly welcomes other narrative formats. WHAT YOU GET: unrestricted funding of USD 15,000 to 30,000, usually in three instalments, to spend as the Fellow chooses (reporting, travel, fact-checkers, research assistance, multimedia production, or income to free up time); a cohort with monthly virtual sessions; and launch events and newsletter coverage for the finished work. FORMAT: nonresidential, so Fellows can keep a full-time job. The in-person opening gathering (three days in the week of 4 October 2027) and closing gathering (May 2028) in Washington require full attendance, with travel, lodging and most meals paid; Fellows are asked to attend at least 75 percent of the virtual sessions. ELIGIBILITY: non-US citizens and people based abroad for all or part of the year may apply; teams of two or more may apply through one applicant. SELECTIVITY: 10 to 15 Fellows a year; the Class of 2027 had 538 applications for 14 places. Selection looks for a novel perspective on an important issue, a link to New America's mission or a newsworthy or culturally significant topic, a clear plan, and evidence the applicant can finish. APPLICATION: online, with a statement of interest, plan of approach, up to three work samples in total (filmmakers must include a clip, trailer or sizzle reel of the proposed film) and two reference letters requested through the portal. AI POLICY: the application narrative must be written entirely by the applicant; using generative AI to draft or rewrite it is prohibited and can disqualify. TIMELINE: finalists (40 to 50) are told in late April and interviewed; final decisions in early July 2027; the class is announced in September 2027. DEADLINE: Monday 1 February 2027, 11:59 PM EST; reference letters by 8 February 2027, 11:59 PM EST. Apply at https://newamerica.smapply.org/prog/classof2028/.

TO APPLY: read the FAQ and application guide on the IWMF site, then apply online. DEADLINE: rolling; applications reviewed as they arrive, with decisions in about 4 to 6 weeks.
