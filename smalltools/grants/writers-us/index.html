Open calls

  1. Tarbell Fellowship 2027-28 (AI journalism)

    Tarbell Center for AI Journalism · Remote training, then newsroom placement in the US, UK or Hong Kong. In person. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 75,000 to 120,000 depending on experience and placement cost of living; senior fellows (7+ years experience) USD 115,000 to 135,000. Summit travel and accommodation covered. No application fee.

    A year-long fellowship for journalists covering artificial intelligence. Fellows complete a three-month full-time remote programme on AI and journalism fundamentals, attend a weeklong summit, then work for nine months as reporters embedded in a partner newsroom (current partners are in Hong Kong, London, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle, Washington DC and Cambridge). ELIGIBILITY: journalism or AI expertise, aged 18+ by 7 June 2027, no degree required; primarily for applicants with the right to work in the US, UK or Hong Kong, as visa sponsorship is uncommon and needs partner-outlet approval.

  2. Nieman Fellowships at Harvard, Class of 2028

    Nieman Foundation for Journalism at Harvard · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (residential academic year at Harvard) · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus additional allowances for housing, childcare and health insurance. Fellows audit courses across Harvard and participate in Nieman seminars, master classes and workshops. No application fee.

    The Nieman Foundation brings around two dozen journalists to Harvard each year for two semesters of study, reflection and community, one of the oldest and best-known midcareer journalism fellowships in the world. Fellows design their own course of study, audit classes across Harvard's schools and take part in Nieman's own seminars and master classes; many use the year to deepen a beat such as technology, AI accountability, science or press freedom. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 87,500 stipend paid over the nine-month academic year (2026-27 figure), plus allowances toward housing, childcare and health insurance. WHO IS ELIGIBLE: working journalists, staff or freelance, in any medium, with at least five years of full-time media experience; open to applicants from any country. Freelancers may use a recent supervising editor for one of the three required recommendation letters. Special fellowships within the program include the Nieman-Berkman Klein Fellowship in Journalism Innovation. TO APPLY: applications for the class of 2028 open in the first week of October 2026 and require two essays (personal statement and study proposal), work samples and three recommendation letters. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026 for international applicants; 31 January 2027 for US citizens.

  3. J. Anthony Lukas Work-in-Progress Awards 2027

    Columbia Journalism School and the Nieman Foundation · USA. Requires a fully executed contract with a US-based publisher. · Deadline: 03 Dec 2026 · Award: Two awards of USD 25,000 each. No entry fee for the Work-in-Progress awards.

    The J. Anthony Lukas Work-in-Progress Awards, given jointly by Columbia Journalism School and the Nieman Foundation at Harvard, are open for 2027 submissions. Two annual awards of USD 25,000 each help writers complete a book-length work of narrative nonfiction on a topic of American political or social concern; books on science, technology and media accountability have won in past years. Applicants need a fully executed contract with a US-based publisher for the book in progress. There is no entry fee for the Work-in-Progress awards; note that the Lukas Book Prize and the Mark Lynton History Prize run on the same portal with the same deadline but carry a USD 75 entry fee. TO APPLY: submit via the Columbia Journalism Awards Submittable portal. DEADLINE: 3 December 2026, 11:59 PM ET.

  4. NRDC Climate Storytelling Fellowship 2027 (via The Black List)

    Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) in partnership with The Black List · United States (verify international eligibility on Black List program page) · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: $20,000 to each of three writers ($60,000 total) to support revision of a feature screenplay or pilot.

    Annual screenwriting fellowship from the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) administered through The Black List, awarding $20,000 each to three writers to revise a feature screenplay or pilot that engages with climate change in a compelling way. Submission and selection happen on The Black List platform; applicants should review program eligibility and any associated hosting/submission fees on blcklst.com before applying. Strong fit for narrative writers using fiction to dramatise climate, ecology, energy, or environmental-justice themes (rather than documentary). 2027 CYCLE: submissions are open until 4 December 2026 and are capped at a maximum of 500, so early entry is advised. All accepted submissions receive a free script evaluation and one free month of script hosting on the platform. Fellows also receive consultation with NRDC's Rewrite the Future team, which works to transform the onscreen portrayal of climate change beyond dystopia and apocalypse toward solutions, plus six months of mentorship from an established screenwriter; past mentors include Lucia Aniello, Dustin Lance Black, Roxane Gay, Nick Kroll and Daniel Scheinert.

  5. Craigardan Trillium Fellowship 2027 (residency for artists, activists and scholars of color, USD 4,600 + USD 500 honorarium)

    Craigardan · Elizabethtown, New York, USA (Adirondack Park). In person; rural, car recommended. · Deadline: 15 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 4,600 fellowship toward a minimum two-week residency (covers the residency fee), plus a USD 500 honorarium. Travel, materials and personal expenses are not covered. USD 25 application fee (waivers available on request).

    The Trillium Fellowship supports artists, activists and scholars of color working in any discipline who would benefit from the time and space to pursue their work or find creative retreat at Craigardan, a residency and farm in the Adirondacks. One Trillium fellowship is awarded each year, need-based, by invitation or application (nominations accepted). The fellow offers a public talk in their discipline. OFFER: USD 4,600 toward a minimum two-week residency plus a USD 500 honorarium; all residents get a private room, a farm share and pantry, shared kitchen and living spaces, and studio space suited to their discipline. Transportation, materials and personal expenses are not covered (bus or train pickup can be arranged for a USD 40 flat fee). ELIGIBILITY: no citizenship, age or degree limits are published; applicants should be able to work independently in their field. APPLY through the general 2027 summer residency application (residencies 1 May to 1 November 2027): select the fellowship option and add a supporting statement covering your financial position and creative accomplishments, along with preferred dates, a short bio, CV, Statement of Intent and work samples. USD 25 application fee, waivers via program@craigardan.org. DEADLINE: 15 December 2026; notifications by 15 January 2027.

  6. Leon Levy Center for Biography Fellowships 2027-28 (incl. Levy/Sloan Science Biography)

    Leon Levy Center for Biography, CUNY Graduate Center · New York City, USA (in residence at the CUNY Graduate Center, September to May) · Deadline: 04 Jan 2027 · Award: $72,000 stipend plus writing space, full research facilities and research assistance

    Four resident biography fellowships plus the Levy/Sloan Fellowship, funded by the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, for a biography of a figure in science or technology; separate Sherwin fellowship for nuclear-age history. Open to writers of all nationalities, with explicit preference for first-time biographers who have not yet held a biography fellowship, though mid-career and senior writers are welcome; independent writers eligible. Fellows join monthly seminars and the center's public events. Apply with CV, career narrative, project description, writing sample and two references (agents, editors and publishers are discouraged as referees). No application fee.

  7. Museum of Fine Arts, Houston: Core Residency Program 2027-29 (USD 100,000 over 23 months, artists and critical writers)

    Core Residency Program, Glassell School of Art, Museum of Fine Arts, Houston (MFAH) · Houston, Texas, USA (in-person residency with private studio). International applicants welcome; limited J-1 visa support through International Arts & Artists. Houston-based applicants get priority when qualifications are comparable. · Deadline: 08 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 100,000 (USD 50,000 per year) plus a health and wellness stipend, private studio, and free or discounted Glassell School classes. Application fee USD 12.

    Two-year residency for exceptional, highly motivated emerging artists and critical writers committed to developing a sustainable practice. Over the 23-month term, Core Fellows receive USD 100,000 (USD 50,000 a year) plus a health and wellness stipend, a private studio and regular studio visits from regional, national and international artists and curators. They get free and discounted classes at the Glassell School of Art and can request access to its studios (painting, printmaking, digital and film photography, sculpture, jewelry, ceramics), and borrowing privileges at the MFAH Hirsch Library and Rice University's Fondren Library. PROGRAMME: a group exhibition in the spring of year one and a solo exhibition or project in the spring of year two (critics-in-residence propose their own second-year outcome); a public artist lecture and open studio in year one; a second open studio and gallery talk in year two. SELECTION: a jury of arts professionals selects fellows on the quality of submitted work and the potential impact of the Houston residency on their practice; when qualifications are comparable, priority goes to a Houston-based artist or critic. VISAS: limited administrative support for international applicants seeking J-1 visas via International Arts & Artists; no support for other visa types. FEE: USD 12 application fee. Apply via SlideRoom by 8 January 2027. Accommodation requests: coreprogram@mfah.org.

  8. Knight Science Journalism Academic-Year Fellowship 2027-28 (MIT)

    Knight Science Journalism Program at MIT · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (in residence for the academic year). International applicants welcome; MIT sponsors a J-1 visa. · Deadline: 09 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 87,500 stipend over 9.5 months, plus USD 5,000 for travel and housing, health insurance and a childcare subsidy. No application fee.

    An academic-year fellowship at MIT for journalists covering science, health, technology or the environment, with seminars, MIT and Harvard courses and a self-directed study project. ELIGIBILITY: full-time staff or freelance journalists with at least 3 consecutive years of science, health, technology or environment coverage; international applicants welcome. Applications open 15 November 2026; deadline 9 January 2027.

  9. Folger Institute Artistic Research Fellowships 2027-28 (USD 4,000 to 5,000 per month)

    Folger Shakespeare Library (Folger Institute) · Washington DC, USA, or virtual/hybrid. · Deadline: 15 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 4,000 per virtual month or USD 5,000 per onsite month.

    Artistic research fellowships for artists whose work needs primary research on the early modern world (about 1400 to 1800) and its legacies, using the Folger's collections. ELIGIBILITY: any artist; no degree required and no citizenship rule stated. OFFER: USD 4,000 per virtual month or USD 5,000 per onsite month for one to three months; no studio is provided. APPLY via the Folger fellowships portal with an abstract, a 1,500-word proposal, CV, a list of Folger sources, one letter and a portfolio; an information session is held on 1 December 2026. DEADLINE: 15 January 2027, 23:59 ET.
