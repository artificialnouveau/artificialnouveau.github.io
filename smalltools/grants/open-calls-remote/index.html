  1. Briarpatch Magazine: AI Resistance Issue, Winter 2027 Call for Pitches

    Briarpatch Magazine · Regina, Canada (remote pitching; Canadian focus) · Deadline: 18 Sep 2026 · Award: CAD 150 for profiles, short essays, book reviews and parting shots (under ~1,500 words); CAD 250 for feature stories and photo essays; CAD 350 for research-based articles and investigative reporting (2,000-2,500 words). No pitch fee.

    Briarpatch, the Canadian magazine of grassroots politics, dedicates its Winter 2027 issue to AI Resistance and is paying for articles, investigative reporting, historical analysis, photo essays, timelines, profiles, reading lists and art. SUGGESTED ANGLES include Indigenous-led and local resistance to data centres, exploitation and outsourcing of labour in LLM training, AI's impact on work and union resistance, data mining and data sovereignty, environmental and climate impacts, artists and AI (crisis of creativity and ownership), the language and inevitability narratives of AI, policing and the surveillance state, AI and disinformation, and investigations into Big Tech lobbying and media influence in Canada. PITCH: outline what ground your contribution covers, estimated word count, and your relevant experience; first-time Briarpatch writers should add a brief writing sample. Send to pitch [at] briarpatchmagazine.com. Accepted first drafts are due 14 October 2026 with collaborative editing until late November; publication is not guaranteed. No pitch fee. Deadline 18 September 2026.

  2. This is Gender Regional Curatorial Fellowship 2026

    This is Gender (Global 50/50) · Remote and part-time, working within a global photography competition. Applications are invited from practitioners in global majority countries and historically underrepresented communities, including Asia, Africa, Latin America, the Middle East, Oceania, and diasporic communities worldwide. · Deadline: 22 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of up to GBP 3,000 for the seven-month part-time fellowship. No application fee.

    This is Gender, the photography and visual culture platform run by Global 50/50, is launching its inaugural Regional Curatorial Fellows Programme for early-career curators and cultural practitioners working across photography, visual culture and gender justice. The premise: images shape how gender, power and social realities are understood, feeding public debate, cultural memory, research, advocacy and policy, yet the communities and visual practices that should inform those narratives stay underrepresented in the institutions that circulate them. The programme's argument is that diversifying who makes images is not enough, and that it also matters who selects, contextualises, writes about, commissions and circulates them. For the first time, This is Gender is embedding regional curators directly inside its global photography competition, which has run six cycles and receives thousands of submissions a year. WHAT THE FELLOWSHIP INVOLVES: working inside the competition and seeing first-hand how a major platform discovers, reviews and champions new work; shaping curatorial decisions by bringing regional knowledge and cultural insight into the selection and interpretation of work; contributing to public-facing projects including exhibitions, editorial outputs and digital storytelling while developing your own writing, framing and curatorial voice; direct engagement with international judges, curators, artists, editors and cultural practitioners; and building a network across an international cohort of peers. WHO SHOULD APPLY: curators, critics, writers, researchers, artistic directors, photographers, organisers and cultural practitioners from global majority countries and historically underrepresented communities. Early-career practitioners and recent graduates are strongly encouraged, and a formal curatorial title is not required. HOW TO APPLY: read the full role description and FAQs on the fellowship page and submit the online application form; the organisers offer the application in alternative formats and are open to a conversation before applying. Applications close 22 September 2026.

  3. Sheer Spite Press: Digital Distro Call for Zine Submissions

    Sheer Spite Press · Montreal, Canada (digital distribution, open worldwide) · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: Revenue share, not a grant: creators set their own PDF pricing and keep up to 80 percent of each sale, paid quarterly (modest sums expected); non-exclusive, you can keep selling elsewhere

    Sheer Spite Press, a Montreal-based independent press running since 2024, is open for submissions of zines to be distributed digitally as PDFs through its new digital distro (no print at this time). PRIORITIZING work that is long, text-centric, passionate, personal, anti-authoritarian and accessible (probably not poetry), on topics like community care, friendship, history, food, sex and kink, memoir, DIY and adventures. TERMS: the press sells your PDFs, you choose your own pricing and keep up to 80 percent of sales, paid quarterly (very modest sums expected); distribution is non-exclusive, so you can keep selling your zines anywhere else. Note this is a revenue-share distribution arrangement, not a grant or stipend. Submit via the CryptPad form linked from the call; questions to lee@sheerspite.ca. No submission fee. Open until 31 December 2026.
