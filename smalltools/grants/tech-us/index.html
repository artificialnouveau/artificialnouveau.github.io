Tech and Infrastructure Grants in the US

Currently 17 active tech and infrastructure grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the US. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Foresight Institute: AI for Science and Safety Nodes RFP 2026 (Local Compute; Coordination and Accountability; AI-first Science)

    Foresight Institute · Remote-friendly worldwide; optional in-person membership at the Foresight AI Nodes in San Francisco or Berlin. · Deadline: 31 Oct 2026 · Award: Grants typically USD 30,000 to 100,000 (smaller awards for the Human Empowerment and AI Insurance and Open Governance areas, larger for the rest; bigger amounts possible for the right project), usually paid as one lump sum, with up to 10 percent overhead. Optional office space in San Francisco or Berlin and access to private compute clusters. No application fee.

    Foresight Institute's AI for Science and Safety Nodes request for proposals funds open, AI-first projects across three calls: I. Local Compute (compute that individuals and communities physically own and control), II. Coordination and Accountability (supercollaboration and decentralised alignment, human empowerment, and AI insurance and open governance: multi-agent oversight and verification, tools for collective deliberation, decision rules for when human control adds value, and independent risk assessment or insurance mechanisms for frontier labs), and III. AI-first Science (automated nanotechnology, whole-brain emulation, frontier bio). Grantees can also get workspace in the San Francisco or Berlin AI Nodes (a community xNode runs in Cape Town) and private compute. All funded code, data and outputs must be open-sourced. Projects are judged on fit with the focus area, impact on reducing existential risk from AI, feasibility within 1 to 3 years, AI-first working methods, ability to execute, and high-risk, high-reward potential. ELIGIBILITY: individuals, teams and organisations worldwide; nonprofits and for-profits welcome (for-profits must justify the need for grant funding). Priority goes to applicants who want to work in person at one of the Nodes, but it is not required. Review takes about three months; applications can opt in to sharing with Lightcone Commons. DEADLINE: 31 October 2026, 23:59 PDT.

  2. NBER i3: Open Data Fellows Program 2027

    National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER), Innovation Information Initiative (i3) · United States (NBER, Cambridge, MA); international applicants welcome, including those on US student visas (payment may be affected, check with your international student office) · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 1,500 per fellow, at least five fellowships, plus travel reimbursement to present at the i3 Technical Working Group meeting in December 2027. No application fee.

    The fourth cohort of the NBER Innovation Information Initiative (i3) Open Data Fellows supports graduate students building public datasets, tools, code or metadata for research on innovation and entrepreneurship. Fellows receive USD 1,500, join periodic one-on-one and group meetings through 2027, and present their work at the i3 Technical Working Group meeting in December 2027, with travel reimbursed. ELIGIBILITY: current Master's or PhD students, or those who graduated within the past six months, in economics, innovation, entrepreneurship or related fields; international applicants welcome. Apply with a proposal for a new or in-progress project intended for public release, via the Google form linked on the call page, by 1 November 2026. Fellows are announced in November 2026. Contact: info@nber.org.

  3. Science & Technology Policy Fellowships, 2027-28 Class

    American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS) · Washington DC, USA. Fellows are placed in US federal executive, legislative and judicial branch offices and must reside in the DC area. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Stipend depending on experience and placement, roughly USD 85,000 to 140,000; the dedicated AI legislative branch cohort is paid at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. Includes health insurance, a travel and professional development allowance, and relocation support for some placements. No application fee.

    The AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships place scientists and engineers inside US federal agencies, congressional offices and the judicial branch for a year of hands-on policymaking, contributing technical knowledge and analytical skills to the federal policy process. AI, data and computing policy placements are among the largest tracks, and AAAS runs a dedicated AI legislative branch cohort that places AI experts in congressional offices at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. ELIGIBILITY: US citizens holding a PhD or equivalent doctoral degree in any science or engineering field, or a master's degree in engineering plus three years of post-degree professional experience; some tracks accept a bachelor's plus five years. Relevant expertise includes AI, machine learning, computer science, robotics and data science. Fellows must reside in DC, Maryland or northern Virginia during the fellowship year. TIMELINE: the application portal opens 1 August 2026 and closes 1 November 2026 at 11:59pm ET, with eligibility notification in late November, interview notification January to February 2027, semi-finalist interviews in March, placement offers by early May, and the fellowship year running 1 September 2027 to 31 August 2028. Apply through fellowshipapp.aaas.org.

  4. Processing Foundation Fellowship 2027

    Processing Foundation · USA only. The Collective Intelligence track is remote (US-based); Gaming the System is hybrid in Los Angeles; DIY Computing is hybrid in New York City. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 10,000 stipend over the 8-month fellowship, plus open-source software mentorship, cohort collaboration, professional development and public presentation opportunities. No application fee.

    The Processing Foundation's 2027 fellowship cycle supports artists, educators and creative technologists working on open-source projects, with a USD 10,000 stipend over an 8-month fellowship period. THREE TRACKS: Collective Intelligence (remote, US-based), co-designing AI education tools with youth, aimed at educators with software development or AI/ML experience; Gaming the System (hybrid, Los Angeles), building an open-source toolkit for video game design with underserved communities, for game makers and creative coders based in LA; and DIY Computing (hybrid, New York City), developing open-source hardware and software environments for custom computers, for early-career hardware artists in NYC (applicants currently enrolled in degree programs are ineligible for this track). ELIGIBILITY: US applicants only; hybrid tracks require residency in LA or NYC respectively. International applicants are pointed to the Foundation's Microgrants and Processing Community Day instead. APPLICATION: via nomination form, selecting one track; self-nominations are allowed. Selection by a panel of the Processing Foundation team and external reviewers, judging strength of the project, fit with the Foundation's mission and potential impact. TIMELINE: nominations open 7 October 2026, close 1 November 2026 at 11:59 PM PST; fellows notified 14 December 2026; fellowship runs 25 January to 26 September 2027.

  5. ACLS Digital Justice Grants 2026-27 (Seed and Development)

    American Council of Learned Societies (ACLS), funded by the Mellon Foundation · Grant funds must be administered by a US institution of higher education. No citizenship or residency restriction is stated for the principal investigator. · Deadline: 03 Nov 2026 · Award: Seed Grants USD 10,000 to 25,000 for start-up and prototyping work; Development Grants USD 50,000 to 100,000 for projects past proof of concept. Institutional indirect costs are not covered. No application fee.

    ACLS Digital Justice Grants fund digital projects in the humanities and interpretive social sciences that critically engage with the interests and histories of people of colour and other historically marginalised communities, including Black, Latinx, Indigenous, disabled and LGBTQ+ communities. The programme's stated aims are to diversify the digital domain, advance justice and equity in digital scholarly practice, and contribute to public understanding of racial and social justice issues, with an explicit interest in scholars working with under-utilised or understudied source materials and those at institutions with less infrastructure for digital work. TWO TRACKS, one deadline: SEED GRANTS of USD 10,000 to 25,000 for projects in the start-up or prototyping phase, covering planning, prototyping and methodology testing; and DEVELOPMENT GRANTS of USD 50,000 to 100,000 for projects that have moved past proof of concept and can show preliminary work and evidence of engagement. ELIGIBILITY: at least one principal investigator must be a scholar in the humanities or interpretive social sciences, and awarded funds must be administered by a US institution of higher education. WHAT IS NOT FUNDED: creative works such as novels or films, textbooks, straightforward translations, purely pedagogical projects, and institutional indirect costs. Note that outputs are expected to be made as widely available as intellectual property allows, ideally under the most liberal open-source or Creative Commons licence. TIMING: applications go through the ACLS online fellowship and grant administration system (ofa.acls.org) by 3 November 2026 at 17:00 ET; grant terms begin between 1 July and 31 December 2027 and run 12 to 18 months. The programme is funded by the Mellon Foundation.

  6. FAS Metascience Fellowship 2027 (full-time placement in a US federal research agency)

    Federation of American Scientists (FAS) · Washington, DC area, United States. Full-time and mostly in person at agency offices. · Deadline: 09 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 174,000 per year, prorated monthly, plus USD 1,500 for travel and USD 1,500 for professional development. Fellows are not eligible for employer-sponsored benefits; the salary is expected to cover health and living costs. No application fee.

    The Federation of American Scientists' Metascience Fellowship places three full-time fellows inside federal research agencies such as the NSF or DOE for one year, after a pre-service residency at FAS. Fellows advise agency leaders on evidence-based research reform, analyse grantmaking, peer review and open-science practices, design experiments with built-in evaluation, write briefing memos, coordinate metascience work across agencies and give hands-on technical help. Placements may be extended depending on funding and performance. ELIGIBILITY: US citizens only; current federal employees cannot apply. Mid- to senior-career professionals with an advanced degree or equivalent experience in a scientific, engineering or public-policy field, willing to work mostly in person in the Washington, DC area. Fellows must start at FAS no later than 4 January 2027. Applications take a CV and three responses of up to 300 words each, submitted through the FAS platform. DEADLINE: 9 November 2026, 11:59PM ET; late applications are not reviewed.

  7. Eyebeam AGENCY NYC Residency, Spring 2027

    Eyebeam · New York City, USA. Applicants must live within the five boroughs and be able to work on site at Eyebeam at least one day a week for the full residency term (15 March to 11 June 2027). · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend per selected artist, plus mentorship and access to tools and technical expertise through Eyebeam's network. No application fee (via Submittable).

    The NYC track of Eyebeam's AGENCY residency cycle supports New York City-based artists exploring questions of agency at a moment when AI systems increasingly act, decide and carry out tasks on our behalf: how to preserve human agency as these systems shape human behavior, how to assert agency in imagining and building the societies we inhabit, and what agency becomes when understood as collective, relational, entangled and more-than-human. Projects might engage AI directly but do not have to; critical and creative approaches to power, autonomy, technological systems, collective action, human and nonhuman relationships, and the social and material structures of agency are all welcome, including reimagining AI through collective governance, repair, rehearsal or mischief, and repurposing AI systems toward new tools, methods and models for the public good. Residents receive a USD 5,000 stipend, mentorship, and access to tools and technical expertise through Eyebeam's network. ELIGIBILITY: individuals only (no collectives this cycle); not enrolled in a full-time degree program; not committed to another residency during the term; must live in New York City within the five boroughs and be able to work on site at Eyebeam at least one day a week for the full term. TIMELINE: deadline Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:59 PM ET; interviews 7-10 December; notifications 14 December; residency 15 March to 11 June 2027. Info session Monday 19 October 2026, 1 PM ET. Apply via Submittable, free.

  8. Data Through Design 2027 Open Call: 10th Anniversary Exhibition (New York City)

    Data Through Design (DxD) · New York City, USA. Open call; applicants must be approved to work in the US to be paid the stipend. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 900 stipend per selected project, paid half in the weeks leading up to the exhibition and half after. No application fee.

    Data Through Design, the annual exhibition of artworks that creatively analyze, interpret and interrogate data from New York City's Open Data portal, opens its 10th anniversary call, held in conjunction with NYC Open Data Week. THEME: reflections, reverberations and the arc of data over time; the evidence of time (footprints, traces, scars, markers of change), temporal mirrors and kaleidoscopes, messages from the past and future, fractals of time and visuals of scale. Artists may also respond to a previous DxD theme in light of time, and alumni artists are particularly encouraged to apply, including by revisiting past projects. FORMATS: digital, analog, physical, performance, sound works, walks, installations, creative coding, participatory mapping, drawings and more; the organizers highlight participatory data collection, radical and cognitive mapping, and data experienced through time, sound and the senses. REQUIREMENT: projects must reference or use at least one NYC Open Data dataset; other data, including novel and experimental collection systems, is welcome alongside it. WHAT YOU GET: USD 900 stipend, half before and half after the exhibition, plus inclusion in the multi-media group show in New York City, tentatively 26 March to 11 April 2027. Work must be installation-ready by 19 March 2027 and delivered to the venue 22 to 24 March. ELIGIBILITY: open to artists, civic technologists, data enthusiasts, city dwellers and creative practitioners; applicants must be approved to work in the US to be paid the stipend. The submission process has been revamped for this edition, with a new rubric and submission materials to review. APPLICATION: free, via the submission form on the DxD website. DEADLINE: 15 November 2026 at 23:59 EST.

  11. Postdoctoral Fellow 2027, Crockett Lab (Princeton Psychology and University Center for Human Values)

    Princeton University, Crockett Lab (Department of Psychology and University Center for Human Values) · Princeton, New Jersey, USA. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 80,000 annual salary from a stable institutional funding source (not a cancellable grant).

    Molly Crockett's lab at Princeton, based in the Department of Psychology and the University Center for Human Values, is hiring a postdoc to start in summer or fall 2027. The research topic is flexible. The lab uses methods from computational social science and cognitive science informed by theory from science and technology studies and feminist epistemology, studying feedback loops between individual psychology, institutions and culture, and how the tech industry co-opts these processes to concentrate power. It is especially interested in bridging quantitative and qualitative methods for understanding human experience, and in how culture and power shape which questions are valued and which methods are legitimised. The lab is industry-independent and accepts no research funding from the tech industry; it uses a variety of ML methods and aims to develop thoughtful approaches to automation in scholarship, so it suits researchers excited about AI in science but wary of hype and industry influence. WHAT YOU GET: an USD 80,000 salary from stable institutional funds, initial one-year appointment renewable up to three years. APPLICATION: via Interfolio; questions about fit to mj.crockett@princeton.edu. Review of applications begins 20 November 2026 and continues until the position is filled.

  17. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.
