  1. IAPS: Special Projects Roles in AI Governance (2 to 5 hires)

    Institute for AI Policy and Strategy (IAPS) · Washington, D.C. or the Bay Area, USA; London considered for exceptional candidates. US visa sponsorship available. · Deadline: 08 Sep 2026 · Award: Special Projects Manager USD 122,000 to 165,000; Senior Associate USD 97,000 to 126,500; Associate USD 86,000 to 101,000. Between 2 and 5 roles are being filled.

    IAPS is hiring 2 to 5 special projects roles to support or own new workstreams in AI governance, helping the world navigate towards a positive future with transformative AI. LEVELS AND SALARY: Special Projects Manager, USD 122,000 to 165,000; Senior Associate, USD 97,000 to 126,500; Associate, USD 86,000 to 101,000. LOCATION: Washington, D.C. or the Bay Area, with London open to exceptional candidates; IAPS can sponsor US visas. PROJECT AREAS THEY ARE EXCITED ABOUT: (1) Bay Area to DC interface, closing the gap between where frontier AI is built and where it is governed by surfacing what is happening in AI labs and technical circles for DC policy audiences, and vice versa; (2) AI security fieldbuilding, bridging a national security community short on people who deeply understand frontier AI risk and an AI community short on people who understand national security, through workshops, convenings and partnerships in DC that put technical experts from both worlds in the same room; (3) DC-based special projects, leveraging IAPS's research output and policy networks to build talent pipelines and the wider field of AI governance, drawing on a new 45-seat office in Dupont Circle and a 30-person fellowship cohort arriving in DC every six months; and (4) programs targeting senior policy audiences, bringing policy entrepreneurs and technical professionals into existing and developing research areas such as institutional resilience for advanced AI or AI and defence, with participants working semi-independently to scope and explore new policy domains. DEADLINE: applications are considered on a rolling basis, with applications sent by 8 September 2026 prioritised. HOW TO APPLY: apply at iaps.ai/special-projects. Questions to careers@iaps.ai.

  2. New_ Public Entrepreneur in Residence, Pilot Cohort

    New_ Public · Remote, with an in-person kickoff retreat in Philadelphia, USA (19 to 22 October 2026). Open to applicants based in the United States or Canada. · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 22,500, paid in three monthly installments across the residency, each contingent on continued active participation. Plus USD 1,500 toward an AI tool of the resident's choice for coding, prototyping or design. Two residents are selected in the pilot cohort. Also provided: hands-on product and strategy support from senior New_ Public staff and outside experts; access to New_ Public's five years of original research on moderation, governance, community stewardship and platform design, plus direct access to the researchers behind it; and introductions across New_ Public's network of major platforms, startups, university researchers and funders for hiring, fundraising, early user testing and community partnerships. Residents must submit a Form W-9 or W-8BEN to receive the stipend. No application fee.

    New_ Public is piloting a virtual Entrepreneur in Residence programme for solo founders building alternatives to the mainstream social platforms. Its framing: people are turning away from algorithmic social media, AI bots are taking over the big platforms, and users are retreating into private closed spaces, which leaves an opening for builders working on a new information ecosystem rather than the status quo. The residency supplies research, experts, connections and tools; the founder brings the vision and experience. TWO FOCUS AREAS: humans connecting to humans, meaning products that bring people into meaningful contact online (forums, messaging apps, groups, microblogging, in-person meetups), including AI-powered platforms and tools as long as they connect people to people rather than people to bots; and local and civic information environments, meaning tools for distributing local news, building community knowledge and facilitating collective sensemaking, so people can get trustworthy information about their communities. WHAT RESIDENTS DO: use self-directed time to prototype ideas, test assumptions and gain clarity; participate in regular check-ins, engage with the other resident and the wider team, and take candid feedback on product and strategy; engage seriously with go-to-market questions about who the user is, how they find you, and the path to sustainability; and help shape the programme itself, since the pilot cohort's feedback directly informs how the EIR programme runs going forward. WHO THEY WANT: entrepreneurial founders ready to build their next thing within the focus areas, with a track record of or clear drive toward work that builds social trust, connection and belonging; strong product thinkers with good judgment, a relentless focus on user needs, and openness to coaching on mission-aligned business models. An early idea is enough, no working product required. The pilot is open to founders with something else going on alongside, though the full programme will most likely be full time. APPLICATION PROCESS: submit the written application online; shortlisted applicants submit a short video by 18 September 2026 covering background, motivation and a quick pitch; full applications and videos go to the advisory board, with offers made in early October ahead of the kickoff. FUTURE COHORTS: New_ Public plans to open the next cohort to the UK and EU from spring 2027. Deadline 11:59pm Pacific on Wednesday 9 September 2026.

  3. after/time gallery: Networks Group Exhibition Open Call (Portland)

    after/time gallery · Portland, Oregon, US · Deadline: 02 Oct 2026 · Award: No artist fee or award published; USD 20 submission fee, and artists cover shipping both ways

    after/time gallery in Portland, Oregon invites submissions for Networks, an end-of-year group exhibition curated by Catherine Ulrich examining the enmeshment of humanity and manufactured technology. The exhibition investigates systems of connection, consumption, tension and exchange in lived and digital realities, encouraging artists to reimagine historical and contemporary human relationships as they parallel or abstract technological systems, offering alternatives grounded in resistance, care, community and embodied experience. PRACTICALITIES: exhibition runs 5 November - 15 December 2026 with a First Thursday opening on 5 November. NOTE: the gallery publishes no artist fee or award for this call; there is a USD 20 submission fee (via Venmo) and artists are responsible for shipping costs both ways (non-local participants must provide return labels). Deadline: 2 October 2026.

  4. Science & Technology Policy Fellowships, 2027-28 Class

    American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS) · Washington DC, USA. Fellows are placed in US federal executive, legislative and judicial branch offices and must reside in the DC area. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Stipend depending on experience and placement, roughly USD 85,000 to 140,000; the dedicated AI legislative branch cohort is paid at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. Includes health insurance, a travel and professional development allowance, and relocation support for some placements. No application fee.

    The AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships place scientists and engineers inside US federal agencies, congressional offices and the judicial branch for a year of hands-on policymaking, contributing technical knowledge and analytical skills to the federal policy process. AI, data and computing policy placements are among the largest tracks, and AAAS runs a dedicated AI legislative branch cohort that places AI experts in congressional offices at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. ELIGIBILITY: US citizens holding a PhD or equivalent doctoral degree in any science or engineering field, or a master's degree in engineering plus three years of post-degree professional experience; some tracks accept a bachelor's plus five years. Relevant expertise includes AI, machine learning, computer science, robotics and data science. Fellows must reside in DC, Maryland or northern Virginia during the fellowship year. TIMELINE: the application portal opens 1 August 2026 and closes 1 November 2026 at 11:59pm ET, with eligibility notification in late November, interview notification January to February 2027, semi-finalist interviews in March, placement offers by early May, and the fellowship year running 1 September 2027 to 31 August 2028. Apply through fellowshipapp.aaas.org.

  5. ACLS Digital Justice Grants 2026-27 (Seed and Development)

    American Council of Learned Societies (ACLS), funded by the Mellon Foundation · Grant funds must be administered by a US institution of higher education. No citizenship or residency restriction is stated for the principal investigator. · Deadline: 03 Nov 2026 · Award: Seed Grants USD 10,000 to 25,000 for start-up and prototyping work; Development Grants USD 50,000 to 100,000 for projects past proof of concept. Institutional indirect costs are not covered. No application fee.

    ACLS Digital Justice Grants fund digital projects in the humanities and interpretive social sciences that critically engage with the interests and histories of people of colour and other historically marginalised communities, including Black, Latinx, Indigenous, disabled and LGBTQ+ communities. The programme's stated aims are to diversify the digital domain, advance justice and equity in digital scholarly practice, and contribute to public understanding of racial and social justice issues, with an explicit interest in scholars working with under-utilised or understudied source materials and those at institutions with less infrastructure for digital work. TWO TRACKS, one deadline: SEED GRANTS of USD 10,000 to 25,000 for projects in the start-up or prototyping phase, covering planning, prototyping and methodology testing; and DEVELOPMENT GRANTS of USD 50,000 to 100,000 for projects that have moved past proof of concept and can show preliminary work and evidence of engagement. ELIGIBILITY: at least one principal investigator must be a scholar in the humanities or interpretive social sciences, and awarded funds must be administered by a US institution of higher education. WHAT IS NOT FUNDED: creative works such as novels or films, textbooks, straightforward translations, purely pedagogical projects, and institutional indirect costs. Note that outputs are expected to be made as widely available as intellectual property allows, ideally under the most liberal open-source or Creative Commons licence. TIMING: applications go through the ACLS online fellowship and grant administration system (ofa.acls.org) by 3 November 2026 at 17:00 ET; grant terms begin between 1 July and 31 December 2027 and run 12 to 18 months. The programme is funded by the Mellon Foundation.

  6. American Sunlight Project: Free Expression Fellow

    American Sunlight Project · Fully remote with flexible scheduling. Applicants must hold U.S. citizenship or legal residency. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 2,500 honorarium for the engagement. No application fee.

    The American Sunlight Project works to ensure that citizens have access to trustworthy sources to inform the choices they make in their daily lives. It is looking for a Free Expression Fellow to produce its regular newsletter on censorship, helping readers stay informed on a rapidly evolving US political landscape. RESPONSIBILITIES: compile and write 'Last Week in Censorship', a clear and engaging newsletter-style roundup of platform policy changes, censorship news and regulatory developments that erode American democracy; and, in support of the organisation's censorship tracker, compile instances from the weekly briefing into a database. IDEAL CANDIDATE: motivated to document instances of democratic backsliding in the United States and happy to take on a part-time nonprofit commitment in service of that goal. The posting explicitly welcomes students, retirees and people looking to moonlight for a good cause. REQUIRED: U.S. citizenship or legal residency; exceptional writing skills with a strong command of tone, clarity and synthesis; and knowledge of U.S. politics and social media. DESIRABLE: experience in research, journalism, public policy or a related field. STRONG PLUS: familiarity with tech policy, internet culture, free expression issues and/or platform governance. Familiarity with Substack is a plus but not required. TERMS: roughly 5 to 8 hours per week, fully remote with flexible scheduling, starting as soon as possible and running through December 2026 with possible extension pending funding, for a USD 2,500 honorarium. APPLY by emailing your CV, a short cover note of a couple of paragraphs explaining your interest, and a writing sample (preferably on a tech or policy topic) to benjamin@americansunlight.org with 'Fellow Application' in the subject line. No closing date is stated.

  7. CSET (Georgetown): Frontier AI Research Lead (Research Fellow or Senior Fellow)

    Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET), Georgetown University Walsh School of Foreign Service · Washington DC, US. Based in the United States, no relocation assistance. NO VISA SPONSORSHIP: applicants must be legally eligible to work in the US without sponsorship from Georgetown University. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Research Fellow: roughly USD 100,000 to 130,000 per year. Senior Fellow: roughly USD 160,000 to 190,000 per year. Commensurate with experience. Georgetown University benefits. No application fee.

    CSET, the policy research organisation inside Georgetown's Walsh School of Foreign Service, is hiring someone to lead a new Frontier AI team, at either Research Fellow or Senior Fellow level depending on experience. THE RESEARCH: expanding CSET's work on cutting-edge general-purpose AI, building on the CyberAI Project and the Foundational Research Grants program, to give policymakers clarity about current capabilities, use cases and risks; where the frontier moves next, including the prospects for AGI or superintelligence and what would alter them; and how new evidence should change expectations. Named priority areas: Chinese frontier AI efforts, the development and deployment of increasingly capable AI agents, making sense of conflicting evidence about capabilities and risks, and assessing the compute demands of frontier systems. Understanding why experts disagree, and what that means for policy, is a recurring theme. THE ROLE: shape priorities and overall research strategy, oversee execution and report production, and help hire and manage supporting researchers including Research Analysts and Student Research Assistants. Fellows also brief policymakers, speak at public events, act as subject-matter experts for media, and work with CSET's data team on empirical analyses. Reports to CSET's Director of Analysis. WHO THEY WANT: comfort interpreting technical AI/ML research (large language models, multimodal systems, reinforcement learning) and distilling it into policy-relevant insight, plus comfort with empirical data and qualitative methods such as in-depth interviews. Deep familiarity with the frontier AI landscape and expertise in at least one of: safety, security and governance debates including dangerous capabilities, loss-of-control risks and systemic effects; applied experience developing, training, deploying or evaluating large-scale models; compute requirements, scaling laws and infrastructure; or Chinese AI research, policy and deployment. REQUIRED OF ALL CANDIDATES: a graduate degree (Master's, JD or PhD) in a relevant field, ability to research, write, brief and manage with limited supervision, familiarity with interpreting and visualising data, and project or team management experience. Research Fellow level needs at least 3 years in research and policy analysis; Senior Fellow needs at least 5 years of public service in senior roles or 10 years of progressive private-sector roles, plus demonstrated mentoring experience. DEADLINE: none published; applications are reviewed on a rolling basis.

    A part-time remote contractor research role mapping the supply chains behind frontier AI and writing strategic proposals for government and industry decision-makers. RESPONSIBILITIES: map segments of frontier AI and adjacent technology supply chains, with possible focus on specific country ecosystems or on actors across segments such as capital allocation, raw materials, mineral processing, chip design, fabrication, advanced packaging, datasets, datacentres and frontier labs; analyse how ownership stakes, trade relationships and control are likely to evolve under different technological, commercial and geopolitical scenarios; and develop strategic proposals for stakeholders across government and industry. The posting explicitly asks for people 'adept at using AI models and tools as part of research process' who know when to use them to augment critical thinking and strategic taste rather than replace it. Self-direction and responsive communication matter because the arrangement is part-time and remote; experience in government, policy research, investment analysis or supply chain intelligence is a plus.   9. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.

  10. NPR: Lead Data Scientist, AI Labs

    NPR (National Public Radio) · Hybrid in Washington, D.C., USA, with in-office presence at the Washington, D.C. or New York City location two to three days a week. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 164,000 to 201,000 salary range, plus benefits (medical, dental, vision, life and AD&D, long-term and short-term disability, paid time off and voluntary retirement savings for employees working 30+ hours a week).

    NPR is hiring a Lead Data Scientist for its AI Labs team, to serve as the technical and ethical anchor for NPR's artificial intelligence initiatives. The role leads data science expertise for a content metadata overhaul that will power new audience-focused personalisation engines. Rather than building models from scratch, the work is fine-tuning, evaluating and scaling existing foundation models and deploying machine learning for public media, with a high emphasis on accuracy, journalistic ethics, data privacy and responsible scaling. RESPONSIBILITIES: lead the selection, fine-tuning and optimisation of open-source and proprietary LLMs tailored to NPR's content voice; design automated ML pipelines that transform decades of unstructured audio, transcripts and text into automated semantic metadata; work with product, design and engineering to turn user needs into production-ready data science workflows; architect recommendation frameworks that use enriched metadata for style-based personalisation while preserving editorial curation; build clean self-service data pipelines and audience analytics models in BigQuery with the Data Products team; support newsroom research by prototyping API-driven tooling and secure database search models; establish evaluation, testing and benchmarking frameworks that hold model outputs to NPR's standards for factual accuracy and neutrality; identify, audit and mitigate algorithmic bias in metadata generation and audience discovery; and ensure AI applications scale securely and cost-effectively within data privacy guardrails. MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS: 8+ years in data science, machine learning or NLP shipping production-grade systems; a track record of applying, fine-tuning and evaluating LLMs and foundation architectures; experience with automatic speech recognition, diarisation and audio preprocessing pipelines; large-scale data architectures, vector databases and semantic search pipelines; production recommendation or personalisation systems at scale; ML workflows on Azure, AWS or GCP; and cross-disciplinary collaboration in matrixed organisations. SKILLS: Python, ML and agentic engineering frameworks, vector search and two-stage retrieval, SQL and cloud warehouses such as BigQuery or Snowflake, RAG patterns and semantic search, model evaluation, prompt engineering and API integration, plus a strong commitment to algorithmic ethics and data privacy compliance. PREFERRED: large volumes of unstructured multimedia or speech-to-text workflows, prior work in a media organisation, digital newsroom or public service institution, and automated LLM evaluation gates and regression testing in CI/CD. EDUCATION: advanced degree, PhD preferred, in computer science, data science or machine learning. No closing date is published.
