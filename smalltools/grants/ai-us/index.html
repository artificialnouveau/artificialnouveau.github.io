Open calls

  1. Eyebeam AGENCY NYC Residency, Spring 2027

    Eyebeam · New York City, USA. Applicants must live within the five boroughs and be able to work on site at Eyebeam at least one day a week for the full residency term (15 March to 11 June 2027). · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend per selected artist, plus mentorship and access to tools and technical expertise through Eyebeam's network. No application fee (via Submittable).

    The NYC track of Eyebeam's AGENCY residency cycle supports New York City-based artists exploring questions of agency at a moment when AI systems increasingly act, decide and carry out tasks on our behalf: how to preserve human agency as these systems shape human behavior, how to assert agency in imagining and building the societies we inhabit, and what agency becomes when understood as collective, relational, entangled and more-than-human. Projects might engage AI directly but do not have to; critical and creative approaches to power, autonomy, technological systems, collective action, human and nonhuman relationships, and the social and material structures of agency are all welcome, including reimagining AI through collective governance, repair, rehearsal or mischief, and repurposing AI systems toward new tools, methods and models for the public good. Residents receive a USD 5,000 stipend, mentorship, and access to tools and technical expertise through Eyebeam's network. ELIGIBILITY: individuals only (no collectives this cycle); not enrolled in a full-time degree program; not committed to another residency during the term; must live in New York City within the five boroughs and be able to work on site at Eyebeam at least one day a week for the full term. TIMELINE: deadline Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:59 PM ET; interviews 7-10 December; notifications 14 December; residency 15 March to 11 June 2027. Info session Monday 19 October 2026, 1 PM ET. Apply via Submittable, free.

  2. Postdoctoral Fellow 2027, Crockett Lab (Princeton Psychology and University Center for Human Values)

    Princeton University, Crockett Lab (Department of Psychology and University Center for Human Values) · Princeton, New Jersey, USA. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 80,000 annual salary from a stable institutional funding source (not a cancellable grant).

    Molly Crockett's lab at Princeton, based in the Department of Psychology and the University Center for Human Values, is hiring a postdoc to start in summer or fall 2027. The research topic is flexible. The lab uses methods from computational social science and cognitive science informed by theory from science and technology studies and feminist epistemology, studying feedback loops between individual psychology, institutions and culture, and how the tech industry co-opts these processes to concentrate power. It is especially interested in bridging quantitative and qualitative methods for understanding human experience, and in how culture and power shape which questions are valued and which methods are legitimised. The lab is industry-independent and accepts no research funding from the tech industry; it uses a variety of ML methods and aims to develop thoughtful approaches to automation in scholarship, so it suits researchers excited about AI in science but wary of hype and industry influence. WHAT YOU GET: an USD 80,000 salary from stable institutional funds, initial one-year appointment renewable up to three years. APPLICATION: via Interfolio; questions about fit to mj.crockett@princeton.edu. Review of applications begins 20 November 2026 and continues until the position is filled.

  3. Tarbell Fellowship 2027-28 (AI journalism)

    Tarbell Center for AI Journalism · Remote training, then newsroom placement in the US, UK or Hong Kong. In person. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 75,000 to 120,000 depending on experience and placement cost of living; senior fellows (7+ years experience) USD 115,000 to 135,000. Summit travel and accommodation covered. No application fee.

    A year-long fellowship for journalists covering artificial intelligence. Fellows complete a three-month full-time remote programme on AI and journalism fundamentals, attend a weeklong summit, then work for nine months as reporters embedded in a partner newsroom (current partners are in Hong Kong, London, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle, Washington DC and Cambridge). ELIGIBILITY: journalism or AI expertise, aged 18+ by 7 June 2027, no degree required; primarily for applicants with the right to work in the US, UK or Hong Kong, as visa sponsorship is uncommon and needs partner-outlet approval.

    Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI) · London, UK or Washington, DC, USA; in person, full time. Open worldwide; temporary work visa sponsorship available. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 14,000 for 3 months plus travel support (UK Research Track figure; DC track has its own stipend). No application fee.

    GovAI's Summer Fellowship brings early-career researchers and practitioners into AI governance for three months of full-time work on a research or applied project under the mentorship of a GovAI researcher. TRACKS: UK Research Track and UK Applied Track in London, plus a Washington DC track, each with its own application page. TOPICS include risk management, economics and geopolitics of AI, public policy and technical governance. ELIGIBILITY: open to applicants worldwide; must be able to work in person full time from 7 June to 27 August 2027; GovAI can sponsor temporary work visas. DEADLINE: 22 November 2026, 23:59 GMT for the UK tracks (11:59 pm EST for DC).

  6. RJI Professional Innovation Fellowships 2027 (Individual, Institutional and Emerging Technology tracks)

    Reynolds Journalism Institute (University of Missouri) · Remote, with a few paid visits to the Reynolds Journalism Institute in Columbia, Missouri, United States. · Deadline: 08 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 75,000 for the Individual and Institutional tracks, USD 100,000 for the Emerging Technology track, paid in three instalments tied to deliverables; RJI also pays for the fellow's visits to its Columbia, Missouri campus. No application fee.

    The Reynolds Journalism Institute at the University of Missouri funds people and teams to build and launch a tool, workflow or product that helps newsrooms, in three tracks: Individual (USD 75,000), Institutional (USD 75,000, paid to a host organisation or the fellow) and Emerging Technology (USD 100,000, where AI or another new technology must be essential to the solution). The output must be a free, open-source resource usable by US journalists; tools limited to one city or state are unlikely to be funded. The fellowship is remote and runs for eight months from 28 June 2027 with an in-person kickoff at RJI and a couple of paid visits. ELIGIBILITY: anyone, anywhere; no age, background or journalism credential required. Applications open 2 November 2026 on Submittable; a shortlist is invited in early to mid February 2027 to submit a fuller application, with finalist interviews 5 to 23 April 2027. DEADLINE: 8 January 2027 (first round; no time zone stated, RJI is on US Central time).

  7. NSF: Security, Privacy, and Trust in Cyberspace (SaTC 2.0), January 2027 target date

    U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) · United States. US universities and colleges and US-based non-profit, non-academic organizations only. · Deadline: 25 Jan 2027 · Award: Research projects up to USD 1,200,000 over up to 4 years; education projects up to USD 500,000 over up to 3 years (education-research proposals may add USD 100,000); seedling projects up to USD 300,000 over up to 2 years. About 75 awards (around 60 research, 15 education), roughly USD 60,000,000 per year subject to available funds. No application fee.

    NSF's SaTC 2.0 programme (solicitation NSF 25-515) funds research and education on the security, privacy and trustworthiness of digital systems. Named topics include privacy-enhancing technologies, attacks on and trustworthiness of AI systems, and 'human aspects' work using social, behavioural and economic methods, so policy-oriented and socio-technical research on tech is in scope alongside technical work. ELIGIBILITY: US institutions of higher education and US-based non-profit, non-academic organizations such as independent research labs and professional societies. Each person may appear on at most 4 SaTC proposals in any 12-month period; projects over USD 600,000 need additional collaboration and participation plans. PROCESS: proposals via Research.gov or Grants.gov. Target date is the last Monday in January annually (25 January 2027, 5 p.m. submitter's local time by NSF default); proposals are accepted anytime but may miss that review panel.

    Institute for Law & AI (LawAI) · Washington, DC, USA (relocation expected; USD 10,000 relocation/housing stipend for DC-based fellows). Requires valid US work authorization; visa sponsorship available but not guaranteed. · Deadline: 29 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 2,000 per week subsidy, plus a USD 10,000 relocation/housing stipend for DC-based fellows and covered travel, accommodation and meals for the Convening Week; higher subsidies possible for exceptional candidates.     The Institute for Law & AI's US Summer Fellowship is a paid research fellowship at the leading edge of US AI law and policy for law students, professionals, and academics, including the Summer Institute on Law and AI in Washington DC and mentorship from LawAI research staff.   9. CHAI: Research Fellowship (Center for Human-Compatible AI, UC Berkeley)

    Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI), UC Berkeley · Berkeley, California, USA (in-person) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Salary commensurate with qualifications and experience, on UC Berkeley salary scales (negotiated). Funding for two years, renewable up to three given satisfactory performance. Eligible for visa sponsorship, with CHAI also contributing to visa costs for fellows and their dependents. No application fee.

    The CHAI Research Fellowship trains highly qualified postdoctoral researchers to advance beneficial AI, working with CHAI faculty Stuart Russell, Pieter Abbeel and Anca Dragan and collaborating with affiliate faculty at Berkeley AI Research and beyond. Fellows have broad freedom to pursue novel research toward provably beneficial AI systems in areas such as reasoning, decision making, learning, multi-agent systems and philosophical foundations, using probability theory, game theory and control theory. Current topics of interest include theoretical foundations of intelligent agent architecture, expressive formal languages for probability models (including probabilistic programming), decision making over long time scales, cumulative lifelong learning, human-in-the-loop planning and reinforcement learning, human-robot interaction, the structure of human preferences, acting on behalf of multiple humans, robust cooperation in heterogeneous multi-agent systems, and mechanism design for human-machine systems. Fellows may also lead collaborations, advise junior researchers, and teach. QUALIFICATIONS: a PhD (or about to be obtained) in a relevant technical discipline (computer science, statistics, mathematics, or theoretical economics) and a record of high-quality published research; prior work on the AI control problem is not required. International applicants are eligible (visa sponsorship provided). TO APPLY: submit via the CHAI application form a resume, a one-page statement of interest describing the research you would like to undertake, and the names and emails of two academic referees. Start date is flexible and applications are reviewed on a rolling basis (no fixed deadline). General enquiries: chai-info@berkeley.edu.

  10. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.

  11. Anthropic Fellows Program (funded AI research fellowship, January 2027 cohort)

    Anthropic (with Constellation as recruiting partner) · San Francisco, London, or Ontario, with remote-friendly options; applicants must hold work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Shared workspaces available in Berkeley and London. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,850 / GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300 per week for four months of full-time research, plus roughly USD 15,000 per month in compute funding and other research expenses. No application fee.

    The Anthropic Fellows Program is a four-month, fully funded research fellowship with direct mentorship from Anthropic researchers. Fellows work on empirical projects aligned with Anthropic's research priorities using external infrastructure, aiming to produce public outputs such as papers. Five workstreams: AI Safety, AI Security, ML Systems and Performance, Reinforcement Learning, and Economics and Policy; safety topics include scalable oversight, adversarial robustness, mechanistic interpretability, and model organisms. FUNDING: a weekly stipend of USD 3,850 (GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300), around USD 15,000 per month in compute funding, and other research expenses. ELIGIBILITY: fluent Python; available full-time for four months; strong technical background in computer science, mathematics, or physics; work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Locations: San Francisco, London, Ontario, or remote within those countries. APPLICATION: rolling, via the Constellation portal; the process includes an initial application, reference checks, technical assessments, interviews, and a research discussion. The next cohort is expected to start in January 2027, so apply well before the end of 2026.

  12. Anthropic Economic Futures Research Fund (research grants on AI's economic impacts)

    Anthropic · Global fund with an initially US-centric focus (Anthropic is headquartered in San Francisco and Claude usage is highest in the US). Institutions worldwide may apply. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 200 million committed overall. Grants primarily in the USD 5-30 million range per project, with a USD 1 million minimum and flexibility upward for well-scoped, high-potential-impact projects. The separate small-grant Research Awards tier (USD 10,000-50,000 plus USD 5,000 in Claude API credits) is currently paused. No application fee.

    NOTE: this tier is institutions-only; individual researchers cannot apply in a personal capacity but can serve as principal investigators through their institution. Anthropic's earlier Economic Futures Research Awards (USD 10,000-50,000 plus USD 5,000 Claude API credits for empirical research on AI's economic impacts, rolling, with results presented within six months) are currently not accepting applications; this entry covers the larger Research Fund now open for proposals. THE FUND: Anthropic has committed USD 200 million to the Economic Futures Research Fund to support ambitious external research on interventions that prepare society for the economic impacts of AI, studying what programs could make the economy more flexible and resilient, ensure AI's benefits are shared, and minimise harm from AI-driven disruption. FIVE RESEARCH PRIORITIES: (1) AI's impact on workers at the firm and workplace level; (2) equipping people for AI-driven transitions; (3) modernising income support for displacement; (4) building worker stakes in AI-driven growth; (5) generating evidence on public investments. FUNDING: projects primarily in the USD 5-30 million range, minimum USD 1 million, flexible upward for well-scoped, high-potential-impact projects. ELIGIBILITY: accredited universities and other degree-granting institutions, independent research institutes and policy research organisations, and nonprofits with a track record of running field experiments at scale; a global fund with an initially US-centric focus. APPLICATION: via the request for proposals linked from the research agenda announcement (Google Form); no deadline published as of September 2026.

    Iliad · London or San Francisco Bay Area. In person. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 6,000 per month travel and housing allowance for the three-month fellowship (four months fully funded including the Iliad Intensive). No application fee.
