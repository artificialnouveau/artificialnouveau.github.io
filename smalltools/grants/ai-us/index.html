  1. Collider: Program Manager (AI safety fieldbuilding, New York)

    Collider · Hybrid in New York City, USA. No visa sponsorship: candidates must already have the right to work in the US. · Deadline: 23 Aug 2026 · Award: USD 150,000 to 180,000 base salary, plus health insurance, 401(k) and 20 days paid time off. The one-day in-person work trial is paid.

    Collider, the New York coworking and convening space for AI safety and other high-impact work, is hiring a Program Manager to design, run and scale the programmes that grow the AI safety talent pipeline and infrastructure in New York. The role owns programmes end to end, from concept to launch to iteration, working with researchers, practitioners, educators, funders and partner organisations. WHAT YOU DO: design and operate fieldbuilding programmes (fellowships, training programmes, workshops, conferences, cohort courses and new formats); own budget, timeline, vendor relationships, participant experience and post-programme evaluation; recruit and coordinate mentors, instructors, advisors and participants, and build the pipelines and selection processes that hold quality as things scale; define and track outcomes with metrics beyond participant satisfaction, and iterate on the evidence; maintain relationships across research labs, university groups, training programmes (MATS, ARENA, BlueDot, SPAR, AI Safety Camp are named), grantmakers and adjacent communities; represent Collider externally; write programme reports, strategy memos, post-mortems and public materials; and work with leadership on which programmes to launch, scale or sunset. REQUIRED: 3+ years of programme or project management, ideally running cohort, fellowship or training programmes; substantive understanding of the AI safety field, its organisations, its main research agendas (alignment, interpretability, evaluations, governance) and its open problems; strong operational instincts; an entrepreneurial, initiative-taking approach to judgement calls about people, partnerships and priorities; and clear written communication. NICE TO HAVE: prior experience at an AI lab, AI safety organisation, university group, training programme or grantmaker; a background in education, talent development or community building; enough ML or alignment literacy to follow a paper's argument; policy literacy on the AI governance landscape; or a background in finance, law or another professional field Collider wants to build pipelines into. PROCESS: apply via the form in the job document by 23 August 2026 (applications are reviewed on a rolling basis and an offer may be made before the deadline, so applying early is advised), then a 15 to 25 minute screening call with hiring partner Impact Ops, an interview with the prospective manager, a paid one-day in-person work trial at the Collider coworking space, and 2 to 3 references. Reports to the Director.

  2. Horizon Fellowship, 2027 Cohort

    Horizon Institute for Public Service · Washington DC, USA (residence required for placement). Part-time remote training January to March 2027. · Deadline: 30 Aug 2026 · Award: Base salary of USD 78,000 for junior fellows, USD 130,000 for fellows, and USD 190,000 or more for senior fellows, plus a USD 17,000 benefits stipend, a USD 3,000 professional development stipend, and up to USD 3,000 relocation assistance. No application fee.

    The Horizon Fellowship places technologists, scientists and researchers with emerging-technology expertise into US federal agencies, congressional offices and Washington DC think tanks to work on AI, biotechnology and emerging-technology policy. The 2027 cohort runs three tiers: junior fellows (under three years of experience, no PhD) at a USD 78,000 base for six months; fellows (three or more years of relevant experience, or a PhD) at USD 130,000 for twelve months; and a newly added senior fellow tier (twelve or more years of experience with a leadership background) at USD 190,000 or more for twelve months. All tiers receive a USD 17,000 benefits stipend, a USD 3,000 professional development stipend and up to USD 3,000 in relocation assistance, and all are potentially renewable. The programme year begins with part-time remote training from January to March 2027, followed by matching in April and May and placement from spring or summer 2027 onward. Most offers are expected by the end of November 2026. Placements run up to two years in total: fellows and senior fellows take a one-year term at a federal agency, congressional office or think tank with the option to renew for a second one-year term, potentially on a different institutional track, while junior think tank fellows take a six-month term renewable once. Horizon reports that nearly all finalists across its five cohorts to date have secured a placement at a host organization. WHAT THE FELLOWSHIP INCLUDES: dedicated training on the policy landscape and applied skills such as writing and policy analysis; a tight-knit peer community; introductions to host offices central to US emerging technology policy; and mentorship from senior leaders and the broader Horizon community. ELIGIBILITY: no prior policy experience or specific degree is required, and Horizon explicitly welcomes candidates from diverse backgrounds; fellows must reside in the Washington DC area for the placement. WORK AUTHORISATION: applicants need pre-existing work authorisation that does not depend on employer sponsorship. Non-US citizens on E-3, TN or H-1B1 visas may qualify case by case; F-1 OPT during the first year after graduation is acceptable but STEM OPT extensions are not; green card holders are eligible but may face restrictions on executive branch roles. Junior fellows have no specific degree requirement and may apply while finishing college, but are limited to the think tank track. SELECTION PROCESS (September to November 2026): application decision, then a paid work sample of up to 6 hours, a 20-minute screening interview, a 40-minute technical interview and a 25-minute final interview. Applications close 30 August 2026 at 11:59pm Anywhere on Earth.

  3. ZwillGen Fellowship Program 2027-2028 (Technology Law)

    ZwillGen · Washington, D.C., USA (must work out of the DC office) · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Salary USD 115,000 to USD 125,000, with a reduced annual billable target (1,250 hours) to create space for writing, professional development, and policy engagement. No application fee.

    The ZwillGen Fellowship is a 12-month position for recent law-school graduates and early-career attorneys interested in working at the intersection of law, technology, and public policy. Fellows work alongside attorneys on matters involving privacy, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, online platforms, surveillance, digital advertising, fintech, online safety, and technology regulation, contributing to client counseling, investigations, litigation, and emerging regulatory matters, as well as research, writing, and speaking. The program includes a reduced billable target (1,250 hours) to make room for thought leadership and engagement with the technology policy, privacy, and civil liberties community. ELIGIBILITY: must have graduated from law school before the program starts; must have taken or plan to take a bar exam near the start of the Fellowship; must be able to work out of the DC office and be eligible to apply for admission to the DC Bar. Candidates primarily interested in traditional IP practice may find it less aligned. A small number of applicants may also be considered for the Hannah Schaller Memorial Skylark Fellowship (privacy-law focus, with a reduced 1,100-hour billable target for additional community engagement). Following the Fellowship, fellows may be invited into a full-time attorney role or supported in pursuing other technology law and policy opportunities. APPLY with a resume, law-school transcript, writing sample, and cover letter by 31 August 2026.

  4. Science & Technology Policy Fellowships, 2027-28 Class

    American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS) · Washington DC, USA. Fellows are placed in US federal executive, legislative and judicial branch offices and must reside in the DC area. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Stipend depending on experience and placement, roughly USD 85,000 to 140,000; the dedicated AI legislative branch cohort is paid at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. Includes health insurance, a travel and professional development allowance, and relocation support for some placements. No application fee.

    The AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships place scientists and engineers inside US federal agencies, congressional offices and the judicial branch for a year of hands-on policymaking, contributing technical knowledge and analytical skills to the federal policy process. AI, data and computing policy placements are among the largest tracks, and AAAS runs a dedicated AI legislative branch cohort that places AI experts in congressional offices at GS-12 Step 10 on the DC locality scale. ELIGIBILITY: US citizens holding a PhD or equivalent doctoral degree in any science or engineering field, or a master's degree in engineering plus three years of post-degree professional experience; some tracks accept a bachelor's plus five years. Relevant expertise includes AI, machine learning, computer science, robotics and data science. Fellows must reside in DC, Maryland or northern Virginia during the fellowship year. TIMELINE: the application portal opens 1 August 2026 and closes 1 November 2026 at 11:59pm ET, with eligibility notification in late November, interview notification January to February 2027, semi-finalist interviews in March, placement offers by early May, and the fellowship year running 1 September 2027 to 31 August 2028. Apply through fellowshipapp.aaas.org.

  5. Physical AI Safety Institute: Research Fellowship (expression of interest)

    Physical AI Safety Institute (PAISI) · Not stated in the call. PAISI is a US 501(c)(3) nonprofit and its team is affiliated with UC Berkeley and the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago; because fellows are given access to physical robot platforms, expect an in-person or hybrid component. No nationality restriction is stated for expressions of interest. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Stipend amount is not published. Fellows receive a stipend, access to physical robot platforms, compute for simulation and training, and structured mentorship. PAISI was seeded by a grant from BlueDot Impact. Free to submit an expression of interest.

    Robot foundation models (pi-zero, OpenVLA, GR00T and others) are approaching widespread deployment on the back of more than USD 18 billion in funding, and almost nobody is working on their safety. The Physical AI Safety Institute, a new nonprofit founded by Bear Haon and Kaylene Stocking and seeded by a grant from BlueDot Impact, describes itself as a capacity-building organisation growing the community that develops techniques to interpret, align and control robot foundation models. Its research fellowship runs in 12-week cohorts: each fellow works within a stream led by a mentor on a concrete problem in physical AI safety, with mentors drawn from researchers with track records in robot foundation models, mechanistic interpretability, safe control or adversarial robustness for embodied systems. Each cohort ends with a research symposium where fellows present their work. STREAMS: mechanistic interpretability (sparse autoencoders and activation patching for VLA and WAM architectures); adversarial robustness (jailbreaking and prompt injection in the embodied action space); runtime monitoring (detecting when a policy operates outside its competence envelope); sim-to-real safety transfer (do safety behaviours survive deployment on physical hardware); and robot security (cyberattack vectors, data poisoning and supply chain for deployed robot models). WHO SHOULD APPLY: researchers with strong technical foundations in robotics or machine learning and a demonstrated interest in safety; prior safety experience is not required. PROCESS: this stage is an expression of interest, not a full application. The form takes about two minutes and asks you to pick your stream and preferred cohort dates; concrete details including stipend figures and application deadlines are shared through that channel, and neither is published on the site as of listing. PAISI also publishes seven free curriculum modules running from classical robot safety through VLA interpretability, adversarial robustness and runtime monitoring to deployment evaluation, plus a white paper, The Case for Physical AI Safety, at paisi.ai/the-case-for-physical-ai-safety. Contact: bear@paisi.ai.

Posted 29 July 2026 (job number 200045204); open for a minimum of 5 days and then until filled, so apply early.   8. IAS Civic Science Fellow: AI and Society (Nelson Lab) 2026-27

    Institute for Advanced Study (IAS), Science, Technology, and Social Values Lab · Princeton, NJ, USA (in-person) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 7,277.77 per month (about USD 87,300/year) for the 18-month full-time appointment, plus a comprehensive benefits package (medical, dental and vision insurance; life and short/long-term disability coverage; flexible spending accounts; paid holidays; retirement plans; tuition assistance; employee assistance program).     The Institute for Advanced Study is seeking a 2026-27 Civic Science Fellow to advance understanding of the social, democratic and scientific implications of artificial intelligence. The full-time, 18-month position is based in the Science, Technology, and Social Values Lab led by Harold F. Linder Professor of Social Science Alondra Nelson. Working closely with Professor Nelson and Lab members, the Fellow contributes to research and policy analysis and to scholarly and public-facing publications, and supports multidisciplinary, multisector initiatives at the intersection of AI, science and governance. DUTIES (75% Lab work): conduct original research on the Lab's AI-and-society agenda; synthesize scholarship on the social implications of AI, sociotechnical approaches to AI governance and the epistemological implications of AI in research; contribute policy research on governance mechanisms that strengthen democratic institutions; help organize convenings and working groups; collaborate on manuscripts, reports and grant proposals; support editorial development of publications; and develop public-facing essays, policy briefs and digital materials. The remaining 25% supports the Fellow's own independent research, network-building and full participation in the intellectual life of IAS. MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS: a PhD in the social sciences, sciences or humanities; a strong record of scholarly research, writing and publication on the social implications of science and technology or related fields; and exceptional writing and editing skills for both academic and non-academic audiences. Preferred: specialization in science and technology studies, science and technology policy, organizational studies or qualitative methods, and demonstrated engagement with AI governance and technology policy debates. Apply with a cover letter and resume via the IAS/Paylocity posting.

  9. CHAI: Research Fellowship (Center for Human-Compatible AI, UC Berkeley)

    Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI), UC Berkeley · Berkeley, California, USA (in-person) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Salary commensurate with qualifications and experience, on UC Berkeley salary scales (negotiated). Funding for two years, renewable up to three given satisfactory performance. Eligible for visa sponsorship, with CHAI also contributing to visa costs for fellows and their dependents. No application fee.

    The CHAI Research Fellowship trains highly qualified postdoctoral researchers to advance beneficial AI, working with CHAI faculty Stuart Russell, Pieter Abbeel and Anca Dragan and collaborating with affiliate faculty at Berkeley AI Research and beyond. Fellows have broad freedom to pursue novel research toward provably beneficial AI systems in areas such as reasoning, decision making, learning, multi-agent systems and philosophical foundations, using probability theory, game theory and control theory. Current topics of interest include theoretical foundations of intelligent agent architecture, expressive formal languages for probability models (including probabilistic programming), decision making over long time scales, cumulative lifelong learning, human-in-the-loop planning and reinforcement learning, human-robot interaction, the structure of human preferences, acting on behalf of multiple humans, robust cooperation in heterogeneous multi-agent systems, and mechanism design for human-machine systems. Fellows may also lead collaborations, advise junior researchers, and teach. QUALIFICATIONS: a PhD (or about to be obtained) in a relevant technical discipline (computer science, statistics, mathematics, or theoretical economics) and a record of high-quality published research; prior work on the AI control problem is not required. International applicants are eligible (visa sponsorship provided). TO APPLY: submit via the CHAI application form a resume, a one-page statement of interest describing the research you would like to undertake, and the names and emails of two academic referees. Start date is flexible and applications are reviewed on a rolling basis (no fixed deadline). General enquiries: chai-info@berkeley.edu.

  11. Good Life Residents Program: AI and Finance

    Roosevelt Institute, in partnership with Data & Society · Remote. Travel may be requested for select convenings and for the culminating public event. US economic policy focus. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,000 stipend, plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event if needed. No application fee.

    The Good Life Residents Program is a six-month Roosevelt Institute initiative that develops new leaders and elevates bold ideas across six economic policy priorities under its Good Life Agenda. Each topic area hosts a cohort of 3 to 4 residents plus 1 to 2 senior Topic Advisors (academics, policy leaders, organizers or government practitioners) who provide strategic and intellectual mentorship and moderate the closing event. Residents write a roughly 1,500-word original policy essay advancing a people-centered policy vision against a topic-specific prompt, take part in structured dialogue with peers, Topic Advisors and Roosevelt Think Tank directors, help prepare and appear at a culminating public event, contribute to press opportunities such as op-eds and reporter calls where applicable, and act as ambassadors for the Good Life Agenda in their professional and public networks. The current round, the AI and Finance Residents Program, is run in partnership with Data & Society and asks for bold ideas on how to shape, redirect, interrupt or make more visible and contestable the flows of capital into generative AI. Framing: breakneck investment in generative AI technology and infrastructure is distorting the stock market, profit measures, bond issuance, the pace and composition of growth, job creation and trade policy, with seven tech companies accounting for more than a third of S&P 500 market capitalisation and complex, sometimes circular financial arrangements obscuring risk. Suggested lines of inquiry include what government interventions could improve public oversight, risk allocation and direction-setting in private markets; how other investments could be coordinated and scaled if AI spending is crowding them out; what role public spending or new institutions could play in catalysing technology R&D designed to support workers, sustainability and supply chains; what democratic input mechanisms would keep state influence over capital flows less vulnerable to corruption; and how federal AI policy should address public hostility to data centre investments alongside national security and competitiveness. Compensation is USD 3,000 plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event. FLAG: Roosevelt does not publish an application deadline for the AI and Finance round on the programme page, and earlier rounds (Social Security financing, Federal Reserve powers, housing) each ran their own short application window. Check the Formstack application form linked from the programme page for the current closing date before applying.
