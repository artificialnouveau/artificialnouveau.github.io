Open calls

  1. Tarbell Fellowship 2027-28 (AI journalism)

    Tarbell Center for AI Journalism · Remote training, then newsroom placement in the US, UK or Hong Kong. In person. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 75,000 to 120,000 depending on experience and placement cost of living; senior fellows (7+ years experience) USD 115,000 to 135,000. Summit travel and accommodation covered. No application fee.

    A year-long fellowship for journalists covering artificial intelligence. Fellows complete a three-month full-time remote programme on AI and journalism fundamentals, attend a weeklong summit, then work for nine months as reporters embedded in a partner newsroom (current partners are in Hong Kong, London, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle, Washington DC and Cambridge). ELIGIBILITY: journalism or AI expertise, aged 18+ by 7 June 2027, no degree required; primarily for applicants with the right to work in the US, UK or Hong Kong, as visa sponsorship is uncommon and needs partner-outlet approval.

  2. Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme 2027/28 (HKD 344,400 a year for up to three years; 400 fellowships, practice-led art PhDs included)

    Research Grants Council (RGC) of Hong Kong, with eight Hong Kong universities · Hong Kong: City University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong Baptist University, Lingnan University, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, The Education University of Hong Kong, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, HKUST and The University of Hong Kong. Open to applicants of any country of origin. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Annual stipend of HKD 344,400 (about USD 44,150) plus a conference and research travel allowance of HKD 14,400 a year, for up to three years (2026/27 rates as published by the RGC). Some universities add top-ups: HKBU lists a HKD 40,000-a-year Elite Scholar Award for three years, a further HKD 20,000 travel top-up, four years of tuition waiver and two years of guaranteed accommodation. 400 fellowships for 2027/28.

    The Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme, run by the Research Grants Council, funds around 400 new full-time PhD students a year at eight Hong Kong universities, in any discipline, including AI, computing and data science programmes and practice-led art research (Hong Kong Baptist University's Academy of Visual Arts promotes the scheme for practice-led and practice-based PhDs with access to its studios). FUNDING: HKD 344,400 a year stipend and HKD 14,400 a year travel allowance for up to three years, with university top-ups (HKBU: Elite Scholar Award HKD 40,000 a year, extra travel funds, tuition waiver, housing). ELIGIBILITY: anyone seeking admission as a new full-time PhD student at a participating university, irrespective of country of origin, prior work experience or ethnic background; selection weighs academic excellence, research ability, communication and leadership. HOW TO APPLY: first make the Initial Application to the RGC (open since 1 September 2026) by 12:00 noon Hong Kong time, 1 December 2026, then apply to up to two programmes at one or two universities by their own deadlines (listed as 1 December 2026, 11:59pm Hong Kong time; some fall right after the RGC deadline, so apply early). Many departments, HKBU's AVA included, require a proposed supervisor.

  3. n-Space Fellowship, second cohort (Prediction): 18-month research fellowship at Somerset House Studios, London (GBP 10,000 fee plus shared GBP 30,000 R&D fund)

    Somerset House Studios, with UAL Creative Computing Institute, Abandon Normal Devices, Lancaster University and the National Arts Council Singapore; supported by the Rothschild Foundation · Somerset House Studios, London, UK. London-track fellows must be based in London and use the n-Space workspace at least two days a week. A separate track brings one Singapore citizen or permanent resident to London for three months. · Deadline: 07 Dec 2026 · Award: London track: GBP 10,000 fee and access to a GBP 30,000 shared R&D fund, with 24/7 workspace. No travel, relocation or accommodation support. Singapore track: GBP 5,000 fee plus return flights, accommodation, per diems and ground transport, and the same shared fund. Up to GBP 200 towards an access support worker for the application. No application fee mentioned.

    n-Space at Somerset House Studios is a space for experimental, collaborative research across art, technology and society, aimed at outcomes that do not fit traditional institutional platforms. Its second fellowship cohort is convened around PREDICTION. THEME: once the domain of divination and prophecy, prediction now runs through sensing infrastructures, statistical forecasting and AI systems that shape markets, governance and public life, and often produce the futures they anticipate. Research can treat prediction as a subject, a method or a medium. WHO: artists, designers, engineers, lawyers, scientists and technologists who want to work in a collaborative research cohort, across fields from economics and law to computation and the arts. Applicants need five or more years of practice in their current field and may not be in full-time education, PhD programmes included, during the fellowship. At most three of the fellows will be artists. Duos and collectives can apply but split the fee and name up to two representatives. Nationality and visa rules are not stated. COHORT: six fellows, five based in London and one from Singapore. COMMITMENT: London fellows must be based in London, use n-Space as their main base at least two days a week, attend about 80% of sessions, and join fortnightly Friday sessions at UAL's Creative Computing Institute in person (12 April to 18 June and 4 October to 10 December 2027). SUPPORT: GBP 10,000 fee, a GBP 30,000 R&D fund shared by the cohort, 24/7 workspace, access to rehearsal, recording and project spaces and the Makerversity workshops, expert sessions, curatorial support from curator Linda Rocco, and mentorship from Constant Dullaart, Gary Zhexi Zhang, Stephanie Dinkins and Xin Liu. OUTPUT: at least one research output for the final presentation at Somerset House in September 2028. It need not be a finished artwork; a prototype, software, model, writing or proposal all count. SINGAPORE TRACK: with the National Arts Council Singapore, one Singapore citizen or permanent resident with five or more years in art and technology gets a nine-month fellowship, with three months in London (April to June 2027), a GBP 5,000 fee and travel, accommodation and per diems covered; it has its own application form on the call page. APPLICATION: online form linked from the call page. DEADLINE: Monday 7 December 2026, 10:00 UK time, for both tracks.

  4. Jumping Frames 2027: Hong Kong International Movement-Image Festival, Open Call (Copy Right Copy Left)

    City Contemporary Dance Company (CCDC) · Hong Kong (festival August 2027). Open to filmmakers internationally. · Deadline: 16 Dec 2026 · Award: Jumping Frames International Award HKD 15,000 (figure from secondary listings of the 2027 call; confirm on the official site). Entry fees vary by deadline tier.

    Open call for dance and movement-image films up to 20 minutes for the 2027 Jumping Frames festival. The 2027 theme, Copy Right Copy Left, explicitly invites AI and digital tools and asks about authorship in the AI era. Entries via FilmFreeway (https://filmfreeway.com/CCDC_JumpingFrames2027); earlier tiers closed 6 and 26 November, final deadline 16 December 2026. Entry fees apply on most tiers.

  5. FutureTense Awards, 4th Edition "Oneiric": media art and digital art awards for artists aged 30 or under based in Asia (up to HKD 15,000)

    FutureTense (Hong Kong) · Hong Kong. For artists based in Asia: Hong Kong, Macau, China, Taiwan, Mongolia, Japan, Korea and South East Asia. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: Media Art Award and Digital Art Award: up to HKD 15,000 each for artwork production, with honourable mentions of HKD 5,000. Best Popularity Award: up to HKD 10,000 for artwork production. No entry fee stated.

    FutureTense is a Hong Kong non-profit platform for art, design and technology, running since 2019. The 4th edition of its awards takes the theme Oneiric (of dreams): an open call for works at the intersection of mind, machine and myth, with threads including the Cybernetic Unconscious, Organic Digital, Kantian Threshold, Archetypal Dreamscape and Fragmented Self. CATEGORIES: Media Art (physical: experiential and performative installations, immersive and interactive art) and Digital Art (virtual: any genre presenting an original digital expression). WHO: artists aged 30 or below, based in Asia (Hong Kong, Macau, China, Taiwan, Mongolia, Japan, Korea and South East Asia). AWARDS: up to HKD 15,000 for artwork production in each category, honourable mentions of HKD 5,000, and a public-vote Best Popularity Award of up to HKD 10,000; shortlisted entries are entered for the popularity vote automatically. Winners take part in the award ceremony and exhibition in August to September 2027. JURY includes Fei Jun, Lim Liwon, Miu Ling Lam, Tobias Klein, Isaac Leung, Ruini Shi and Wu Dar-Kuen. APPLICATION: via Zealous, linked from the award site. DEADLINE: the award site gives the open call as October to December 2026 without an exact day; the date shown here is the end of that window (the previous edition closed on 31 December), so check the Zealous form and apply early.
