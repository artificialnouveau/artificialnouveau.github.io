Grants, Fellowships and Residencies (Remote)

Currently 16 active paid grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in Remote (work-from-anywhere), across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Mozilla Foundation Incubator: Human-Scale AI cohort 2027 (call for proposals)

    Mozilla Foundation (Mozilla Foundation Incubator) · Remote cohort worldwide, with a platform at the Mozilla Festival. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 300,000 non-dilutive funding plus 12 months of support for each of seven selected projects; projects showing strong progress may receive up to USD 200,000 more and two further years of support, for a total of up to USD 500,000 over three years. No application fee.

    The Mozilla Foundation Incubator's next cohort funds seven Human-Scale AI projects: working AI technologies that are small (small models, less compute, able to run locally or on low-power devices), intentional (users and communities get insight into and control over their data, and systems can be configured, forked or community-governed) and tailored (fit-for-purpose for a specific community, context or problem, with particular interest in resource-constrained regions, people with disabilities and other underserved groups). Projects must already be deployed with the people they serve, even at pilot scale, and show early product-community fit. Not wanted: concepts without working technology, standalone governance or policy work, generic chatbots, or products whose core depends on a frontier model or a slimmed-down version of one. The cohort runs for 12 months from April 2027 with coaching, a Mozilla programme officer, network access and a showcase at the Mozilla Festival; required sessions are about one or two hour-long virtual meetings a month, and teams must work in the open. ELIGIBILITY: worldwide; you do not need to be a nonprofit but the project must have a charitable purpose and you must be legally able to receive Mozilla Foundation grant funds. Applications in English (translation tools welcome). Multi-stage process: an initial project proposal, then a full application for a shortlist. Info session 12 November 2026, 17:00 UTC. DEADLINE: Friday 20 November 2026, 11:59PM PST (UTC-8).

  2. PRISM 2027: Call for AI Safety Research Mentors

    PRISM (Peer-Driven Research Initiative for Safety Methodologies) · Fully remote, all timezones welcome · Deadline: 25 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 1,000 honorarium per mentor (about 20 mentors), plus co-authorship on the team's conference submission (venues like NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR). Research manager and support staff included.

    PRISM (Peer-Driven Research Initiative for Safety Methodologies) is recruiting about 20 mentors to guide teams of 4-5 emerging AI safety researchers from research question to a conference submission, with a focus on supporting women and underrepresented groups. The 2026 cohort ran 12 teams and produced 9 papers accepted to NeurIPS workshops. ROLE: you propose the project and set the research direction; help fellows build research taste (choosing good questions, designing sound experiments, knowing when a result is worth trusting); run weekly milestone reviews of work the team has already refined among themselves; own the quality bar for the final paper. PRISM provides a clear structure and support staff, which you are free to adjust. TEAM: PRISM hands you 15-20 pre-filtered candidates matched to your criteria (advanced undergraduates to experienced professionals, many with graduate degrees); you pick your team, no cold outreach. ELIGIBILITY: research experience at roughly late-PhD or MATS-scholar level and the ability to guide a team on AI safety research. Fully remote, all timezones. TIMELINE: mentor applications 29 October to 25 November 2026, reviewed on a rolling basis and may close early if spots fill; shortlisted applicants are interviewed and record a 5-10 minute pitch of their research question for fellow applicants; mentors announced end of November; fellow recruitment 14 December to 8 January (one Q&A session); fellow selection 11 to 20 January; program 31 January to 23 May 2027.

  3. RJI Professional Innovation Fellowships 2027 (Individual, Institutional and Emerging Technology tracks)

    Reynolds Journalism Institute (University of Missouri) · Remote, with a few paid visits to the Reynolds Journalism Institute in Columbia, Missouri, United States. · Deadline: 08 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 75,000 for the Individual and Institutional tracks, USD 100,000 for the Emerging Technology track, paid in three instalments tied to deliverables; RJI also pays for the fellow's visits to its Columbia, Missouri campus. No application fee.

    The Reynolds Journalism Institute at the University of Missouri funds people and teams to build and launch a tool, workflow or product that helps newsrooms, in three tracks: Individual (USD 75,000), Institutional (USD 75,000, paid to a host organisation or the fellow) and Emerging Technology (USD 100,000, where AI or another new technology must be essential to the solution). The output must be a free, open-source resource usable by US journalists; tools limited to one city or state are unlikely to be funded. The fellowship is remote and runs for eight months from 28 June 2027 with an in-person kickoff at RJI and a couple of paid visits. ELIGIBILITY: anyone, anywhere; no age, background or journalism credential required. Applications open 2 November 2026 on Submittable; a shortlist is invited in early to mid February 2027 to submit a fuller application, with finalist interviews 5 to 23 April 2027. DEADLINE: 8 January 2027 (first round; no time zone stated, RJI is on US Central time).

  4. Awesome Foundation Conservation and Climate Chapter: USD 1,000 Micro-grants (worldwide, rolling)

    Awesome Foundation (Conservation and Climate Chapter) · Worldwide; applications in English. Remote. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per grant, no strings attached, paid via PayPal, Wise or Venmo. No application fee.

    A volunteer-run, self-funded giving circle in the Awesome Foundation network that awards USD 1,000 micro-grants to innovative projects on wildlife conservation, habitat protection, climate change, ecotourism or environmental communication. Recent grantees include a solar e-waste mobile repair unit, a thermal-imaging wildlife detection project and environmental conservation origami art. WHAT THEY WANT: genuinely new ideas or unusual approaches (the chapter says it gets hundreds of tree-planting, upcycling, plastic clean-up, beekeeping and community-garden applications and rarely funds them), a USD 1,000 tipping point where the grant makes the project happen, a clear budget, and work close to implementation. ELIGIBILITY: individuals and small teams worldwide; you need a PayPal, Wise or Venmo account to receive funds. NOT FUNDED: personal expenses, marketing, salaries or operating costs. APPLY: free, short online form on the chapter page. DEADLINE: rolling.

  5. Awesome Foundation On the Water Chapter: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants for Water-focused Projects (worldwide)

    Awesome Foundation (On the Water Chapter) · Worldwide; applications in English. Remote. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per month, one grant, no strings attached. No application fee.

    An Awesome Foundation chapter run by artists and cultural producers that gives a USD 1,000 grant every month to a water-focused project that "adds awesome to the world". It asks: does your project bring creativity, culture and inclusion to the water, help people think about water in a new way, or build relationships with it? Recent grantees include Mulino Umano (a human-powered watermill), a free DJ set on the Thames foreshore, a water-themed radio podcast and Waterway Horns, a brass project on boats. ELIGIBILITY: open worldwide to individuals and groups. APPLY: free, short online form; lead with one sentence saying what you are making, say where you are, keep it brief and specific, and explain how it relates to water and who benefits. DEADLINE: rolling, decided monthly.

  6. Pantheon: Research Fellowship in robot learning (remote, USD 6,000/month)

    Pantheon · Remote · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 6,000 per month stipend (about USD 12,000 to 18,000 over the fellowship) plus enterprise-level compute for experiments. No application fee.

    Pantheon, which builds general-purpose robot models, runs a remote research fellowship in which each fellow leads a project on an open problem the team meets while training and evaluating its models; strong results are folded into the next generation of those models. WHO: graduate students and exceptional undergraduates with backgrounds in pretraining large models, reinforcement learning or adjacent fields who want to work on general-purpose robotics. CURRENT RESEARCH QUESTIONS: value learning for general-purpose tasks; model drift and test-time adaptation in world models; multimodal representation (vision, touch, force, proprioception at different rates or missing); scaling laws for forward dynamics models versus video pretraining; learning from deliberately induced failure. OFFER: USD 6,000/month stipend, enterprise-level compute, weekly 1:1 mentorship (team includes ETH Zurich alumni, ICML authors, NeurIPS award winners, and researchers from xAI and Jane Street), work toward a paper or other public research output; outstanding fellows may be offered full-time roles. Past fellows came from ETH Zurich, IIT Bombay, Yale, Stanford and Cambridge. APPLY: email your CV, a short note on the research questions that excite you, and any relevant work to fellowship@pantheon.inc. No deadline is published (rolling).

  7. AIXI Labs: Research Fellowship (rolling, quarterly rounds)

    AIXI Labs · Fully remote, open worldwide; fellows may also work from the organisation's London base in the United Kingdom. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 2,000 to GBP 3,500 per month stipend, plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. Free to apply.

    AIXI Labs is a small London-based non-profit doing AI safety research grounded in algorithmic information theory. Its stated position is that most AI research today is either mathematically clean but narrow, or practical but too opaque for rigorous safety analysis; the lab targets a third category, methods general enough to describe powerful agents and provable enough to support real safety claims, using AIXI (the theoretical model of unbounded artificial superintelligence) and then porting the strongest of those ideas to modern LLM-based agents. The fellowship is a quarterly, rolling programme for researchers who want to work on this agenda under close mentorship from the core team: Cole Wyeth (Founder and Executive Director), Marcus Hutter (Research Director) and Aram Ebtekar (Founding Research Scientist). Fellows pursue a focused research project over roughly a quarter and receive a monthly stipend plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. The fellowship is fully remote, so fellows may work from anywhere in the world or from the London base. RESPONSIBILITIES: pursue a self-directed research project aligned with the lab's algorithmic-information-theoretic approach to AI safety, spanning anything from foundational theory to empirical tests with LLM-based agents; shape the project direction around your own strengths and interests, as the lab supports a range of work within this framework; and contribute papers, blog posts or other written output communicating results to the broader AI safety and ML research communities. MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS: a research background at the level of a current PhD student (for primarily empirical projects, a master's-level command of the relevant mathematics paired with substantial industry or applied research experience can also qualify); solid grounding in probability, statistics, information theory and reinforcement learning, sufficient to engage seriously with AIXI-style theoretical models (prior expertise in algorithmic information theory or AIXI is NOT required); strong verbal and written communication skills; genuine motivation to reduce existential risk from advanced AI and enthusiasm for the lab's kind of work. PREFERRED QUALIFICATIONS: publications at top ML, AI or theory venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ALT or COLT, though the lab values the strength of your best work over volume; background in algorithmic information theory, computability theory, learning theory or related theoretical computer science; experience fine-tuning, evaluating or red-teaming LLM-based and/or RL agents; prior engagement with the UAI/AIXI community or related fields, including members of the lab's online reading groups. APPLICATIONS are reviewed on a rolling basis and previous applicants remain eligible for later rounds.

  8. Digital Impact Alliance (DIAL) Research Fellowship

    Digital Impact Alliance (DIAL) · Remote; international organisation. Experience working in or with global majority countries encouraged. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 5,000 stipend for a 6-8 month fellowship. No application fee.

    The Digital Impact Alliance (DIAL) Research Fellowship supports curious, passionate mid-career researchers and practitioners to explore frontier questions on digital public infrastructure (DPI) and data governance. Fellows bring their own knowledge and research agenda while contributing to DIAL's core thematic priorities: creating more value for more people from data; understanding how digital systems and services impact people, including and especially the most marginalised; and accelerating the adoption of data-sharing and governance practices that fuel innovation and empower people. SUPPORT: a USD 5,000 stipend for research completed over 6-8 months, plus mentorship from DIAL staff and industry experts, access to DIAL's network and resources, collaboration with partner organisations, and support amplifying the work. Each fellow produces two preliminary pieces and one final, more extensive output (research papers or reports, policy briefs, expert-comment series, etc.). ELIGIBILITY: professionals from academia, the private sector, and civil society, typically with 8-15 years of experience in digital development, policy, or research; demonstrated work at the intersection of technology and society (including DPI and data governance); a track record of high-quality writing, research, or policy briefs; experience working in or with global majority countries; and strong analytical and communication skills. TO APPLY (rolling basis): submit a CV or resume, a writing sample or portfolio, and a research proposal. Questions: fellows@dial.global. Apply via https://dial.global/apply-to-our-fellows-program/.

  9. Manifund: AI Safety Regranting (Rolling)

    Manifund · Remote; US-based platform, open worldwide. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Grants typically USD 5,000 to 50,000, recommended by independent regrantors from their own budgets. No application fee. No legal entity required.

    Manifund's regranting program funds AI safety and global-catastrophic-risk work through a network of expert regrantors, each given an independent budget to recommend grants to people and projects they find promising. Applicants submit a project proposal on the Manifund platform; regrantors can then offer funding. GRANTS: typically USD 5,000 to 50,000, though amounts vary. ELIGIBILITY: individuals (no legal entity or institutional affiliation required) and organizations working on AI safety or reducing catastrophic risk from AI. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis; per-regrantor budget availability varies over time. Learn more at the regranting page and apply on the Manifund platform (manifund.org).

  10. Luminos.AI Legal Engineering Fellowship (Fall 2026)

    Luminos.AI · Remote · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 35 per hour, part-time. No application fee.

    Luminos.AI, an embedded AI-governance and legal-risk infrastructure company (built by former Fortune 500 AI governance leads and company-builders), runs a part-time fellowship for a technically inclined law student or recent graduate who wants to work at the frontier of AI agent oversight and risk evaluation. Fellows work directly with senior lawyers and engineers on the practical problem of how to test, evaluate and govern generative and agentic AI systems that change every quarter. WHAT YOU DO: research and help design rigorous testing and evaluation approaches for generative and agentic AI, translating fast-moving legal and regulatory requirements into concrete, testable standards; track the global AI regulatory landscape in real time and turn it into practical guardrails; draft frameworks, workflows and risk assessments that hold up under real deployment conditions; and partner closely with the technical team, where comfort around code, models or data is an asset. Fellows with technical skills who want to spend part of their time working directly with engineering are encouraged to do so. WHO YOU ARE: currently pursuing a JD (or a recent graduate) from a top law program; an excellent researcher and writer comfortable applying existing law to genuinely new situations; genuinely enthusiastic about AI and willing to learn the technical details. Bonus: coding ability, ML/GenAI exposure, or a background in privacy, security or data governance law. TERMS: USD 35 per hour, remote, part-time, flexible 3 to 6 month term, with direct mentorship from senior legal and technical leadership. TO APPLY: send a cover letter, resume, transcript and writing sample to careers@luminos.ai with the subject line 'Application for Luminos.AI Fall 2026 Fellowship'. Rolling review with remote interviews.

  11. Rest of World: Labor x Tech Reporting Commissions (Year 3)

    Rest of World, underwritten by the Ford Foundation · Remote. Reporting from anywhere in the world except India; writers based in India are not eligible for this series. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per commissioned story of 800 to 1,000 words. No application fee.

    Rest of World is in the third year of its Labor x Tech program, underwritten by the Ford Foundation, commissioning reported stories on the changing nature of work at a moment of upheaval: AI tools and robotics reshaping professions at every rung of society, from self-driving cars replacing drivers to humanoid robots working alongside nurses; lay-offs across the tech industry; and the gig work model continuing to reshape traditional employment. The programme's premise is that this moment needs documenting now, in stories that help prepare society for what comes next. WHAT THEY COMMISSION: reported stories of 800 to 1,000 words from around the world on this beat, at a fee of USD 1,000 per story. WHAT THEY ARE LOOKING FOR, based on previous years: how new technology is transforming existing industries in unique ways and creating unexpected new pockets of employment; how technology is leading to significant job gain, job loss, and government responses to those trends; whether large demographic shifts are following the adoption of new technology or the arrival of new industries; how workplace technology is changing workplace dynamics; how new industries and tools are changing workers' incomes; and the expansion of the gig work model alongside the threats of automation. EXAMPLES of the kind of story they hope to commission, all previously published by Rest of World: workers in Chinese data factories teaching humanoid robots boring tasks; entry-level tech workers describing the AI-fuelled jobpocalypse; Microsoft and Google claiming their data centres create thousands of jobs while their permit filings say otherwise; AI helping judges close cases quickly and lawyers open them quickly; tech workers fuelling a mini baby boom in Taiwan's Silicon Valley; Dubai's robotaxi ambitions coming for human drivers; and the job brokers who run the lives of the migrant workers staffing Taiwan's chip plants. ELIGIBILITY NOTE: the programme is unable to commission reporting in India or from writers based in India for this series. HOW TO PITCH: send 2 to 3 paragraphs laying out the nutgraf of the proposal and explaining how the pitch fits the editorial mission of the programme. Indicate what sources will be used, whether any pre-reporting has been completed, what reporting has already been done on the topic at other outlets, and what the story contributes to the discourse. Pitches go to laborxtech@restofworld.org. Pitching is open on a rolling basis; no closing date is stated.

  12. Anthropic Fellows Program (funded AI research fellowship, January 2027 cohort)

    Anthropic (with Constellation as recruiting partner) · San Francisco, London, or Ontario, with remote-friendly options; applicants must hold work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Shared workspaces available in Berkeley and London. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,850 / GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300 per week for four months of full-time research, plus roughly USD 15,000 per month in compute funding and other research expenses. No application fee.

    The Anthropic Fellows Program is a four-month, fully funded research fellowship with direct mentorship from Anthropic researchers. Fellows work on empirical projects aligned with Anthropic's research priorities using external infrastructure, aiming to produce public outputs such as papers. Five workstreams: AI Safety, AI Security, ML Systems and Performance, Reinforcement Learning, and Economics and Policy; safety topics include scalable oversight, adversarial robustness, mechanistic interpretability, and model organisms. FUNDING: a weekly stipend of USD 3,850 (GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300), around USD 15,000 per month in compute funding, and other research expenses. ELIGIBILITY: fluent Python; available full-time for four months; strong technical background in computer science, mathematics, or physics; work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Locations: San Francisco, London, Ontario, or remote within those countries. APPLICATION: rolling, via the Constellation portal; the process includes an initial application, reference checks, technical assessments, interviews, and a research discussion. The next cohort is expected to start in January 2027, so apply well before the end of 2026.

  13. Sequoia Open Source Fellowship (living-expense stipend for open-source developers, rolling)

    Sequoia Capital · Remote, location agnostic. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Stipend covering reasonable living expenses for 6 to 12 months (no fixed figure published). Sequoia takes no equity and expects nothing in return; 2 to 3 fellows are selected per year. No application fee.

    Sequoia's Open Source Fellowship pays developers to work full time on open-source software without starting a company. WHO: any developer working on an open-source project, anywhere in the world; selection favours existing projects with real-world adoption (not just GitHub stars), including web frameworks, tooling and infrastructure. Fellows should plan significant improvements or investments in the project or support its expansion, and keep full ownership of all code written before, during and after the fellowship. WHAT YOU GET: a stipend sized to cover reasonable living expenses for 6 to 12 months, with no equity or obligations; roughly 2 to 3 fellows a year. APPLICATION: a brief questionnaire on the fellowship page, reviewed on a rolling basis; Sequoia contacts applicants whose projects are a good fit. DEADLINE: rolling.
