Film and Video Grants in the US

Currently 12 active film and video grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the US. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Script Pipeline: First Look Project 2026 (screenplay and TV pilot contest)

    Script Pipeline · Online submission; open to writers worldwide. Script Pipeline is based in Los Angeles, US. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 15,000 in total prizes across six winners: USD 2,500 to the winner of each of four screenplay genre categories (Action and Sci-Fi, Comedy, Drama, Horror and Thriller) and USD 2,500 to the winner of each of two TV pilot categories (hour and half-hour). All winners also receive long-term circulation to Script Pipeline's industry network of producers, managers, agents and directors, 1-on-1 development assistance from Script Pipeline execs, additional script reviews and consideration of other material, career guidance and mentorship, and invitations to private Symposium events. Entry fee: USD 60 at the regular deadline, rising to USD 65 (late, 15 October) and USD 70 (extended, 30 October).

    The 15th annual First Look Project from Script Pipeline searches for higher-concept material across six categories: feature screenplays in Action and Sci-Fi, Comedy, Drama, and Horror and Thriller, plus hour and half-hour original TV pilots. Unlike Script Pipeline's Screenwriting and TV Writing contests, entries are judged on both writing ability and market potential, in addition to originality of concept; a script that still needs refining can win if the story could stand out in the current landscape, as the aim is a first look at the next buzzworthy specs before they hit the market. GUIDELINES: screenplays must be feature length; genres without a listed category go to the closest one (Fantasy, Western or Adventure under Action and Sci-Fi; Crime and Mystery under Thriller). TV pilots run roughly 30 to 60 pages, all genres accepted including animation and genre hybrids. The script must not be under an option or shopping agreement at the time of entry, and the same script cannot compete in multiple categories. CIRCULATION: Script Pipeline's industry relationships (Bellevue Productions, LBI Entertainment, Hungry Man, Alibi Management and others) have led to writers signed, specs sold and films produced. Regular deadline 30 September 2026; late deadline 15 October; extended deadline 30 October. Winners announced 15 December 2026.

  2. Ann Arbor Film Festival 2027: Call for Entries (65th Edition)

    Ann Arbor Film Festival · Ann Arbor, Michigan, United States. Open internationally. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: All selected artists receive a screening fee: USD 50 per short film, USD 100 per feature. Nearly USD 40,000 in total awards, including Oscar-qualifying prizes. Entry fee for the final deadline tier is USD 100 (the earlier USD 65 and USD 80 tiers have passed).

    The Ann Arbor Film Festival, founded in 1963 and the oldest experimental and avant-garde film festival in North America, is accepting entries for its 65th edition (23 to 28 March 2027). The festival seeks boundary-pushing films and expanded cinema works across experimental, documentary, animation and hybrid forms, and is a natural home for artists' moving image and essayistic work. PAY: unlike most festivals, every selected artist receives a screening fee (USD 50 for a short, USD 100 for a feature), and films compete for nearly USD 40,000 in awards including Academy Award qualifying prizes. SUBMISSION: via FilmFreeway or the festival site; the final deadline is 30 September 2026 at the USD 100 late tier (early bird USD 65 closed 31 July and regular USD 80 closed 31 August). The entry fee is substantial, but payment on selection is guaranteed, which is rare among festivals of this stature.

  3. Gurl Grant: USD 2,500 for female and non-binary short film directors

    Gurl Grant (Kat VanCleave) · Online application. For female-identifying and non-binary film directors who have taken the 51% Pledge; winnings are paid by ACH transfer. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 2,500 cash grant toward the production of a narrative short film, awarded twice yearly. No application fee.

    The Gurl Grant, founded by filmmaker Kat VanCleave in 2025, puts money directly into the hands of female and non-binary film directors. Twice a year it awards a USD 2,500 cash grant toward the production of a narrative short film made with a majority women or non-binary crew. ELIGIBILITY: female-identifying and non-binary film directors who have taken the 51% Pledge, a good-faith commitment to dedicate 51% of filmmaking resources to traditionally underrepresented filmmakers (women, BIPOC, LGBTQIA2S+); narrative short scripts of 15 pages or fewer; no major studio affiliations or distribution deals; directors may win only once per calendar year and submit once per cycle. HOW TO APPLY: a single PDF with a one-page cover letter, the script (max 15 pages), a completed 51% Pledge certificate and a pitch deck. No application fee. Submissions are capped at 100 entries per cycle, first come first served. Winnings are distributed by ACH transfer. DEADLINE: 30 September 2026 at 11:55 PM EST, or earlier once 100 submissions are received.

  4. No Drama 2.0: USD 50,000 horror short film initiative (Monkeypaw x Universal x American Cinematheque)

    Monkeypaw Productions, Universal Pictures and American Cinematheque · United States; winners must be able to participate in person in Los Angeles. Open to writer-directors with legal authorization to work in the US. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: Up to three filmmakers each receive USD 50,000 to fund production of a short film / proof of concept, plus mentorship from Monkeypaw, Universal and American Cinematheque executives, eligibility to screen at Beyond Fest 2027, and first-look consideration from Universal Pictures and Monkeypaw. No application fee.

    No Drama 2.0 is a short-film development and production initiative from Jordan Peele's Monkeypaw Productions, Universal Pictures and American Cinematheque, seeking writer-directors with bold, genre-centered stories. This edition's theme, 'Fear is universal. Monsters are personal', asks filmmakers to redefine horror across cultures, generations and societies, exploring the monsters we carry within us, interpreted broadly as personal fears, afflictions or internal struggles rather than literal creatures. SUPPORT: up to three filmmakers each receive USD 50,000 to produce their submitted short film as a proof of concept for a feature, plus mentorship from Monkeypaw, Universal and American Cinematheque executives, eligibility to screen at Beyond Fest 2027, and first-look consideration from Universal and Monkeypaw. ELIGIBILITY: solo writer-directors (no teams) aged 21+ by 1 December 2026 with legal authorization to work in the United States, prior directing experience (short, proof of concept, music video, web series, TV episode or feature), full ownership of submitted material, and availability to participate in person in Los Angeles. HOW TO APPLY: via FilmFreeway with an original short script (max 10 pages), a feature treatment (max 5 pages), a lookbook (8-15 pages), a personal statement or pitch video (max 6 minutes), a creative portfolio (minimum 2 samples), a resume and a hand-signed release form. No application fee. DEADLINE: 30 September 2026 at 11:59 PM PDT.

  5. Pandora Short Film Grant 2026

    Pandora Short Film Grant · United States (early-career US filmmakers) · Deadline: 01 Oct 2026 · Award: $12,000 production grant to the winner, plus a Panavision camera package (worth about $15,000), an optional $1,000 Kodak Film Grant toward film stock and lab expenses, mentorship from an award-winning filmmaker, and fiscal sponsorship. Three finalists receive mentorship, fiscal sponsorship, consideration for the Panavision New Filmmaker Program, and $500 off Athos production insurance. ENTRY FEE: tiered, $30 regular ($27 Gold members) and $40 late ($36); Pandora states fee waivers are available on request.

    The Pandora Short Film Grant is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that awards an annual $12,000 grant to an early-career US filmmaker for the production of a narrative short film. Projects at script stage or already filmed and seeking finishing funds are both eligible. The winner also receives a Panavision camera package (about $15,000), an optional $1,000 Kodak Film Grant, mentorship from an award-winning filmmaker (2026 mentors include Alma Har'el, Lila Neugebauer, Lauren Wolkstein, Sarah Elizabeth Mintz and Chris Merola), and fiscal sponsorship; three finalists receive mentorship, fiscal sponsorship, Panavision New Filmmaker Program consideration and discounted production insurance. ELIGIBILITY: open to all early-career US filmmakers; applicants are asked NOT to apply if they have directed a feature film with a budget over $250K or directed or produced television. Selection is blind, based on script and pitch materials, weighting artistic vision over industry resume. TIMELINE: submissions open 30 May 2026, close 1 October 2026, notifications 15 November 2026.

  6. Black Public Media: BPM Open Call 2026 (USD 20,000 for nonfiction features)

    Black Public Media · US. Applicant (or a key member of the production team) must be a US citizen or green-card holder; nonfiction feature projects about the global Black experience. · Deadline: 01 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 20,000 in production funding for up to five projects, each also invited to pitch at PitchBLACK in June 2027, where one project receives an additional USD 150,000 award. Structured as licensing agreements for public media distribution. Free to apply.

    Black Public Media, the US public-media organization funding stories of the global Black experience, invites nonfiction feature-length projects (60+ minutes) in a range of formats, styles and genres, with experimental and hybrid approaches welcome as long as they are grounded in real people and events (confirmed 24 September 2026). Up to five projects each receive USD 20,000 to finance pre-production, production or early post-production, plus an invitation to pitch at PitchBLACK in June 2027, where one project receives an additional USD 150,000 award. BPM's 2026 priorities include anti-DEI efforts in higher education, housing inequities, and redistricting's impact on voting rights, though all subjects are considered. IMPORTANT: the applicant or a key member of the production team must be a US citizen or green-card holder, with at least three years of professional producing/directing experience or a senior producer attached; the applicant must hold artistic, budgetary and editorial control and own the copyright, and the funding is a licensing agreement for public media distribution rather than an unrestricted grant. No industrial, promotional or student films. TO APPLY: submit the project description, production team bios, financials and a work sample via BPM's Submittable portal by 1 October 2026, 11:59 pm PT.

  7. Frameline Completion Fund 2027

    Frameline · Administered from San Francisco, United States. Open to filmmakers of all nationalities. · Deadline: 09 Oct 2026 · Award: Grants of up to USD 5,000, with a standard range of USD 1,000 to 5,000; the jury can recommend amounts above or below that range. No application fee.

    Long-running post-production grant from Frameline, the San Francisco organisation behind the world's longest-running LGBTQ+ film festival, for films that represent and reflect LGBTQ+ life in all its complexity and richness. Grants run up to USD 5,000, typically USD 1,000 to 5,000, and the jury may recommend more or less than that range. WHAT IT COVERS: any post-production cost, explicitly including editing, special effects and graphics, music licensing, photo licensing, and, notably, closed captioning and audio description, so accessibility work is fundable rather than an afterthought. STAGE GATE, the thing that disqualifies most applicants: your project must be at least 90 percent complete with a rough cut available, and in or about to enter post-production. Work still in development or mid-production is not eligible. WHO AND WHAT: all formats, lengths, genres and nationalities are welcome, spanning documentary, narrative, experimental, animated and episodic work, so long as the film is about LGBTQ+ people and their communities. Directors must have creative and financial control of the project. NO APPLICATION FEE, which is worth flagging given how many completion funds charge one. TIMING: the submission window opens 31 August 2026 and closes 9 October 2026, with decisions in January 2027. The 2026 cycle's grantees have already been announced ahead of Frameline's 50th festival, so this is the next round.

  8. Vimeo Short Film Grant (USD 60,000 for 5 filmmakers)

    Vimeo · Online application. Open to residents of the United States and Canada. · Deadline: 16 Oct 2026 · Award: Five filmmakers each receive USD 60,000 in cash for production, plus a one-year Vimeo Advanced subscription, mentorship from celebrated filmmakers, a loaned Panavision camera package, OWC storage hardware, and distribution on Vimeo with in-person screenings. Free to apply.

    Vimeo's Short Film Grant awards five emerging filmmakers USD 60,000 in cash each to produce a short film, alongside mentorship from celebrated filmmakers, a loaned Panavision camera package, OWC storage hardware, a one-year Vimeo Advanced subscription, and distribution on Vimeo with in-person screenings. ELIGIBILITY: residents of the United States or Canada, at least 18 years old, who are emerging filmmakers having directed at least two short films but no feature films; a valid Vimeo account is required. TIMELINE: winners announced by 15 March 2027; production must be completed by September 2027 and post-production by March 2028. HOW TO APPLY: via SlideRoom with project details, an artist statement, team bios, creative materials (screenplay or treatment), a budget proposal, a production timeline and mentorship goals. No fee to apply. DEADLINE: 16 October 2026 at 11:59 PM ET (opened 21 September 2026).

  9. Black List: Cassian Elwes Independent Screenwriting Fellowship 2027 (all-expenses-paid Sundance trip)

    The Black List with producer Cassian Elwes · Sundance Film Festival, Boulder, Colorado, US (21 to 31 January 2027). Entry via the Black List website; open to unrepresented writers worldwide. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: One fellow receives an all-expenses-paid trip to the 2027 Sundance Film Festival, including a festival pass for the full 21 to 31 January 2027 run in Boulder, Colorado, attending the festival, meetings and events one-on-one with producer Cassian Elwes (Mudbound, Lee Daniels' The Butler, Dallas Buyers Club). No cash grant. NOTE: consideration requires a project hosted on the Black List with at least one completed or pending paid evaluation, so budget for the site's hosting and evaluation fees; fee waivers are available.

    Annual fellowship from the Black List and independent producer Cassian Elwes, now in its second decade, designed to encourage and identify new talent in independent cinema. THE AWARD: one screenwriter receives an all-expenses-paid trip to the 2027 Sundance Film Festival, the festival's first Boulder, Colorado edition (21 to 31 January 2027), with a festival pass and the chance to attend the festival, meetings and events alongside Elwes, whose credits include Mudbound, Lee Daniels' The Butler and Dallas Buyers Club. ELIGIBILITY: unrepresented feature writers whose work shows an independent sensibility and who have made less than USD 5,000 in aggregate in their film or television writing careers. HOW IT WORKS: opt in to consideration via the Black List website; a project hosted on the platform with at least one completed or pending evaluation is mandatory, so factor in the site's hosting and evaluation fees (fee waivers available). After submissions close on 1 November 2026, the Black List selects ten screenplays imbued with an independent spirit and sends them to Elwes, who picks the fellow by the end of the calendar year.

  10. NRDC Climate Storytelling Fellowship 2027 (via The Black List)

    Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) in partnership with The Black List · United States (verify international eligibility on Black List program page) · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: $20,000 to each of three writers ($60,000 total) to support revision of a feature screenplay or pilot.

    Annual screenwriting fellowship from the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) administered through The Black List, awarding $20,000 each to three writers to revise a feature screenplay or pilot that engages with climate change in a compelling way. Submission and selection happen on The Black List platform; applicants should review program eligibility and any associated hosting/submission fees on blcklst.com before applying. Strong fit for narrative writers using fiction to dramatise climate, ecology, energy, or environmental-justice themes (rather than documentary). 2027 CYCLE: submissions are open until 4 December 2026 and are capped at a maximum of 500, so early entry is advised. All accepted submissions receive a free script evaluation and one free month of script hosting on the platform. Fellows also receive consultation with NRDC's Rewrite the Future team, which works to transform the onscreen portrayal of climate change beyond dystopia and apocalypse toward solutions, plus six months of mentorship from an established screenwriter; past mentors include Lucia Aniello, Dustin Lance Black, Roxane Gay, Nick Kroll and Daniel Scheinert.

  11. CFA Institute x IMGN Grant 2026

    CFA Institute (in partnership with IMGN) · United States (50 states + DC; applicants must be 18+) · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: $4,500 to one filmmaker. If the project is already completed before funds are disbursed, the $4,500 is paid as a reimbursement upon submission of a final cut.

    One-off film grant awarding a single filmmaker $4,500 to support an independent narrative project in 2026. Application is free to submit. Centerpiece of the application is a production book built on the IMGN platform: script breakdown, schedule, coverage, and pre-visualization for the project (tutorials are provided inside the application). Eligibility: US-based filmmakers in the 50 states plus DC, aged 18 and older. If the project is already completed before funds are disbursed, the grant is paid as a reimbursement upon submission of a final cut of the film. Winner announced and funds disbursed by end of January 2027.

  12. Field of Vision: Documentary Commissions (Rolling)

    Field of Vision · United States and international; filmmakers based anywhere. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Commissioning fees and production funding (many films are commissioned assignments); specific amounts are not published publicly. No submission fee.

    Field of Vision is a filmmaker-driven documentary unit that creates and commissions original short-form and feature nonfiction visual journalism. It funds and commissions filmmakers, often as assignments, and supports projects in development and production (it does not acquire finished films). SUPPORT: commissioning fees and production funding; specific amounts are not published publicly. ELIGIBILITY: filmmakers of all experience levels, within and outside the United States. APPLICATIONS are accepted on a rolling basis, with no fixed funding cycles or deadlines. Submit via the Field of Vision submissions page.
