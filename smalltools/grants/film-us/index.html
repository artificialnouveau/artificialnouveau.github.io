Film and Video Grants in the US

Currently 11 active film and video grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the US. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Oregon Media Arts Fellowship 2026 (two USD 5,000 awards for Oregon media artists)

    Portland Art Museum (PAM CUT) and Oregon Arts Commission · Oregon, United States (Oregon residents only). · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: Two fellowships of USD 5,000 each, no matching funds required; paid within two months of notification. No application fee.

    The Oregon Media Arts Fellowship, run by the Portland Art Museum's Center for an Untold Tomorrow with the Oregon Arts Commission, gives Oregon media artists money to create or complete new work. Eligible work is artist-driven moving image, sound, digital or time-based media: narrative, documentary and experimental film, animation, video art, artist-made television, audio and podcasts, immersive and interactive media, web-based and digital work, AR, VR and mixed reality, and emerging forms. Review criteria include sustained professional achievement and potential future contribution to the field. ELIGIBILITY: artists aged 18 or over who have lived in Oregon for at least one year as their fixed, permanent principal home, not enrolled in a creative degree programme, and who have not received a Media Arts Fellowship or any Oregon Arts Commission fellowship in the previous five years; one application per year, joint applications split a single award. DEADLINE: 15 November 2026 (no time stated; late applications are not guaranteed processing).

  2. Wavelength: WAVE Grant 2027 for emerging filmmakers (scripted short directorial debut)

    Wavelength · USA. US-based applicants only (proof of residency required). · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: Five grantees each receive a USD 5,000 seed grant for production, plus mentorship, Nice Shoes colour grading and Seed&Spark crowdfunding workshops. No application fee.

    The WAVE Grant from Wavelength supports emerging filmmakers from diverse backgrounds making their first scripted short. Five grantees each receive a USD 5,000 seed grant plus hands-on mentorship as they produce a roughly 10-minute narrative short; colour grading by Nice Shoes and crowdfunding mentorship from Seed&Spark are included (all applicants are invited to Seed&Spark workshops). ELIGIBILITY: US-based (proof of residency); this must be your scripted directorial debut (student films do not count; having directed a scripted short or feature disqualifies you); no directing duos or teams. Not eligible: documentaries, features, series, films already in production, or finishing funds. MATERIALS: 10-page script (drafts accepted), schedule, budget, collaborators, 150-word bio, CV and a 1 to 3 minute video pitch. Notification 30 January 2027. DEADLINE: 15 November 2026.

  3. Puffin Foundation: Grants 2027 cycle (Film/Video and Theater)

    Puffin Foundation Ltd. · Teaneck, New Jersey, USA. RESTRICTED: US residents with a Social Security number, or US-based nonprofits/fiscal sponsors. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: Maximum grant USD 3,500; typical awards smaller. No application fee.

    Small project grants for socially engaged art; the 2027 cycle covers film/video and theater projects, a fit for experimental film and video work with a social edge. Two-step process: mail in an application request form first, then submit the full proposal via the portal. ELIGIBILITY: US residents with a Social Security number, or US-based nonprofits and fiscally sponsored projects. TIMELINE: request forms accepted 14 September through 20 November 2026 (postmark by 25 November); completed proposals due 16 December 2026, 5pm EST.

  4. Sundance Institute: 2027 Graton Fellowship (USD 25,000 for California Native artists)

    Sundance Institute, Indigenous Program · USA. RESTRICTED: artists from a California-based tribe (federally or non-federally recognised). · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 25,000 cash grant to each of 2 fellows, plus a yearlong support programme. No application fee.

    The Graton Fellowship, run by the Sundance Institute Indigenous Program, supports two Native storytellers from California tribes with an unrestricted cash grant and a year of creative and professional support from Sundance, including mentorship and access to Institute programmes. ELIGIBILITY: artists who are members of, or can show lineal descent from, a California-based tribe, federally or non-federally recognised; proof of affiliation or descent is required. APPLY via the Sundance application portal. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026, 4:30 pm MT (3:30 pm PT), as listed on the application portal.

  5. NRDC Climate Storytelling Fellowship 2027 (via The Black List)

    Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) in partnership with The Black List · United States (verify international eligibility on Black List program page) · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: $20,000 to each of three writers ($60,000 total) to support revision of a feature screenplay or pilot.

    Annual screenwriting fellowship from the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) administered through The Black List, awarding $20,000 each to three writers to revise a feature screenplay or pilot that engages with climate change in a compelling way. Submission and selection happen on The Black List platform; applicants should review program eligibility and any associated hosting/submission fees on blcklst.com before applying. Strong fit for narrative writers using fiction to dramatise climate, ecology, energy, or environmental-justice themes (rather than documentary). 2027 CYCLE: submissions are open until 4 December 2026 and are capped at a maximum of 500, so early entry is advised. All accepted submissions receive a free script evaluation and one free month of script hosting on the platform. Fellows also receive consultation with NRDC's Rewrite the Future team, which works to transform the onscreen portrayal of climate change beyond dystopia and apocalypse toward solutions, plus six months of mentorship from an established screenwriter; past mentors include Lucia Aniello, Dustin Lance Black, Roxane Gay, Nick Kroll and Daniel Scheinert.

  6. CFA Institute x IMGN Grant 2026

    CFA Institute (in partnership with IMGN) · United States (50 states + DC; applicants must be 18+) · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: $4,500 to one filmmaker. If the project is already completed before funds are disbursed, the $4,500 is paid as a reimbursement upon submission of a final cut.

    One-off film grant awarding a single filmmaker $4,500 to support an independent narrative project in 2026. Application is free to submit. Centerpiece of the application is a production book built on the IMGN platform: script breakdown, schedule, coverage, and pre-visualization for the project (tutorials are provided inside the application). Eligibility: US-based filmmakers in the 50 states plus DC, aged 18 and older. If the project is already completed before funds are disbursed, the grant is paid as a reimbursement upon submission of a final cut of the film. Winner announced and funds disbursed by end of January 2027.

  7. School for Advanced Research: Native Artist Fellowships 2027 (USD 15,000 plus Santa Fe residency)

    School for Advanced Research, Indian Arts Research Center · Santa Fe, New Mexico, USA. RESTRICTED: Native American artists. · Deadline: 15 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 15,000 stipend plus housing, studio space, a supplies allowance and travel. No application fee stated.

    The Indian Arts Research Center at the School for Advanced Research offers three residential fellowships for Native American artists: the Eric and Barbara Dobkin Fellowship for Native women artists working in any media (including digital art and film/video), the Ronald and Susan Dubin Fellowship for traditional arts, and the Rollin and Mary Ella King Fellowship for Native visual artists from Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico or Utah. Each includes a USD 15,000 stipend, housing and studio space on the SAR campus, a supplies allowance and travel. ELIGIBILITY: Native American artists, emerging or established, per each fellowship's criteria. Applications open 1 November and are accepted through 15 January each year. DEADLINE: 15 January 2027.

  8. AT&T Untold Stories 2027 (Tribeca Festival): USD 1.2 million film production prize

    Tribeca Festival and AT&T · USA. Applicants must be 18+ and live in the US or Puerto Rico. Finalists pitch in person at the Tribeca Festival, New York, June 2027. · Deadline: 18 Jan 2027 · Award: One winner receives USD 1,200,000 to produce the film; the four other finalists receive USD 15,000 grants each. No entry fee.

    AT&T Untold Stories, now in its 10th year, is a production prize run by the Tribeca Festival and AT&T that funds a feature film from an underrepresented filmmaker. Industry judges select five projects; their filmmakers pitch to a jury of media professionals at the 2027 Tribeca Festival in June. One project wins USD 1.2 million and the other four finalists receive USD 15,000 each. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be at least 18 and reside in the US or Puerto Rico; each project needs an attached producer who is not the director; a complete English-language screenplay is required, registered with the WGA or the US Copyright Office. Submissions are reviewed on a rolling basis, so applying early helps. Virtual info session: 17 November 2026, 11am ET. Applicants are notified by 3 May 2027. TO APPLY: submit online via the Tribeca Untold Stories page; read the official rules first. DEADLINE: 18 January 2027, 11:59 PM ET.

  9. Kala Art Institute: Media Arts Fellowship 2027-28 (Berkeley; USD 3,000 stipend)

    Kala Art Institute · Berkeley, California, USA. On site. · Deadline: 01 Mar 2027 · Award: USD 3,000 stipend plus facility access.

    Kala's long-running Media Arts Fellowship supports four artists working in film and video, sound, animation, performance cinema and media installation. ELIGIBILITY: local, national and international artists not enrolled in a degree programme. OFFER: USD 3,000 stipend and up to nine months of unlimited access to Kala's media lab and facilities. APPLY through kala.slideroom.com (call opens December 2026) with a CV, up to 10 images or 5 minutes of video, and two references; notification May 2027. DEADLINE: 1 March 2027.

  10. Vision Maker Media: Creative Shorts Fellowship (up to USD 25,000 for emerging Native filmmakers, rolling)

    Vision Maker Media · USA. RESTRICTED: emerging Native American filmmakers aged 21+, US citizens or legal residents. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to USD 25,000 production funding, plus USD 5,000 paid separately to the mentor. No application fee shown.

    Vision Maker Media's Creative Shorts Fellowship funds emerging Native filmmakers to make a short film of 40 minutes or less (documentary, drama, experimental or animation), with guidelines inviting work that pushes the limits of art and technology. Each fellow works with a named film-professional mentor, who is paid USD 5,000 separately. ELIGIBILITY: emerging Native American filmmakers aged 21 or over, US citizens or legal residents, with a mentor identified at application; the film must be completed within 12 months. Applications are accepted year-round via Submittable; confirm current status with info@visionmakermedia.org if unsure.

  11. Field of Vision: Documentary Commissions (Rolling)

    Field of Vision · United States and international; filmmakers based anywhere. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Commissioning fees and production funding (many films are commissioned assignments); specific amounts are not published publicly. No submission fee.

    Field of Vision is a filmmaker-driven documentary unit that creates and commissions original short-form and feature nonfiction visual journalism. It funds and commissions filmmakers, often as assignments, and supports projects in development and production (it does not acquire finished films). SUPPORT: commissioning fees and production funding; specific amounts are not published publicly. ELIGIBILITY: filmmakers of all experience levels, within and outside the United States. APPLICATIONS are accepted on a rolling basis, with no fixed funding cycles or deadlines. Submit via the Field of Vision submissions page.
