Funded Fellowships in Europe

Currently 11 active fellowships open to applicants in Europe, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Jane Street Graduate Research Fellowship 2027 (tuition plus USD 50,000 stipend)

    Jane Street · For full-time PhD students at accredited research institutions in the US, Canada, UK, Europe, Australia, New Zealand or Singapore. International students welcome. · Deadline: 13 Nov 2026 · Award: Full tuition and fees for the award year plus a USD 50,000 stipend (or local-currency equivalent; taxable). Finalists receive USD 5,000. No application fee.

    Jane Street's Graduate Research Fellowship supports exceptional doctoral students in electrical engineering and computer science, mathematics, physics or statistics; research does not need to relate to trading. ELIGIBILITY: PhD students with at least one year remaining in their programme, or those committed to start a PhD at a specified university in fall 2027; fellows must be active full-time PhD students at an accredited research institution in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Europe, Australia, New Zealand or Singapore during the award year. International students are eligible and encouraged. WHAT YOU GET: full tuition and fees for the upcoming academic year plus a USD 50,000 stipend (or local-currency equivalent; taxable); finalists receive USD 5,000. APPLICATION: CV, an optional one-to-two-page research statement and recommender contact details. DEADLINES: student applications 13 November 2026 AoE; recommendation letters 1 December 2026; notifications in February 2027. Questions: graduate-research-fellowship@janestreet.com.

  2. ARCAthens: Athens Residency Fellowships (Spring 2027)

    ARCAthens · Athens, Greece (in-person residency). Open to international visual artists and curators residing outside Greece. · Deadline: 14 Nov 2026 · Award: Fully funded: housing, travel, a Fellowship Prize and a weekly stipend (most recent published terms, Fall 2026: USD 3,000 Fellowship Prize and USD 200/week stipend, round-trip airfare reimbursed). No application fee.

    The Athens Residency is the flagship program of ARCAthens, a not-for-profit founded in 2017, offering fully funded Fellowships for visual artists and curators to live and work in Athens. The residency is structured and research-driven, built around arranged studio and private collection visits, institutional access and community events, balanced with time for independent work. Fellows take part in one public engagement event organized by ARCAthens and must stay based in Athens for the full residency. ELIGIBILITY: international curators and visual artists residing outside Greece. SUPPORT: ARCAthens provides housing (private bedroom and bathroom, shared common spaces), travel, a Fellowship Prize and a weekly stipend; Visual Art Fellows get studio access. In the Fall 2026 round this was a USD 3,000 Fellowship Prize plus USD 200 per week. Fellows cover food, local transport and any production or shipping costs; there is no production budget. TIMELINE: applications 16 October to 14 November 2026 (closes 11:00 p.m. ET); notification late November; residency 4 February to 26 April 2027.

  3. DOMA Initiatives x British Council: Venice Biennale Fellowship 2027 (Malaysia, GBP 5,050)

    DOMA Initiatives, in partnership with the British Council · Venice, Italy (British Pavilion, 62nd International Art Exhibition). Only for early-career professionals based in Malaysia. · Deadline: 27 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 5,050 fellowship grant covering travel, accommodation in Venice, visa processing and daily living expenses. No separate artist fee or honorarium beyond this grant. One fellowship place. No application fee mentioned.

    DOMA Initiatives, in partnership with the British Council, nominates one Malaysia-based fellow for the British Council Venice Fellowship Programme at the 2027 Venice Biennale (62nd International Art Exhibition). The fellow spends one month in Venice: half the time as an exhibition ambassador at the British Pavilion (stewarding, engaging visitors, supporting daily gallery operations) and half on independent research or a creative or architectural project inspired by Venice, the Pavilion and the Biennale themes. Fellows complete 15 hours of online training in March-April 2027 and, on return, run a sharing session, workshop or exhibition for DOMA Initiatives. ELIGIBILITY: active early-career professionals in architecture or related creative fields, based in Malaysia, with a portfolio integrating research or creative practice with the themes of La Biennale di Venezia; strong spoken and written English; must also meet the British Council criteria in the Venice Fellowship Programme Guidance Notes 2027. HOW TO APPLY: complete the application form (Google Doc linked from the call page) and email it with a CV and portfolio/research proposal to the selection committee. Shortlisting and interviews 30 November to 3 December 2026; nomination to the British Council 4 December 2026; final selection announced January 2027. Contact: Veronica Ng, DOMA Initiatives Coordinator, veronican@sunway.edu.my. DEADLINE: 27 November 2026, 12:00 noon MYT.

  4. Weizenbaum Institute: Affiliated Fellowship with a research group (Berlin; EUR 3,000 to 5,000/month, deadline 30 Nov 2026)

    Weizenbaum Institute for the Networked Society · Berlin, Germany. In person. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: Monthly allowance of EUR 3,000 (doctoral), EUR 4,000 (postdoc incl. assistant/associate professors) or EUR 5,000 (professors); EUR 2,500 / 3,500 if salary continues. Plus EUR 500/month child allowance and a travel lump sum of EUR 200 (Germany), 500 (Europe) or 1,200 (outside Europe). No application fee.

    Short guest fellowships embedded in Weizenbaum Institute research groups working on platforms, digital society, AI and technology. ELIGIBILITY: researchers from doctoral candidates upward; artists are not mentioned. PROCESS: first contact one or more research groups to confirm a stay is possible; then submit a one-page motivation letter, CV and publication list, the application form, and a project description of up to two pages (research question, methods, work plan, institutional fit, expected output). The research groups select fellows. OFFER: monthly allowance of EUR 3,000 to 5,000 depending on career stage, child allowance and a travel lump sum. Rolling, no fixed deadline. The older Open Fellowship programme ended in 2025.

  5. KHK CURE Fellowships 2027/2028, Saarland University (theme: Things)

    Kaete Hamburger Centre for Cultural Practices of Reparation (CURE), Saarland University · Saarbruecken, Germany. Fellows must live in Saarbruecken during the fellowship and participate actively in centre events; extended research absences are not permitted. Open internationally; retired persons and Saarland University staff are not eligible. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: Up to twelve fellowships. Fellows on unpaid leave from their institution receive approximately EUR 6,100 to 7,200 per month before taxes, depending on qualifications; alternatively, fellows retaining their salary can have their home institution reimbursed for replacement teaching. One round trip to Saarbruecken is reimbursed, a fully equipped individual workspace is provided, and an apartment can be arranged for around EUR 1,250 per month. Eligible accompanying family members may receive additional support. No application fee.

    The Kaete Hamburger Centre for Cultural Practices of Reparation (KHK CURE) at Saarland University invites applications for up to twelve fellowships for its fourth year, October 2027 to September 2028. CURE welcomes scholars from cultural studies, the humanities and the social sciences whose work examines questions of reparation and irreparability; artists holding a doctorate and experienced in academic collaboration may also apply. ANNUAL THEME: 'Things', foregrounding the material dimension of reparation: objects, artefacts and material environments through which practices such as repair, mending and reuse acquire cultural and political significance. Proposals must align with at least one of the Centre's three thematic fields (history, experience or nature) and address the annual theme. CONDITIONS: fellowships run six to twelve months from 1 October 2027; fellows on unpaid leave receive approximately EUR 6,100 to 7,200 per month before taxes depending on qualifications, or the home institution is reimbursed for replacement teaching; one round trip to Saarbruecken is reimbursed under the Saarland Travel Expense Act; a fully equipped individual workspace is provided and an apartment can be arranged for around EUR 1,250 per month; fellows must live in Saarbruecken and take part in the Centre's international, interdisciplinary research community. ELIGIBILITY: a completed PhD (submitted and defended) by the deadline; retired persons and Saarland University staff are not eligible. HOW TO APPLY: via the online form with a one-page motivation letter, CV (max 3 pages), list of publications, and a five-page project description with bibliography, in English, German or French. Notification by 30 April 2027. Deadline 30 November 2026.

  6. Leibniz Summer Fellowships 2027 (ZZF Potsdam, contemporary history)

    Leibniz Centre for Contemporary History Potsdam (ZZF) · Potsdam / Berlin, Germany. Open to international postdoctoral researchers residing outside Germany. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: Two fellowships of EUR 3,000 per month for two months (EUR 6,000 total each), plus office space, computer facilities, specialist library access and participation in ZZF's academic community. No application fee.

    The Leibniz Centre for Contemporary History Potsdam (ZZF) invites applications for two Leibniz Summer Fellowships in German and/or European (including Eastern European) history in the twentieth century. Fellows spend two consecutive months between April and October 2027 in Potsdam or Berlin pursuing independent research while taking part in departmental activities and presenting work in ZZF's research seminar; the institute's five research departments include Contemporary History in the Age of Media and Information Society. GRANT: EUR 3,000 per month for two months, with office space, computer facilities and specialist library resources. ELIGIBILITY: international scholars holding a doctorate and residing outside Germany at the time of application. HOW: send a letter of interest, CV, publication list and a research proposal of up to 1,500 words as a single PDF to fellowships@zzf-potsdam.de. DEADLINE: 30 November 2026.

  7. European Cultural Foundation: ERASMUS 40 Creative Research Fellowship (Florence archives, Brussels presentation)

    European Cultural Foundation (ECF), with the Historical Archives of the European Union (EUI) and the House of European History · Research stay in Florence, Italy (Historical Archives of the EU) in the first trimester of 2027; final work presented in Brussels in September 2027. Open to creative practitioners residing in a Council of Europe member state. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: EUR 10,000 grant covering travel, living expenses and artistic production. One fellowship.

    To mark the 40th anniversary of ERASMUS in 2027, the European Cultural Foundation is opening its historical archives for a Creative Research Fellowship: one professional creative practitioner will research ECF's lesser-known role in shaping the ERASMUS student mobility programme (from the European Institute of Education and the Office for Cooperation in Education to the ERASMUS Bureau, which ran the scheme until 1994) and translate that history into a public-facing artwork about what European belonging feels like and what the next ERASMUS could be. WHAT YOU GET: a EUR 10,000 grant for travel, living costs and production; a research residency at the Historical Archives of the European Union in Florence during the first trimester of 2027; and presentation of the finished work in Brussels in September 2027, in partnership with the House of European History. WHO: creative practitioners residing in a Council of Europe member state with a track record of research-based work for museum contexts. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026, 17:00 CET. The full call is linked from ECF's open calls and social channels; a dedicated call page could not be located at the time of adding.

  8. Mensch Maschine VISIT Residency Fellowship 2027: Energy Ghosts (E-WERK Luckenwalde / Akademie der Künste)

    JUNGE AKADEMIE of the Akademie der Künste, VISIT (E.ON Foundation), E-WERK Luckenwalde and Ujazdowski Castle Centre for Contemporary Art · E-WERK Luckenwalde, Brandenburg, Germany (3 fellows) and Akademie der Künste, Berlin (1 fellow), with a retreat at Ujazdowski Castle CCA in Warsaw, Poland. Open to artists worldwide. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Four fully funded fellowships of EUR 20,000 each, intended for artist fees and production costs of a new project (research and development, travel, materials and presentation costs), plus studio space and accommodation for the three-month residency.

    The Mensch Maschine VISIT Residency Program is an annual fellowship offering four fully funded fellowships to artists from around the world, run by the JUNGE AKADEMIE of the Akademie der Künste (Berlin) with VISIT, the artist-in-residence programme of the E.ON Foundation, E-WERK Luckenwalde and Ujazdowski Castle Centre for Contemporary Art (Warsaw). The 2027 edition particularly encourages projects at the intersection of art, energy and ecology exploring the theme ENERGY GHOSTS: the unseen dimensions of energy approached through mythology, cosmology or speculative fiction, the invisible infrastructures, histories and residues that haunt contemporary life, and energy as an ecological, political or spiritual force. Proposals are welcome from visual art, performance, film, installation, sound, writing and new media. WHAT YOU GET: EUR 20,000 per fellow for artist fees and production costs of a new project (research and development, travel, material and presentation costs); three fellows receive studio space and accommodation at E-WERK Luckenwalde (a former power station about 40 minutes south of Berlin), one is accommodated by the Akademie der Künste in Berlin. The three-month residency runs roughly September to November 2027 with on-site presence of at least 75% required, a retreat at Ujazdowski Castle in Warsaw in September 2027, events across the three venues, and a closing symposium in October 2027 at the Akademie der Künste where fellows show work in progress alongside the 2026 cohort. JURY: Nolan Oswald Dennis; Chus Martinez (Institute Art Gender Nature, TBA21); Sarah Johanna Theurer (Fondation Beyeler); Anh-Linh Ngo (Akademie der Künste); and Tiara Roxanne. APPLICATION: via the VISIT portal at visit-energy.com/bewerben. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026, 23:59 CET.

  9. HIIG Impact AI Fellowship 2027 (EUR 5,000, 10 weeks, Berlin and remote)

    Alexander von Humboldt Institute for Internet and Society (HIIG) · Berlin, Germany (two mandatory in-person weeks; other weeks can be remote). No nationality restriction stated. · Deadline: 15 Jan 2027 · Award: EUR 5,000 fixed stipend per fellow for the ten weeks (inclusive of any tax or social security in the fellow's home country), with case-by-case partial support for in-person costs. No application fee mentioned.

    HIIG's Impact AI Fellowship invites people interested in measuring the effects of AI on society and the environment to assess an existing, operational AI application and take lead authorship of a published case report. Applicants do not need their own case; HIIG helps match fellows with cases, and proposed cases must be deployed applications rather than prototypes or research ideas. WHO: holders of a master's degree; field experience is an advantage but an academic track record is not required. FORMAT: ten weeks from 16 August to 22 October 2027, with mandatory in-person weeks in Berlin (16-20 August and 4-8 October 2027), an independent research phase that can be done remotely, regular online check-ins, and a mandatory online preparatory session on 11 May 2027. WHAT YOU GET: a fixed EUR 5,000 stipend and case-by-case partial support for in-person costs; health insurance, visa and meals are the fellow's responsibility. APPLICATION: online form with one combined file (CV up to 4 pages, motivation letter up to 2 pages, optional project description up to 2 pages). Info sessions on 10 November 2026 (10:00) and 17 November 2026 (15:00). DEADLINE: 15 January 2027.

  10. KB Fellowship at NIAS 2027-2028 (National Library of the Netherlands collections incl. born-digital; artists and researchers)

    NIAS-KNAW (Netherlands Institute for Advanced Study) with the KB, National Library of the Netherlands · Amsterdam (NIAS) with access to the KB in The Hague. Present 4 days a week. · Deadline: 15 Mar 2027 · Award: Personal stipend (figure not published); scholars at Dutch institutions get a Dutch University Grant paid to their institution instead. Office, subsidised Amsterdam housing where eligible (otherwise local transport reimbursement). No application fee.

    One fellowship a year for work with the KB's collections, facilities and expertise, including its large digitised and born-digital holdings. Artists are explicitly eligible, both for artistic production as a research strategy and for research ahead of production, though NIAS provides no studio, materials or tools. ELIGIBILITY: researchers with at least 3 years of post-PhD experience, or creative practitioners with at least 3 years of independent practice since graduating (or equivalent), with working English. OFFER: stipend (or university grant), office at NIAS, subsidised housing where eligible, and KB access. APPLY online between 15 January and 15 March 2027 (12:00 CET), and confirm submission by email within 48 hours, before the deadline. Separate from the KB Researcher-in-Residence programme.

  11. Schloss Wiepersdorf Individual and Collaborative Fellowships 2028 (Brandenburg; EUR 1,200/month plus room and board)

    Schloss Wiepersdorf (state of Brandenburg) · Wiepersdorf, Brandenburg, Germany. In person; fellows spend most of the period on site. · Deadline: 15 Apr 2027 · Award: EUR 1,200 per month stipend, a one-time EUR 480 material grant, room and board, and German visa costs where needed. No application fee.

    Three-month residential fellowships at Schloss Wiepersdorf for artists, writers, composers and scholars from Germany and abroad. Fellows get room, board, a EUR 1,200 monthly stipend and a material grant; fine artists get a studio where space allows. Collaborative fellowships host one or two named partners from any artistic field. ELIGIBILITY: open internationally, no age limit; fields are fine arts, composition, literature, and humanities or social sciences on Romanticism or divided Germany. Applicants need a degree, doctoral candidacy, publications or long professional practice, plus German or English; students below doctoral level and recent fellows are excluded. APPLY via the online form with work samples, open 15 February to 15 April 2027 (23:59 CEST).
