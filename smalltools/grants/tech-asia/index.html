Open calls

  1. i Light Singapore 2027 Open Call: light art installations for Marina Bay (budget up to SGD 18,000 per artwork, theme: Resonance)

    i Light Singapore (Urban Redevelopment Authority, managed by Pico Art International) · Marina Bay, Singapore. For artists and collectives whose country of representation or legal residence is within Asia. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Artwork budget capped at SGD 18,000 per selected installation, covering artist and design fee, production, certification, manpower and (for overseas artists) withholding tax. On top of that the festival covers return sea freight, insurance, venue and approvals, general maintenance and security, and airfare plus accommodation for one person for up to 5 days. No application fee mentioned.

    i Light Singapore, the light festival run by Singapore's Urban Redevelopment Authority since 2010, is commissioning light art installations for its 13th edition around Marina Bay. WHO: artists and collectives whose country of representation or legal residence is in Asia, including architects, lighting, product and industrial designers, artists and multimedia specialists. THEME: Resonance, on how actions create responses and how light, sound and movement can make invisible connections something people can see, hear and feel. Keywords: response, rhythm, connection, collective experience. An artistic interpretation is preferred over a literal one. MONEY: each selected work gets a budget capped at SGD 18,000, which has to cover the artist and design fee, materials and structures, electrical work, light fittings, weatherproofing, safety certification, spare parts, installation and dismantling labour, and withholding tax for overseas artists. The festival separately pays for return sea freight, transit and artwork insurance, venue costs, regulatory approvals, daily maintenance and security, and airfare and accommodation for one person for up to 5 days (4 nights) for installation and the opening. Artists may bring in extra sponsors if they declare them. REQUIREMENTS: footprint of at least 4 m by 4 m; fit for outdoor display in tropical weather for up to a month; robust against public handling; power supply of up to 3 x 16 A provided. Proposals are judged on response to the theme, sustainability, public interactivity, creative use of light, technology and materials, originality, mass appeal, technical feasibility, and production method. Artists keep ownership of the artwork. APPLICATION: online form with a concept description, one proposal board (template provided), proposed location, timeline, a detailed budget in SGD (template provided), technical drawings, and CVs or portfolios. One form per proposal; several proposals are allowed. Only shortlisted artists are notified. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026, 23:59 Singapore time.

  2. n-Space Fellowship, second cohort (Prediction): 18-month research fellowship at Somerset House Studios, London (GBP 10,000 fee plus shared GBP 30,000 R&D fund)

    Somerset House Studios, with UAL Creative Computing Institute, Abandon Normal Devices, Lancaster University and the National Arts Council Singapore; supported by the Rothschild Foundation · Somerset House Studios, London, UK. London-track fellows must be based in London and use the n-Space workspace at least two days a week. A separate track brings one Singapore citizen or permanent resident to London for three months. · Deadline: 07 Dec 2026 · Award: London track: GBP 10,000 fee and access to a GBP 30,000 shared R&D fund, with 24/7 workspace. No travel, relocation or accommodation support. Singapore track: GBP 5,000 fee plus return flights, accommodation, per diems and ground transport, and the same shared fund. Up to GBP 200 towards an access support worker for the application. No application fee mentioned.

    n-Space at Somerset House Studios is a space for experimental, collaborative research across art, technology and society, aimed at outcomes that do not fit traditional institutional platforms. Its second fellowship cohort is convened around PREDICTION. THEME: once the domain of divination and prophecy, prediction now runs through sensing infrastructures, statistical forecasting and AI systems that shape markets, governance and public life, and often produce the futures they anticipate. Research can treat prediction as a subject, a method or a medium. WHO: artists, designers, engineers, lawyers, scientists and technologists who want to work in a collaborative research cohort, across fields from economics and law to computation and the arts. Applicants need five or more years of practice in their current field and may not be in full-time education, PhD programmes included, during the fellowship. At most three of the fellows will be artists. Duos and collectives can apply but split the fee and name up to two representatives. Nationality and visa rules are not stated. COHORT: six fellows, five based in London and one from Singapore. COMMITMENT: London fellows must be based in London, use n-Space as their main base at least two days a week, attend about 80% of sessions, and join fortnightly Friday sessions at UAL's Creative Computing Institute in person (12 April to 18 June and 4 October to 10 December 2027). SUPPORT: GBP 10,000 fee, a GBP 30,000 R&D fund shared by the cohort, 24/7 workspace, access to rehearsal, recording and project spaces and the Makerversity workshops, expert sessions, curatorial support from curator Linda Rocco, and mentorship from Constant Dullaart, Gary Zhexi Zhang, Stephanie Dinkins and Xin Liu. OUTPUT: at least one research output for the final presentation at Somerset House in September 2028. It need not be a finished artwork; a prototype, software, model, writing or proposal all count. SINGAPORE TRACK: with the National Arts Council Singapore, one Singapore citizen or permanent resident with five or more years in art and technology gets a nine-month fellowship, with three months in London (April to June 2027), a GBP 5,000 fee and travel, accommodation and per diems covered; it has its own application form on the call page. APPLICATION: online form linked from the call page. DEADLINE: Monday 7 December 2026, 10:00 UK time, for both tracks.

  3. Sado Island Galaxy Art Festival 2027: Open Call (Japan; production grant up to about JPY 2,000,000)

    Sado International Art Promotion Organization · Sado Island, Niigata, Japan. Festival 17 July to 7 November 2027. · Deadline: 22 Dec 2026 · Award: Production grant of up to about JPY 2,000,000 per project, with artist fees itemised in the budget. JPY 1,500 application fee per proposal.

    About 4 to 5 site-specific projects are selected for an 85-day island art festival on Sado. There are six categories, including one for advanced technology integration, and proposals should engage the island's history, ecology, climate and marine issues. ELIGIBILITY: all nationalities, age 18 and over; individuals and groups. OFFER: production grant up to about JPY 2,000,000 including itemised artist fees; the organiser helps with accommodation, research and construction, while travel and accommodation go in the applicant's budget. APPLY by Google Form with a proposal PDF (max 8 pages), CV/portfolio PDF, signed licence form and proof of the JPY 1,500 fee; round 2 is an interview, lineup announced from March 2027. DEADLINE: 22 December 2026, 17:00 JST.

  4. Awesome Foundation Local Chapters: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants (US, UK, Canada, Australia, NL, Asia)

    Awesome Foundation (local chapters) · City and regional chapters; each funds projects in or benefiting its own area. US, Canada, Australia, UK (Glasgow), Netherlands (Utrecht), Malaysia, Singapore, Armenia, Uzbekistan. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Usually USD 1,000 (or local equivalent) per grant, no strings attached, typically one grant per chapter per month. No application fee.

    The Awesome Foundation is a network of volunteer giving circles: in each chapter about ten trustees pool their own money to give a USD 1,000 no-strings micro-grant, usually monthly, to a project that adds "awesome" to their area: art, public interventions, community tech, events, science and civic ideas. One application on the main site can be sent to the chapter for your location. ACTIVE CHAPTERS (made grants in 2026, checked 9 Nov 2026): US: Ann Arbor, Austin, Baltimore, Cass Clay, Chicago, Cleveland, Detroit, Gainesville, Georgetown MA, Gloucester MA, Hamilton-Wenham MA, Ipswich MA, Los Angeles, Newburgh NY, Newburyport MA, New York City, Oahu, Orlando, Peekskill NY, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Portland OR, Poughkeepsie NY, Rhode Island, Rockport MA, San Antonio, San Francisco, San Jose, Santa Cruz, South Bend, St. Paul, State College PA, Washington DC, Westminster MD (Durham, Miami and Seattle each made one grant early in 2026). Canada: Kingston, Kitchener-Waterloo, Newmarket, Ottawa, Toronto. Australia: Adelaide, Fleurieu, Lake Mac, Lower Hunter, Newcastle, Sydney. UK: Glasgow. Netherlands: Utrecht (one grant in early 2026; Leiden and Rotterdam have been quiet since 2025 and 2024). Asia: Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Tashkent, Yerevan. ELIGIBILITY: set per chapter, generally individuals or groups doing the project in that chapter's area. APPLY: free, short online form. DEADLINE: rolling. Worldwide themed chapters are listed separately (Disability, Conservation and Climate, On the Water).
