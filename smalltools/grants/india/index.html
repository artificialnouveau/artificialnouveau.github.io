Open calls

  1. Alkazi Foundation: Curatorial Research Fellowship 2027 (with CWIT and the V&A)

    Alkazi Foundation for the Arts, with the Charles Wallace India Trust and the Victoria and Albert Museum · New Delhi, India (organiser); the funded research residency takes place at the V&A Photography Section, London. RESTRICTED: Indian citizens domiciled in India, aged 25 to 45, mid-career. · Deadline: 07 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 1,600 monthly stipend for 3 to 6 months, plus a GBP 800 contribution from CWIT towards international return airfare; visa expenses covered by the Alkazi Foundation. No application fee.

    A funded research residency (3rd edition of the Visiting Curatorial Research Fellowship) for mid-career cultural producers from India: artists, scholars, writers/editors and curators pursuing humanities-driven research in the V&A's global photography and visual-culture collections in London. The awardee is announced in February 2027 and the fellowship starts April 2027. SUPPORT: GBP 1,600 per month, GBP 800 towards return airfare (CWIT) and visa costs covered. ELIGIBILITY: Indian citizens domiciled in India, aged 25 to 45, mid-career. APPLY by email to alkazifoundation01@gmail.com with subject 'Curatorial Research Fellowship 2027'. DEADLINE: 7 November 2026.

  2. Rest of World: Labor x Tech Reporting Commissions (Year 3)

    Rest of World, underwritten by the Ford Foundation · Remote. Reporting from anywhere in the world except India; writers based in India are not eligible for this series. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per commissioned story of 800 to 1,000 words. No application fee.

    Rest of World is in the third year of its Labor x Tech program, underwritten by the Ford Foundation, commissioning reported stories on the changing nature of work at a moment of upheaval: AI tools and robotics reshaping professions at every rung of society, from self-driving cars replacing drivers to humanoid robots working alongside nurses; lay-offs across the tech industry; and the gig work model continuing to reshape traditional employment. The programme's premise is that this moment needs documenting now, in stories that help prepare society for what comes next. WHAT THEY COMMISSION: reported stories of 800 to 1,000 words from around the world on this beat, at a fee of USD 1,000 per story. WHAT THEY ARE LOOKING FOR, based on previous years: how new technology is transforming existing industries in unique ways and creating unexpected new pockets of employment; how technology is leading to significant job gain, job loss, and government responses to those trends; whether large demographic shifts are following the adoption of new technology or the arrival of new industries; how workplace technology is changing workplace dynamics; how new industries and tools are changing workers' incomes; and the expansion of the gig work model alongside the threats of automation. EXAMPLES of the kind of story they hope to commission, all previously published by Rest of World: workers in Chinese data factories teaching humanoid robots boring tasks; entry-level tech workers describing the AI-fuelled jobpocalypse; Microsoft and Google claiming their data centres create thousands of jobs while their permit filings say otherwise; AI helping judges close cases quickly and lawyers open them quickly; tech workers fuelling a mini baby boom in Taiwan's Silicon Valley; Dubai's robotaxi ambitions coming for human drivers; and the job brokers who run the lives of the migrant workers staffing Taiwan's chip plants. ELIGIBILITY NOTE: the programme is unable to commission reporting in India or from writers based in India for this series. HOW TO PITCH: send 2 to 3 paragraphs laying out the nutgraf of the proposal and explaining how the pitch fits the editorial mission of the programme. Indicate what sources will be used, whether any pre-reporting has been completed, what reporting has already been done on the topic at other outlets, and what the story contributes to the discourse. Pitches go to laborxtech@restofworld.org. Pitching is open on a rolling basis; no closing date is stated.

  3. BlueDot Impact: Rapid Grants for AI Safety and Biosecurity (up to USD 20,000, rolling)

    BlueDot Impact · Remote (worldwide). Funds cannot be sent to a list of restricted countries, including China, India, Iran and Russia; see the programme page. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to USD 20,000 per grant, paid as a single lump sum after acceptance. No application fee.

    BlueDot Impact's rolling Rapid Grants fund people taking a promising next step in AI safety or biosecurity where money is the bottleneck. Grants of up to USD 20,000 cover time and living costs, research access, compute and tools, testing an idea, meetups, venues and community events, and conference, collaboration and fieldwork travel. The application takes about 15 minutes (plan and budget), and accepted grants are paid as a single lump sum through a claims portal. ELIGIBILITY: open to anyone, though BlueDot course participants, alumni, facilitators and active members of the AI safety and biosecurity community are more likely to be funded. Funds cannot go to Afghanistan, Belarus, Burundi, Central African Republic, Chad, China, Congo Republic, Cuba, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Eritrea, India, Iraq, Iran, Libya, Myanmar, North Korea, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Russia, Venezuela and Yemen. Requests above USD 20,000 belong in BlueDot's Career Transition Grants (individuals) or Seed Grants (new projects). Rolling applications, no deadline.

  4. Experimenter: Generator Cooperative Art Production Fund (rolling)

    Experimenter, with its cooperative of artists and supporters · Kolkata, India. Open to all visual artists worldwide, individuals or collectives; no age, nationality or medium restrictions. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Production bursaries of up to INR 150,000 (1.5 lakhs); one bursary per artist per year, funds disbursed within 30 days of selection. No application fee.

    Production fund run by Kolkata gallery Experimenter with a cooperative of artists and supporters, juried by a rotating panel of artists and curators. It explicitly supports film, video, sound, digital and new media art, artistic research, installation, performance and public art among other practices. GRANT: up to INR 1.5 lakhs (INR 150,000) toward realising a project, one bursary per artist per year, disbursed within 30 days of selection. ELIGIBILITY: open to all visual artists worldwide, individuals or collectives, no age, nationality or medium restrictions; applications preferred in English but vernacular and foreign languages accepted. ROLLING: applications may be submitted at any time and are reviewed when the next round is announced. Apply via the Google Form linked from the Generator page; contact generator@experimenter.in.

  5. BlueDot Impact: Career Transition Grant (AI safety and biosecurity, rolling)

    BlueDot Impact (funded mainly by Coefficient Giving) · Remote (worldwide). Funds cannot be sent to a list of sanctioned or restricted countries, including China, India, Iran and Russia; see the programme page. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 20,000 to 200,000 per grant (requests under USD 20,000 go to BlueDot Rapid Grants). Amount and duration agreed around the applicant's plan. No application fee.

    Fellowship grants for a defined period of full-time work transitioning into AI safety or biosecurity: research, policy or technical output, building evidence, testing an uncertain career path, closing a skill gap, or moving into a role. Grantees also get structured advising, introductions and a peer community. BlueDot has taken over Coefficient Giving's former career development and transition funding. ELIGIBILITY: ready to work full-time on the transition (not alongside a job); evidence of recent relevant action (strong analogous work counts); a plausible plan with milestones and a theory of change linked to reducing catastrophic risk from advanced AI or biological threats. No prior BlueDot course needed. Master's and PhD programmes are generally not funded. PROCESS: a roughly 45-minute form, then routing, evidence review, interview and decision; grantees send weekly updates and a short end-of-grant report.

  6. BlueDot Impact: Seed Grants for new AI safety and biosecurity projects (USD 20,000 to 200,000, rolling)

    BlueDot Impact · Remote (worldwide). Funds cannot be sent to a list of restricted countries, including China, India, Iran and Russia; see the programme page. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 20,000 to 200,000 for 6 to 12 months (requests under USD 20,000 go to BlueDot Rapid Grants). No application fee.

    BlueDot Impact's Seed Grants fund the first 6 to 12 months of a new AI safety or biosecurity project or organisation, covering founders' living costs, compute, tools and running costs, and travel. WHAT YOU GET: USD 20,000 to 200,000 (smaller requests go to Rapid Grants). ELIGIBILITY: anyone can apply; no BlueDot course is needed and the project does not have to match BlueDot's stated missions. Solo founders are welcome. Grants are currently paid to individual founders; if the money needs to go to an organisation, say so in the application. For-profits are considered in some cases where the work clearly serves the public good. Funds cannot go to Afghanistan, Belarus, Burundi, Central African Republic, Chad, China, Congo Republic, Cuba, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Eritrea, India, Iraq, Iran, Libya, Myanmar, North Korea, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Russia, Venezuela and Yemen. PROCESS: the form takes about 45 minutes (choose 'Starting an organization'); BlueDot aims to decide within about four weeks. Grantees send short monthly updates and a final report, and return unspent funds if they stop. Rolling, no deadline.
