Grants, Fellowships and Residencies in Canada

Currently 12 active paid grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in Canada, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Ottawa School of Art: Call for Exhibition Proposals 2027-2028

    Ottawa School of Art (OSA) · Ottawa, Ontario, Canada. Two venues: the J.W. Stellick Gallery at the ByWard Market campus, and the OSA Orleans Gallery on the Centrum Level of the Shenkman Arts Centre. · Deadline: 04 Sep 2026 · Award: Artists receive an Ontario CARFAC exhibition fee (the amount is not stated in the call and follows the CARFAC schedule). No application fee.

    The Ottawa School of Art invites artists and collectives to submit exhibition proposals for its 2027-2028 season, welcoming current and proposed work across all media including painting, sculpture, photography, installation and video. Group exhibition proposals are accepted. Successful applicants exhibit at ONE campus gallery, but proposals may be submitted for either or both venues; an application to both must demonstrate a clear understanding of how the exhibition would work best within each gallery's dimensions (floor plans are published with the call). VENUES: the J.W. Stellick Gallery at the ByWard Market campus, dedicated to giving OSA students and the community a wide variety of contemporary art, and the OSA Orleans Gallery on the Centrum Level of the Shenkman Arts Centre. Artists receive an Ontario CARFAC exhibition fee. SELECTION: a jury of three art professionals selects exhibitions on the quality of the work, the integrity of the proposal, and its relation to OSA's programs. SUBMISSION: all six components are required, incomplete proposals are withdrawn without notification, and poorly organised proposals will not be reviewed. (1) Completed application cover form with contact information and exhibition details. (2) A written exhibition proposal outlining the concept and intent of the proposed or current body of work. (3) One artist statement per proposal; group proposals submit a single collective statement, not individual statements. (4) An up-to-date CV for the exhibiting artist, or a CV for each participating artist in a group proposal. (5) Up to 10 visual files of work completed within the last two years. An artwork and a detail of the same artwork count as two images; images must be JPG only, 300 dpi, no larger than 2MB each, RGB, maximum 1240 pixels on the longest side. Each video counts as one file, total video time per proposal must not exceed 15 minutes, and videos must be AVI, MOV or MP4 and no larger than 500MB each. Precede each file name with a zero-padded number and the title, for example 01Artwork title. (6) A corresponding numbered visual file list with title, year, medium and dimensions (H x W x D), specifying inches or centimetres. FILES MUST BE SENT VIA WETRANSFER to boutique@artottawa.ca; proposals submitted by post, hand delivery or email will not be accepted. Deadline 4:30 pm ET, Friday 4 September 2026.

  2. New_ Public Entrepreneur in Residence, Pilot Cohort

    New_ Public · Remote, with an in-person kickoff retreat in Philadelphia, USA (19 to 22 October 2026). Open to applicants based in the United States or Canada. · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 22,500, paid in three monthly installments across the residency, each contingent on continued active participation. Plus USD 1,500 toward an AI tool of the resident's choice for coding, prototyping or design. Two residents are selected in the pilot cohort. Also provided: hands-on product and strategy support from senior New_ Public staff and outside experts; access to New_ Public's five years of original research on moderation, governance, community stewardship and platform design, plus direct access to the researchers behind it; and introductions across New_ Public's network of major platforms, startups, university researchers and funders for hiring, fundraising, early user testing and community partnerships. Residents must submit a Form W-9 or W-8BEN to receive the stipend. No application fee.

    New_ Public is piloting a virtual Entrepreneur in Residence programme for solo founders building alternatives to the mainstream social platforms. Its framing: people are turning away from algorithmic social media, AI bots are taking over the big platforms, and users are retreating into private closed spaces, which leaves an opening for builders working on a new information ecosystem rather than the status quo. The residency supplies research, experts, connections and tools; the founder brings the vision and experience. TWO FOCUS AREAS: humans connecting to humans, meaning products that bring people into meaningful contact online (forums, messaging apps, groups, microblogging, in-person meetups), including AI-powered platforms and tools as long as they connect people to people rather than people to bots; and local and civic information environments, meaning tools for distributing local news, building community knowledge and facilitating collective sensemaking, so people can get trustworthy information about their communities. WHAT RESIDENTS DO: use self-directed time to prototype ideas, test assumptions and gain clarity; participate in regular check-ins, engage with the other resident and the wider team, and take candid feedback on product and strategy; engage seriously with go-to-market questions about who the user is, how they find you, and the path to sustainability; and help shape the programme itself, since the pilot cohort's feedback directly informs how the EIR programme runs going forward. WHO THEY WANT: entrepreneurial founders ready to build their next thing within the focus areas, with a track record of or clear drive toward work that builds social trust, connection and belonging; strong product thinkers with good judgment, a relentless focus on user needs, and openness to coaching on mission-aligned business models. An early idea is enough, no working product required. The pilot is open to founders with something else going on alongside, though the full programme will most likely be full time. APPLICATION PROCESS: submit the written application online; shortlisted applicants submit a short video by 18 September 2026 covering background, motivation and a quick pitch; full applications and videos go to the advisory board, with offers made in early October ahead of the kickoff. FUTURE COHORTS: New_ Public plans to open the next cohort to the UK and EU from spring 2027. Deadline 11:59pm Pacific on Wednesday 9 September 2026.

  3. Call for Proposals: Activate Yonge + St. Clair During DesignTO Festival 2027 (Toronto)

    DesignTO · Yonge + St. Clair, Toronto, Canada. Preference given to artists based in Toronto and the Greater Toronto Area. · Deadline: 13 Sep 2026 · Award: Between $5,000 and $7,500 CAD per selected project, inclusive of artist fees, materials, fabrication, transportation, installation, and de-installation. Free to apply.

    Artists, architects, designers, fabricators, and multidisciplinary creatives are invited to submit proposals for a series of site-specific installations at Yonge + St. Clair as part of DesignTO Festival 2027. For more than seven years the Yonge + St. Clair BIA has partnered with DesignTO to transform publicly accessible spaces throughout the neighbourhood into an exhibition of art, design, and placemaking. For the 2027 Festival, SEVEN installation opportunities are available across lobbies, galleries, and window spaces: 2 St. Clair Ave W (Lobby), 2 St. Clair Ave E (Lower Level Window), 30 St. Clair Ave W (Lobby), 40 St. Clair Ave W (Lobby), 55 St. Clair Ave W (Lobby), 95 St. Clair Ave W (Gallery), and 1521 Yonge St (Window). One installation will be commissioned per location. THEME: 'Intersections' - proposals may explore convergence, transformation, movement, identity, exchange, and the evolving relationship between people and place. Artists may submit proposals for multiple locations, but each proposal must be tailored to its site and submitted separately; only one proposal per artist or team may be selected. WHO SHOULD APPLY: emerging and established artists, designers, architects, fabricators, and multidisciplinary creative practitioners working in any medium; preference given to artists based in Toronto and the Greater Toronto Area. BUDGET: selected projects receive $5,000 to $7,500 CAD inclusive of artist fees, materials, fabrication, transportation, installation, and de-installation. Artists are responsible for the fabrication, transportation, installation, and de-installation of their work. KEY DATES: submission deadline 13 September 2026 at 5:00pm ET; selected artists notified late September 2026; final project information due October 2026; installation period 14 to 21 January 2027; DesignTO Festival exhibition 22 to 31 January 2027; de-installation 1 to 2 February 2027. HOW TO APPLY: submit a single PDF (artist information and biography, project description and executive summary, visual materials, detailed line-item budget, and current resume) via email or Dropbox link to info@yongestclair.ca with the subject line 'DesignTO Festival 2027 - Installation Name - Artist Name'. Free to apply.

  4. Guggenheim Fellowship 2027

    John Simon Guggenheim Memorial Foundation · Anywhere; the fellowship funds the fellow's own work rather than residence at a host institution. Open to citizens and permanent residents of the United States and Canada. · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: Variable cash grant sized to the applicant's proposed budget and needs; the Foundation does not publish a fixed figure or range, and recent awards have typically run into the tens of thousands of US dollars. Roughly 175 to 200 fellowships are awarded each year across more than 50 fields. The Foundation charges no application fee and has no affiliated application consultants.

    The Guggenheim Fellowship is awarded to mid-career individuals who have already demonstrated exceptional capacity for productive scholarship or exceptional creative ability in the arts, giving them six to twelve months of funded time to work with as much creative freedom as possible. Around 175 to 200 fellows are chosen each year from a field of several thousand applicants (a success rate of roughly five percent), across more than 50 fields spanning the creative arts, the humanities, the natural sciences and the social sciences, including architecture, film, music, photography, fine arts, computer science and interdisciplinary work. ELIGIBILITY: citizens and permanent residents of the United States and Canada; applicants must be mid-career with a significant record of published or exhibited work, and current students and previous Guggenheim Fellows are not eligible. APPLICATION: through the Foundation's own portal, which opens in late summer 2026, so check the site if it is not yet live. The application deadline is 15 September 2026 with no late submissions accepted; work samples are due 29 September 2026 and reference letters 13 October 2026. Results are announced in late April 2027. No fee.

  6. Al Purdy A-frame Association: Al and Eurithe Purdy Residency 2027

    The Al Purdy A-frame Association · Ameliasburgh, Prince Edward County, Ontario, Canada. Canadian citizens or permanent residents only. · Deadline: 18 Sep 2026 · Award: CAD 750 per week stipend plus paid return travel to Ameliasburgh (funding subject to the Association's fundraising and Canada Council matching). Terms of 3 to 12 weeks. No application fee.

    The Al Purdy A-frame Residency offers Canadian writers time and space to work at the historic A-frame home built by poet Al Purdy and his wife Eurithe on Roblin Lake in Ameliasburgh, Prince Edward County, Ontario. Poetry is prioritized, but all writers are welcome. SUPPORT: CAD 750 per week stipend and paid return travel (subject to the Association's fundraising and Canada Council matching); residencies run 3 to 12 weeks. ELIGIBILITY: Canadian citizens or permanent residents only. DEADLINE: 18 September 2026 (postmark, or 4:00pm PT for electronic submissions). Apply via the residency page.

  7. Artists in the Library 2027 (Toronto Arts Council)

    Toronto Arts Council, in partnership with Toronto Public Library · Toronto, Canada. Programming takes place at a Toronto Public Library branch outside the downtown core, in Etobicoke York, North York, Toronto and East York, or Scarborough. Applicants must be City of Toronto residents. · Deadline: 06 Oct 2026 · Award: Maximum grant of CAD 20,000, which may cover up to 100 percent of project costs. Payment of professional artist fees is a requirement of the programme. Recipients also get access to space and facilities at the assigned library branch for arts programming and presentation, and support from the branch team. Funded by the City of Toronto. No application fee is mentioned.

    Created in 2014 as a partnership between Toronto Arts Council and Toronto Public Library, Artists in the Library gives Toronto artists paid opportunities and space while widening access to the arts in Etobicoke, York, North York, East York and Scarborough. It funds professional artists, arts organisations and collectives in any artistic discipline to create and deliver free community-engaged arts programming at a Toronto Public Library branch. TAC encourages applications from its equity priority groups and from artists working in their local communities. WHAT RECIPIENTS GET OUT OF IT: a chance to explore their practice in a community setting, access to branch space and facilities, meaningful engagement with the branch and its community, close support from the branch team, experience navigating a multi-stakeholder environment, and the delivery of a community-engaged arts project from start to finish. WHAT COUNTS: community engagement is required but its format can vary, covering exhibitions, installations, performances, concerts, screenings, readings, filmmaking, storytelling, workshops, artist talks, participatory programmes, arts education and mentorship. Permanent exhibitions and installations are not eligible. Projects are produced by the recipient, hosted and supported by the assigned branch, and must be delivered on site within branch operating hours. SCALE: because host slots at TPL branches are limited, this is not for one-off workshops or small-scale events; the offering must be substantial with a budget to match. LOCATION: there is no list of eligible branches for 2026. Applicants indicate a preferred area or neighbourhood outside the downtown core and TPL staff assign the branch based on suitability and availability, so a different branch than the one requested may be assigned. For Toronto and East York, downtown is excluded, defined as south of St Clair, east of Jane and west of the Don Valley. Only one branch may be proposed. ELIGIBILITY: individuals must be Canadian citizens, permanent residents, have a pending PR application, or be a protected person, must hold a Social Insurance Number, and must have lived in the City of Toronto for at least one year before the deadline and live and work there at least eight months a year (no PO boxes). Artists in graduate study may not apply for work related to their programme without a letter from the programme director. Organisations must be incorporated non-profit arts organisations, and collectives must operate on a non-profit basis, be located in Toronto, and have both members (for a pair) or a majority of members resident in Toronto; successful collectives must supply bank account details in the collective's name. IMPORTANT: proposals must align with TPL's programming policy, and proposals that TPL staff judge unsuitable for a branch or impossible in its context will not be funded, so read the 'Working With Toronto Public Library' section of the guidelines. First-time applicants are encouraged to consult the programme manager and to attend the Zoom office hours on eligibility and project fit (17 September) and on budgets (29 September). Contact: Lena Golubtsova, lena@torontoartscouncil.org. Deadline 6 October 2026.

  8. Canada Council for the Arts: Arts Across Canada and Abroad, Circulation and Touring (October 2026 round)

    Canada Council for the Arts · Touring and circulation within Canada and internationally. · Deadline: 07 Oct 2026 · Award: CAD. Minimum and maximum are NOT published publicly; amounts are disclosed only inside the Council's client portal at arts-services.ca, which requires a login. No application fee. Council funds cannot cover expenses already covered by another revenue source, and combined municipal, provincial and federal grants must demonstrate complementarity of public support.

    Funds the circulation and touring of artists, exhibitions and artistic works by artists, groups and organisations from Canada, nationally and internationally. Media arts, digital arts and new media, inter-arts, and music and sound art are all explicitly named as supported practices, so experimental sound and creative technology work is in scope. This component sits inside Arts Across Canada and Abroad, the merged programme introduced in 2025 that replaced the separate Arts Across Canada and Arts Abroad programmes; the old Digital Now programme is discontinued. IMPORTANT AND UNDERUSED: non-Canadian organisations are eligible via the Foreign arts festival, biennale or book fair profile, defined as an incorporated organisation headquartered outside Canada running an international media arts festival, an international literary or storytelling festival, an international book fair, or a major international visual arts biennial, triennial or quinquennial. That support is restricted to presenting Canadian media, literary or visual artists and their work, and it is not advertised on the programme page. For digital and media arts, requests must be for work independent of the commercial film, television and video game industries, and only the creator or director may apply, not producers or production companies. Deadlines run three times a year. The sister component Representation and Promotion has continuous rolling intake and the same foreign-festival eligibility.

  9. Canada Council for the Arts: Arts Across Canada and Abroad, Public Outreach (October 2026 round)

    Canada Council for the Arts · Activities must build audiences and public appreciation of the arts in Canada. Organisations must be incorporated or registered in Canada. · Deadline: 14 Oct 2026 · Award: CAD. Minimum and maximum are NOT published publicly; disclosed only in the client portal at arts-services.ca, which requires a login. No application fee. Council funds cannot cover expenses already covered by another revenue source; combined public grants must demonstrate complementarity.

    Funds activities that build connections between artists and diverse audiences and contribute to public appreciation and enjoyment of the arts in Canada. This is the closest Canada Council fit for running a public programme, a single festival edition, a talks series or audience-facing exhibition programming, as distinct from creating the artwork itself. It is open to arts festivals, presenters and event organisers under the Presenting organization profile, alongside Artistic organizations, Artistic groups and Arts supporting groups, and the profile explicitly includes visual arts festivals, biennials, triennials and quinquennials. Digital arts and new media, media arts, inter-arts, and music and sound art are named among funded practices, so experimental sound art and creative technology programming qualify. IMPORTANT: unlike the Circulation and Touring component, international organisations are NOT eligible for Public Outreach. The Council will not fund projects whose primary focus is educational, therapeutic, recreational or social service delivery, nor fundraising activities or prize and award ceremonies. Deadlines fall twice a year. The related Composite Activities component offers up to 3 years of support for bundled multi-project programmes and may suit a recurring festival arc better.

  11. SAT: Research-Creation Residency Program in Arts and Technologies 2026-28 (Montreal)

    Societe des arts technologiques (SAT) · Societe des arts technologiques, 1201 Boulevard Saint-Laurent, Montreal, QC, Canada. Open to professional artists and collectives from anywhere in the world; no nationality restriction stated. · Deadline: 18 Oct 2026 · Award: CAD 10,000 grant per selected project, for individual artists and collectives alike. Also included: access to SAT workspaces during the residency periods, access to equipment and software resources subject to availability, mentoring and training activities (on site and remote), creative and technical support from the SAT teams, and documentation and archiving of the work. Travel, meals and accommodation beyond the grant are NOT covered. No application fee.

    The Societe des arts technologiques (SAT) in Montreal runs a research-creation residency program in arts and technologies for artists and collectives working in the performing arts and/or media arts. The residency funds a research-creation stage of a project in technological arts rather than the finalisation of a finished work, so the call weighs the exploration process and the innovation in it above a polished outcome. Each selected project receives a CAD 10,000 grant, whether the applicant is an individual artist or a collective, plus access to SAT spaces and equipment, technical and creative support from the SAT teams, mentoring and training activities, and documentation and archiving. Access to spaces and equipment is assessed against the project's needs and granted subject to availability. Travel, meal and accommodation costs beyond the grant are not covered. Open to professional artists and artistic collectives worldwide, at any level of experience and from any primary discipline, provided the profile and project suit immersive technologies; collectives must submit a joint portfolio showing they can carry out the project independently, particularly the visual and sound creation inside a dome. Each application must include a form and supporting documents setting out the concept, the technical solutions considered, and what will be explored during the residency. One application per project and per person; incomplete applications are not evaluated; use permanent, downloadable links rather than WeTransfer or similar temporary services. Accommodations and alternative support documents (large print and so on) are available on request at residences@sat.qc.ca. Deadline 18 October 2026. Apply through the Google Form linked from the SAT open-call page.

  12. Anthropic Fellows Program (funded AI research fellowship, January 2027 cohort)

    Anthropic (with Constellation as recruiting partner) · San Francisco, London, or Ontario, with remote-friendly options; applicants must hold work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Shared workspaces available in Berkeley and London. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,850 / GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300 per week for four months of full-time research, plus roughly USD 15,000 per month in compute funding and other research expenses. No application fee.

    The Anthropic Fellows Program is a four-month, fully funded research fellowship with direct mentorship from Anthropic researchers. Fellows work on empirical projects aligned with Anthropic's research priorities using external infrastructure, aiming to produce public outputs such as papers. Five workstreams: AI Safety, AI Security, ML Systems and Performance, Reinforcement Learning, and Economics and Policy; safety topics include scalable oversight, adversarial robustness, mechanistic interpretability, and model organisms. FUNDING: a weekly stipend of USD 3,850 (GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300), around USD 15,000 per month in compute funding, and other research expenses. ELIGIBILITY: fluent Python; available full-time for four months; strong technical background in computer science, mathematics, or physics; work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Locations: San Francisco, London, Ontario, or remote within those countries. APPLICATION: rolling, via the Constellation portal; the process includes an initial application, reference checks, technical assessments, interviews, and a research discussion. The next cohort is expected to start in January 2027, so apply well before the end of 2026.
