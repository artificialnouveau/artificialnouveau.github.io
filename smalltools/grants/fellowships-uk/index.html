  1. Tarbell Fellowship 2027-28 (AI journalism)

    Tarbell Center for AI Journalism · Remote training, then newsroom placement in the US, UK or Hong Kong. In person. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 75,000 to 120,000 depending on experience and placement cost of living; senior fellows (7+ years experience) USD 115,000 to 135,000. Summit travel and accommodation covered. No application fee.

    A year-long fellowship for journalists covering artificial intelligence. Fellows complete a three-month full-time remote programme on AI and journalism fundamentals, attend a weeklong summit, then work for nine months as reporters embedded in a partner newsroom (current partners are in Hong Kong, London, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle, Washington DC and Cambridge). ELIGIBILITY: journalism or AI expertise, aged 18+ by 7 June 2027, no degree required; primarily for applicants with the right to work in the US, UK or Hong Kong, as visa sponsorship is uncommon and needs partner-outlet approval.

  2. GovAI Summer Fellowship 2027 (AI governance, London or Washington DC, GBP 14,000 for 3 months)

    Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI) · London, UK or Washington, DC, USA; in person, full time. Open worldwide; temporary work visa sponsorship available. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 14,000 for 3 months plus travel support (UK Research and Applied Tracks, London); USD 24,500 plus travel support for the Washington DC track. No application fee.

    GovAI's Summer Fellowship brings early-career researchers and practitioners into AI governance for three months of full-time work on a research or applied project under the mentorship of a GovAI researcher. TRACKS: UK Research Track and UK Applied Track in London, plus a Washington DC track, each with its own application page. TOPICS include risk management, economics and geopolitics of AI, public policy and technical governance. ELIGIBILITY: open to applicants worldwide; must be able to work in person full time from 7 June to 27 August 2027; GovAI can sponsor temporary work visas for the UK tracks, but the DC track requires existing right to work in the US. DEADLINE: 22 November 2026, 23:59 GMT for the UK tracks (11:59 pm EST for DC).

  3. n-Space Fellowship, second cohort (Prediction): 18-month research fellowship at Somerset House Studios, London (GBP 10,000 fee plus shared GBP 30,000 R&D fund)

    Somerset House Studios, with UAL Creative Computing Institute, Abandon Normal Devices, Lancaster University and the National Arts Council Singapore; supported by the Rothschild Foundation · Somerset House Studios, London, UK. London-track fellows must be based in London and use the n-Space workspace at least two days a week. A separate track brings one Singapore citizen or permanent resident to London for three months. · Deadline: 07 Dec 2026 · Award: London track: GBP 10,000 fee and access to a GBP 30,000 shared R&D fund, with 24/7 workspace. No travel, relocation or accommodation support. Singapore track: GBP 5,000 fee plus return flights, accommodation, per diems and ground transport, and the same shared fund. Up to GBP 200 towards an access support worker for the application. No application fee mentioned.

    n-Space at Somerset House Studios is a space for experimental, collaborative research across art, technology and society, aimed at outcomes that do not fit traditional institutional platforms. Its second fellowship cohort is convened around PREDICTION. THEME: once the domain of divination and prophecy, prediction now runs through sensing infrastructures, statistical forecasting and AI systems that shape markets, governance and public life, and often produce the futures they anticipate. Research can treat prediction as a subject, a method or a medium. WHO: artists, designers, engineers, lawyers, scientists and technologists who want to work in a collaborative research cohort, across fields from economics and law to computation and the arts. Applicants need five or more years of practice in their current field and may not be in full-time education, PhD programmes included, during the fellowship. At most three of the fellows will be artists. Duos and collectives can apply but split the fee and name up to two representatives. Nationality and visa rules are not stated. COHORT: six fellows, five based in London and one from Singapore. COMMITMENT: London fellows must be based in London, use n-Space as their main base at least two days a week, attend about 80% of sessions, and join fortnightly Friday sessions at UAL's Creative Computing Institute in person (12 April to 18 June and 4 October to 10 December 2027). SUPPORT: GBP 10,000 fee, a GBP 30,000 R&D fund shared by the cohort, 24/7 workspace, access to rehearsal, recording and project spaces and the Makerversity workshops, expert sessions, curatorial support from curator Linda Rocco, and mentorship from Constant Dullaart, Gary Zhexi Zhang, Stephanie Dinkins and Xin Liu. OUTPUT: at least one research output for the final presentation at Somerset House in September 2028. It need not be a finished artwork; a prototype, software, model, writing or proposal all count. SINGAPORE TRACK: with the National Arts Council Singapore, one Singapore citizen or permanent resident with five or more years in art and technology gets a nine-month fellowship, with three months in London (April to June 2027), a GBP 5,000 fee and travel, accommodation and per diems covered; it has its own call page and application form, listed here as a separate entry. APPLICATION: online form linked from the call page. DEADLINE: Monday 7 December 2026, 10:00 UK time, for both tracks.

    Somerset House Studios with the National Arts Council Singapore; research partner UAL Creative Computing Institute; supported by the Rothschild Foundation · Hybrid. In person at Somerset House Studios, London, UK from April to June 2027 and for the final presentation in March 2028; online from July 2027 to February 2028. Open to Singapore citizens and permanent residents. · Deadline: 07 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 5,000 fellowship fee, plus return flights to London, accommodation, per diems and ground transport for both London visits, and access to a GBP 30,000 R&D fund shared by the six-person cohort. No application fee mentioned.

    The Singapore place in the second n-Space fellowship cohort at Somerset House Studios, run with the National Arts Council Singapore. n-Space is a space for experimental, collaborative research across art, technology and society; this cohort of six (five London-based fellows plus the Singapore fellow) is convened around PREDICTION as a line of enquiry, from forecasting and sensing infrastructures to AI systems. WHO: Singapore citizens and permanent residents with five or more years of practice in art and technology, artists and technologists alike. Applicants may not be in full-time education, PhD programmes included, during the fellowship. Collectives can apply but appoint one representative. COMMITMENT: be in London from April to June 2027, attend all fellowship sessions and one-to-one meetings, including the Friday sessions at UAL's Creative Computing Institute, then continue online (on UK time, in English) until an in-person public presentation at Somerset House in March 2028. Outcomes are also shared with the Singapore arts community in a format agreed with NAC. SUPPORT: GBP 5,000 fee; flights, accommodation, per diems, airport transfers and a travel card for the London periods; access to the cohort's shared GBP 30,000 R&D fund; 24/7 access to the n-Space workspace, rehearsal and recording studios, project spaces and the Makerversity workshops; mentorship from Constant Dullaart, Gary Zhexi Zhang and Xin Liu and curatorial support from Linda Rocco. OUTPUT: not necessarily a finished artwork; a prototype, software, writing or research proposal all count. APPLICATION: FormSG form linked from the call page; audio or video applications are accepted as access support. Queries to harry.leek@somersethouse.org.uk or chrystal_ho@nac.gov.sg. DEADLINE: Monday 7 December 2026, 10:00 UK time; applicants hear back in February 2027. London-based applicants should use the separate London track, listed on its own.

  5. AIXI Labs: Research Fellowship (rolling, quarterly rounds)

    AIXI Labs · Fully remote, open worldwide; fellows may also work from the organisation's London base in the United Kingdom. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 2,000 to GBP 3,500 per month stipend, plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. Free to apply.

    AIXI Labs is a small London-based non-profit doing AI safety research grounded in algorithmic information theory. Its stated position is that most AI research today is either mathematically clean but narrow, or practical but too opaque for rigorous safety analysis; the lab targets a third category, methods general enough to describe powerful agents and provable enough to support real safety claims, using AIXI (the theoretical model of unbounded artificial superintelligence) and then porting the strongest of those ideas to modern LLM-based agents. The fellowship is a quarterly, rolling programme for researchers who want to work on this agenda under close mentorship from the core team: Cole Wyeth (Founder and Executive Director), Marcus Hutter (Research Director) and Aram Ebtekar (Founding Research Scientist). Fellows pursue a focused research project over roughly a quarter and receive a monthly stipend plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. The fellowship is fully remote, so fellows may work from anywhere in the world or from the London base. RESPONSIBILITIES: pursue a self-directed research project aligned with the lab's algorithmic-information-theoretic approach to AI safety, spanning anything from foundational theory to empirical tests with LLM-based agents; shape the project direction around your own strengths and interests, as the lab supports a range of work within this framework; and contribute papers, blog posts or other written output communicating results to the broader AI safety and ML research communities. MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS: a research background at the level of a current PhD student (for primarily empirical projects, a master's-level command of the relevant mathematics paired with substantial industry or applied research experience can also qualify); solid grounding in probability, statistics, information theory and reinforcement learning, sufficient to engage seriously with AIXI-style theoretical models (prior expertise in algorithmic information theory or AIXI is NOT required); strong verbal and written communication skills; genuine motivation to reduce existential risk from advanced AI and enthusiasm for the lab's kind of work. PREFERRED QUALIFICATIONS: publications at top ML, AI or theory venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ALT or COLT, though the lab values the strength of your best work over volume; background in algorithmic information theory, computability theory, learning theory or related theoretical computer science; experience fine-tuning, evaluating or red-teaming LLM-based and/or RL agents; prior engagement with the UAI/AIXI community or related fields, including members of the lab's online reading groups. APPLICATIONS are reviewed on a rolling basis and previous applicants remain eligible for later rounds.

  6. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.

  7. Anthropic Fellows Program (funded AI research fellowship, January 2027 cohort)

    Anthropic (with Constellation as recruiting partner) · San Francisco, London, or Ontario, with remote-friendly options; applicants must hold work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Shared workspaces available in Berkeley and London. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,850 / GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300 per week for four months of full-time research, plus roughly USD 15,000 per month in compute funding and other research expenses. No application fee.

    The Anthropic Fellows Program is a four-month, fully funded research fellowship with direct mentorship from Anthropic researchers. Fellows work on empirical projects aligned with Anthropic's research priorities using external infrastructure, aiming to produce public outputs such as papers. Five workstreams: AI Safety, AI Security, ML Systems and Performance, Reinforcement Learning, and Economics and Policy; safety topics include scalable oversight, adversarial robustness, mechanistic interpretability, and model organisms. FUNDING: a weekly stipend of USD 3,850 (GBP 2,310 / CAD 4,300), around USD 15,000 per month in compute funding, and other research expenses. ELIGIBILITY: fluent Python; available full-time for four months; strong technical background in computer science, mathematics, or physics; work authorization in the US, UK, or Canada (no visa sponsorship). Locations: San Francisco, London, Ontario, or remote within those countries. APPLICATION: rolling, via the Constellation portal; the process includes an initial application, reference checks, technical assessments, interviews, and a research discussion. The next cohort is expected to start in January 2027, so apply well before the end of 2026.

  8. Iliad Fellowship (applied mathematics for AI alignment, rolling)

    Iliad · London or San Francisco Bay Area. In person. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 6,000 per month travel and housing allowance for the three-month fellowship (four months fully funded including the Iliad Intensive). No application fee.

    Mentored, individual research in applied mathematics for AI alignment, including foundational questions that get less attention elsewhere, drawing on mathematics, theoretical physics and computer science. ELIGIBILITY: designed for PhD students, postdocs and researchers with equivalent experience in mathematics, theoretical physics, computer science or related fields; selection weighs academic credentials, technical ability, intentions and fit with the cohort's research coverage. APPLICATION: rolling, with fellowships starting at the beginning of most months.
