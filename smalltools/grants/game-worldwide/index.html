Open calls

  1. Game Design Emerging Talents: She Got Game

    Pro Helvetia (Swiss Arts Council) · Remote/international mentoring programme; applicants must be based in one of Pro Helvetia's liaison-office regions (Arab region, East Asia, South America, South Asia and Vietnam, and West, Central, East and Southern Africa). · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: CHF 3,000 toward the production of a prototype or demo version, plus individual coaching and community access; potential access to international trade fairs. When attending trade fairs, applicants with childcare responsibilities can request up to CHF 250 per week for childcare or children's travel expenses. No application fee.

    She Got Game is a Pro Helvetia mentoring programme promoting equal opportunities in the Swiss and international game industry, supporting the representation of women in management positions through dialogue with experienced international experts from the game industry. The call is aimed at professional game designers who identify as women from the regions of Pro Helvetia's liaison offices: the Arab region, East Asia, South America, South Asia and Vietnam, and West, Central, East and Southern Africa. ELIGIBILITY: Emerging Talents are game developers or game studios whose relevant training or registration (self-employed, sole proprietorship, or company) dates back less than seven years. Selection criteria include digital content for devices such as computers, consoles, mobile devices, or mixed-reality devices requiring a high level of interaction; clearly defined objectives in terms of participation and career; originality, feasibility, degree of innovation, and marketability of the project; and project leaders holding the copyright and rights of use. Excluded: commissioned work and projects that are part of an advertising or promotional campaign, and purely technological projects without obvious narrative and/or aesthetic content. WHAT IS OFFERED: individual coaching and input talks on project development, business strategy and finances, marketing, PR, and pitch training; involvement in a growing community of female game developers; potential access to international trade fairs; CHF 3,000 toward the production of a prototype or demo version; and up to CHF 250 per week toward childcare or children's travel expenses when attending international trade fairs (a budget must be presented in advance). All modules are conducted in English; the programme is a time-consuming commitment of roughly 30 hours over six months, plus potential additional time attending events or trade fairs. APPLICATION: submit a short CV (including education/training, nationality, and residency status in Switzerland), a short description of the current project (type and genre, gameplay, platform, development status, target audience, with relevant visual material, max. 10 A4 pages), a project portfolio, and expectations regarding the programme's objectives. All documents should be submitted in English; if not possible, applications may be submitted in German, French, or Italian. KEY DATES: applications open 1 July 2026; submission deadline 1 September 2026, 23:59 CEST; notification of decision in November 2026; earliest project start 1 January 2027. Apply via the Pro Helvetia application portal.

  2. Slamdance Film Festival 2027: DIG (Digital, Interactive and Gaming)

    Slamdance · Los Angeles, California, United States (festival 18-24 February 2027). Open to international entries. · Deadline: 06 Oct 2026 · Award: Festival exhibition at Slamdance in Los Angeles. DIG welcomes interactive documentaries, VR/AR, experiential installations, and genre-bending games. Entry fees: USD 60 regular, USD 70 late, USD 90 extended/final.

    Slamdance's DIG (Digital, Interactive and Gaming) showcase presents work at the edges of cinema and interactivity: interactive documentaries, VR/AR, experiential installations, and experimental games, a strong fit for interactive AI and data-driven work. Film categories run on similar deadline tiers. DIG deadlines: regular 10 August 2026 (USD 60), late 14 September 2026 (USD 70), extended 6 October 2026 (USD 90).

  3. Reverse Alignment: Fast-Grants for Storytellers (AI and Humanity)

    Reverse Alignment coalition, administered by PopShift (Pathos Labs) · Not stated. The application is an online form and no geographic restriction is published. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: NOT PUBLISHED. The call is framed as a fast-grant / flash-grant for creators but names no figure, no budget range and no number of awards anywhere on the site or in the application form. Ask before you invest time. No application fee.

    Rolling fast-grants for writers, filmmakers, game makers and video creators who build the sociotechnical challenges of AI into their work. THE COALITION: Reverse Alignment argues that most AI money goes to making systems more capable and to aligning them with human values, while almost nothing goes to redesigning the institutions, norms and skills that decide whether the benefits are shared. Its public statement is the Noema essay 'What Humanity Needs to Flourish in the Next Decade'. The coalition list is unusually senior and cross-sector: Audrey Tang, Danielle Allen, Blaise Aguera y Arcas, Tim O'Reilly, Glen Weyl, Divya Siddarth, Vilas Dhar, James Evans, Ben Buchanan and others. THE ASK: 'We are funding storytellers who weave these ideas into their work. If you write, film, build games, or make things people watch, and you want to incorporate or reinterpret any challenge here, we want to support it. You don't have to explain the policy. You just have to make people feel what's at stake.' THE MATERIAL: twelve sociotechnical grand challenges (identity, privacy, provenance, data value, agentic collaboration, communal sensemaking, democracy, law and liberties, workplace, research, education, labour transition), plus a free storyteller's playbook, 'What AI Can't Build', that turns each into a high-concept pitch and a writers'-room prompt, downloadable as a PDF from the site. MISMATCH WORTH KNOWING BEFORE YOU APPLY: the public framing is broad (write, film, build games), but the actual application form is a Typeform internally titled 'YouTuber Flash-Grant' and publicly titled 'AI and Humanity - Fast Grant', described as 'for TV and Film writers'. It asks for your YouTube account, a 150 to 300 word pitch for a VIDEO, and which of EIGHT challenges (not twelve) your content addresses. In practice this reads as a video and screen-creator grant. If you work in games or prose, expect to have to make the case. APPLICATION: short form at popshift.typeform.com/to/NvKBeode, asking for name and email, YouTube account, the video pitch, the challenge you are addressing, which creators are inspiring you, and who is shaping your views on AI. Applicants are told to expect a reply 'in the next several weeks'. Questions go to team@pathoslabs.org. DEADLINE: none published; applications appear to be rolling.
