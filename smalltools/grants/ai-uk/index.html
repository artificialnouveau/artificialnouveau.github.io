  1. Human Rights Watch: AI Strategy and Solutions Lead (one-year fixed term)

    Human Rights Watch (HRW), Operations Department · Must be within commuting distance of an HRW office: Amsterdam, Amman, Berlin, Brussels, London, Stockholm, New York, Washington DC, Miami, Sao Paulo, Toronto or Chicago. Reports to the Chief Information Officer in Berlin. No relocation support and no work-authorisation sponsorship. · Deadline: 30 Aug 2026 · Award: USD 102,000 to 112,000 plus employer-paid benefits if based in the US; ranges outside the US are set by local market conditions. No application fee.

    Human Rights Watch is hiring an AI Strategy and Solutions Lead into its Operations Department to build the foundation for responsible AI adoption across the organisation. The role deliberately sits between strategy and execution: assessing organisational readiness, mapping partnerships and governance, and also prototyping and shipping working systems. THE WORK, in four strands. (1) STRATEGY AND READINESS: with the CIO and the Information Security and Technology (IST) leadership team, build an AI strategy covering data foundation requirements, infrastructure gaps and organisational readiness; assess current state and shape a roadmap balancing quick wins against long-term capacity; research what peer nonprofit, humanitarian and human rights organisations are doing and find collaboration openings. (2) FLAGSHIP SOLUTIONS: work with departments to find high-impact use cases, translate business needs into technical approaches, build and deploy AI/ML solutions with measurable value (time saved, efficiency, better outcomes), using the Microsoft AI ecosystem (Copilot Studio, Azure AI, Power Platform) among other tools, and oversee consultants and vendors to extend delivery capacity. (3) ADOPTION AND CAPACITY: balance onboarding staff onto AI tools against building custom solutions where they amplify the organisation's strengths; write training material pitched at different work areas and skill levels covering prompting and responsible use; support working groups and communities of practice; produce playbooks and guides for non-technical staff. (4) GOVERNANCE: partner with IST leadership on risk assessment frameworks, align solutions with security, infrastructure and architecture requirements, contribute to AI policy development and its operationalisation, set standards for evaluating proofs of concept and MVPs, and engage business units so outcomes are co-owned; all of it balanced against data quality, security and resource constraints and HRW's mission as a human rights organisation. WHO THEY WANT: a bachelor's degree or equivalent work experience in computer science, data science, information systems, mathematics, AI/ML implementation or a related technical field; a minimum of seven years' relevant experience including two to three years on AI and two to three years implementing AI/ML in production; experience contributing to technology strategy and assessing organisational readiness for new technology; strong Python and ML library/framework skills; hands-on predictive modelling, data science and statistical analysis; experience with low-code/no-code AI platforms such as Copilot Studio or Power Platform; a track record of scoping and delivering under resource constraints; a grasp of both generative AI and traditional ML; and the ability to explain technical concepts plainly to mixed audiences, engage stakeholders and lead technology adoption as an internal partner to business teams. PREFERRED: nonprofit, humanitarian or human rights experience; data privacy, security and ethical AI knowledge; familiarity with responsible AI frameworks and governance models; vendor and consultant management; and a background in politically sensitive or high-risk environments. APPLY: through the HRW job portal with a cover letter and CV, preferably as PDFs, by 30 August 2026. Only complete applications are reviewed and only shortlisted candidates are contacted; no calls or email enquiries. HRW states that it does not discriminate on the basis of disability, age, gender identity and expression, national origin, race and ethnicity, religious beliefs, sexual orientation or criminal record.

    Institute for AI Policy and Strategy (IAPS) · Washington, D.C. or the Bay Area, USA; London considered for exceptional candidates. US visa sponsorship available. · Deadline: 08 Sep 2026 · Award: Special Projects Manager USD 122,000 to 165,000; Senior Associate USD 97,000 to 126,500; Associate USD 86,000 to 101,000. Between 2 and 5 roles are being filled.

    IAPS is hiring 2 to 5 special projects roles to support or own new workstreams in AI governance, helping the world navigate towards a positive future with transformative AI. LEVELS AND SALARY: Special Projects Manager, USD 122,000 to 165,000; Senior Associate, USD 97,000 to 126,500; Associate, USD 86,000 to 101,000. LOCATION: Washington, D.C. or the Bay Area, with London open to exceptional candidates; IAPS can sponsor US visas. PROJECT AREAS THEY ARE EXCITED ABOUT: (1) Bay Area to DC interface, closing the gap between where frontier AI is built and where it is governed by surfacing what is happening in AI labs and technical circles for DC policy audiences, and vice versa; (2) AI security fieldbuilding, bridging a national security community short on people who deeply understand frontier AI risk and an AI community short on people who understand national security, through workshops, convenings and partnerships in DC that put technical experts from both worlds in the same room; (3) DC-based special projects, leveraging IAPS's research output and policy networks to build talent pipelines and the wider field of AI governance, drawing on a new 45-seat office in Dupont Circle and a 30-person fellowship cohort arriving in DC every six months; and (4) programs targeting senior policy audiences, bringing policy entrepreneurs and technical professionals into existing and developing research areas such as institutional resilience for advanced AI or AI and defence, with participants working semi-independently to scope and explore new policy domains. DEADLINE: applications are considered on a rolling basis, with applications sent by 8 September 2026 prioritised. HOW TO APPLY: apply at iaps.ai/special-projects. Questions to careers@iaps.ai.

  3. BOLD Fellow, British Open-ended Learning and Discovery Lab

    University of Oxford, Department of Engineering Science (BOLD Lab, with UCL and Imperial College London) · Oxford, UK, with posts also based at UCL and Imperial College London. The 12 posts are employed across the three universities. · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: Salary on Oxford Grade 10R, GBP 65,336 to GBP 84,663 per year, with the posting noting that further salary options are being explored. Benefits include associate membership at Reuben College, 38 days annual leave, pension scheme, childcare services, and travel expenses for stakeholder and conference visits. Fellows also get access to substantial compute: 3 million GPU hours on Isambard for the first 18 months, with the lab working towards 5,000 H100-equivalents in total across industry and government support. No application fee.

    The British Open-ended Learning and Discovery (BOLD) Lab is a new UKRI-funded initiative establishing an AI research laboratory led from the University of Oxford, bringing more than 80 researchers from Oxford, UCL and Imperial College London under one vision. It carries an initial investment of around GBP 8 million with the potential for a further GBP 22 million extending the investment over six years, making it one of the UK's largest bets on fundamental AI research. The lab's remit is ambitious, high-risk work on the fundamental science of artificial intelligence, alongside training future leaders in the field. THE ROLE: up to 12 BOLD Fellows are being recruited across the three universities. Fellows are expected to operate with a high level of independence and responsibility, setting their own research direction, publishing at the highest level, and mentoring junior researchers and doctoral students. They also play a leading role in managing the lab and take responsibility for developing its strategic and scientific direction, with primary responsibility for a programme of independent funded research projects. The posting frames the draw as exceptional independence, direct collaboration with world-leading colleagues across the three institutions, access to substantial and rapidly growing compute, and real influence over the intellectual direction and culture of a major national lab. WHO SHOULD APPLY: senior researchers with an established, original research track record in AI or a closely related field, who are a recognised authority in their area. HOW TO APPLY: submit an online application with a covering letter or supporting statement, a CV, and details of two referees. Informal enquiries are welcomed beforehand to the BOLD Exec Team at boldexec-team@eng.ox.ac.uk. NOTE: this is a salaried fixed-term research post rather than a grant. Only applications received before midday on Tuesday 15 September 2026 can be considered.

  4. Trust me, I'm an AI/rtist: Open Call for Creative Interventions

    Waag Futurelab (Horizon Europe project HAMLET) · Europe. Engaging with a specific AI Enabler includes an on-site visit to its technology partner: CERTH (Greece), The Audience Agency (Warrington, UK), the Centre of Excellence for Research & Innovation (Cyprus), Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (Greece), or CAUSA Creation (Austria). Final presentations September 2027. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 20,000 commission fee excluding VAT, inclusive of artist fee, travel, accommodation and production costs. Applicants must have a VAT number in Europe. No application fee.

    Waag Futurelab invites artists, theatre makers, choreographers, dancers, game designers and other creative practitioners to engage directly with seven experimental generative AI Enablers being co-created with technologists and dance, theatre and gaming professionals. The call is an invitation into the development process: selected artists get access to the technologies, datasets, interfaces and assumptions that shape these systems while they are still being built. Rather than testing whether the systems work, artists are asked to interrogate why they work the way they do, who they are designed for, what forms of creativity they enable or constrain, and what assumptions about culture, intelligence, authorship, labour, bodies, audiences and society are embedded in them. Waag is looking for individuals and collectives willing to move beyond adoption into investigation, and welcomes proposals that are speculative, critical, poetic, provocative, humorous, performative, constructive or disruptive, including work that challenges, misuses, reimagines, hacks, subverts or plays with the tools, exposes hidden biases, questions environmental costs, or imagines alternative technological futures. THE SEVEN AI ENABLERS, each requiring an on-site visit to its technology partner: the Generic Procedural Content Generation Enabler (text-to-3D assets and avatars, CERTH, Greece); the Data-Driven Cultural Content Adaptation Enabler (audience-aware creative content ideation with ranked candidate ideas, CERTH, Greece); the Cross-Domain Audience Engagement Analytics Enabler (turning heterogeneous cultural participation data into interpretable engagement signals, The Audience Agency, Warrington, UK); the Dance Style Analysis and Generation Enabler (dance video into virtual 3D choreography, dissected into body, effort, shape and space components, Centre of Excellence for Research & Innovation, Cyprus); the Immersive Design Integration Enabler for Dance & Theatre (NLP-driven XR environments with speech-to-3D scene editing, Cyprus); the Music-to-Movement Translation Enabler (audio into a time-aligned feature set spanning genre classification, emotional valence-arousal analysis, rhythm extraction and latent-space projection, feeding motion generation, spatial audio and visual choreography, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, Greece); and the Adaptive Narrative Enabler for Games & Theatre (an evolutionary AI trained on creator-written story elements that generates infinite story variations, explicitly rejecting LLMs because of their dependence on copyrighted or public-domain corpora that encode 19th and early 20th century sexism, racism and imperialism, CAUSA Creation, Austria). ELIGIBILITY: artists, designers, creative technologists, theatre makers, dancers, choreographers and game designers, as individuals or collectives; applicants must hold a European VAT number. An info session with live demonstrations of the AI Enablers runs 31 July 2026, 10:00 to 11:30 CEST (meeting ID 995 7016 2341). Applications go through Homerun using the official Application Template, uploaded as a PDF; read the Guide for Applicants, which contains the full Enabler descriptions, before applying. Questions: zoenie at waag dot org. The AI Enablers and this call are part of the Horizon Europe project HAMLET, which aims to make generative AI accessible to cultural organisations of all sizes. Closes 30 September 2026, 23:59 CEST. Free to apply.

  5. AIXI Labs: Research Fellowship (rolling, quarterly rounds)

    AIXI Labs · Fully remote, open worldwide; fellows may also work from the organisation's London base in the United Kingdom. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 2,000 to GBP 3,500 per month stipend, plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. Free to apply.

    AIXI Labs is a small London-based non-profit doing AI safety research grounded in algorithmic information theory. Its stated position is that most AI research today is either mathematically clean but narrow, or practical but too opaque for rigorous safety analysis; the lab targets a third category, methods general enough to describe powerful agents and provable enough to support real safety claims, using AIXI (the theoretical model of unbounded artificial superintelligence) and then porting the strongest of those ideas to modern LLM-based agents. The fellowship is a quarterly, rolling programme for researchers who want to work on this agenda under close mentorship from the core team: Cole Wyeth (Founder and Executive Director), Marcus Hutter (Research Director) and Aram Ebtekar (Founding Research Scientist). Fellows pursue a focused research project over roughly a quarter and receive a monthly stipend plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. The fellowship is fully remote, so fellows may work from anywhere in the world or from the London base. RESPONSIBILITIES: pursue a self-directed research project aligned with the lab's algorithmic-information-theoretic approach to AI safety, spanning anything from foundational theory to empirical tests with LLM-based agents; shape the project direction around your own strengths and interests, as the lab supports a range of work within this framework; and contribute papers, blog posts or other written output communicating results to the broader AI safety and ML research communities. MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS: a research background at the level of a current PhD student (for primarily empirical projects, a master's-level command of the relevant mathematics paired with substantial industry or applied research experience can also qualify); solid grounding in probability, statistics, information theory and reinforcement learning, sufficient to engage seriously with AIXI-style theoretical models (prior expertise in algorithmic information theory or AIXI is NOT required); strong verbal and written communication skills; genuine motivation to reduce existential risk from advanced AI and enthusiasm for the lab's kind of work. PREFERRED QUALIFICATIONS: publications at top ML, AI or theory venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ALT or COLT, though the lab values the strength of your best work over volume; background in algorithmic information theory, computability theory, learning theory or related theoretical computer science; experience fine-tuning, evaluating or red-teaming LLM-based and/or RL agents; prior engagement with the UAI/AIXI community or related fields, including members of the lab's online reading groups. APPLICATIONS are reviewed on a rolling basis and previous applicants remain eligible for later rounds.

  6. Apollo Research: (Senior) AI Governance Researcher (London or San Francisco)

    Apollo Research · In-person in Apollo's London or San Francisco office, with flexible hours and work-from-home arrangements. Visa sponsorship available in the UK and the US, not guaranteed for every candidate. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 150,000 to 225,000 salary plus equity. Benefits include unlimited vacation and sick leave, up to 6 months paid parental leave, health/dental/vision insurance, employer-matched retirement savings, meals on workdays, paid work trips and retreats, and a USD 1,000 yearly professional development budget. Note: a widely circulated newsletter listing quoted this range in pounds; the posting itself says USD.

    A mid to senior research role on the Governance team at Apollo Research, a startup working on the threats from scheming frontier AI systems that works with and is trusted by every frontier AI lab, doing fundamental research, pre-deployment model testing and scheming mitigations. THE TEAM: Governance researches the institutional, legal and strategic implications of AI scheming and loss-of-control threats, working alongside the Science of Scheming, Evaluations and Monitoring teams. Its stated bet is that the governance, risk-management and legal frameworks built inside and outside frontier labs over the next two years will help determine collective ability to stay in control of increasingly advanced systems. Focus areas: scheming, loss of control, governance of models deployed internally by frontier labs, government testing of frontier AI for high-stakes use, and the handoff of AI R&D to AIs. TRACK RECORD: the loss-of-control chapter of the International AI Safety Report 2026; the report 'AI Behind Closed Doors: A Primer on the Governance of Internal Deployment'; 'The Loss of Control Playbook: Degrees, Dynamics, and Preparedness'; a TIME op-ed with Yoshua Bengio; peer-reviewed work on precursory capabilities, AI incident regimes and National Security Benchmarks for Loss of Control presented at ICML, IASEAI and the UK AI Security Institute's Conference on Frontier AI Safety Frameworks. The team briefs governments on both sides of the Atlantic, including Congressional committees and the intelligence community; some work is non-public. THE WORK: design and drive AI governance research on critical safety and security questions; lead research across formats including peer-reviewed papers, in-depth reports, technical memos and legal analyses; scope forward-looking projects grounded in the technical state of the art; engage with legal, policy and governance frameworks alongside partners in companies and governments; and translate technical AI safety literature and colleagues' empirical work into governance-relevant proposals. REQUIREMENTS: a minimum of 6 years work experience from academia, industry and/or private practice, and a track record of substantive research outputs that shifted thinking in the field (peer-reviewed publications, high-impact reports, a relevant PhD thesis or comparable). Apollo says it will assess fit on individual merit and can tailor scope and title for the right person. APPLICATION: resume plus relevant work samples or links; a cover letter is optional but welcome, especially if it sketches the research direction you would pursue. DEADLINE: none. Reviewed on a ROLLING basis, and Apollo says it aims to finalise a hire as soon as possible, so apply early.

RESPONSIBILITIES: map segments of frontier AI and adjacent technology supply chains, with possible focus on specific country ecosystems or on actors across segments such as capital allocation, raw materials, mineral processing, chip design, fabrication, advanced packaging, datasets, datacentres and frontier labs; analyse how ownership stakes, trade relationships and control are likely to evolve under different technological, commercial and geopolitical scenarios; and develop strategic proposals for stakeholders across government and industry.   8. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.
