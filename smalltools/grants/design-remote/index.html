  1. New_ Public Entrepreneur in Residence, Pilot Cohort

    New_ Public · Remote, with an in-person kickoff retreat in Philadelphia, USA (19 to 22 October 2026). Open to applicants based in the United States or Canada. · Deadline: 09 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 22,500, paid in three monthly installments across the residency, each contingent on continued active participation. Plus USD 1,500 toward an AI tool of the resident's choice for coding, prototyping or design. Two residents are selected in the pilot cohort. Also provided: hands-on product and strategy support from senior New_ Public staff and outside experts; access to New_ Public's five years of original research on moderation, governance, community stewardship and platform design, plus direct access to the researchers behind it; and introductions across New_ Public's network of major platforms, startups, university researchers and funders for hiring, fundraising, early user testing and community partnerships. Residents must submit a Form W-9 or W-8BEN to receive the stipend. No application fee.

    New_ Public is piloting a virtual Entrepreneur in Residence programme for solo founders building alternatives to the mainstream social platforms. Its framing: people are turning away from algorithmic social media, AI bots are taking over the big platforms, and users are retreating into private closed spaces, which leaves an opening for builders working on a new information ecosystem rather than the status quo. The residency supplies research, experts, connections and tools; the founder brings the vision and experience. TWO FOCUS AREAS: humans connecting to humans, meaning products that bring people into meaningful contact online (forums, messaging apps, groups, microblogging, in-person meetups), including AI-powered platforms and tools as long as they connect people to people rather than people to bots; and local and civic information environments, meaning tools for distributing local news, building community knowledge and facilitating collective sensemaking, so people can get trustworthy information about their communities. WHAT RESIDENTS DO: use self-directed time to prototype ideas, test assumptions and gain clarity; participate in regular check-ins, engage with the other resident and the wider team, and take candid feedback on product and strategy; engage seriously with go-to-market questions about who the user is, how they find you, and the path to sustainability; and help shape the programme itself, since the pilot cohort's feedback directly informs how the EIR programme runs going forward. WHO THEY WANT: entrepreneurial founders ready to build their next thing within the focus areas, with a track record of or clear drive toward work that builds social trust, connection and belonging; strong product thinkers with good judgment, a relentless focus on user needs, and openness to coaching on mission-aligned business models. An early idea is enough, no working product required. The pilot is open to founders with something else going on alongside, though the full programme will most likely be full time. APPLICATION PROCESS: submit the written application online; shortlisted applicants submit a short video by 18 September 2026 covering background, motivation and a quick pitch; full applications and videos go to the advisory board, with offers made in early October ahead of the kickoff. FUTURE COHORTS: New_ Public plans to open the next cohort to the UK and EU from spring 2027. Deadline 11:59pm Pacific on Wednesday 9 September 2026.

  2. Playlab: Senior Visual Communication Designer (remote)

    Playlab (nonprofit) · Remote, full time. Salary quoted in USD; the posting does not state a geographic restriction. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 120,000 to 150,000 base salary. Full-time.

    Playlab is a nonprofit building public infrastructure for educational AI, equipping communities to create AI tools that reflect their own needs and values; more than 60,000 educators have published apps on the platform. Its premise is that AI is a new design material that should be shaped by many, and that educators should be designers of AI systems rather than end users of someone else's. THE ROLE: visual communication designers own the brand and the surfaces where people encounter and build with it, shaping how educators learn about AI and gain the confidence to tinker with it, and making sophisticated ideas legible without flattening them. Work spans brand expression, storytelling, data and information design, motion, interface and print, taken from concept through produced work and shipped as reusable systems. The job includes getting close to the people using Playlab and the contexts they work in, and helping collect and share their stories. WHAT THEY LOOK FOR: craft that is production-grade with a clear point of view and good judgement about when to ship fast versus slow down; narrative sense, meaning well-reasoned positions on how Playlab tells its story and the ability to turn complex systems into intuitive visuals that stay true to their depth; initiative on fuzzy problems, from defining the space to shipped work, thinking ahead to dependencies and second-order questions; technical fluency across print, web and motion, building with and on AI as a creative material and curating tooling and workflows; and taste, a distinct visual voice with the restraint to leave a rough edge when it serves the work. FIT: at least 5 years' experience with a strong graphic design foundation, a home base in visual communication (brand, editorial, campaign, web) and a track record in at least one other design discipline; able to lead design on owned projects while collaborating across the team; broad knowledge of creative tools and workflows including AI-leveraged ones; genuine interest in learning environments and the people in them; and strength as a producer comfortable owning the last mile to production. BONUS: education or edtech experience, designing creative tech, and a love of creative side quests. The posting explicitly encourages applications from people who do not meet every listed qualification. Culturally the organisation describes itself as sitting between a startup and an R&D lab, with nonprofit governance intended to keep incentives tied to teaching and learning rather than growth.

  3. Playlab: Senior Product Designer (remote)

    Playlab (nonprofit) · Remote, full time. Salary quoted in USD; the posting does not state a geographic restriction. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 120,000 to 150,000 base salary. Full-time.

    Playlab is a nonprofit building public infrastructure for educational AI, with more than 60,000 educators publishing apps on its platform. Designers there are responsible first for researching, prototyping and implementing interaction patterns that let educators be makers of technology rather than recipients of someone else's system, then for shaping how educators learn about AI and gain confidence to tinker with it, and finally for the storytelling that carries the work into the world. The two standing questions the role works on: how to expand and deepen what educators can create with AI, and how to lower the floor for who can create and how easily AI systems can be meaningfully deployed in real educational contexts. THE ROLE: as Senior Product Designer you own the design of Playlab's surfaces from prototype through shipped product. WHAT THEY LOOK FOR: craft that is production-grade with a clear point of view, and instincts for when to ship fast versus slow down; product sense grounded in staying close to the problems Playlab is solving, with the ability to hold unresolved questions and keep moving; initiative on fuzzy goals, defining the design space, gathering context and driving through prototyping to a shipped solution while bringing leadership along; technical fluency, building with and on AI, judging what is worth building, and curating custom tooling; and taste across visual, interaction and communication design. FIT: at least 5 years as a strong design generalist with a home base in product design and a track record in another design discipline; able to lead design on owned projects while collaborating across the team; broad knowledge of creative and AI-leveraged tools; interest in learning environments and the people who create them; and comfort getting technical with engineers, possibly pushing code. BONUS: education or edtech experience, designing creative tech, and a love of creative side quests. The posting explicitly encourages applications from people who do not meet every listed qualification. The organisation describes itself as sitting between a startup and an R&D lab, with nonprofit governance keeping incentives aligned to teaching and learning rather than growth.
