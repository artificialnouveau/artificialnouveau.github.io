Research and Journalism Grants in the UK

Currently 11 active research and journalism grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the UK. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Jane Street Graduate Research Fellowship 2027 (tuition plus USD 50,000 stipend)

    Jane Street · For full-time PhD students at accredited research institutions in the US, Canada, UK, Europe, Australia, New Zealand or Singapore. International students welcome. · Deadline: 13 Nov 2026 · Award: Full tuition and fees for the award year plus a USD 50,000 stipend (or local-currency equivalent; taxable). Finalists receive USD 5,000. No application fee.

    Jane Street's Graduate Research Fellowship supports exceptional doctoral students in electrical engineering and computer science, mathematics, physics or statistics; research does not need to relate to trading. ELIGIBILITY: PhD students with at least one year remaining in their programme, or those committed to start a PhD at a specified university in fall 2027; fellows must be active full-time PhD students at an accredited research institution in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Europe, Australia, New Zealand or Singapore during the award year. International students are eligible and encouraged. WHAT YOU GET: full tuition and fees for the upcoming academic year plus a USD 50,000 stipend (or local-currency equivalent; taxable); finalists receive USD 5,000. APPLICATION: CV, an optional one-to-two-page research statement and recommender contact details. DEADLINES: student applications 13 November 2026 AoE; recommendation letters 1 December 2026; notifications in February 2027. Questions: graduate-research-fellowship@janestreet.com.

  2. Digital Democracy Edinburgh 2027: Call for Proposals (TICTeC, mySociety, AI4CI, Scottish Government)

    mySociety (TICTeC) with the AI for Collective Intelligence (AI4CI) Hub, the Scottish Government and the Edinburgh Futures Institute, University of Edinburgh · Edinburgh Futures Institute, Edinburgh, Scotland, UK. Primarily in-person, with a limited number of remote presentation slots. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: NO GUARANTEED CASH, listed as an exception at the editor's request: accepted speakers get complimentary conference registration, and a limited number of travel scholarships are available for speakers who could otherwise not attend (priority to applicants from ODA-eligible countries and under-29s; amount not published). Free to submit.

    Digital Democracy Edinburgh is a two-day conference from mySociety's TICTeC (The Impacts of Civic Technology) series, run with the AI for Collective Intelligence (AI4CI) Research Hub, the Scottish Government and the Edinburgh Futures Institute, for researchers, policy makers and practitioners reimagining technology for more resilient democracies. CORE QUESTION: how can technology reimagine democratic decision-making and build a more resilient democracy? STRANDS: building democratic technology (digital public infrastructure, participatory platforms, AI-assisted and augmented deliberation, open-source civic tech, tech for infrastructure-poor settings); democracy in a digital age (quality of participation, hybrid online and offline participation, transparency and accountability, digital exclusion); and embedding digital democracy around the world (open government, scaling and transferring innovations, lessons from implementation, lower-income democracies). Topics also include collective intelligence, prioritising public values in AI and the limits of digital democracy. FORMATS: 15-minute presentations and 5-minute lightning talks (individuals or groups), and 75-minute collaborative workshops or roundtables for about 20 to 30 participants (groups of contributors only); session proposals up to 300 words. The organisers want real-world experience, evaluation and implementation learning, and warn that generic AI-generated proposals are unlikely to score well. SUPPORT: complimentary registration for accepted speakers; limited travel scholarships (flag the need on the form; priority to ODA-eligible countries and under-29s); visa invitation letters on registration. Speakers must be available for both full days. TIMELINE: submit via the Google Form linked from the event page by 23:59 GMT on 15 November 2026; notification by 7 December 2026; presenters must register by 18 December 2026 to confirm their slot. Contact: tictec@mysociety.org.

  3. Radical Sabbatical 2027: six-month residencies on touch at the University of Birmingham (GBP 3,500 fee + GBP 500 production, UK-based artists)

    Eastside Projects with University Collections, University of Birmingham · University of Birmingham, Birmingham, UK (in person, research residency). Open to artists based in the UK only. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget per artist; two artists selected.

    Radical Sabbatical is a collaboration between Eastside Projects and University Collections at the University of Birmingham that gives two artists a six-month residency centred on research and development. The 2027 edition is themed around touch: artists are matched with one or more academics and asked to interrogate and interpret academic research, with areas including digital touch and technology, haptic and sensory experience, embodied knowledge, disability and inclusive design, medical perspectives, materiality and craft, memory and emotion, and human and non-human sensory connection. Academic partners include the Active Touch Lab and the Department of Creative Industries. Artists have regular one-to-one conversations with staff and present at a final sharing event. ELIGIBILITY: artists based in the UK. SUPPORT: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget. TIMELINE: deadline Friday 20 November 2026, 11:59pm; interviews for shortlisted artists 10 December 2026; residencies begin January 2027; final sharing event and evaluation June/July 2027. APPLY using the artists' callout pack linked from the call page; questions to info@eastsideprojects.org.

  4. Tarbell Fellowship 2027-28 (AI journalism)

    Tarbell Center for AI Journalism · Remote training, then newsroom placement in the US, UK or Hong Kong. In person. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 75,000 to 120,000 depending on experience and placement cost of living; senior fellows (7+ years experience) USD 115,000 to 135,000. Summit travel and accommodation covered. No application fee.

    A year-long fellowship for journalists covering artificial intelligence. Fellows complete a three-month full-time remote programme on AI and journalism fundamentals, attend a weeklong summit, then work for nine months as reporters embedded in a partner newsroom (current partners are in Hong Kong, London, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle, Washington DC and Cambridge). ELIGIBILITY: journalism or AI expertise, aged 18+ by 7 June 2027, no degree required; primarily for applicants with the right to work in the US, UK or Hong Kong, as visa sponsorship is uncommon and needs partner-outlet approval.

  5. GovAI Summer Fellowship 2027 (AI governance, London or Washington DC, GBP 14,000 for 3 months)

    Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI) · London, UK or Washington, DC, USA; in person, full time. Open worldwide; temporary work visa sponsorship available. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 14,000 for 3 months plus travel support (UK Research Track figure; DC track has its own stipend). No application fee.

    GovAI's Summer Fellowship brings early-career researchers and practitioners into AI governance for three months of full-time work on a research or applied project under the mentorship of a GovAI researcher. TRACKS: UK Research Track and UK Applied Track in London, plus a Washington DC track, each with its own application page. TOPICS include risk management, economics and geopolitics of AI, public policy and technical governance. ELIGIBILITY: open to applicants worldwide; must be able to work in person full time from 7 June to 27 August 2027; GovAI can sponsor temporary work visas. DEADLINE: 22 November 2026, 23:59 GMT for the UK tracks (11:59 pm EST for DC).

  6. Cove Park Awarded Residencies 2027-2028 (Scotland, UK, Central and South America and diasporas)

    Cove Park · Cove Park, Peaton Hill, Argyll and Bute, Scotland (in person, private self-catering accommodation and studio). Open to people based in the UK, Central or South America, Central and South American diaspora artists based in Europe or North America, and Scotland-born artists living anywhere. Students in formal education are not eligible. · Deadline: 11 Dec 2026 · Award: Fully funded: GBP 775 per week fee (VAT inclusive, freelance), travel allowance (Scotland GBP 65 to 200, rest of UK up to GBP 250, Europe up to GBP 400, other international up to GBP 1,250), materials up to GBP 150 for studio-based practitioners, private accommodation and studio, visa and access support. Application Access Bursary available (request by 30 November 2026). No application fee.

    Cove Park's biennial call for fully funded Awarded Residencies at its 50-acre rural residency site in Argyll, on the west coast of Scotland, programmed between May 2027 and December 2028. Residencies run from 2 weeks up to 12 weeks (average four weeks) and can be split across the two years around caring or work commitments; international residents can count slow-travel time as part of the funded period. Open to artists, creative practitioners, writers, translators and researchers in all art forms and at every career stage, including collectives and collaborative groups; no formal qualification needed. Priorities include work responding to climate breakdown, sound artists and musicians (new Sound Studio), and work on home, place, language (Scots, Gaelic, BSL), displacement and belonging. No final presentation is required. ELIGIBILITY: based in the UK, Central America or South America; Central and South American diaspora artists based in Europe or North America; or born in Scotland and living elsewhere. Anyone in full- or part-time formal education on 11 December 2026 is not eligible. SUPPORT: GBP 775 per week fee; travel allowance (Scotland GBP 65 to 100 Central Belt, up to GBP 200 Borders/Highlands/Islands; rest of UK up to GBP 250; Europe up to GBP 400; other international up to GBP 1,250); materials up to GBP 150; visa support; private self-catering accommodation (suitable for collaborators and those attending with children) and private studios. An Application Access Bursary (e.g. to pay a support worker) is available for applicants facing barriers due to disability or neurodivergence; request it from Alex Marrs by 30 November 2026. Video or audio applications accepted. Applicants are asked not to use generative AI in their application. APPLY via the online form: 500-word statement, 100-word bio, CV, up to 8 images or video/audio links or writing samples. SELECTION: shortlisting by the programme team and former residents; shortlist notified by 12 February 2027; Zoom interviews the week of 22 February 2027. Contact: Alex Marrs, alex@covepark.org. DEADLINE: Friday 11 December 2026, 5pm GMT.

  7. Royal Institute of Philosophy Essay Prize 2026: Humans and AI

    Royal Institute of Philosophy · Remote submission by email. Open internationally. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 1,000 cash plus GBP 500 of Cambridge University Press books; the winning essay is published in the journal Philosophy.

    The Royal Institute of Philosophy's annual essay prize asks for an original philosophical essay on the 2026 topic, Humans and AI. Essays of up to 8,000 words, anonymised for review, are submitted by email. No eligibility restrictions are stated. Note: the Cambridge Core prize page still shows the older 2024 call at a different amount; the institute's own announcement is current. No application fee.

  8. EVA London 2027: Call for Proposals (Electronic Visualisation and the Arts, 12-16 July 2027)

    EVA London (BCS Computer Arts Society) · BCS London offices, Moorgate, London, UK, and online via Zoom (hybrid). Open internationally. · Deadline: 11 Jan 2027 · Award: NO CASH, listed as an exception at the editor's request (conference presentation slot and publication in the proceedings). Free to submit, but at least one author of each paper and all panel and workshop presenters must pay conference registration (rates not yet published; to be set in spring 2027, with a special rate for Research Workshop presenters).

    EVA London is a long-running hybrid conference on electronic visualisation and the arts, covering digital art, creative technology, computing and visual culture, held at BCS in Moorgate, London, and online. STRANDS: long papers (8 pages, draft PDF required) and short papers (2 pages, abstract-only proposal), both in 20-minute slots; papers with demonstration (8 pages); demonstrations (2 pages, 10 or 20 minutes, online demos possible); panel sessions (30 minutes); workshops and tutorials (usually 90 or 120 minutes); and a Research Workshop for postgraduate students and unaffiliated digital artists (15-minute talks, optional two-page written submission). TO APPLY: a summary proposal of up to 400 words via EasyChair (https://easychair.org/conferences/?conf=evalondon2027). DEADLINES: main proposals Monday 11 January 2027; Research Workshop applications by email to Graham Diprose (grahamdiprose@gmail.com) by Monday 22 February 2027, only while places remain. Notification 22 February 2027; final papers 12 March 2027; fees published 12 April 2027; registration opens 11 May 2027. Presenters must register; no honorarium, prize or current bursary scheme is stated.

  9. Daiwa Foundation Small Grants, March 2027 round (GBP 2,000-9,000 for UK-Japan projects)

    Daiwa Anglo-Japanese Foundation · For individuals, societies, associations and other bodies in the UK or Japan running projects that connect the two countries. · Deadline: 31 Mar 2027 · Award: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (typical awards GBP 3,000-4,000); flights up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day. A contribution, not full funding. No application fee.

    The Daiwa Foundation Small Grants support projects that promote interaction between the UK and Japan in any field, including arts exchanges, exhibitions, conferences, grassroots and educational exchanges and research travel, with travel between the two countries as the main focus. New initiatives are encouraged, and gender equality and SDG themes are welcome; continuing or regular events (such as an annual festival edition) are unlikely to be funded without a strong reason or special occasion. NOT ELIGIBLE: commissions of artworks, capital costs, consumables, salaries or professional fees, tuition and commercial activity. WHAT YOU GET: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (most awards GBP 3,000-4,000), with flights funded up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day, plus GBP 50 extra per flight for environmental projects in Japan. APPLICATION: read the Application Notes, then apply via the Foundation's online portal (separate forms for UK- and Japan-based applicants); advice from grants@dajf.org.uk. Decisions by late May or early June 2027. DEADLINE: 31 March 2027 (the following round closes 30 September).

  10. ILIAD Intensive: Foundational AI Alignment Program (London and Berkeley)

    ILIAD · In person at LISA in London, UK, and in Berkeley, California, USA. Five days a week, 10am to 6pm. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: No tuition. USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance per participant, plus office space at the venue and lunch and dinner five days a week. Cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. Participants arrange and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance.

    The ILIAD Intensive is a four-week, full-time, in-person program for mathematically strong researchers interested in foundational AI alignment, running in London (at LISA) and in Berkeley. It runs five days a week, 10am to 6pm, in cohorts of 15 to 30 participants. CURRICULUM, across five clusters: Alignment (decomposing the alignment problem, pretraining, post-training and deployment, reward learning); Learning (deep learning theory, singular learning theory, training dynamics); Abstractions, Representations and Interpretability (mechanistic interpretability, natural latents); Agency (reinforcement learning, decision theory, agent foundations); and Safety Guarantees (debate, steganography, the limits of interpretability). MONEY: no tuition is charged. Each participant receives a USD 5,000 travel and housing allowance, office space at the venue, and lunch and dinner five days a week. Participants book and pay for their own travel and accommodation out of the allowance. ELIGIBILITY: a strong mathematical background, typically graduate-level study or a completed degree in mathematics, physics or theoretical computer science, though mathematical maturity is weighted more heavily than specific credentials. Research experience, general competence and motivation are also assessed. Prior machine learning or alignment experience is not required. DEADLINE: no fixed closing date is published. A single application covers several upcoming programs and applicants indicate their preferred dates and locations; applications are being accepted for the October 2026 and November 2026 cohorts. HOW TO APPLY: through the application portal at iliad.ac/apply. Questions to contact@iliad.ac.

  11. Iliad Fellowship (applied mathematics for AI alignment, rolling)

    Iliad · London or San Francisco Bay Area. In person. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 6,000 per month travel and housing allowance for the three-month fellowship (four months fully funded including the Iliad Intensive). No application fee.

    Mentored, individual research in applied mathematics for AI alignment, including foundational questions that get less attention elsewhere, drawing on mathematics, theoretical physics and computer science. ELIGIBILITY: designed for PhD students, postdocs and researchers with equivalent experience in mathematics, theoretical physics, computer science or related fields; selection weighs academic credentials, technical ability, intentions and fit with the cohort's research coverage. APPLICATION: rolling, with fellowships starting at the beginning of most months.
