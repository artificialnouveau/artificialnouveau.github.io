  2. Building Narrative Power: EUR 2m funding and learning programme

    European AI & Society Fund · Organisations registered in Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Republic of Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden or Switzerland. The UK, the Western Balkans and other non-European countries are not eligible for this programme. · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Grants of up to EUR 150,000 from a EUR 2 million programme, plus a place in the accompanying learning programme and community of practice. Payments are usually 50% on grant signing, 40% midway through the grant period subject to previous funds having been spent, and 10% on completion. As a general rule organisations should not request more than their previous annual budget unless the need is motivated and demonstrated; without a previous annual budget, request an amount proportional to the project. No application fee.

    The European AI & Society Fund is launching Building Narrative Power, a EUR 2 million funding and learning programme to counter Big Tech's dominance over how AI is understood, and to help people and communities shape new ways of thinking and talking about the technology they want. The Fund's strategy is to build the power of the public interest community to shape the direction of AI in Europe; narratives are central to creating the conditions for a greater say over how AI is adopted and used. THREE THEMES where research shows the public already feels strongly: (1) DATA CENTRES: as the EU aims to triple data centre numbers in the next five to seven years, pollution, energy prices and compromised grid access have mobilised communities across the political spectrum; proposals should explore narrative questions that move communities beyond resistance towards engagement in shaping data centre expansion. (2) AI AND JOBS: 57% of the public in a recent study expressed concern about how AI will change employment, more pronounced among lower-income people, and the concern is not only displacement but the quality and nature of work, autonomy and dignity; proposals should move the debate from generalised anxiety into a mobilising issue that shapes the future of work. (3) YOUNG PEOPLE: beyond the strident social media ban debate, young people encounter AI in education, friendships and future careers, and their wider views are rarely heard; proposals should build narratives that help young people shape the technologies that will influence their lives. LEARNING PROGRAMME: grantees join a community of practice, sharing experiences across issues and places, supporting each other through delivery and reflecting on which strategies and stories have most salience, with findings feeding back to the Fund and other funders. ABOUT THE FUNDER: the European AI & Society Fund is a pooled fund with 18 funding partners that mobilises a public interest community in Europe to fight for AI that serves people, society and planet; it takes no EU, government or Big Tech money. ELIGIBILITY: this call is only open to organisations in Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, the Republic of Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland; applications from the UK, the Western Balkans and other non-European countries are not accepted. Guidance for applicants and a full application-form preview are published so you can prepare a draft before submitting. HOW TO APPLY: create an account on the Fund's application platform Optimy (link in the guidance documents) and submit the first-stage application before 31 August 2026. KEY DATES: call launched 16 July 2026; information sessions 22 July, 29 July and 24 August 2026 (11:00 to 12:00 CET); first-stage deadline 31 August 2026; unsuccessful applicants notified 16 October 2026; shortlisted applicants invited to submit a detailed proposal on 19 October 2026, due 16 November 2026; funding decisions notified 14 December 2026; grants start February 2027. Questions to info@europeanaifund.org; an FAQ covering eligibility, coalitions, non-profit status and what the grant can be spent on is published at the bottom of the programme page.

  6. Arcane Grant 2026 for Artist Books

    ADAGP and SGDL (Societe des Gens de Lettres) · France. Open to a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or of French nationality living abroad) paired with a French-language author. · Deadline: 07 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 9,000 to the single winning project, supporting the conception and production of an artist's book. NOTE ON TAX: ADAGP grants count as primary artistic income under the French Intellectual Property Code (art. L112-2), so the sum falls under the artist-author social security and tax regime, subject to URSSAF contributions and income tax declared as non-commercial profits (micro-BNC or controlled declaration). VAT is not due. No application fee.

    Artists' books get little support, financially or in distribution, despite their place in art history and in the careers of the people who make them. ADAGP, the French collecting society for copyright in the visual arts, and SGDL, the French society of authors, created the Arcane grant in response: EUR 9,000 to one winning project for the conception and production of an artist's book made collaboratively by a visual artist and a writer. WHO CAN APPLY: a pair consisting of a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or a French national living abroad) and a French-language author working in fiction, non-fiction, poetry, children's literature or theatre who has already been published by a publisher. A trio is also possible where a publishing structure joins the project. WHAT COUNTS AS AN ARTIST'S BOOK: a creation, a work of art in book form, in a limited print run of between 1 and 500 copies, which may be signed or numbered. Explicitly not eligible: magazines, fanzines, limited editions of an ordinary book, and any publication that simply presents an artist's work, such as monographs or illustrated books. APPLICATION: the file must be in French and includes a jointly written project presentation of up to three pages covering the artistic and editorial vision, constituent elements, methods of realisation and desired final form; a one-page technical sheet giving title, print run, format, page count, paper quality, printing techniques and materials; a provisional timetable; and a detailed balanced provisional budget of expenses and financing. Artistic elements are a CV of exhibitions and publications (2 pages) and a portfolio with website link (10 pages) for the artist, a CV and website link for the author (2 pages), and a one-page editorial line for the publishing structure if there is one; contact details must be omitted from these. Administrative elements are sworn declarations that the project is a new creation with copyright held solely by the pair, that the artist is from the French scene, and, if applicable, that the publishing structure undertakes to publish the work. Templates are downloadable from the Required Documents page. TIMELINE: call launched 7 July 2026, applications close 7 September 2026 at 2pm, jury meets November 2026, winner announced end of November 2026, and the work must be published within two years of that announcement. The regulations must be read before applying, and submitting an application constitutes acceptance of them. Contact: arcane@adagp.fr.

  8. Programme PAUSE: Emergency Hosting for Scientists and Artists in Exile (autumn 2026 call)

    Programme PAUSE, hosted by the College de France, with the French Ministries of Higher Education and Research and of Culture · France (hosting at a French higher education, research or cultural institution) · Deadline: 24 Sep 2026 · Award: PAUSE co-funds the hosting project (salary and support costs) together with the host institution; amounts depend on the project and institution. No application fee.

    PAUSE is the French national programme for the emergency hosting of scientists and artists in exile, created in 2017 and run from the College de France. It co-funds a stay at a French higher education or research establishment or at a cultural institution so that a researcher, doctoral candidate or artist at risk can keep working, and it also supports their family. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be able to prove status as a doctoral student (with a thesis in progress interrupted by the emergency), researcher, teacher-researcher or artist-teacher in their country of origin; must be in a crisis situation because of the security situation in that country or because of persecution or fear of persecution on grounds of ethnicity, religion, political convictions, opinions or sexual orientation, or the content of their research or teaching; and must be forced into exile or have been in France for less than three years. HOST REQUIREMENT: a French host institution is mandatory and submits the application; eligible hosts are institutions under the French higher education and research ministry, EESPIG private institutions, and structures supported by the Ministry of Culture. PAUSE publishes a list of at-risk candidates looking for a host, which institutions can use to find a match. The call opens at the end of August 2026 with a deadline of 24 September 2026.

    Founded by the artist and philanthropist Jerome Hill, the Camargo Foundation has run this residency in Cassis on the Mediterranean coast since 1971, and more than 1,500 fellows have been through it. Fourteen fellowships are awarded per cycle, split roughly seven artists and seven scholars or thinkers, which is the point: the interdisciplinary cohort is the programme, not a side effect. IMPORTANT: the Foundation has stated that this is the FINAL fellowship awarded through a public open call before it moves to a new selection process from 2027, so if you have been meaning to apply, this is the cycle. You propose a project or line of inquiry, which can be development of a new work, research preceding future creative work, or a broader investigation in your practice. Visual artists are explicitly reminded that research is prioritised over production, and the Foundation supplies no art materials, technical equipment or artwork shipping, so anything needing extensive fabrication is a poor fit. Fellows lead one resident session built around their own research or methodology, and attendance at these sessions is mandatory for everyone. Optional public Open Studios and introductions to organisations across the Aix-Marseille-Provence region are also part of it. ELIGIBILITY: professional artists, scholars and thinkers of any nationality. Artists should have at least five years of professional practice and a mature body of work evidenced by exhibitions, performances, publications, awards or grants. Performance artists, theatre directors, choreographers, multidisciplinary artists, writers, filmmakers, composers and visual artists are all eligible within their categories. Collaborative applications of up to three members are accepted. APPLICATION: four separate written responses covering the project, the research questions, why Camargo specifically, and why now in your career; reviewers are reading for a coherent research trajectory rather than a production proposal. An applicant webinar runs 1 September 2026. Deadline 1 October 2026 at 17:59 EST / 23:59 CET; notifications April 2027.

    Art Explora Foundation with the Cite internationale des arts · Paris, France (Cite internationale des arts, Montmartre site, 24 rue Norvins, 75018). Open to applicants of all nationalities with no age restriction. · Deadline: 06 Nov 2026 · Award: Monthly living grant of EUR 1,000, production grant of up to EUR 3,000, a round-trip travel grant, and a fully equipped studio-apartment of roughly 50 sqm.     Residency programme run by the Art Explora Foundation with the Cite internationale des arts at its Montmartre site, supporting work that engages scientific and technological exploration and the major social and environmental issues of the present. Residents receive a studio-apartment of around 50 sqm, a monthly living grant of EUR 1,000, a production grant of up to EUR 3,000, and a round-trip travel grant, plus access to production facilities and local networks. Two tracks are offered: a SOLO programme for artists and writers across visual arts, performance, digital practices, literary and other experimental disciplines, and a COLLECTIVE programme for duos or collectives working collaboratively on interdisciplinary, experimental or research-based projects, for which a single studio-apartment designed for collaborative work hosts two residents sharing a bed, and only one member should submit the application. Eligible artistic disciplines are visual arts, performance and live arts, digital arts, curatorial practices, and writing and literary practices; eligible research fields are humanities, social sciences, cultural studies and history, technological innovation, and ecology. No output is required at the end of the residency, but participation in the Open Studios is mandatory. ELIGIBILITY: artists, artist collectives, researchers, curators and writers of all nationalities, no age restriction, with a minimum of five years of professional activity and works or research exhibited at national or international level. The Cite lists 20 to 22 residencies per year on this programme. Deadline 6 November 2026, 23:59 Europe/Paris. Questions: residences@artexplora.org. FLAG: the grant figures above come from the open-call text supplied to the desk; neither the Cite nor Art Explora publishes amounts on the programme page, so confirm them, along with the exact deadline, before applying.
