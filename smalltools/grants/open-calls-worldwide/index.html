    Sundance Collab and Merrell are selecting five filmmakers from around the world to each make an original 60 to 90 second vertical film for social platforms, built around Merrell's 'It Starts Outside' brand platform. The brief is not a commercial: it asks for a complete, emotionally resonant human story in which the outside acts as a catalyst for change, exploring how stepping outside is an antidote to increasingly screen-locked, indoor lives. Stories should reflect transformation (a moment of reset, reconnection or personal shift), connection (belonging, community, renewed relationship) and human truth (an observed, emotionally grounded moment). The core submission is a written response of up to 500 words on how you would approach the brief. CRITICAL GUIDELINES: design for vertical with a strong hook in the first five seconds; prioritise human, observational storytelling over polished commercial aesthetics; feature real people and authentic moments; treat the outside as context rather than the hero; favour everyday, intimate moments over epic outcomes; avoid overt brand narration; no competitor products (Merrell product is provided but must not be the focus); no AI-generated content in consumer-facing materials. Selection criteria are originality, emotional arc, a distinct point of view and cultural relevance. TIMELINE: submissions opened 28 July 2026; deadline 11:59 p.m. PT on Tuesday 18 August 2026 (08:59 CEST on 19 August); selected artists notified by 16 September 2026.   3. AIxDESIGN: Grain, Open Call for Critical AI Literacy Recipes

    AIxDESIGN, co-produced with the Netherlands Film Academy · Remote/online; open to educators and practitioners based anywhere · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Need-based fee of EUR 150 per recipe for freelance and independent practitioners: say so in the proposal form if making the time would be hard without it, and it is settled in the same reply.     Grain is a cookbook of critical and creative AI literacy exercises for educators in film and the wider audiovisual and creative field, co-produced by AIxDESIGN and the Netherlands Film Academy and led by Nadia Piet. It publishes in March/April 2027, digital and print, under CC BY-SA 4.0. The current open call is to propose a recipe by the end of August 2026 and join as a launch contributor. WHAT THEY WANT: tried-and-tested group exercises you have actually run with people in teaching or workshops, specifically in the context of film, audiovisual work, time-based media and storytelling. Hands-on or analog, 15 minutes or a full day, beginner or advanced; the one hard requirement is that another educator can read your recipe and run it themselves. What that might look like: hands-on experiments with specific AI tools (image generators, chatbots, code assistants, video models); analog activities that reveal computational logic through embodied practice; critical readings, watchings or listenings with structured discussion; provocations, games or role-plays that get at how AI sees, thinks or makes; and exercises that help people work out how they want to relate to AI in their own practice. WHAT MAKES A GOOD RECIPE: it helps a room of people think together and trusts the room to be smart given the right container; it emerges from real practice rather than an untested idea; it is specific about what participants do, step by step, so a stranger could pick it up and adapt it to their group, time, tools or context; it has at least one designed social moment where the thinking happens between people rather than privately; it is about something larger than technique (how a machine sees an image, where your values bump up against what the tools want from you, what a film's rhythm looks like laid out in space instead of time); it takes a position without telling people what to think; and it points beyond itself with references, reading and reference projects. Published example recipes include Image Forgery: Archival Mimesis with AI (Artefacto), Machine Ways of Seeing (Nadia Piet), AI Film Now: A Close Watching Exercise (Nadia Piet) and Outside the Timeline: Spatial Grid + Visual Clustering (Artefacto), alongside a root recipe template. WHAT LAUNCH CONTRIBUTORS GET: your recipe in the first edition, print and online, credited to you; a print copy when it lands and an invite to the launch event in Amsterdam in spring 2027; support turning your exercise into a recipe, via Recipe Press (their LLM helper), the cook-along, or directly with the team; and a need-based fee of EUR 150 per recipe for freelance and independent practitioners, which you request in the proposal form. After this round contributions stay open but web-only: no fee, no dedicated support, no print. COOK-ALONG: one live 30-minute online session on Wednesday 12 August 2026 at 16:00 CEST, where you bring a rough exercise and turn it into a recipe on the spot with Recipe Press (sign up at luma.com/84w148in). HOW IT WORKS: (1) propose your recipe through the proposal form (who you are, what the exercise is, where you have run it, and whether you are requesting the fee); a short pitch is enough, and if it is already written you can just link your draft; (2) they reply on a rolling basis, with one reply covering both whether it is a match for the first edition and the fee if you requested it, so the earlier you send the earlier you hear back; (3) once it is a yes, write it up using the structure on the Contribute a Recipe page, with Recipe Press or at the cook-along. TIMELINE: proposals open until the end of August 2026, reviewed as they come in; accepted recipes due in full by mid-September 2026; Grain publishes March/April 2027. Questions to hello@aixdesign.co.

    Normsy, a project of Civic Health Project, runs a small methods incubator for new strategies to measure or estimate the impact of prosocial social-media interventions. Normsy identifies toxic posts (typically content attacking or violating civic norms) and offers real users a set of AI-generated prosocial comments to choose from and post; the open question is how to estimate the effect of those comments on content producers, other commenters and bystanders who consume content without interacting measurably. The current approach combines survey experiments (how active X users respond emotionally to threads with and without the intervention, and how it shifts agreement with and support for the original post) with paid X API platform data (classifying comments before and after an intervention, plus coarse engagement metrics that cost money each time they are recollected). WHAT THEY WANT: a systematic way to leverage available platform data and maximise the utility of the survey experiments. Example proposals include a strategy for measuring toxicity/prosociality and reach/visibility of a thread over time with a randomisation design generating a control group of threads, or an approach to benchmarking the prosocial potential of AI-generated comments through an iterative arena with human judges or a theory-backed LLM validation approach with human validation. The brief is explicit that methodological innovation, causal identification, scalable measurement frameworks and novel observational approaches matter far more than proposing new intervention types; proposals need not study Normsy exclusively but must explain how the evaluation design transfers to it. Ethics and IRB considerations are central and Normsy will collaborate with selected researchers on ethics review and implementation planning. Researchers retain publication independence and IP; the partnership is expected to produce data for an academic paper. SUBMIT: one zipped folder through the linked Google Form containing a 3-page proposal (design, work already done, work still needed to scale and implement), a budget document for the USD 20,000, a timeline with concrete deliverables inside 6 months, and short bios for all team members. Award notifications mid-October 2026. Questions to kylan@civichealthproject.org; informal and exploratory conversations welcome.

But we can live our lives over again', the call proposes that when a single 'me' encounters others and reconnects relationships, a new consciousness of 'us' emerges. Art projects are treated as a form of cultural prescription, reconnecting people with people, people with places and people with memories. ELIGIBILITY: open to artists, curators, researchers, collectives and practitioners in related fields active in Japan and abroad; applicants must be 18 or older at the time of application; there are no restrictions on nationality, place of residence or base of operations; participants must be able to work in Tokyo during the designated period. ACCEPTED FORMATS: artwork creation, exhibitions, performances, video works, tours, workshops, participatory projects, research projects and curatorial projects. Applications cover both artwork production by artists and art project proposals by curators. APPLY via the Google Form linked from socialdive.jp/en (a Google account is required) during the application period 17 July to 30 September 2026, 23:59 JST. Read the official call documentation for full eligibility requirements and guidelines.

  22. ESA Open Space Innovation Platform (OSIP): rolling open call for ideas

    European Space Agency (ESA) · Ideas can be submitted from anywhere in the world via ideas.esa.int. To implement a funded idea yourself, you or your affiliated entity must be eligible to do business with ESA under its procurement regulations, which in practice means being based in an ESA member state (the Netherlands qualifies). · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Varies by route and campaign. Successful ideas are supported through PhD or postdoctoral research co-funded by ESA and universities, early technology development activities, or system studies. Free to submit.

    OSIP is the European Space Agency's standing front door for unsolicited novel ideas, launched in 2019 and now the main entry point for new concepts into ESA, both in response to specific problems and through open calls. HOW IT WORKS: ideas are submitted through two mechanisms. Campaigns are targeted calls responding to specific questions with defined timelines (recent examples include Future Navigation Demonstrators and elements for the EOCognitiveLab mission); Channels are permanently open, open-ended calls on broader topics with no deadline. Submissions can go through an optional community discussion phase, with voting and comments, before expert evaluation by ESA. WHO CAN SUBMIT: individuals, businesses, universities and organisations anywhere in the world. The catch for funding: to implement a funded idea yourself, you must be eligible to do business with ESA under Article 18 of its procurement regulations, which in practice means affiliation with an entity in an ESA member state. WHAT FUNDING LOOKS LIKE: successful ideas are typically supported as co-funded PhD or postdoctoral research (ESA plus a university), early technology development activities, or system studies; amounts vary by route and campaign. WHY IT IS ON THE DESK: this is the one genuinely public, always-open route into ESA for independent researchers, technologists and artists working with space data or space technology concepts; new campaigns appear regularly, so checking the platform periodically is worthwhile. Submit via ideas.esa.int.
