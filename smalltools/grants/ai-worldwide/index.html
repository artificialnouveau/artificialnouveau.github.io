  2. AIxDESIGN: Grain, Open Call for Critical AI Literacy Recipes

    AIxDESIGN, co-produced with the Netherlands Film Academy · Remote/online; open to educators and practitioners based anywhere · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Need-based fee of EUR 150 per recipe for freelance and independent practitioners: say so in the proposal form if making the time would be hard without it, and it is settled in the same reply. Published under CC BY-SA 4.0; you keep authorship. No application fee.

    Grain is a cookbook of critical and creative AI literacy exercises for educators in film and the wider audiovisual and creative field, co-produced by AIxDESIGN and the Netherlands Film Academy and led by Nadia Piet. It publishes in March/April 2027, digital and print, under CC BY-SA 4.0. The current open call is to propose a recipe by the end of August 2026 and join as a launch contributor. WHAT THEY WANT: tried-and-tested group exercises you have actually run with people in teaching or workshops, specifically in the context of film, audiovisual work, time-based media and storytelling. Hands-on or analog, 15 minutes or a full day, beginner or advanced; the one hard requirement is that another educator can read your recipe and run it themselves. What that might look like: hands-on experiments with specific AI tools (image generators, chatbots, code assistants, video models); analog activities that reveal computational logic through embodied practice; critical readings, watchings or listenings with structured discussion; provocations, games or role-plays that get at how AI sees, thinks or makes; and exercises that help people work out how they want to relate to AI in their own practice. WHAT MAKES A GOOD RECIPE: it helps a room of people think together and trusts the room to be smart given the right container; it emerges from real practice rather than an untested idea; it is specific about what participants do, step by step, so a stranger could pick it up and adapt it to their group, time, tools or context; it has at least one designed social moment where the thinking happens between people rather than privately; it is about something larger than technique (how a machine sees an image, where your values bump up against what the tools want from you, what a film's rhythm looks like laid out in space instead of time); it takes a position without telling people what to think; and it points beyond itself with references, reading and reference projects. Published example recipes include Image Forgery: Archival Mimesis with AI (Artefacto), Machine Ways of Seeing (Nadia Piet), AI Film Now: A Close Watching Exercise (Nadia Piet) and Outside the Timeline: Spatial Grid + Visual Clustering (Artefacto), alongside a root recipe template. WHAT LAUNCH CONTRIBUTORS GET: your recipe in the first edition, print and online, credited to you; a print copy when it lands and an invite to the launch event in Amsterdam in spring 2027; support turning your exercise into a recipe, via Recipe Press (their LLM helper), the cook-along, or directly with the team; and a need-based fee of EUR 150 per recipe for freelance and independent practitioners, which you request in the proposal form. After this round contributions stay open but web-only: no fee, no dedicated support, no print. COOK-ALONG: one live 30-minute online session on Wednesday 12 August 2026 at 16:00 CEST, where you bring a rough exercise and turn it into a recipe on the spot with Recipe Press (sign up at luma.com/84w148in). HOW IT WORKS: (1) propose your recipe through the proposal form (who you are, what the exercise is, where you have run it, and whether you are requesting the fee); a short pitch is enough, and if it is already written you can just link your draft; (2) they reply on a rolling basis, with one reply covering both whether it is a match for the first edition and the fee if you requested it, so the earlier you send the earlier you hear back; (3) once it is a yes, write it up using the structure on the Contribute a Recipe page, with Recipe Press or at the cook-along. TIMELINE: proposals open until the end of August 2026, reviewed as they come in; accepted recipes due in full by mid-September 2026; Grain publishes March/April 2027. Questions to hello@aixdesign.co.

  4. Normsy: Social Media Measurement Methods Incubator RFP

    Normsy (a project of Civic Health Project) · Remote. Open to individuals and teams from academic, academic-adjacent and industry backgrounds; no stated country restriction. Research context is X/Twitter (proposals may be multi-platform but must include X). · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 1,000 awarded to each of the top 5 proposals, plus up to USD 20,000 to fund R&D and/or testing with one selected partner individual or team. Preference for budgets where at least 50 percent goes to research costs (survey respondents, LLM or X API credits). No application fee.

    Normsy, a project of Civic Health Project, runs a small methods incubator for new strategies to measure or estimate the impact of prosocial social-media interventions. Normsy identifies toxic posts (typically content attacking or violating civic norms) and offers real users a set of AI-generated prosocial comments to choose from and post; the open question is how to estimate the effect of those comments on content producers, other commenters and bystanders who consume content without interacting measurably. The current approach combines survey experiments (how active X users respond emotionally to threads with and without the intervention, and how it shifts agreement with and support for the original post) with paid X API platform data (classifying comments before and after an intervention, plus coarse engagement metrics that cost money each time they are recollected). WHAT THEY WANT: a systematic way to leverage available platform data and maximise the utility of the survey experiments. Example proposals include a strategy for measuring toxicity/prosociality and reach/visibility of a thread over time with a randomisation design generating a control group of threads, or an approach to benchmarking the prosocial potential of AI-generated comments through an iterative arena with human judges or a theory-backed LLM validation approach with human validation. The brief is explicit that methodological innovation, causal identification, scalable measurement frameworks and novel observational approaches matter far more than proposing new intervention types; proposals need not study Normsy exclusively but must explain how the evaluation design transfers to it. Ethics and IRB considerations are central and Normsy will collaborate with selected researchers on ethics review and implementation planning. Researchers retain publication independence and IP; the partnership is expected to produce data for an academic paper. SUBMIT: one zipped folder through the linked Google Form containing a 3-page proposal (design, work already done, work still needed to scale and implement), a budget document for the USD 20,000, a timeline with concrete deliverables inside 6 months, and short bios for all team members. Award notifications mid-October 2026. Questions to kylan@civichealthproject.org; informal and exploratory conversations welcome.

  10. Physical AI Safety Institute: Research Fellowship (expression of interest)

    Physical AI Safety Institute (PAISI) · Not stated in the call. PAISI is a US 501(c)(3) nonprofit and its team is affiliated with UC Berkeley and the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago; because fellows are given access to physical robot platforms, expect an in-person or hybrid component. No nationality restriction is stated for expressions of interest. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Stipend amount is not published. Fellows receive a stipend, access to physical robot platforms, compute for simulation and training, and structured mentorship. PAISI was seeded by a grant from BlueDot Impact.     Robot foundation models (pi-zero, OpenVLA, GR00T and others) are approaching widespread deployment on the back of more than USD 18 billion in funding, and almost nobody is working on their safety. The Physical AI Safety Institute, a new nonprofit founded by Bear Haon and Kaylene Stocking and seeded by a grant from BlueDot Impact, describes itself as a capacity-building organisation growing the community that develops techniques to interpret, align and control robot foundation models. Its research fellowship runs in 12-week cohorts: each fellow works within a stream led by a mentor on a concrete problem in physical AI safety, with mentors drawn from researchers with track records in robot foundation models, mechanistic interpretability, safe control or adversarial robustness for embodied systems. Each cohort ends with a research symposium where fellows present their work. STREAMS: mechanistic interpretability (sparse autoencoders and activation patching for VLA and WAM architectures); adversarial robustness (jailbreaking and prompt injection in the embodied action space); runtime monitoring (detecting when a policy operates outside its competence envelope); sim-to-real safety transfer (do safety behaviours survive deployment on physical hardware); and robot security (cyberattack vectors, data poisoning and supply chain for deployed robot models). WHO SHOULD APPLY: researchers with strong technical foundations in robotics or machine learning and a demonstrated interest in safety; prior safety experience is not required. PROCESS: this stage is an expression of interest, not a full application. The form takes about two minutes and asks you to pick your stream and preferred cohort dates; concrete details including stipend figures and application deadlines are shared through that channel, and neither is published on the site as of listing. PAISI also publishes seven free curriculum modules running from classical robot safety through VLA interpretability, adversarial robustness and runtime monitoring to deployment evaluation, plus a white paper, The Case for Physical AI Safety, at paisi.ai/the-case-for-physical-ai-safety. Contact: bear@paisi.ai.

  11. AIXI Labs: Research Fellowship (rolling, quarterly rounds)

    AIXI Labs · Fully remote, open worldwide; fellows may also work from the organisation's London base in the United Kingdom. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 2,000 to GBP 3,500 per month stipend, plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. Free to apply.

    AIXI Labs is a small London-based non-profit doing AI safety research grounded in algorithmic information theory. Its stated position is that most AI research today is either mathematically clean but narrow, or practical but too opaque for rigorous safety analysis; the lab targets a third category, methods general enough to describe powerful agents and provable enough to support real safety claims, using AIXI (the theoretical model of unbounded artificial superintelligence) and then porting the strongest of those ideas to modern LLM-based agents. The fellowship is a quarterly, rolling programme for researchers who want to work on this agenda under close mentorship from the core team: Cole Wyeth (Founder and Executive Director), Marcus Hutter (Research Director) and Aram Ebtekar (Founding Research Scientist). Fellows pursue a focused research project over roughly a quarter and receive a monthly stipend plus a compute budget for machine-learning work. The fellowship is fully remote, so fellows may work from anywhere in the world or from the London base. RESPONSIBILITIES: pursue a self-directed research project aligned with the lab's algorithmic-information-theoretic approach to AI safety, spanning anything from foundational theory to empirical tests with LLM-based agents; shape the project direction around your own strengths and interests, as the lab supports a range of work within this framework; and contribute papers, blog posts or other written output communicating results to the broader AI safety and ML research communities. MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS: a research background at the level of a current PhD student (for primarily empirical projects, a master's-level command of the relevant mathematics paired with substantial industry or applied research experience can also qualify); solid grounding in probability, statistics, information theory and reinforcement learning, sufficient to engage seriously with AIXI-style theoretical models (prior expertise in algorithmic information theory or AIXI is NOT required); strong verbal and written communication skills; genuine motivation to reduce existential risk from advanced AI and enthusiasm for the lab's kind of work. PREFERRED QUALIFICATIONS: publications at top ML, AI or theory venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ALT or COLT, though the lab values the strength of your best work over volume; background in algorithmic information theory, computability theory, learning theory or related theoretical computer science; experience fine-tuning, evaluating or red-teaming LLM-based and/or RL agents; prior engagement with the UAI/AIXI community or related fields, including members of the lab's online reading groups. APPLICATIONS are reviewed on a rolling basis and previous applicants remain eligible for later rounds.
