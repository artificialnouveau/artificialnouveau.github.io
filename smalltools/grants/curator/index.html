Open calls

  1. World of Art / SCCA-Ljubljana: Award for Curatorial Practices 2026

    World of Art School, SCCA-Ljubljana, with Moderna galerija (MG+MSUM), Cukrarna/MGML and Center for Contemporary Arts Celje · Ljubljana, Slovenia. For curators working in Slovenia's cultural sector, or Slovenian curators realizing projects in international institutions. · Deadline: 23 Oct 2026 · Award: Two cash awards: a Large Award of EUR 5,000 gross (minimum 10 completed projects in the last 15 years) and a Small Award of EUR 2,500 gross (minimum 3 completed projects in the last 5 years). No application fee.

    To mark the 30th anniversary of its World of Art school for curatorial practices and writing, SCCA-Ljubljana establishes the Award for Curatorial Practices, recognizing outstanding achievements in curating contemporary visual art. The award is organized in partnership with Moderna galerija (MG+MSUM), Cukrarna/MGML and the Center for Contemporary Arts Celje, with support from the Slovenian Ministry of Culture. WHAT YOU GET: two tiers are awarded in 2026, a Large Award of EUR 5,000 gross for an established practice (minimum 10 completed curatorial projects within the last 15 years) and a Small Award of EUR 2,500 gross for an emerging practice (minimum 3 completed projects within the last 5 years). ELIGIBILITY: curators working within Slovenia's cultural sector, or Slovenian curators whose projects have been realized in international institutions. APPLICATION: submit a single PDF (max 5MB) containing a portfolio, a curatorial statement, a biography and a statement on the relevance of the practice (each up to one A4 page), plus consent, via the Google Form linked from the call; materials in English, filename formatted NAME_SURNAME_Award for Curatorial Practices_2026. DEADLINE: 23 October 2026, with the ceremony in December 2026.

  2. Asymmetry Assistant Curator Fellowship 2027-2029 at Nottingham Contemporary

    Asymmetry Art Foundation, with Nottingham Contemporary · Nottingham Contemporary, Nottingham, UK. Open internationally to early-career curators who identify with Chinese or Sinophone cultures or backgrounds; visa and relocation support provided. · Deadline: 25 Oct 2026 · Award: Annual salary of GBP 27,556 for two years, plus pension and National Insurance contributions, annual leave in line with organisational policy plus UK bank holidays, visa and legal fee assistance where required, and a travel and relocation allowance. No application fee.

    Asymmetry Art Foundation and Nottingham Contemporary offer a fully funded two-year Assistant Curator Fellowship embedded in the curatorial team of one of Europe's leading contemporary art institutions. The fellowship is geared towards an early-career curator who, to any extent, identifies with Chinese or Sinophone cultures or backgrounds, continuing Asymmetry's programme of curatorial fellowships at UK institutions (previous placements include Chisenhale Gallery and Whitechapel Gallery). WHAT YOU DO: contribute to exhibitions, artist commissions, live programmes, research, writing and institutional development, working across all stages of projects while developing independent curatorial work and joining Asymmetry's international network of scholars and practitioners. WHAT YOU GET: an annual salary of GBP 27,556 plus pension and National Insurance contributions, annual leave plus UK bank holidays, visa and legal fee assistance where required, and a travel and relocation allowance. ELIGIBILITY: early-career curators with roughly 1 to 3 years of experience, an educational background in art history, cultural studies or related fields, familiarity with contemporary artistic practices, direct experience collaborating with artists, and proficiency in English. Applications must be in English; accessibility support is available via fellowship@asymmetryart.org. APPLICATION: via the Asymmetry application portal. DEADLINE: 25 October 2026. The fellowship runs 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2029.

  3. Stroom Den Haag: PRO Art Program

    Stroom Den Haag · The Hague, Netherlands. Aimed at non-profit organisations, artist initiatives, collectives and independent curators/organisers; applicants from outside The Hague apply with a CV, provided the programme benefits The Hague's art climate. · Deadline: 25 Oct 2026 · Award: Maximum EUR 10,000 per application, up to EUR 20,000 per applicant per calendar year. Requests above EUR 5,000 require at least 30 percent co-financing of the total budget from third parties. Compliance with the kunstenaarshonorarium.nl artist fee guideline is part of the assessment. No application fee.

    Stroom Den Haag's PRO Art Program scheme contributes to the costs of an art programme: a series of activities (exhibitions, presentations, lectures, workshops, etc.) with an artistic, substantive, thematic and/or formal connection that take place within a certain period. It also covers exchange activities with other countries and programmes for guest studios and artist-in-residencies. WHO CAN APPLY: organisations that realise the programme on a non-profit basis, including artist initiatives, collectives and associations, plus independent curators and organisers. Applicants do not have to be based in The Hague: Hague artists apply via their Stroom registration (Mijn Stroom), outsiders apply with a CV, and all participants must provide CVs or current Stroom registration. ASSESSMENT: intrinsic quality and coherence, added value for The Hague's art climate, potential to catalyse broader activity, budget proportionality, audience engagement, and fair artist pay. APPLICATION: via the Stroom portal with an 80-word summary, a plan of max 2,500 words, a budget using Stroom's model, 10 to 20 images with a documentation list, and optionally up to 9 minutes of video. NOT ELIGIBLE: applications connected to formal (art) education programmes or from current students. DEADLINE: the last 2026 round closes 25 October 2026. The grant is valid for up to two years after the decision.

  4. The Photographers' Gallery: Future of Archives, Whose Archive is it Anyway? (Call for Speakers)

    The Photographers' Gallery · London, UK. Selected speakers must be available to speak in person at The Photographers' Gallery on 16 April 2027. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 200 speaker fee for each selected speaker. No application fee.

    Whose Archive is it Anyway? is the first in a three-part series of events at The Photographers' Gallery exploring the future of archives and archiving. This one-day event on Friday 16 April 2027 kickstarts the conversation with commissioned talks addressing what it means to make an archive today, who decides what an archive consists of, who creates an archive, who an archive is for, and who has access. The gallery welcomes submissions of real-life experiences rather than academic papers that challenge or answer these or other points related to the future of archives. SUGGESTED TOPICS: artist and photographers' legacies; institutional archives; memory (forgotten or lost histories made visible again); truth within archives (who is telling the stories and how); ownership (who has the authority to tell stories, who owns the images and their stories); relationships between those who create archives and those who use them; radical, non-traditional archives that redesign how stories are gathered and held to safeguard living memories; family archives; reclaimed photographic archives; counter archives that question dominant historical narratives; grassroots archives; technologies that reduce barriers to archives while safeguarding material; digital preservation and digitisation; and dialogic archives. ELIGIBILITY: open to all, especially those with experience of working with archives and the stories within them; applicants must be 18 or older and available to attend The Photographers' Gallery in London on 16 April 2027 to speak at the event. Selected speakers receive a fee of GBP 200 and will be notified by 18 January 2027. Questions to archive@tpg.org.uk. Deadline 1 November 2026, 11:59pm GMT.

  5. Alkazi Foundation: Curatorial Research Fellowship 2027 (with CWIT and the V&A)

    Alkazi Foundation for the Arts, with the Charles Wallace India Trust and the Victoria and Albert Museum · New Delhi, India (organiser); the funded research residency takes place at the V&A Photography Section, London. RESTRICTED: Indian citizens domiciled in India, aged 25 to 45, mid-career. · Deadline: 07 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 1,600 monthly stipend for 3 to 6 months, plus a GBP 800 contribution from CWIT towards international return airfare; visa expenses covered by the Alkazi Foundation. No application fee.

    A funded research residency (3rd edition of the Visiting Curatorial Research Fellowship) for mid-career cultural producers from India: artists, scholars, writers/editors and curators pursuing humanities-driven research in the V&A's global photography and visual-culture collections in London. The awardee is announced in February 2027 and the fellowship starts April 2027. SUPPORT: GBP 1,600 per month, GBP 800 towards return airfare (CWIT) and visa costs covered. ELIGIBILITY: Indian citizens domiciled in India, aged 25 to 45, mid-career. APPLY by email to alkazifoundation01@gmail.com with subject 'Curatorial Research Fellowship 2027'. DEADLINE: 7 November 2026.

  6. UCLA Modern Endangered Archives Program (MEAP): 2026-27 Call for Applications

    UCLA Library, Modern Endangered Archives Program · Worldwide. Projects digitize at-risk collections where they are held; digitized materials are published open access in the UCLA Library Digital Collections. · Deadline: 16 Nov 2026 · Award: Planning Grants up to USD 20,000 (up to 1 year) for collection evaluation, survey and inventory work before digitization. Project Grants up to USD 70,000 (up to 2 years) for digitization and metadata creation on well-organized collections, or curation of existing digital assets. Regional Grants up to USD 100,000 (up to 2 years) for digital collections drawing on three or more institutions, families or repositories around one theme, hosted by an organization with prior MEAP experience. A separate AV Grants track (up to USD 100,000, for audio, video and film) opens in April 2027. No application fee.

    The UCLA Library Modern Endangered Archives Program (MEAP), funded by Arcadia, grants its ninth cohort of awards to preserve, document and digitize cultural heritage collections at risk from environmental conditions, political uncertainty, inherently unsustainable media, inappropriate storage, or communal and social change. IN SCOPE: 20th and 21st century materials, including print, photographic, film, video, audio, ephemeral and born-digital objects; all funded materials are published open access in the UCLA Library Digital Collections repository. GRANT TYPES: Planning Grants up to USD 20,000 over one year for organization, survey and inventory work before digitization; Project Grants up to USD 70,000 over two years for digitization and metadata creation on collections ready to digitize, or for curating existing digital assets; Regional Grants up to USD 100,000 over two years for digital collections combining materials from three or more institutions, families or archival repositories around one theme, community or historical event, hosted by an organizing institution with prior MEAP grant experience (consult MEAP staff before applying). There is also an invitation-only Emergency Grant track up to USD 10,000 (email meap@library.ucla.edu), and a new AV Grants track up to USD 100,000 for audiovisual archives opening April 2027 with selections in September 2027. TIMELINE: applications opened 14 September 2026; preliminary application webinar 15 October 2026; preliminary applications due Monday 16 November 2026 at 11:59pm Pacific Time via meap.smapply.io; preliminary decisions December 2026; detailed applications due 12 February 2027; awards announced summer 2027. Applications name teams working with the institutions, families or repositories that hold the collections; read the Program Guidelines and Project Eligibility documents on the MEAP site before applying.

  7. VisArts: Emerging Curator Program 2027 (Rockville, Maryland)

    VisArts · VisArts, Rockville Town Square, Rockville, Maryland, USA. International applicants must already be eligible to work in the United States (no visa assistance). · Deadline: 18 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 10,000 budget covering exhibition costs and curatorial fees, plus a year of mentorship and staff support for printing, promotion and programming. No application fee.

    VisArts, a nonprofit arts center in Rockville, Maryland, pairs one emerging curator with mentoring curator Fabiola R. Delgado for a year-long program culminating in an exhibition at the Kaplan Gallery. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 10,000 budget covering exhibition costs and curatorial fees, year-long mentorship, and staff support for printing, promotions and exhibition programming. The mentoring curator's exhibition runs 27 August to 10 October 2027; the Emerging Curator's exhibition runs 15 October 2027 to 9 January 2028. ELIGIBILITY: emerging curators or artists with an interest in exhibition-making or curating; international applicants must be eligible to work in the United States (VisArts cannot provide or assist with visas). APPLICATION: free, via Submittable; application form, CV, evidence of past curatorial projects (up to 10 images, up to 5 minutes of video/audio, image list and supporting PDFs) and a statement on goals, curatorial interests, audience enrichment and community engagement. TIMELINE: finalists notified 8 January 2027, interviews 13 to 15 January, selection announced 20 January 2027. DEADLINE: 18 December 2026, midnight.

  8. A4 Residency Art Center: International Residency Fellowship 2028 (Chengdu)

    A4 Residency Art Center (A4 Art Museum) · Luxelakes Eco-City, Tianfu New Area, Chengdu, China. Open worldwide. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: Fully funded fellowship: private studio and accommodation, round-trip economy airfare or second-class train ticket within a set budget, and a production grant of RMB 10,000 per person/team for materials and production (excluding living expenses). No application fee.

    The A4 Residency Art Center in Chengdu, the residency platform of the A4 Art Museum in Luxelakes Eco-City, opens its full-year 2028 recruitment. The interdisciplinary, open and collaborative platform fosters creative exchange among artists, scientists, writers and other professionals, with residents initiating participatory local activities that connect them with the city and community; A4 also organises group activities, sharing sessions, media promotion and exhibition opportunities. CYCLES: Spring 2028 (April to June) and Autumn 2028 (July to October), each approximately eight weeks. WHAT YOU GET (International Residency Fellowship, fully funded): private studio and accommodation, round-trip economy airfare or second-class train within a set budget, a production grant of RMB 10,000 per person or team for materials and production (excluding living expenses), community engagement and cultural experiences, media promotion and networking. ELIGIBILITY: multidisciplinary creators worldwide, including visual and performing artists, musicians, filmmakers, architects, curators, designers and creatives with interdisciplinary backgrounds bridging art, business and culture. APPLICATION: free; submit a photo, portfolio, CV and project proposal in English and Chinese to a4residency@a4am.cn, with the subject line specifying the programme tier and preferred cycle. DEADLINE: 31 December 2026 for the 2028 full-year recruitment. A parallel self-funded light track exists; this listing covers the funded fellowship only.

  10. apexart Open Calls: Curatorial Proposals for NYC and International Exhibitions

    apexart · Exhibitions in apexart's New York City space (October call) or anywhere in the world except NYC (February call). Anyone, from anywhere, can submit. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 10,000 per selected exhibition: USD 8,000 for expenses (curator travel, artwork shipping, artist and participant honorariums, equipment, installation) plus an honorarium of up to USD 2,000 for the curator/organizer. For exhibitions outside NYC, apexart matches up to USD 1,000 for venue rental if needed. apexart additionally covers insurance, advertising, publicity, brochure design/printing/mailing, the opening reception and (for NYC) documentation, and handles most logistics. No application fee.

    apexart's Open Calls turn a curatorial idea into a funded apexart exhibition, selected by a crowdsourced international jury of more than 600 people in 70 countries. WHO CAN SUBMIT: anyone, from anywhere, with no fees at any level: trained and student curators, artists, writers and creative people regardless of experience, background or location. HOW: a 500-word proposal for a focused, idea-driven, original group exhibition, submitted through the online form during the proposal window. TWO ANNUAL WINDOWS: every October for exhibitions in apexart's NYC space, and every February (to early March) for exhibitions taking place anywhere in the world except NYC; for the international call you must propose a location you have actually visited, with the specific venue settled after selection. SELECTION: jurors vote anonymously online (each proposal gets an equal number of views, submitter names hidden); proposals are judged on the strength of the idea and how well it is communicated, not on connections or CV. WHAT WINNERS GET: a USD 10,000 budget (USD 8,000 for expenses, up to USD 2,000 curator honorarium, plus up to USD 1,000 venue-rental match outside NYC), with apexart covering insurance, publicity, the printed brochure, the opening reception and most logistics including loan agreements and shipping, and staff working closely with the guest curator. Winning proposals become part of apexart's next exhibition season. Check the site for the current window's exact dates; submission forms only go live during the proposal periods.

  11. FfAI (Foundation for Arts Initiatives): Research & Travel Grants (USD 10,000, rolling)

    Foundation for Arts Initiatives (FfAI) · Worldwide (independent research and travel; US organizations must be 501(c)(3), non-US organizations charitable/educational equivalents) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 10,000 for individual research grants; institutional grants typically USD 10,000 to 25,000 (organizations with annual budgets under USD 1,000,000). No application fee.

    FfAI gives grants to individuals active in visual art and culture (artists, curators, critics, researchers, writers, organizers) for independent travel and research, and to small institutions for general activities and projects. AMOUNTS: USD 10,000 for individual research grants; institutional grants typically USD 10,000 to 25,000, limited to organizations with annual budgets under USD 1,000,000 (US organizations must be IRS 501(c)(3) nonprofits; non-US organizations must be created and operated exclusively for charitable and/or educational purposes, with a signed statement from an authorized officer). NOT FUNDED: scholarships, residency programs, production of work, or major equipment; research and travel must be independent of institutional or professional obligations, and funds cannot be transferred to anyone else. PROCESS: two-stage and rolling; anyone can submit an inquiry via the form at apply.ffaiarts.net, but a full proposal must be invited; if you do not hear back within four months, the inquiry was not selected. DEADLINE: none (rolling inquiries). NOTE: there is no conventional 'open call' page; the grants page only sketches the two grant types, the guidelines page holds the amounts and rules, and the entry point is the inquiry form at apply.ffaiarts.net.
