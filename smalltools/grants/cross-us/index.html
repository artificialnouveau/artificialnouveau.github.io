Open calls

  1. Culture Push: Fellowship for Utopian Practice 2027 (New York City)

    Culture Push · New York City, US. Project work must occur within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of NYC. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 2,250 stipend, plus fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, feedback and mentoring. No application fee.

    Culture Push, the New York nonprofit founded by artists to support hybrid art and civic experimental practice, selects 6 to 8 fellows per cycle for its yearlong Fellowship for Utopian Practice. The fellowship backs boundary-pushing, socially engaged projects that are new and without prior institutional support. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 2,250 stipend, fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, and structured feedback and mentoring across the year. ELIGIBILITY: minimum of three years of experience; currently enrolled undergraduates cannot apply; group and collaborative proposals are welcome; project work must take place within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of New York City (not strictly NYC residents). The same deadline also covers the 2027 Black Utopian Fellowship, Disability Arts + Dreaming Fellowship, and Climate Justice Fellowship. DEADLINE: 15 October 2026 at 11:59 pm ET. Fellowship runs January to December 2027.

  2. Culture Push: Black Utopian Fellowship 2027 (New York City)

    Culture Push · New York City, US. Project work must occur within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of NYC. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 2,250 stipend, plus fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, mentorship and strategizing support, per the umbrella Fellowship for Utopian Practice guidelines. No application fee.

    The Black Utopian Fellowship is a yearlong incubator fellowship created by #DayOnesArt in collaboration with Culture Push, for Black artists, researchers, scientists and inventors, with career development and mentorship through Black-run institutions. The 2027 call, themed Return to Real, asks for project proposals that critique what is real in 2026 around themes such as economics, health, artificial intelligence, culture, politics, media and the education system, and that explore how legacy is taught, inherited and reimagined across generations. The Fellowship for Utopian Practice is the umbrella program and the same guidelines apply: a USD 2,250 stipend, fiscal sponsorship, mentoring, and access to Materials for the Arts. ELIGIBILITY: Black artists and other professionals in any discipline with at least 3 years of experience; currently enrolled undergraduates cannot apply; group proposals welcome; projects must be new, socially engaged, without prior institutional support, and grounded in a specific NYC neighborhood or community; fellows must live within commuting distance of New York City. Applicants may apply to only one Culture Push fellowship per cycle; unselected themed applications pass into the General Fellowship pool. CONTACT: buf@culturepush.org. DEADLINE: 15 October 2026 at 11:59 pm ET.

  3. Culture Push: Disability Arts + Dreaming Fellowship 2027 (New York City)

    Culture Push · New York City, US. Project work must occur within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of NYC. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 2,250 stipend, plus fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, a Thought Partner, mentors in the field, and access check-ins, per the umbrella Fellowship for Utopian Practice guidelines. No application fee.

    Culture Push's yearlong Disability Arts + Dreaming Fellowship supports self-identifying disabled artists, organizers, creative thinkers and collectives developing disability-centered, seed-stage projects that treat Access as Art or collective accessibility and advance emergent Disability Culture and Justice within the five boroughs of NYC. It centers Disability Justice as a way of moving together across disability, race, sexuality, gender and class, with mixed-abilities programming, access check-ins, co-thinking and strategizing meetings across the fellowship year. The Fellowship for Utopian Practice is the umbrella program and the same guidelines apply: a USD 2,250 stipend, fiscal sponsorship, a Thought Partner and mentors in the field. ELIGIBILITY: at least 3 years of experience; currently enrolled undergraduates cannot apply; group proposals welcome; projects must be new, socially engaged, without prior institutional support, and grounded in a specific NYC neighborhood or community; fellows must live within commuting distance of New York City. Applicants may apply to only one Culture Push fellowship per cycle; unselected themed applications pass into the General Fellowship pool. DEADLINE: 15 October 2026 at 11:59 pm ET.

  4. Culture Push: Climate Justice Fellowship 2027 (New York City)

    Culture Push · New York City, US. Project work must occur within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of NYC. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 2,250 stipend, plus fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, mentorship and strategizing support, per the umbrella Fellowship for Utopian Practice guidelines. No application fee.

    Culture Push's yearlong Climate Justice Fellowship invites artists, researchers and activists to develop community-based projects exploring the intersection of climate change and urban socio-political life in New York City, across site-specific geographies and topics such as technology, housing, parks, urban forests, food systems, watersheds, pollution and infrastructures. The Fellowship for Utopian Practice is the umbrella program and the same guidelines apply: a USD 2,250 stipend, fiscal sponsorship, mentoring, and access to Materials for the Arts. ELIGIBILITY: at least 3 years of experience; currently enrolled undergraduates cannot apply; group proposals welcome; projects must be new, socially engaged, without prior institutional support (pre-existing performances, established projects and fully funded works are not eligible), and grounded in a specific NYC neighborhood or community; fellows must live within commuting distance of New York City. Applicants may apply to only one Culture Push fellowship per cycle; unselected themed applications pass into the General Fellowship pool. DEADLINE: 15 October 2026 at 11:59 pm ET.

  5. Humanity AI: Open Call for Proposals 2026

    Humanity AI (coalition of 10 philanthropies) · United States. Lead applicants must be US-based 501(c)(3) organizations (or Tribal governments); all proposals must serve US-based communities. Individuals can apply through a fiscal sponsor or as part of a collaboration. · Deadline: 21 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 10 million total pool, grants of USD 75,000 to 1,000,000 across three tiers: Tier 1 USD 75,000-150,000; Tier 2 USD 150,000-500,000; Tier 3 USD 500,000-1,000,000. Grants run up to three years. No application fee.

    Humanity AI, an initiative of ten leading philanthropies, opens a USD 10 million call for community-led initiatives that shape how AI affects people's lives. ISSUE AREAS: Education (student and educator-centered work shaping AI's educational impact); Labor and Economy (worker-led efforts to ensure AI enhances rather than replaces employment); and Humanities, Arts, and Culture (artist and creator-focused work protecting cultural contributions). ELIGIBLE APPROACHES: shaping the rules for AI governance and deployment; building AI controlled by communities rather than corporations; and challenging misaligned AI deployments. Community-led means the affected community defines the goals and shapes the approach. Priority goes to open-weight and open-source AI models over proprietary ones. OUT OF SCOPE: AI literacy training, AI adoption promotion, and research-only work. GRANTS: three tiers from USD 75,000 to 1,000,000, up to three years. ELIGIBILITY: US-based 501(c)(3) organizations as lead applicants; individuals via fiscal sponsors or collaborations; Tribal governments treated as States under IRC Section 7871; for-profit entities only as collaborators, not leads; all proposals must serve US-based communities. TIMELINE: launched 10 September 2026; applicant webinar 14 September 2026; initial applications due 21 October 2026 at 4:59pm PT; finalists notified December 2026; awards announced early spring 2027. Apply via Submittable; an organizational readiness tool on the site helps assess eligibility first.

  6. Open Interval: An Artist-Scientist Exploration of Turbulence

    Simons Foundation · United States (trio must be regionally co-located) · Deadline: 21 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 30,000 per awarded trio

    Open Interval is a Simons Foundation program that supports trios consisting of an artist, a scientist, and a representative from a 501(c)(3) arts organization to collaborate around a shared scientific theme. The current cycle centers on the theme of turbulence, a phenomenon at the edge of chaos and beauty. Awarded trios receive USD 30,000 to support a sustained period of thought partnership and exploration rather than the production of finished works or exhibitions. The program runs from April to December 2027 with a touchstone meeting in May 2027 and possible cohort convenings. Trio members must be regionally co-located anywhere in the United States so they can collaborate in person. The scientist should hold a Ph.D. and be conducting basic research, with priority given to fields such as astrophysics, neuroscience, quantum physics, biology, or mathematics. The application portal opens September 9, 2026, and all three trio members must submit for the application to be complete.

  7. Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans 2027 (US graduate study)

    The Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans · USA. For immigrants and children of immigrants pursuing graduate or professional study at US institutions. · Deadline: 29 Oct 2026 · Award: Up to USD 90,000 over two years (stipend and tuition support) toward graduate study; 30 fellowships awarded annually. No application fee.

    The Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans annually select 30 fellows based on their potential to make significant contributions to American society, culture, and their chosen fields, across disciplines including the arts. WHAT YOU GET: up to USD 90,000 over two years in stipend and tuition support for graduate study beginning fall 2027, plus mentorship, community, all-expenses-paid attendance at the Fall Conference, and lifelong connection to a network of nearly 900 fellows. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be an immigrant or the child of immigrant(s), meet the program's age requirements, and be in the first two years of a graduate or professional program or applying to one during the admissions process; school selectivity, GPA, and test scores are not used to determine eligibility, and reapplying is allowed. APPLICATION: free, via the online portal, with a resume or CV, two essays (1,000 words max each), transcripts, any program-required test scores, and 3 to 5 recommendation letters. TIMELINE: finalists notified mid-January 2027, virtual interviews in February, winners notified in March. DEADLINE: 29 October 2026, 2:00 PM EDT.

  8. Sanford Underground Research Facility: Artist-in-Residence Program 2027 (Lead, South Dakota)

    Sanford Underground Research Facility (SURF) · Sanford Underground Research Facility, Lead, South Dakota, USA, in the Black Hills. The call does not state a residency or nationality restriction; travel, housing and per diem for both visits are covered. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend paid in two installments, plus up to USD 5,000 in additional funds the artist may apply for to create the artwork. Transportation (airfare, rental car or mileage), housing and per diem are covered for both visits; a studio space and guided surface and underground tours are provided; return shipping of the exhibition is covered up to USD 7,000.

    The SURF Artist-in-Residence program invites artists to create work inspired by the Sanford Underground Research Facility in Lead, South Dakota. At 4,850 feet deep, SURF is the deepest underground laboratory in the United States, housed in the former Homestake Gold Mine, and hosts experiments in physics, biology, geology and engineering. The residency is modeled after programs run by the National Park Service, CERN and Fermilab, and aims to create awareness of the science and encourage interdisciplinary work. STRUCTURE: a four-week on-site visit in summer 2027 (between 22 May and 31 July, timed to the artist's schedule) with studio time, guided underground visits, tours of Lead and the surrounding Black Hills sites, and dinners with scientists; then a one-week visit in October 2027 to install an art exhibition, give up to two lectures based on the proposal, and run promotional or outreach events in the local community (which may include schools, universities, interviews or documentation of the residency). FUNDING: a USD 5,000 stipend paid in two installments; the artist may apply for up to USD 5,000 in additional funds for creating the artwork; transportation, housing and per diem for both visits; and return shipping covered up to USD 7,000. CONDITIONS: each artist donates at least one artwork, selected by a SURF representative, to SURF's permanent art collection, and is responsible for installation and de-installation in coordination with SURF and the exhibition space. A timeline and expectations are agreed in an MOU before the residency begins. PHYSICAL REQUIREMENTS: working underground requires being comfortable in dark spaces, following all safety protocols, walking on uneven surfaces for at least a mile at a time, and negotiating wet, slippery conditions and an elevator ride in the dark; safety training and loaned personal protective equipment are mandatory, and a SURF guide accompanies the artist at all times. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026, via the SURF AiR application portal.

  9. MIT Museum: BRAIN Digital Content Open Call

    MIT Museum · Cambridge, Massachusetts, US (content shown on-site; remote creation possible) · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Flat honorarium of USD 2,500 to 5,000 per selected proposal, at the Museum's discretion

    The MIT Museum seeks proposals for compelling digital content exploring humanity's relationship with the BRAIN, the museum's thematic focus from April to September 2027 bringing together art, science, technology and design on cognition, neurotechnology, brain-computer interfaces, mental health and the boundaries of ability. Selected proposals run as ambient content on the two-story digital screen (3840x3024, 80:63 aspect ratio, 20'3" x 16'2") in the museum's central Exchange amphitheater. The museum wants immersive, non-linear, experimental, non-didactic work, such as artful visualizations of data or scientific phenomena; interactivity (touchscreens, QR codes) is out of scope and content-critical audio is discouraged. Content must run on the museum's existing hardware (Windows PC with RTX 3090, NEC LED wall with Analog Way 4K system). WHO CAN APPLY: artists, scientists, engineers, designers and other creative practitioners, from MIT or the broader community. SUPPORT: flat honorarium of USD 2,500-5,000 per selected proposal; no additional development funding. Applications reviewed from 2 November, accepted list finalized January 2027. Deadline: 1 November 2026.
