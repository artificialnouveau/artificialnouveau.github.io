  1. Harvard Radcliffe Institute: Fellowship Program 2027-2028

    Harvard Radcliffe Institute · Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA; fellows must relocate to and reside in the Cambridge/Boston area for the full fellowship (September through May). · Deadline: 10 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 78,000 stipend plus an additional USD 5,000 for project expenses. Relocation, housing, and childcare funds may also be provided. No application fee.

    The Harvard Radcliffe Institute Fellowship offers scientists, writers, scholars, public intellectuals, and artists a year to pursue ambitious projects in a vibrant interdisciplinary setting amid the resources of Harvard University. The Institute welcomes innovative work that confronts pressing social, scientific, and policy issues and seeks to engage audiences beyond academia. Reflecting Radcliffe's history, it welcomes (but does not limit eligibility to) proposals focused on women, gender, and society, or that draw on the Schlesinger Library's collections. It also invites proposals relevant to the Institute's 2024-2029 focus area, academic freedom and connecting across difference (intellectual virtues, free and open inquiry, diversity of thought, political polarization, peace and conflict, inequality, religious pluralism, and related higher-education policy issues), including work that constructively challenges disciplinary orthodoxies or advances transformative perspectives. SUPPORT: USD 78,000 stipend plus USD 5,000 for project expenses, with relocation, housing, and childcare funds available; fellows must reside in the Cambridge/Boston area from September through May. ELIGIBILITY: open to individuals across career stages; tenure is not required and applicants need not be academics. This is NOT a postdoctoral fellowship; those currently enrolled in a degree program are ineligible, as are former Harvard Radcliffe fellows (1999-present). Applicants must meet discipline-specific criteria. APPLY: register on the online portal and select an area (Humanities and Social Sciences; Creative Arts; Nonfiction and Journalism; or Science, Engineering, and Mathematics). Materials: application form, CV, 1,400-word project proposal (with bibliography when appropriate), a writing or work sample, and three references. DEADLINES: humanities, social sciences, creative arts, and nonfiction and journalism by 10 September 2026, 5pm ET; science, engineering, and mathematics by 1 October 2026, 5pm ET.

  2. MAD Artist Studios Residency, Cycles 46 and 47

    Museum of Arts and Design (MAD) · Sixth floor of the Museum of Arts and Design, New York City, USA. Open to residents of the New York City metro area only. · Deadline: 11 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend based on the on-site public schedule: USD 15,000 for three public-facing days per week, or USD 10,000 for two days. Also provided: a dedicated studio of roughly 200 square feet in the Museum with worktable, sink, storage and Wi-Fi; a separate stipend for materials and mentorship; professional development and studio visits with museum staff; additional paid public programming opportunities; admission to paid public programmes; and access to MAD's galleries, public programmes and exhibition openings. Shared equipment includes a Skutt 1027 kiln, a Brent pottery wheel and a 22 inch floor loom. MAD does not pay travel costs or provide housing. Residents are one-time independent contractors and are responsible for their own taxes. Collectives may apply but are paid the individual stipend. APPLICATION FEE: USD 5 per application, with fee waivers available on request by emailing education@madmuseum.org before submitting.

    Established in 2008 and the first of its kind, the Artist Studios at MAD is a public-facing community engagement and professional development programme on the sixth floor of the Museum, where visitors meet artists-in-residence at work. Two artists are selected per six-month session (the application text elsewhere says three, so confirm with MAD). More than 200 artists and designers have come through the programme, which MAD describes as an innovative model for museum education, with a sustained focus on artists from historically underrepresented communities whose work pushes the boundaries of contemporary craft; alumni have exhibited at the Queens Museum, Whitney, Brooklyn Museum and Studio Museum. WHAT MAD MEANS BY CRAFT: both a discipline and a way of thinking, covering traditional studio craft materials such as clay, fiber, wood, glass, metal and stone, and craft-based methods used in new ways, including handwork combined with digital tools, performance or experimental materials. Craft here is defined by deep engagement with process, materiality and the hand rather than by medium. RESIDENCY EXPECTATIONS: this is a substantial public commitment, not quiet studio time. Residents commit to 14 or 21 public open studio hours a week on Thursdays, Fridays and/or Saturdays 10:30am to 5:30pm and/or Wednesdays 11:00am to 7:00pm, on a schedule fixed with MAD's Education Department for the whole six months. During those hours residents host K-12 school groups (in person and virtual) and public tour groups. Because of the contact with schoolchildren, all selected artists must pass a background check. ELIGIBILITY: an artist or designer with an active studio practice in the applied arts; a resident of the New York City metro area, defined as the five boroughs plus nearby New York, New Jersey and Connecticut counties within reasonable commuting distance, typically on MTA, PATH or NJ Transit; at least 21 at the time of application; and a US citizen or legal US resident with a valid SSN or EIN. The Museum does not sponsor visas and states this is not subject to exception. Programme alumni may not reapply, though unsuccessful past applicants may. SELECTION: the committee looks for a mature body of work engaging critically with the transdisciplinary fields of contemporary craft, design, art and technology, a strong conceptual framework, deep engagement with process and materials, a clear direction or question for the residency, and genuine readiness for the open studios format. Top applications go to interview with the Deputy Director of Education and Manager of Public Programs and Studios, and finalists' references are contacted. Applicants are strongly encouraged to visit the studios in person first. NOT AN EXHIBITION PROGRAMME: residents may show work in progress in their studio and finished work in the sixth-floor vitrines, but the residency does not include a gallery exhibition or a proposal for MAD's galleries. STUDIO RESTRICTIONS: no open flames, all hot work approved by the Director of Facilities, no large or unsafe power tools such as saws, and no toxic materials including turpentine, certain resins, glazes, lusters and lead-based paints. Applicants submit one application indicating their preferred schedule and which session or sessions they want, and are selected for one. Initial notifications October 2026, finalist interviews November 2026, offers December 2026. Questions: education@madmuseum.org. Deadline 11 September 2026.

  3. GLEAN 2027 Call for Artists

    GLEAN (Recology Oregon Recovery, Metro and ReClaim It), with Rainmaker Craft Initiative · Portland, Oregon, USA. Open to artists from Portland; work takes place at the Metro Central transfer station. · Deadline: 13 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of USD 3,000 per artist, increased from previous years. Five local artists are selected. Artists also receive five months of access to the Metro Central transfer station to gather discarded materials, and the programme culminates in a group exhibition at a local gallery. This round includes another collaboration with Rainmaker Craft Initiative. No application fee is mentioned.

    GLEAN, meaning to collect gradually bit by bit, is a juried art programme that taps the creativity of artists to make people think about their consumption habits, the waste they generate and the resources they throw away. Each year five local artists, chosen by a jury of arts and environmental professionals, get access to the Metro Central transfer station for five months to glean discarded materials and make work from them. The programme ends with a group exhibition of the results at a local gallery in the summer. The 2027 round carries an increased stipend of USD 3,000 per artist and another collaboration with Rainmaker Craft Initiative. Launched in 2010, GLEAN is a partnership between Recology Oregon Recovery Inc., an employee-owned company managing resource recovery facilities, Metro, the regional government running the Portland area's garbage and recycling system, and ReClaim It, a nonprofit environmental arts organization. ELIGIBILITY: artists from Portland, Oregon. Applications are open 1 August to 13 September 2026.

Open to citizens and permanent residents of the United States and Canada. · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: Variable cash grant sized to the applicant's proposed budget and needs; the Foundation does not publish a fixed figure or range, and recent awards have typically run into the tens of thousands of US dollars. Around 175 to 200 fellows are chosen each year from a field of several thousand applicants (a success rate of roughly five percent), across more than 50 fields spanning the creative arts, the humanities, the natural sciences and the social sciences, including architecture, film, music, photography, fine arts, computer science and interdisciplinary work. ELIGIBILITY: citizens and permanent residents of the United States and Canada; applicants must be mid-career with a significant record of published or exhibited work, and current students and previous Guggenheim Fellows are not eligible. APPLICATION: through the Foundation's own portal, which opens in late summer 2026, so check the site if it is not yet live. The application deadline is 15 September 2026 with no late submissions accepted; work samples are due 29 September 2026 and reference letters 13 October 2026. Results are announced in late April 2027. No fee.

  5. Jentel Artist Residency

    The Jentel Artist Residency offers approximately three-week residencies in the foothills of the Bighorn Mountains near Banner, Wyoming, giving artists, writers, and composers dedicated time for creative work. There is a USD 30 non-refundable application fee, with deadlines of September 15 for Winter and Spring sessions and January 15 for Summer and Fall sessions.

  7. Artists and Creatives Grant, Fall 2026

    Community Engagement · California, USA for the fall round. The spring round (March deadline) is open to artists living or working anywhere in the US. · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: Usually USD 500 to USD 2,500, and in rare circumstances up to USD 5,000. The funder suggests most artists apply for under USD 2,500 unless advised otherwise. No application fee.

    Community Engagement, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit based in Orange County, California, funds independent creatives making or presenting work that relates to or encourages community engagement. Its framing is collaborative placemaking, projects from communal gardens to public mosaics to intergenerational art activities that reimagine shared spaces and stimulate social engagement, creative expression and collective empowerment, with a focus on diverse and underserved neighbourhoods. GEOGRAPHY DEPENDS ON THE ROUND: the fall round, deadline 15 September, is open only to artists living or working in California. The spring round, deadline 15 March, is open to artists living or working anywhere in the US. WHAT IT FUNDS: the execution of community-engaged projects, prioritising the creation of new artworks that present new and imaginative possibilities. The funder says it is not likely to fund art classes, take-home art kits, or exhibitions of studio artwork in preexisting galleries unless the proposal centres a clear and purposeful collaboration with a community. WHO CAN APPLY: individual artists and artist collectives, including collaborators. NOT SUPPORTED: arts festivals, nonprofit and for-profit organizations, student projects (undergraduate or graduate), residencies, research symposia, studio rent, conferences, theaters, and any activity affiliated with religious organizations. Successful grantees must wait at least two years before reapplying. The grant explicitly encourages applications from artists historically underrepresented in the arts, including Black, Indigenous, People of Color, Queer, Trans, Deaf, Disabled, Immigrant, Refugee and Self-Taught artists. HOW TO APPLY: upload four PDFs to a single publicly accessible online folder (Google Drive, Dropbox or similar) and submit the link via the form; documents not in PDF format will not be considered. Applications that do not follow the guidelines are not reviewed, and if you have not heard back six weeks after the deadline the application was unsuccessful. Deadline 15 September 2026 at 11:59pm PST, with decisions 15 October.

    American Academy in Berlin · Berlin, Germany, at the Hans Arnhold Center. RESTRICTED: fellowships are open only to citizens and permanent residents of the United States. · Deadline: 25 Sep 2026 · Award: Monthly stipend of USD 5,000, plus roundtrip airfare, accommodation at or near the Hans Arnhold Center, partial board, in-house library services and professional networking opportunities. Roughly two dozen fellowships awarded each year. No application fee.

    The American Academy in Berlin is an independent, nonprofit institute for advanced study in the arts, humanities, social sciences, law, journalism and public policy, whose primary mission is to strengthen the cultural and intellectual ties between the United States and Germany. Each year, after a rigorous selection process, the Academy awards roughly two dozen semester-long Berlin Prize fellowships to individuals who demonstrate excellence in their fields. DISCIPLINES: applications are welcome across the humanities, social sciences, law, journalism and various areas of public policy. The Academy generally encourages projects addressing pressing issues of the day, specifically naming democracy, security, technology and society (especially AI), energy including climate, and global order. Dedicated fellowships also support projects in Jewish studies, German studies, and public health and biotechnology. Project proposals need not focus on Germany, but candidates should explain how a residency in Berlin would enhance their work. NOT ACCEPTED: proposals in mathematics or the natural sciences. NOTE FOR ARTISTS: visual artists and composers are invited through separate, nomination-based competitions, so there is no open application route for them here. ELIGIBILITY: fellowships are restricted to citizens and permanent residents of the United States. Both emerging and established figures are eligible. PhD holders qualify as emerging scholars with a strong record of peer-reviewed work beyond the dissertation; candidates in non-academic fields such as journalism, filmmaking and policy need a significant record of publication or production; fiction and nonfiction writers must have published at least one book with a reputable press. WHAT FELLOWS DO: fellows present their work to audiences in and around Berlin through lectures, readings, public discussions, performances and film screenings, and should be willing to engage with relevant German institutions, organizations and the media; these activities are integral to the Academy's mission of fostering transatlantic dialogue. APPLICATION MATERIALS: a project proposal of 1,500 to 1,750 words plus a one-page bibliography; a curriculum vitae of up to 10 pages; two writing samples totalling no more than 60 pages; and three letters of recommendation, which are due 2 October 2026. DEADLINE: Friday 25 September 2026 at 12:00 noon Eastern Time (18:00 Central European Time). TIMELINE: all candidates are notified of the competition outcome by email in late March 2027; the 2027-28 Berlin Prize recipients are announced in May 2027.

    Simons Foundation · United States (trio must be regionally co-located) · Deadline: 21 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 30,000 per awarded trio

    Open Interval is a Simons Foundation program that supports trios consisting of an artist, a scientist, and a representative from a 501(c)(3) arts organization to collaborate around a shared scientific theme. The current cycle centers on the theme of turbulence, a phenomenon at the edge of chaos and beauty. The application portal opens September 9, 2026, and all three trio members must submit for the application to be complete.

Filming a documentary, researching a planned book or film, mounting an exhibition, running a concert series, promotional or PR travel, equipment purchases and full-time residency programmes are all ineligible, as is funding used only to travel to a single event. TIMELINE: the portal opens 1 October 2026 at 9:59am ET, applications close 10 November 2026 at 9:59am ET, and letters of recommendation are due 16 November 2026 at 9:59am ET. Review runs November 2026 to April 2027, notifications go out mid-May 2027, and all funded activity must take place between 1 August 2027 and 31 December 2028. Apply through the GoApply portal; ACC does not accept email applications. Applicants may apply to only one ACC programme per cycle. No application fee.

    The Helene Wurlitzer Foundation of New Mexico · Taos, New Mexico, USA · Deadline: 18 Jan 2027 · Award: Rent-free, utility-paid furnished housing (casita); no stipend (rare stipend support via the Rhonda Artist Stipend Fund); artists cover food and travel USD 30 application fee

    The Helene Wurlitzer Foundation of New Mexico runs one of the oldest artist residency programs in the United States, established in 1954, on a 15-acre campus in Taos, New Mexico. The residency is open to visual artists, literary artists, and music composers, including painters, sculptors, photographers, filmmakers, writers, poets, playwrights, screenwriters, and songwriters. Selected grantees receive rent-free and utility-paid housing in a furnished casita for a session of 10 to 12 weeks, with access to a Commons House offering WiFi, a library, and bicycles. Applications are open to creative artists worldwide regardless of age, experience, or nationality, and the annual deadline is January 18 for residencies the following calendar year.   19. ACA Artist Response Fund (ARF) Emergency Relief Grant

    Artist Communities Alliance · United States (ACA member residencies) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to USD 1,500

    The Artist Response Fund (ARF) is an emergency relief program from the Artist Communities Alliance (ACA) for artists who experience threats to their safety or mental health during a residency and must leave before completing their term. Qualifying situations include assault, harassment, or systemic harm where the artist has attempted to address the issue without success. Grants of up to USD 1,500 help cover relocation costs such as transportation home, temporary lodging, supplies or basic needs, and rent upon returning. Eligible applicants are individual artists currently at, or having attended within the past 12 months, an ACA Organizational Member residency. There is no application fee, and the fund operates on a rolling basis with applications typically processed within one week.
