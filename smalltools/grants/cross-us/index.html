Cross-Disciplinary and Social Impact Grants in the US

Currently 6 active cross-disciplinary and social impact grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the US. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Postdoctoral Associates in AI in the Humanities and Social Sciences (Cornell University) 2027-2029

    Cornell University, College of Arts and Sciences · Ithaca, New York, USA · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 70,000 per year plus full benefits and a USD 4,000 annual research account (further funds by application). No application fee.

    A cohort of up to five two-year postdoctoral fellows working on AI from humanistic and social-science angles: social and cultural impacts of AI, computational methods, and the ethics, politics and history of AI. Applicants need a PhD conferred by 1 August 2027 (or a Cornell PhD from 2022 to 2025) and must be sponsored by a tenure-stream Arts and Sciences faculty member, so line up a sponsor before applying. International applicants are welcome subject to visa eligibility. Deadline extended to 20 November 2026, 23:59 (originally 15 November).

  2. Knight-Wallace Journalism Fellowships 2027-28 (University of Michigan)

    Wallace House Center for Journalists, University of Michigan · Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA (in residence for the academic year). Open to journalists of any nationality. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 85,000 stipend plus USD 5,000 relocation (figures from secondary listings; confirm on the official site).

    An academic-year fellowship for mid-career journalists to pursue a study project at the University of Michigan, with seminars and programming at Wallace House. DEADLINES: international applicants 1 December 2026, 11:59 pm ET; US applicants 1 February 2027. No application fee stated.

  3. University of Notre Dame: Love Ethic Social Innovator Fellowships 2028 (residency plus USD 15,000 and up to USD 50,000 project funding)

    University of Notre Dame, Institute for Ethics and the Common Good · Notre Dame, Indiana, USA. In person: one-week workshop in October 2027 and four-week residency in February 2028. · Deadline: 11 Dec 2026 · Award: Stipend for housing during the residency plus relocation support; USD 15,000 to the fellow's organisation to offset the four-week sabbatical; up to USD 50,000 in subgrant funding after the residency to pilot the proposed project; travel to all programme gatherings covered. No application fee.

    Up to six short-term fellowships for practitioners outside academia (nonprofit leaders, community organisers, social workers, faith leaders, entrepreneurs, public-sector professionals) who want time and space to design a new initiative grounded in love-based ethics. Funded by the John Templeton Foundation. 2026 CYCLE THEME: love of neighbour; the organisation's work and the proposed project must centre this idea philosophically and practically. ELIGIBILITY: members of an organisation's leadership team with significant responsibility for public-facing leadership and for designing and directing projects; must be available for the October 2027 workshop and the four-week February 2028 residency on campus, and have a defined project. OFFER: housing stipend and relocation support for the residency, USD 15,000 to the fellow's organisation, up to USD 50,000 to pilot the project afterwards, covered travel, seminar and campus access. Questions about project fit go to Adam Gustine (agustine@nd.edu). DEADLINE: 11 December 2026.

  4. Craigardan Trillium Fellowship 2027 (residency for artists, activists and scholars of color, USD 4,600 + USD 500 honorarium)

    Craigardan · Elizabethtown, New York, USA (Adirondack Park). In person; rural, car recommended. · Deadline: 15 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 4,600 fellowship toward a minimum two-week residency (covers the residency fee), plus a USD 500 honorarium. Travel, materials and personal expenses are not covered. USD 25 application fee (waivers available on request).

    The Trillium Fellowship supports artists, activists and scholars of color working in any discipline who would benefit from the time and space to pursue their work or find creative retreat at Craigardan, a residency and farm in the Adirondacks. One Trillium fellowship is awarded each year, need-based, by invitation or application (nominations accepted). The fellow offers a public talk in their discipline. OFFER: USD 4,600 toward a minimum two-week residency plus a USD 500 honorarium; all residents get a private room, a farm share and pantry, shared kitchen and living spaces, and studio space suited to their discipline. Transportation, materials and personal expenses are not covered (bus or train pickup can be arranged for a USD 40 flat fee). ELIGIBILITY: no citizenship, age or degree limits are published; applicants should be able to work independently in their field. APPLY through the general 2027 summer residency application (residencies 1 May to 1 November 2027): select the fellowship option and add a supporting statement covering your financial position and creative accomplishments, along with preferred dates, a short bio, CV, Statement of Intent and work samples. USD 25 application fee, waivers via program@craigardan.org. DEADLINE: 15 December 2026; notifications by 15 January 2027.

  5. Mid Atlantic Arts: USArtists International (FY28 round, up to USD 11,000)

    Mid Atlantic Arts, with the National Endowment for the Arts and the Mellon Foundation · Baltimore, Maryland, USA (administration). RESTRICTED: artists and ensembles based in any US state or territory, performing at engagements outside the US. · Deadline: 24 Feb 2027 · Award: Grants of up to USD 11,000 toward eligible expenses, with a 1:1 cost match required. No application fee.

    Matching grants supporting US-based performing artists and ensembles, all performing-arts practices including experimental music and performance, appearing at international festivals and significant presenting engagements outside the United States. GRANT: up to USD 11,000 toward eligible travel and engagement expenses, matched 1:1. ELIGIBILITY: artists/ensembles based in any US state or territory; not open to non-US-based artists. The portal opens early December 2026; the round covers projects between 1 July 2027 and 30 August 2028. DEADLINE: 24 February 2027, 11:59pm ET.

  6. Awesome Foundation Local Chapters: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants (US, UK, Canada, Australia, NL, Asia)

    Awesome Foundation (local chapters) · City and regional chapters; each funds projects in or benefiting its own area. US, Canada, Australia, UK (Glasgow), Netherlands (Utrecht), Malaysia, Singapore, Armenia, Uzbekistan. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Usually USD 1,000 (or local equivalent) per grant, no strings attached, typically one grant per chapter per month. No application fee.

    The Awesome Foundation is a network of volunteer giving circles: in each chapter about ten trustees pool their own money to give a USD 1,000 no-strings micro-grant, usually monthly, to a project that adds "awesome" to their area: art, public interventions, community tech, events, science and civic ideas. One application on the main site can be sent to the chapter for your location. ACTIVE CHAPTERS (made grants in 2026, checked 9 Nov 2026): US: Ann Arbor, Austin, Baltimore, Cass Clay, Chicago, Cleveland, Detroit, Gainesville, Georgetown MA, Gloucester MA, Hamilton-Wenham MA, Ipswich MA, Los Angeles, Newburgh NY, Newburyport MA, New York City, Oahu, Orlando, Peekskill NY, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Portland OR, Poughkeepsie NY, Rhode Island, Rockport MA, San Antonio, San Francisco, San Jose, Santa Cruz, South Bend, St. Paul, State College PA, Washington DC, Westminster MD (Durham, Miami and Seattle each made one grant early in 2026). Canada: Kingston, Kitchener-Waterloo, Newmarket, Ottawa, Toronto. Australia: Adelaide, Fleurieu, Lake Mac, Lower Hunter, Newcastle, Sydney. UK: Glasgow. Netherlands: Utrecht (one grant in early 2026; Leiden and Rotterdam have been quiet since 2025 and 2024). Asia: Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Tashkent, Yerevan. ELIGIBILITY: set per chapter, generally individuals or groups doing the project in that chapter's area. APPLY: free, short online form. DEADLINE: rolling. Worldwide themed chapters are listed separately (Disability, Conservation and Climate, On the Water).
