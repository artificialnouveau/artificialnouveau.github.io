Cross-Disciplinary and Social Impact Grants (Worldwide)

Open calls

  1. Unearthodox Exploration Co-Lab 2027: Edges of Possibility

    Unearthodox · Online; group sessions designed around time zones UTC-6 to UTC+6. Open worldwide to applicants over 18; applications in English. · Deadline: 16 Nov 2026 · Award: CHF 10,000 grant per selected initiative, plus a 10-month incubation programme with tailored coaching, expert support, peer learning, systems mapping and futures-thinking support. No application fee.

    Online incubator from Swiss foundation Unearthodox (formerly the Luc Hoffmann Institute) for innovators with early-stage ideas at the intersection of environmental and societal challenges, aimed at work that tackles root causes in the relationship between people, society and nature rather than symptoms: climate, biodiversity, habitat loss, regeneration and environmental justice among them. WHAT YOU GET: a CHF 10,000 grant plus 10 months of cohort-based incubation (March to December 2027) with coaching, expert and peer support, and access to a global community of systems innovators. ELIGIBILITY: over 18, idea already in motion through research, testing, piloting or community engagement; applications in English; participation fully online with sessions planned for UTC-6 to UTC+6. SELECTION CRITERIA: systems orientation (25 percent), nature-society vision (25 percent), boundary-pushing (25 percent), transformational intent (15 percent), edge-walking (10 percent). DEADLINE: 16 November 2026, 23:59 GMT+1 (window opened 15 September 2026).

  2. Mensch Maschine VISIT Residency Fellowship 2027: Energy Ghosts (E-WERK Luckenwalde / Akademie der Künste)

    JUNGE AKADEMIE of the Akademie der Künste, VISIT (E.ON Foundation), E-WERK Luckenwalde and Ujazdowski Castle Centre for Contemporary Art · E-WERK Luckenwalde, Brandenburg, Germany (3 fellows) and Akademie der Künste, Berlin (1 fellow), with a retreat at Ujazdowski Castle CCA in Warsaw, Poland. Open to artists worldwide. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: Four fully funded fellowships of EUR 20,000 each, intended for artist fees and production costs of a new project (research and development, travel, materials and presentation costs), plus studio space and accommodation for the three-month residency.

    The Mensch Maschine VISIT Residency Program is an annual fellowship offering four fully funded fellowships to artists from around the world, run by the JUNGE AKADEMIE of the Akademie der Künste (Berlin) with VISIT, the artist-in-residence programme of the E.ON Foundation, E-WERK Luckenwalde and Ujazdowski Castle Centre for Contemporary Art (Warsaw). The 2027 edition particularly encourages projects at the intersection of art, energy and ecology exploring the theme ENERGY GHOSTS: the unseen dimensions of energy approached through mythology, cosmology or speculative fiction, the invisible infrastructures, histories and residues that haunt contemporary life, and energy as an ecological, political or spiritual force. Proposals are welcome from visual art, performance, film, installation, sound, writing and new media. WHAT YOU GET: EUR 20,000 per fellow for artist fees and production costs of a new project (research and development, travel, material and presentation costs); three fellows receive studio space and accommodation at E-WERK Luckenwalde (a former power station about 40 minutes south of Berlin), one is accommodated by the Akademie der Künste in Berlin. The three-month residency runs roughly September to November 2027 with on-site presence of at least 75% required, a retreat at Ujazdowski Castle in Warsaw in September 2027, events across the three venues, and a closing symposium in October 2027 at the Akademie der Künste where fellows show work in progress alongside the 2026 cohort. JURY: Nolan Oswald Dennis; Chus Martinez (Institute Art Gender Nature, TBA21); Sarah Johanna Theurer (Fondation Beyeler); Anh-Linh Ngo (Akademie der Künste); and Tiara Roxanne. APPLICATION: via the VISIT portal at visit-energy.com/bewerben. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026, 23:59 CET.

  3. Erase Indifference Challenge 2026 (Auschwitz Pledge Foundation; EUR 90,000 in awards for projects countering hate)

    Auschwitz Pledge Foundation · Worldwide. Remote application. · Deadline: 04 Dec 2026 · Award: EUR 90,000 in total: one EUR 20,000 Main Award plus seven EUR 10,000 awards. Unrestricted, no reporting requirement. No application fee.

    The annual Erase Indifference Challenge rewards existing projects that move bystanders to act against discrimination and hate, through art, education or technology. The project must already exist with demonstrated impact; completed projects must have ended no earlier than December 2023 and the organisation must still be operating. Direct services to people affected by discrimination do not qualify. ELIGIBILITY: NGOs, startups, companies and public institutions anywhere in the world; partnerships are fine if every partner is eligible. Excluded: informal groups, solo individuals, politically linked entities, purely commercial or religious applicants, and academic research without practical application. OFFER: EUR 20,000 Main Award and seven EUR 10,000 awards, unrestricted. APPLY in English via the form linked from the Apply page (question preview and manual online). DEADLINE: 4 December 2026.

  4. The Stringer Awards 2027 (independent accountability journalism)

    Stringer Journalism Foundation · Remote. Open to independent journalists internationally. · Deadline: 31 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 9,000 to 100,000 per award, multi-year and unrestricted; the size depends on funds raised.

    The Stringer Awards give unrestricted, multi-year funding to independent journalists who expose abuses of power, in Senior and Emerging categories. No nationality restriction is stated. No application fee.

  5. Bertha Artivism Awards 2027

    Bertha Foundation · Worldwide. Individuals and collectives working with a social justice organization, campaign or movement. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to USD 20,000 per one-year activist-art project. Free to apply.

    Applications for the 2027 Bertha Artivism Awards are open (confirmed 23 September 2026). The awards fund up to USD 20,000 for a one-year activist-art project made in collaboration with a social justice organization, campaign or movement, and the cohort also receives peer exchange and support from the Bertha Foundation network. Open worldwide to artists and collectives across disciplines. IMPORTANT: no closing date is published on the award page, and the cohort starts January 2027, so apply early rather than waiting; the call has historically opened around August and closed in the autumn. TO APPLY: submit the application form linked from the Artivism Awards page at berthafoundation.org.

  6. Awesome Foundation Disability Chapter: USD 1,000 Monthly Micro-grants

    Awesome Foundation (Disability Chapter) · Worldwide; applications in English. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000, no strings attached, one winner per month. No application fee.

    The Disability Chapter of the Awesome Foundation, a trustee-funded giving circle, awards a USD 1,000 no-strings micro-grant every month to a project benefiting disabled people: accessibility and assistive technology, arts and culture, inclusion and advocacy all qualify. ELIGIBILITY: worldwide; individuals, nonprofits or companies; applicants do NOT need to be disabled themselves, though disability-led projects get priority. NOT FUNDED: personal medical needs, rent, repairs and similar personal expenses. APPLICATION: free, via the chapter page; submissions are only accepted from the 1st through the 10th of each month, with decisions by the end of the month. DEADLINE: rolling monthly windows (1st to 10th).

  7. Emergent Ventures (Mercatus Center): Grants for Individuals

    Mercatus Center, George Mason University · Remote; projects and applicants worldwide · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Grant size is set per project and is not published (ranges from small fast grants to larger awards). No application fee.

    Emergent Ventures funds entrepreneurs and thinkers worldwide with highly scalable, zero-to-one ideas for meaningfully improving society. It is aimed at individuals (applicants must be 13 or older) rather than institutions, and international and non-US applicants are eligible; grants are awarded to people around the world. Dedicated support is available for projects focused on India, Africa, the Caribbean or Ukraine. Grant amounts are not disclosed and are set per project. Applications are accepted on a rolling, continuous basis through an online form. The programme launched in 2018 and is administered by Tyler Cowen at the Mercatus Center.
