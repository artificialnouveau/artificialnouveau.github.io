Open calls

  1. Surface Design Association: Artist in Residence 2026-27 (virtual)

    Surface Design Association (SDA) · Fully virtual. Open to US-based and international applicants; current SDA membership is required. · Deadline: 14 Aug 2026 · Award: $2,000 studio stipend, plus up to $2,000 further compensation for programming work (SDA aims for roughly $4,000 in total per resident). No application fee, but applicants must be current SDA members (membership from $85 per year, $60 for students).

    The Surface Design Association's Artist in Residence programme is built to develop skills, open up relationships and support fiber and textile artists through a studio project that pushes their practice. The residency is entirely virtual and runs across a full year, which is the point: it removes the travel, housing and time-away barriers that keep many practitioners out of in-person residencies. One artist is selected annually by a panel of SDA staff, board members and committee volunteers. WHAT IT INVOLVES: studio visits across the year, social media programming, and meetings with the SDA community, plus further programming shaped around the resident's own skills and interests, which can include teaching online workshops, contributing to SDA publications, jurying exhibitions or joining panels. FUNDING: a $2,000 studio stipend for the project, plus up to $2,000 in additional compensation for programming work, targeting about $4,000 total. ELIGIBILITY: emerging, mid-career and established artists; US-based and international applicants welcome; students and professionals may apply; applicants must hold current SDA membership (from $85 per year, $60 for students). APPLICATION: a portfolio of 10 images with full artwork information (title, year, materials, technique, dimensions, photo credit), a detailed CV, essay responses on artistic goals, programming interests and professional development, and demographic information. No application fee. The 2026-27 cycle closes 14 August 2026.

  2. AI Builders Fellowship 2026 (Internet Archive Canada x BC + AI)

    Internet Archive Canada (IAC) in partnership with BC + AI · Remote, open to builders anywhere in the world. Optional in-person showcase at Futureproof Festival in Vancouver, Canada on 28 October 2026 if the project is ready. · Deadline: 21 Aug 2026 · Award: $5,000 award for one selected builder. No application fee.

    A $5,000 fellowship to spend six weeks turning a very large Canadian public archive into something new. THE MATERIAL: Internet Archive Canada's Government Publications collection, part of the Democracy's Library project, holding over 104,000 text items, roughly 22.1 million scanned pages and about 48.25 TB of Canadian public-record material, browsable at archive.org/details/governmentpublications. WHAT TO BUILD: deliberately open-ended. The call names a search tool that answers a real question with page-level citations, a timeline tracing one policy across decades of government language, and a sonic or visual piece built from archival material, but explicitly invites work nobody has thought of yet. The requirements are that it is useful, that it is imaginative, and that the code is open source when it is done. ORIGIN: the fellowship came out of conversations between BC + AI's Kris Kruger and IAC executive director Andrea Mills about treating a public archive as material for new work rather than only something to preserve. IAC brings the archive, BC + AI brings a community of builders used to working in public. ELIGIBILITY: anyone, anywhere in the world; one builder is selected. APPLICATION: a specific project description grounded in the collection, a phase-by-phase work plan for the six weeks, and a success metric, submitted at bc-ai.ca/fellowship/apply. KEY DATES: applications close 21 August 2026 at 11:59pm Pacific; fellow selected by 31 August; build runs 1 September to mid-October; showcase at Futureproof Festival in Vancouver on 28 October 2026 if the project is ready.

  3. Reverse Alignment: Fast-Grants for Storytellers (AI and Humanity)

    Reverse Alignment coalition, administered by PopShift (Pathos Labs) · Not stated. The application is an online form and no geographic restriction is published. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: NOT PUBLISHED. The call is framed as a fast-grant / flash-grant for creators but names no figure, no budget range and no number of awards anywhere on the site or in the application form. Ask before you invest time. No application fee.

    Rolling fast-grants for writers, filmmakers, game makers and video creators who build the sociotechnical challenges of AI into their work. THE COALITION: Reverse Alignment argues that most AI money goes to making systems more capable and to aligning them with human values, while almost nothing goes to redesigning the institutions, norms and skills that decide whether the benefits are shared. Its public statement is the Noema essay 'What Humanity Needs to Flourish in the Next Decade'. The coalition list is unusually senior and cross-sector: Audrey Tang, Danielle Allen, Blaise Aguera y Arcas, Tim O'Reilly, Glen Weyl, Divya Siddarth, Vilas Dhar, James Evans, Ben Buchanan and others. THE ASK: 'We are funding storytellers who weave these ideas into their work. If you write, film, build games, or make things people watch, and you want to incorporate or reinterpret any challenge here, we want to support it. You don't have to explain the policy. You just have to make people feel what's at stake.' THE MATERIAL: twelve sociotechnical grand challenges (identity, privacy, provenance, data value, agentic collaboration, communal sensemaking, democracy, law and liberties, workplace, research, education, labour transition), plus a free storyteller's playbook, 'What AI Can't Build', that turns each into a high-concept pitch and a writers'-room prompt, downloadable as a PDF from the site. MISMATCH WORTH KNOWING BEFORE YOU APPLY: the public framing is broad (write, film, build games), but the actual application form is a Typeform internally titled 'YouTuber Flash-Grant' and publicly titled 'AI and Humanity - Fast Grant', described as 'for TV and Film writers'. It asks for your YouTube account, a 150 to 300 word pitch for a VIDEO, and which of EIGHT challenges (not twelve) your content addresses. In practice this reads as a video and screen-creator grant. If you work in games or prose, expect to have to make the case. APPLICATION: short form at popshift.typeform.com/to/NvKBeode, asking for name and email, YouTube account, the video pitch, the challenge you are addressing, which creators are inspiring you, and who is shaping your views on AI. Applicants are told to expect a reply 'in the next several weeks'. Questions go to team@pathoslabs.org. DEADLINE: none published; applications appear to be rolling.
