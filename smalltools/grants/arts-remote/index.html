  1. The Impart & Nalli Fellowships 2027-28

    Impart in collaboration with Nalli Silks · Remote and self-directed; applicants may reside anywhere in the world. Projects must engage with textile practices across South Asia: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Myanmar, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Tibet. · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Up to INR 5.5 lakhs (INR 550,000) per fellow towards project execution, covering research-related expenses including travel, fieldwork and documentation subject to approval. Two fellowships are awarded. No application fee.

    A research fellowship from Impart, an online platform building accessible resources on South Asia's art histories, in collaboration with Nalli Silks, a heritage textile brand established in 1928. The programme has supported two research projects a year since 2024-25; for the 2027-28 cycle two fellowships are awarded, each carrying a grant of up to INR 5.5 lakhs for one year of independent research and project development. AREAS OF FOCUS: underexplored textile-related histories, practices and traditions across South Asia. Any textile-centric subject may be proposed; the call particularly encourages textiles in religious, literary, liturgical and mythological contexts; the environmental impact of textile production and consumption; endangered or disappearing textile traditions; critical perspectives on textile revivalism, digitisation, archives and material afterlives; labour in textile production with attention to caste, class and gender; relationships between textiles and other cultural forms including storytelling, poetry, music, architecture and material culture; techniques, technologies and tools tied to specific traditions; South Asian textiles within the contemporary global design economy; intellectual property, ownership and cultural rights; and socio-cultural perspectives on the relationship between handmade and industrial textiles. ELIGIBILITY: open to anyone aged 18 or over residing anywhere in the world, provided the project engages with textile practices in the wider South Asia region (Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Myanmar, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Tibet). Early- and mid-career researchers, designers, archivists, journalists, writers, educators, practitioners, collectives and small organisations are all welcome, and collaborative projects are permitted provided co-creators are credited and listed in the application. Both new and ongoing projects are eligible, but applicants must specify which part of the project the grant will develop and disclose any current or previous funding from other organisations. SUPPORTED FORMATS: journalistic inquiry, documentary film, archive activation, practice-based research, archive generation, digitisation and documentation initiatives, and exhibition or book projects. DELIVERABLES: a comprehensive project report at the end of the grant period; a research dossier developed from that report in collaboration with Impart, presenting key questions and findings and hosted on Impart's website for public benefit; and a set of final project outputs, which may be audio-visual (interviews, short films), visual essays (photographic or process-based documentation), written pieces (essays, features, interviews) or curatorial work (exhibition or digital curation). Fellows also take part in periodic check-ins and submit progress reports. Copyright is settled case by case, but all final outputs must be made openly and freely accessible to the public. APPLICATION: a Google Form structured as a full project proposal, plus a project budget (percentage-based allocation is acceptable, but be as specific as possible), a project timeline for the one-year period with key phases, activities and milestones, and a single supporting PDF containing an up-to-date CV, selected work samples, and up to two letters of support or recommendation. The form is in English; applicants who would prefer another language are invited to write to Impart to arrange an alternative. NOTE: the fellowship has a strict no-tolerance policy on the use of AI or LLMs in the application form or any submitted materials, and any violation results in rejection. KEY DATES: applications close 31 August 2026; awardees notified December 2026; fellowships run January to December 2027. No individual feedback is given on unsuccessful applications.

  2. Art Worker Artist Grant 2026

    ARTIST ENGINEERING · Remote. No geographic restriction is stated in the call; key dates are given in AEST. · Deadline: 14 Sep 2026 · Award: AUD 5,000 to one recipient for the 2026 edition. Funds are unrestricted: they may be used for studio time, travel, healthcare, research, equipment, rest, materials, living expenses, childcare, or something else entirely. There are no required outcomes and no required grant acquittal. No application fee is mentioned.

    The Art Worker Artist Grant is a biennial award for artists whose livelihoods are connected to the production, handling, exhibition, care, movement or stewardship of art. Its premise is that a great many artists fund their practice through labour inside the art industry itself, and that this work rarely comes with support for their own artistic development. WHO IS ELIGIBLE: artists who support their practice by providing any form of labour in any area of the art industry, including but not limited to museums, galleries, artist studios, collections, auction houses, art fairs, fabrication, framing and mountmaking workshops, conservation labs, art logistics and crating companies, and storage facilities. WHAT IT FUNDS: the development of artistic work in whatever form that takes, with the money explicitly unrestricted; the call lists studio time, travel, healthcare, research, equipment, rest, materials, living expenses and childcare as legitimate uses. There are no required outcomes and no required acquittal, so no reporting obligation. APPLICATION: intentionally minimal, consisting of a brief statement describing how the funds could support artistic development or production, and a link to a website, portfolio, social media or other documentation of the work. SELECTION: by an independent jury of Andria Hickey (curator and museum leader, curatorial advisor for the Barclays Center art programme in Brooklyn, previously at The Shed, Pace Gallery and MOCA Cleveland), Clynton Lowry (artist and publisher) and Paul Graham (photographer). IMPORTANT: applications for the 2026 edition are capped at the first 500 submissions, so the call may close before the stated deadline. Key dates in AEST: entries open 24 August 2026, entries close 14 September 2026, recipient announced 2 November 2026. Overseen and funded by ARTIST ENGINEERING.

  3. This is Gender Regional Curatorial Fellowship 2026

    This is Gender (Global 50/50) · Remote and part-time, working within a global photography competition. Applications are invited from practitioners in global majority countries and historically underrepresented communities, including Asia, Africa, Latin America, the Middle East, Oceania, and diasporic communities worldwide. · Deadline: 22 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend of up to GBP 3,000 for the seven-month part-time fellowship. No application fee.

    This is Gender, the photography and visual culture platform run by Global 50/50, is launching its inaugural Regional Curatorial Fellows Programme for early-career curators and cultural practitioners working across photography, visual culture and gender justice. The premise: images shape how gender, power and social realities are understood, feeding public debate, cultural memory, research, advocacy and policy, yet the communities and visual practices that should inform those narratives stay underrepresented in the institutions that circulate them. The programme's argument is that diversifying who makes images is not enough, and that it also matters who selects, contextualises, writes about, commissions and circulates them. For the first time, This is Gender is embedding regional curators directly inside its global photography competition, which has run six cycles and receives thousands of submissions a year. WHAT THE FELLOWSHIP INVOLVES: working inside the competition and seeing first-hand how a major platform discovers, reviews and champions new work; shaping curatorial decisions by bringing regional knowledge and cultural insight into the selection and interpretation of work; contributing to public-facing projects including exhibitions, editorial outputs and digital storytelling while developing your own writing, framing and curatorial voice; direct engagement with international judges, curators, artists, editors and cultural practitioners; and building a network across an international cohort of peers. WHO SHOULD APPLY: curators, critics, writers, researchers, artistic directors, photographers, organisers and cultural practitioners from global majority countries and historically underrepresented communities. Early-career practitioners and recent graduates are strongly encouraged, and a formal curatorial title is not required. HOW TO APPLY: read the full role description and FAQs on the fellowship page and submit the online application form; the organisers offer the application in alternative formats and are open to a conversation before applying. Applications close 22 September 2026.
