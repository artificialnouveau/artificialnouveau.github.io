  1. Inato Gallery: Touch Some Grass (open-call poster exhibition)

    Inato Gallery · Submissions are digital (A3 portrait PDF) and no residency or travel is required; the exhibition is mounted by Inato Gallery, an artist-run space in Iligan City, Philippines. No nationality restriction is stated. · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: No artist fee, stipend or production budget. This is an exhibition opportunity: selected posters are shown in Inato Gallery's Touch Some Grass exhibition. No submission fee is stated in the call.

    Touch Some Grass is an open-call poster exhibition responding to the growing presence of AI-generated imagery and its impact on creative labour, authorship and visual culture. At a time when images can be produced instantly and consumed just as quickly, the exhibition reclaims the poster as a space for ideas, intention and public conversation, inviting artists to make work that questions, challenges or reflects on this changing landscape. As the organisers put it, the question is no longer whether machines can make images, they can; the question is what kind of world we build when speed matters more than thought. NOTE: this call offers exhibition and visibility only, with no artist fee or stipend. SUBMISSION GUIDELINES: artwork size A3 (297 x 420 mm), portrait orientation, 300 DPI, CMYK recommended, print-quality PDF preferred, maximum file size 1 GB, one submission per artist. CAP: participation is limited to 60 artists and the submission form closes automatically once all slots are filled, so the effective deadline may fall well before 31 August 2026 (10 of 60 slots were taken at the time of listing). Inato Gallery is an artist-run space in Iligan City, Philippines, which began as a solo street exhibition by KaraTula and now runs concept-driven solo and group shows, open mics, talks and workshops built on openness, participation and the belief that art should exist where people already are.

  2. The Kitchen / Central Location Open Call (foodculture days Biennale, Vevey 2027)

    foodculture days · Vevey, Switzerland. The Kitchen is built on the Esplanade de la Paix by the lakeside. The selected collective must be present in Vevey for the two-week build and installation, 1 to 16 June 2027, and through the biennale 16 to 20 June 2027. · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: CHF 15,000 all-inclusive project budget (VAT included), covering travel, visas, equipment and additional kitchen equipment rental, per diems, materials and artist fees. Food costs and accommodation in Vevey are covered separately by foodculture days. Three shortlisted collectives each receive a CHF 250 research fee to develop their proposal in Phase 2. One collective is selected. No application fee.

    foodculture days is opening up The Kitchen / Central Location, the living heart of its biennale in Vevey, to an outside collective for the festival's 6th edition. The Kitchen is at once an open-air kitchen, a gathering space and an artistic installation, sited by the lake on the Esplanade de la Paix along a busy promenade, hosting multidisciplinary projects, concerts, themed meals, public discussions, citizens' assemblies and moments of collective cooking. The call asks for new ways of inhabiting it. WHO THEY WANT: a transdisciplinary collective of scientists, artists, designers, craftspeople, cooks, activists and architects, either newly formed or already active; collaboration across generations, cultures and levels of expertise is encouraged. WHAT IS OFFERED: an open-air citizen experimentation laboratory in Vevey's public space, framed around rethinking ways of living and the relationship to time and to creation, and thinking together about the conditions under which multidisciplinary practices can flourish. THE BRIEF: kitchen and hospitality, lunch and dinner service, vegetarian meals with a vegan option. Meals should be affordable, seasonal where fresh produce is involved, and conceived in the spirit of a communal canteen: simple, generous, nourishing and full of flavour. Expected covers are roughly 100 guests Wednesday to Thursday and roughly 150 Friday to Sunday. WHAT THE FESTIVAL PROVIDES: curatorial support to develop the proposal, with particular attention to the themed meals; introductions to local producers, growers and artisans including farm visits and foraging sites; production support and volunteers throughout; and standard professional kitchen equipment and cooking facilities. BUDGET: CHF 15,000 all-inclusive (VAT included) covering travel, visas, equipment rental, any additional kitchen equipment, per diems, materials and artist fees; food costs and accommodation in Vevey are covered separately. THE SPACE: the pavilion, called Cuisine, is a modular metal structure assembled from repurposed agricultural greenhouse components on a 3.1 metre bidirectional grid, with pillars and arches forming ridge vaults, a textile covering naturally dyed yellow with reseda, and a terracotta brick hearth for wood-fired cooking laid directly on the floor. It comes with dining tables, low tables, a reception table, a shelf, a bar, pendant lights, 30 cushions, removable curtains, flags, blackboards and signage panels, and can expand, contract or be reconfigured. TIMELINE: Phase 1 deadline 31 August 2026 at midnight CET; three shortlisted proposals selected in late September, each invited to an online meeting and paid a CHF 250 research fee for Phase 2; final project selected and announced in early October 2026; collaborative development with the foodculture days architectural team October to November; production December to February; build and installation in Vevey 1 to 16 June 2027, with the collective present throughout those two weeks; 6th edition of the biennale 16 to 20 June 2027. PHASE 1 QUESTIONS: tell us about your collective (origins, intention, what makes you special); member biographies with collective or individual portfolios, website, Instagram, city of residence and practices; what the concept consists of in terms of food and cuisine, relationships, and scenography and spatial research; what you want to put into practice in the central pavilion, your vision and the kind of experience you want to create; any special requirements; and references, including a quote representing the collective, two reference texts, three images and optionally video. An Open Call Information Kit, a recording of the online information session and a Q&A are published on the open call site and are worth reading before applying.

  3. The Impart & Nalli Fellowships 2027-28

    Impart in collaboration with Nalli Silks · Remote and self-directed; applicants may reside anywhere in the world. Projects must engage with textile practices across South Asia: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Myanmar, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Tibet. · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Up to INR 5.5 lakhs (INR 550,000) per fellow towards project execution, covering research-related expenses including travel, fieldwork and documentation subject to approval. Two fellowships are awarded. No application fee.

    A research fellowship from Impart, an online platform building accessible resources on South Asia's art histories, in collaboration with Nalli Silks, a heritage textile brand established in 1928. The programme has supported two research projects a year since 2024-25; for the 2027-28 cycle two fellowships are awarded, each carrying a grant of up to INR 5.5 lakhs for one year of independent research and project development. AREAS OF FOCUS: underexplored textile-related histories, practices and traditions across South Asia. Any textile-centric subject may be proposed; the call particularly encourages textiles in religious, literary, liturgical and mythological contexts; the environmental impact of textile production and consumption; endangered or disappearing textile traditions; critical perspectives on textile revivalism, digitisation, archives and material afterlives; labour in textile production with attention to caste, class and gender; relationships between textiles and other cultural forms including storytelling, poetry, music, architecture and material culture; techniques, technologies and tools tied to specific traditions; South Asian textiles within the contemporary global design economy; intellectual property, ownership and cultural rights; and socio-cultural perspectives on the relationship between handmade and industrial textiles. ELIGIBILITY: open to anyone aged 18 or over residing anywhere in the world, provided the project engages with textile practices in the wider South Asia region (Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Myanmar, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Tibet). Early- and mid-career researchers, designers, archivists, journalists, writers, educators, practitioners, collectives and small organisations are all welcome, and collaborative projects are permitted provided co-creators are credited and listed in the application. Both new and ongoing projects are eligible, but applicants must specify which part of the project the grant will develop and disclose any current or previous funding from other organisations. SUPPORTED FORMATS: journalistic inquiry, documentary film, archive activation, practice-based research, archive generation, digitisation and documentation initiatives, and exhibition or book projects. DELIVERABLES: a comprehensive project report at the end of the grant period; a research dossier developed from that report in collaboration with Impart, presenting key questions and findings and hosted on Impart's website for public benefit; and a set of final project outputs, which may be audio-visual (interviews, short films), visual essays (photographic or process-based documentation), written pieces (essays, features, interviews) or curatorial work (exhibition or digital curation). Fellows also take part in periodic check-ins and submit progress reports. Copyright is settled case by case, but all final outputs must be made openly and freely accessible to the public. APPLICATION: a Google Form structured as a full project proposal, plus a project budget (percentage-based allocation is acceptable, but be as specific as possible), a project timeline for the one-year period with key phases, activities and milestones, and a single supporting PDF containing an up-to-date CV, selected work samples, and up to two letters of support or recommendation. The form is in English; applicants who would prefer another language are invited to write to Impart to arrange an alternative. NOTE: the fellowship has a strict no-tolerance policy on the use of AI or LLMs in the application form or any submitted materials, and any violation results in rejection. KEY DATES: applications close 31 August 2026; awardees notified December 2026; fellowships run January to December 2027. No individual feedback is given on unsuccessful applications.

Epic states that if it can legally make payment to you, you are eligible. · Deadline: 04 Sep 2026 · Award: Typical grants range from USD 5,000 to USD 75,000, with some exceptional projects receiving up to USD 150,000.     Epic MegaGrants are financial grants from Epic Games for creative, noteworthy and innovative projects built in and around Unreal Engine, or projects that enhance the open source 3D graphics ecosystem. The programme is explicitly aimed at smaller teams, solo developers and individuals as well as studios: Epic states that individuals and small teams are eligible and encouraged, and that it treats every project equally regardless of who you are. Epic is not offering sponsorships and cannot give feedback on applications. Apply at megagrants.unrealengine.com.

  11. Arcane Grant 2026 for Artist Books

    ADAGP and SGDL (Societe des Gens de Lettres) · France. Open to a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or of French nationality living abroad) paired with a French-language author. · Deadline: 07 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 9,000 to the single winning project, supporting the conception and production of an artist's book. NOTE ON TAX: ADAGP grants count as primary artistic income under the French Intellectual Property Code (art. L112-2), so the sum falls under the artist-author social security and tax regime, subject to URSSAF contributions and income tax declared as non-commercial profits (micro-BNC or controlled declaration). VAT is not due. No application fee.

    Artists' books get little support, financially or in distribution, despite their place in art history and in the careers of the people who make them. ADAGP, the French collecting society for copyright in the visual arts, and SGDL, the French society of authors, created the Arcane grant in response: EUR 9,000 to one winning project for the conception and production of an artist's book made collaboratively by a visual artist and a writer. WHO CAN APPLY: a pair consisting of a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or a French national living abroad) and a French-language author working in fiction, non-fiction, poetry, children's literature or theatre who has already been published by a publisher. A trio is also possible where a publishing structure joins the project. WHAT COUNTS AS AN ARTIST'S BOOK: a creation, a work of art in book form, in a limited print run of between 1 and 500 copies, which may be signed or numbered. Explicitly not eligible: magazines, fanzines, limited editions of an ordinary book, and any publication that simply presents an artist's work, such as monographs or illustrated books. APPLICATION: the file must be in French and includes a jointly written project presentation of up to three pages covering the artistic and editorial vision, constituent elements, methods of realisation and desired final form; a one-page technical sheet giving title, print run, format, page count, paper quality, printing techniques and materials; a provisional timetable; and a detailed balanced provisional budget of expenses and financing. Artistic elements are a CV of exhibitions and publications (2 pages) and a portfolio with website link (10 pages) for the artist, a CV and website link for the author (2 pages), and a one-page editorial line for the publishing structure if there is one; contact details must be omitted from these. Administrative elements are sworn declarations that the project is a new creation with copyright held solely by the pair, that the artist is from the French scene, and, if applicable, that the publishing structure undertakes to publish the work. Templates are downloadable from the Required Documents page. TIMELINE: call launched 7 July 2026, applications close 7 September 2026 at 2pm, jury meets November 2026, winner announced end of November 2026, and the work must be published within two years of that announcement. The regulations must be read before applying, and submitting an application constitutes acceptance of them. Contact: arcane@adagp.fr.

ELIGIBILITY: an artist or designer with an active studio practice in the applied arts; a resident of the New York City metro area, defined as the five boroughs plus nearby New York, New Jersey and Connecticut counties within reasonable commuting distance, typically on MTA, PATH or NJ Transit; at least 21 at the time of application; and a US citizen or legal US resident with a valid SSN or EIN. The Museum does not sponsor visas and states this is not subject to exception. Programme alumni may not reapply, though unsuccessful past applicants may. SELECTION: the committee looks for a mature body of work engaging critically with the transdisciplinary fields of contemporary craft, design, art and technology, a strong conceptual framework, deep engagement with process and materials, a clear direction or question for the residency, and genuine readiness for the open studios format. Top applications go to interview with the Deputy Director of Education and Manager of Public Programs and Studios, and finalists' references are contacted. Applicants are strongly encouraged to visit the studios in person first. NOT AN EXHIBITION PROGRAMME: residents may show work in progress in their studio and finished work in the sixth-floor vitrines, but the residency does not include a gallery exhibition or a proposal for MAD's galleries. STUDIO RESTRICTIONS: no open flames, all hot work approved by the Director of Facilities, no large or unsafe power tools such as saws, and no toxic materials including turpentine, certain resins, glazes, lusters and lead-based paints. Applicants submit one application indicating their preferred schedule and which session or sessions they want, and are selected for one. Initial notifications October 2026, finalist interviews November 2026, offers December 2026. Questions: education@madmuseum.org. Deadline 11 September 2026.
