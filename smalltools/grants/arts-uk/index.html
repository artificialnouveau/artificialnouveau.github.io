Visual and Media Arts Grants in the UK

Currently 18 active visual and media arts grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the UK. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Browse the list below, or use the interactive desk for filtering and shortlisting.

  1. Upe Foundation Curatorial Fellow at Gasworks 2027-2028 (London, GBP 32,000, Baltic connection)

    Upe Foundation (in partnership with Gasworks) · London, UK. For early-career curators with a connection to the Baltic region (Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia or their diasporas); Gasworks will consider visa sponsorship. · Deadline: 09 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 32,000 per year salary, 5% employer pension contribution, relocation flights up to GBP 250 where relevant, visa sponsorship considered. No application fee.

    The Upe Foundation and Gasworks are recruiting a full-time Curatorial Fellow for 12 to 18 months, embedded in Gasworks' curatorial team in south London. The Fellow works closely with the artist selected for the 2027 Gasworks residency for artists based in the Baltics and co-develops their 2028 solo exhibition, alongside wider exhibition, registrar, interpretation and residency duties. WHO: early-career curators with a connection to the Baltic region (Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia or their diasporas); Gasworks will consider visa sponsorship for this fixed-term role. WHAT YOU GET: a GBP 32,000 per year salary, a 5% employer pension contribution and up to GBP 250 towards relocation flights. APPLICATION: download the job description and application form from the call page and upload the completed form; questions to rosaharvest@gasworks.org.uk. DEADLINE: 9 November 2026, 13:00 UK time.

  2. 2027 KCCUK Open Call for Emerging Korean Artists (Korean Cultural Centre UK)

    Korean Cultural Centre UK (KCCUK) · London, UK (group exhibition, February to April 2027, dates TBC). Korean nationality or Korean heritage required, plus a UK art school degree (BA, MA or PhD) or current UK residence. · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 250 participant support fee per selected artist (number of artists not stated), plus exhibition space, invigilation, opening event and KCCUK press/social promotion. Artists cover production, transport and installation costs. No application fee.

    The Korean Cultural Centre UK's annual open call for emerging Korean or Korean-heritage artists, particularly UK art school graduates, leading to a group exhibition from February to April 2027 (exact dates to be confirmed). Open theme; all fine art genres welcome, including video, sound and performance (submit a video link of max. 5 minutes plus stills). Selection by a panel of external curators and art professionals; results announced on the KCCUK website in early December 2026. ELIGIBILITY (all required): Korean nationality or Korean heritage; graduated from a UK-based art school (BA, MA or PhD) or currently living and working in the UK; aged 18+; at least one previous solo or group exhibition; works made within the past 5 years and not previously shown at KCCUK. SUPPORT: exhibition space and invigilation, opening event, certificate of participation, promotion via KCCUK channels (Korean and English press release, social media) and a GBP 250 participant support fee per artist; all other costs (production, transport, equipment, install) are borne by the artist, and support may change depending on KCCUK's circumstances. APPLY: one PDF (max. 7MB, in English) with artist bio (max. 200 words), portfolio of up to 5 works (max. 3 images each, with title, materials, dimensions, year, technical requirements) and artwork statements (150 words per work, max. 700 total), emailed to kccuk.opencall@gmail.com. Enquiries: Jaemin Cha, Curator, jaemin.cha@kccuk.org.uk. DEADLINE: Sunday 15 November 2026, midnight GMT.

    National Gallery (National Gallery X), London · National Gallery, London, UK. Open to UK-based emerging creatives. · Deadline: 17 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 15,000 commission. Up to 10 shortlisted applicants receive GBP 500 each to develop proposals.

Open to UK-based emerging creatives in their first five years of practice who are not in full-time education.   4. Radical Sabbatical 2027: six-month residencies on touch at the University of Birmingham (GBP 3,500 fee + GBP 500 production, UK-based artists)

    Eastside Projects with University Collections, University of Birmingham · University of Birmingham, Birmingham, UK (in person, research residency). Open to artists based in the UK only. · Deadline: 20 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget per artist; two artists selected.

    Radical Sabbatical is a collaboration between Eastside Projects and University Collections at the University of Birmingham that gives two artists a six-month residency centred on research and development. The 2027 edition is themed around touch: artists are matched with one or more academics and asked to interrogate and interpret academic research, with areas including digital touch and technology, haptic and sensory experience, embodied knowledge, disability and inclusive design, medical perspectives, materiality and craft, memory and emotion, and human and non-human sensory connection. Academic partners include the Active Touch Lab and the Department of Creative Industries. Artists have regular one-to-one conversations with staff and present at a final sharing event. ELIGIBILITY: artists based in the UK. SUPPORT: GBP 3,500 artist fee plus GBP 500 production budget. TIMELINE: deadline Friday 20 November 2026, 11:59pm; interviews for shortlisted artists 10 December 2026; residencies begin January 2027; final sharing event and evaluation June/July 2027. APPLY using the artists' callout pack linked from the call page; questions to info@eastsideprojects.org.

  5. Sadler's Wells Digital Stage: The Future Remembers, three GBP 6,000 dance-film commissions

    Sadler's Wells (Digital Stage) · London, UK (Sadler's Wells); open to international applicants and filming can take place anywhere in the world. · Deadline: 22 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 6,000 per commission (three commissions), inclusive of all production costs, plus a Sadler's Wells producer on each project and in-house editing support subject to availability. No application fee.

    Sadler's Wells Digital Stage commissions three new Dance on Screen films of up to four minutes on the theme The Future Remembers, asking artists to consider legacy, responsibility and the futures we are creating together. WHO: filmmakers and dance artists of any style working across dance and moving image, aged 18 or over, applying as individuals, groups or companies; international applicants are welcome and filming can happen anywhere. WHAT YOU GET: a flat GBP 6,000 per commission regardless of team size, which must cover equipment and any external filmmaker or producer; a Sadler's Wells producer helps shape the idea and source collaborators, and the in-house filmmaker may support editing. Finished films premiere at the Dance Digital Film Festival in November 2027. APPLICATION: online form describing your practice and the idea; shortlisted artists are interviewed in early December 2026. Contact digitalstage@sadlerswells.com. DEADLINE: midnight, 22 November 2026.

  6. Hidden Door 2027: Visual Art Open Call (Edinburgh, up to GBP 1,500 per artwork, Scotland-based artists)

    Hidden Door Festival · Edinburgh, Scotland (in person; venue to be announced, festival anticipated early June 2027). Open to artists based wholly or partly in Scotland, or graduates of a Scottish art school within the last three years. · Deadline: 03 Dec 2026 · Award: Up to GBP 1,500 per artwork/application, covering all artist fees and expenses, budgeted against Scottish Artists' Union rates (most awards will be lower). No application fee.

    Hidden Door is a volunteer-run, multi-arts festival that reopens disused and overlooked buildings in Edinburgh. Its 2027 edition (venue still secret, anticipated early June 2027) runs on the theme "Afterlives of Use", and this open call invites visual artists to propose existing work or work in progress that can be shown in the festival's site-specific spaces. The call does not fund the production of entirely new work. ELIGIBILITY: practising artists who either hold an art-related degree or are self-taught with at least five years of sustained practice, and who are based wholly or partly in Scotland or graduated from a Scottish art school in the last three years. Current undergraduates are not eligible; postgraduates are. SUPPORT: up to GBP 1,500 per artwork/application including all artist fees and expenses, in line with Scottish Artists' Union rates; most awards will be lower. Parallel open calls for dance and spoken word close on the same date, and calls for film, theatre and music are to follow. APPLY via the online form linked from the call page. Notification by 11 January 2027. DEADLINE: Thursday 3 December 2026.

    Welcome to the Fringe Palestine, with Independent Arts Projects and Workers Theatre Co-operative · Edinburgh, UK (Edinburgh Festival Fringe, August 2027).   8. Cove Park Awarded Residencies 2027-2028 (Scotland, UK, Central and South America and diasporas)

    Cove Park · Cove Park, Peaton Hill, Argyll and Bute, Scotland (in person, private self-catering accommodation and studio). Open to people based in the UK, Central or South America, Central and South American diaspora artists based in Europe or North America, and Scotland-born artists living anywhere. Students in formal education are not eligible. · Deadline: 11 Dec 2026 · Award: Fully funded: GBP 775 per week fee (VAT inclusive, freelance), travel allowance (Scotland GBP 65 to 200, rest of UK up to GBP 250, Europe up to GBP 400, other international up to GBP 1,250), materials up to GBP 150 for studio-based practitioners, private accommodation and studio, visa and access support. Application Access Bursary available (request by 30 November 2026). No application fee.

    Cove Park's biennial call for fully funded Awarded Residencies at its 50-acre rural residency site in Argyll, on the west coast of Scotland, programmed between May 2027 and December 2028. Residencies run from 2 weeks up to 12 weeks (average four weeks) and can be split across the two years around caring or work commitments; international residents can count slow-travel time as part of the funded period. Open to artists, creative practitioners, writers, translators and researchers in all art forms and at every career stage, including collectives and collaborative groups; no formal qualification needed. Priorities include work responding to climate breakdown, sound artists and musicians (new Sound Studio), and work on home, place, language (Scots, Gaelic, BSL), displacement and belonging. No final presentation is required. ELIGIBILITY: based in the UK, Central America or South America; Central and South American diaspora artists based in Europe or North America; or born in Scotland and living elsewhere. Anyone in full- or part-time formal education on 11 December 2026 is not eligible. SUPPORT: GBP 775 per week fee; travel allowance (Scotland GBP 65 to 100 Central Belt, up to GBP 200 Borders/Highlands/Islands; rest of UK up to GBP 250; Europe up to GBP 400; other international up to GBP 1,250); materials up to GBP 150; visa support; private self-catering accommodation (suitable for collaborators and those attending with children) and private studios. An Application Access Bursary (e.g. to pay a support worker) is available for applicants facing barriers due to disability or neurodivergence; request it from Alex Marrs by 30 November 2026. Video or audio applications accepted. Applicants are asked not to use generative AI in their application. APPLY via the online form: 500-word statement, 100-word bio, CV, up to 8 images or video/audio links or writing samples. SELECTION: shortlisting by the programme team and former residents; shortlist notified by 12 February 2027; Zoom interviews the week of 22 February 2027. Contact: Alex Marrs, alex@covepark.org. DEADLINE: Friday 11 December 2026, 5pm GMT.

  9. Arts Council England: National Portfolio Investment Programme 2028-33 (five-year funding for organisations in England)

    Arts Council England · England, UK. For arts organisations, non-national museums and libraries based in England. · Deadline: 15 Dec 2026 · Award: Annual funding from April 2028 to March 2033. Minimum request GBP 50,000 per year, no maximum; current National Portfolio Organisations received a planning figure in September 2026 (their 2026/27 investment plus the 5 percent uplift). No application fee.

    Arts Council England has opened applications for its next National Portfolio, the core structural funding programme for England's cultural sector. For the first time the programme offers five-year funding from the outset, covering April 2028 to March 2033, after the current portfolio was extended to March 2028. WHO: properly constituted arts organisations, non-national museums and libraries across England, with an independent board or oversight group in place; festivals, venues, companies, studios and digital and media-arts organisations are all within scope. Museums working towards Accreditation must achieve it by 1 April 2028. Applications must be viable without additional investment, and ACE warns that demand will exceed the available funding. WHAT YOU GET: regular annual funding for five years, minimum GBP 50,000 a year with no upper limit. APPLICATION: via the National Portfolio Applicant Hub, which holds the eligibility guidance, the full applicant guidance (published September 2026) and the online application. The window opened 20 October 2026. DEADLINE: 15 December 2026.

  10. The Creative Intelligence Award 2027 (CoSTAR National Lab x Southbank Centre)

    CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre, supported by UKRI · UK (lead applicant must be a UK-registered company; project activity UK-based). Prototyping at Pinewood Studios; showcase at Southbank Centre, London. · Deadline: 17 Dec 2026 · Award: GBP 50,000 grant (one award), plus up to GBP 25,000 in AI compute and cloud credits, 2 x 5 days studio access at Pinewood Studios, a six-week CoSTAR prototyping team sprint, 3 days pre-show testing, Southbank curatorial and producing support, and 20 hours of mentorship. No application fee.

    A new co-commission from CoSTAR National Lab and Southbank Centre to take a creative technology idea that is ready to be completed, honed or technically advanced through focused R&D, then present it to audiences at Southbank Centre's Creative Intelligence festival in autumn 2027. They want original concepts where AI, immersive, real-time, virtual production or other emerging technology is integral rather than an add-on, across any artform (music, theatre, dance, literature, visual and live art, design, architecture, food, film, games), with cross-disciplinary work encouraged and touring potential valued. ELIGIBILITY: lead applicant must be a UK-registered, trading company or other eligible UK legal entity; consortia welcome, and artists, technologists, researchers and sole traders can take part as collaborators. Team and funded activity must be UK-based. Webinar Q&A on 16 November 2026, 14:00 GMT. DEADLINE: 17 December 2026, 14:00 GMT.

    EVA London (BCS Computer Arts Society) · BCS London offices, Moorgate, London, UK, and online via Zoom (hybrid).     EVA London is a long-running hybrid conference on electronic visualisation and the arts, covering digital art, creative technology, computing and visual culture, held at BCS in Moorgate, London, and online. STRANDS: long papers (8 pages, draft PDF required) and short papers (2 pages, abstract-only proposal), both in 20-minute slots; papers with demonstration (8 pages); demonstrations (2 pages, 10 or 20 minutes, online demos possible); panel sessions (30 minutes); workshops and tutorials (usually 90 or 120 minutes); and a Research Workshop for postgraduate students and unaffiliated digital artists (15-minute talks, optional two-page written submission). TO APPLY: a summary proposal of up to 400 words via EasyChair (https://easychair.org/conferences/?conf=evalondon2027). DEADLINES: main proposals Monday 11 January 2027; Research Workshop applications by email to Graham Diprose (grahamdiprose@gmail.com) by Monday 22 February 2027, only while places remain. Notification 22 February 2027; final papers 12 March 2027; fees published 12 April 2027; registration opens 11 May 2027. Presenters must register; no honorarium, prize or current bursary scheme is stated.

  13. Daiwa Foundation Small Grants, March 2027 round (GBP 2,000-9,000 for UK-Japan projects)

    Daiwa Anglo-Japanese Foundation · For individuals, societies, associations and other bodies in the UK or Japan running projects that connect the two countries. · Deadline: 31 Mar 2027 · Award: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (typical awards GBP 3,000-4,000); flights up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day. A contribution, not full funding. No application fee.

    The Daiwa Foundation Small Grants support projects that promote interaction between the UK and Japan in any field, including arts exchanges, exhibitions, conferences, grassroots and educational exchanges and research travel, with travel between the two countries as the main focus. New initiatives are encouraged, and gender equality and SDG themes are welcome; continuing or regular events (such as an annual festival edition) are unlikely to be funded without a strong reason or special occasion. NOT ELIGIBLE: commissions of artworks, capital costs, consumables, salaries or professional fees, tuition and commercial activity. WHAT YOU GET: GBP 2,000 to 9,000 (most awards GBP 3,000-4,000), with flights funded up to GBP 1,200 each and subsistence up to GBP 100 per person per day, plus GBP 50 extra per flight for environmental projects in Japan. APPLICATION: read the Application Notes, then apply via the Foundation's online portal (separate forms for UK- and Japan-based applicants); advice from grants@dajf.org.uk. Decisions by late May or early June 2027. DEADLINE: 31 March 2027 (the following round closes 30 September).

  14. BFI Doc Society: Features Production Fund (UK, Documentary)

    BFI Doc Society · UK · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to £150,000 non-recoupable grant. No application fee.

    The BFI Doc Society Features Production Fund (delivered via BFI with Doc Society as delegate partner) supports UK filmmakers producing non-fiction feature films intended for theatrical release. Two strands: Directions (first- or second-time directors) and Momentum (established talent). AMOUNT: up to £150,000 non-recoupable grant (the total Doc Society contribution per project cannot exceed £150,000). ELIGIBILITY: UK-based filmmakers, both emerging and established directors; experienced and debut producers eligible; projects must meet at least three of six stated priorities: equity/diversity, audience impact, talent development, creative risk, UK-wide access, and environmental sustainability. PROCESS: two-stage — submit an Expression of Interest, then a Full Application if invited. Rolling intake year-round. Opened May 2026. APPLY via BFI Doc Society website. Contact: hellobfi@docsociety.org.

  15. Arts Council England: National Lottery Project Grants (rolling)

    Arts Council England · England, UK. For individual practitioners and organisations; funded activity must primarily benefit audiences in England. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 1,000 to GBP 100,000 per project. Decisions in about 8 weeks for requests up to GBP 30,000, about 12 weeks above that. No application fee.

    England's main always-open public funding route for project work: individual artists, freelancers, collectives and cultural organisations can apply at any time for GBP 1,000 to 100,000. Supported disciplines span visual arts, combined arts (including festivals), dance, theatre, music, literature, museums and libraries, and the fund regularly backs digital and interdisciplinary work within those. WHAT YOU GET: project funding covering artist fees, production, R&D within a project, touring or presentation, with decisions in roughly 8 weeks under GBP 30,000 and 12 weeks above. ELIGIBILITY: applicants based in England (others can apply if the activity substantially takes place in England and benefits English audiences); individuals must be 18+. DEADLINE: none, rolling applications via Grantium on artscouncil.org.uk.

    Creative Scotland · Scotland, UK. For artists and creative practitioners based in Scotland. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 500 to GBP 50,000 (two levels: up to GBP 20,000 with decisions in 10 weeks, GBP 20,001 to 50,000 in 14 weeks). National Lottery funded. No application fee.

  18. Creative Debuts: Black Artists Grant (BAG), GBP 500 monthly no-strings grant for UK-based Black artists (rolling)

    Creative Debuts · UK (remote application). Applicants must be based in the UK. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 500 per recipient, one recipient per month. Unrestricted, no outcomes or reporting required. No application fee.

    The Black Artists Grant (BAG) is a no-strings-attached monthly grant run and sponsored by Creative Debuts, the London-based platform for emerging artists. Each month one recipient receives GBP 500, which can be spent on anything: making new work, equipment or materials, travel, research, visiting exhibitions or conferences, or covering living costs. No outcomes or reporting are expected. ELIGIBILITY: Black artists (those with Black heritage who identify as Black; not politically Black, POC or BAME) based in the UK, working in any creative discipline (painters, musicians, directors, jewellers, set designers, sculptors, choreographers and more), with no age limit. APPLY once via the BAG page; all applications stay on file and are reconsidered each month, and unsuccessful applicants are not notified. DEADLINE: rolling, monthly selection.
