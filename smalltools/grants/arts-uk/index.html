Open calls

  1. The Photographers' Gallery: Future of Archives, Whose Archive is it Anyway? (Call for Speakers)

    The Photographers' Gallery · London, UK. Selected speakers must be available to speak in person at The Photographers' Gallery on 16 April 2027. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 200 speaker fee for each selected speaker. No application fee.

    Whose Archive is it Anyway? is the first in a three-part series of events at The Photographers' Gallery exploring the future of archives and archiving. This one-day event on Friday 16 April 2027 kickstarts the conversation with commissioned talks addressing what it means to make an archive today, who decides what an archive consists of, who creates an archive, who an archive is for, and who has access. The gallery welcomes submissions of real-life experiences rather than academic papers that challenge or answer these or other points related to the future of archives. SUGGESTED TOPICS: artist and photographers' legacies; institutional archives; memory (forgotten or lost histories made visible again); truth within archives (who is telling the stories and how); ownership (who has the authority to tell stories, who owns the images and their stories); relationships between those who create archives and those who use them; radical, non-traditional archives that redesign how stories are gathered and held to safeguard living memories; family archives; reclaimed photographic archives; counter archives that question dominant historical narratives; grassroots archives; technologies that reduce barriers to archives while safeguarding material; digital preservation and digitisation; and dialogic archives. ELIGIBILITY: open to all, especially those with experience of working with archives and the stories within them; applicants must be 18 or older and available to attend The Photographers' Gallery in London on 16 April 2027 to speak at the event. Selected speakers receive a fee of GBP 200 and will be notified by 18 January 2027. Questions to archive@tpg.org.uk. Deadline 1 November 2026, 11:59pm GMT.

  2. Shape Arts: Emergent Residency 2026/27 (Tate St Ives / Porthmeor Studios)

    Shape Arts, with Tate St Ives · Porthmeor Studios, St Ives, Cornwall, UK. Residency bursary only for artists who live or work in Cornwall, Devon or Somerset; disabled artists elsewhere in the UK can apply for the shortlisted cohort. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: GBP 3,000 bursary for the selected residency artist (restricted to artists living or working in Cornwall, Devon or Somerset). Disabled artists in the rest of the UK can join the shortlisted cohort, which brings mentoring and profile but no stated bursary. No application fee.

    Shape Arts' Emergent Residency places an early-career disabled artist at Porthmeor Studios in St Ives, in partnership with Tate St Ives. SUPPORT: GBP 3,000 bursary and a one-week residency (18 to 24 January 2027, plus travel days); a wider shortlisted cohort of disabled artists receives mentoring and support. ELIGIBILITY: self-identify as disabled; aged 18+; within the first five years of your artistic career (career pauses allowed); not in full-time education; not previously shortlisted for Emergent. The bursary and residency are only for artists who live or work in Cornwall, Devon or Somerset; disabled artists elsewhere in the UK can apply for the shortlisted cohort. APPLICATION: via Google Form, or a Word form by email; FAQs available in text, audio, BSL and Easy Read formats. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026, 23:59.

  3. Alpaca Festival 2027: Open Call for Pattern-Based Performances, Workshops and Talks (Sheffield, UK)

    Pattern Club (Alpaca Festival) · Sheffield, UK, with some talks and activities online. Open internationally, but only national (UK) travel is covered, plus a small budget for European train travel. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Performances: artist fee plus national travel expenses. Workshops: artist fee, materials expenses and national travel expenses. Fee amounts are not yet confirmed (tight budget); additional costs may be covered on request. International travel is not covered beyond a small European train budget. Conference talks: no fee or expenses, in-person speakers get a free festival weekend pass and lunch on 6 and 7 May. No application fee.

    Alpaca is a festival and conference on algorithmic patterns in the creative arts, run by the non-profit Pattern Club in Sheffield. It brings together live coders, weavers, braiders, origami makers, musicians and other practitioners who make patterns by procedure, and it welcomes contemporary algorithmic art that is human-authored. THEME (talks): making languages and notations for making patterns, with unexpected interpretations welcome. WHAT YOU CAN PROPOSE: live performances, hands-on workshops, and roughly 20-minute conference talks; accepted abstracts (max two pages) are published open access with a DOI and may be invited into an edited volume. WHAT YOU GET: performers receive an artist fee plus national travel; workshop leaders receive an artist fee, materials and national travel. The organisers state they cannot confirm fee amounts at this stage and may cover extra costs on request; international travel is not covered apart from a small European train budget. Talks are unpaid: in-person speakers get a free festival pass and lunch on the conference days. EXCLUSIONS: submissions focused on generative AI or unsupervised pattern generation by LLMs. APPLICATION: via the online form at calls.alpaca.cc; questions to contact@alpaca.cc. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026.

  4. Arts Council England: National Portfolio Investment Programme 2028-33 (five-year funding for organisations in England)

    Arts Council England · England, UK. For arts organisations, non-national museums and libraries based in England. · Deadline: 15 Dec 2026 · Award: Annual funding from April 2028 to March 2033. Minimum request GBP 50,000 per year, no maximum; current National Portfolio Organisations received a planning figure in September 2026 (their 2026/27 investment plus the 5 percent uplift). No application fee.

    Arts Council England has opened applications for its next National Portfolio, the core structural funding programme for England's cultural sector. For the first time the programme offers five-year funding from the outset, covering April 2028 to March 2033, after the current portfolio was extended to March 2028. WHO: properly constituted arts organisations, non-national museums and libraries across England, with an independent board or oversight group in place; festivals, venues, companies, studios and digital and media-arts organisations are all within scope. Museums working towards Accreditation must achieve it by 1 April 2028. Applications must be viable without additional investment, and ACE warns that demand will exceed the available funding. WHAT YOU GET: regular annual funding for five years, minimum GBP 50,000 a year with no upper limit. APPLICATION: via the National Portfolio Applicant Hub, which holds the eligibility guidance, the full applicant guidance (published September 2026) and the online application. The window opened 20 October 2026. DEADLINE: 15 December 2026.

  5. BFI Doc Society: Features Production Fund (UK, Documentary)

    BFI Doc Society · UK · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Up to £150,000 non-recoupable grant. No application fee.

    The BFI Doc Society Features Production Fund (delivered via BFI with Doc Society as delegate partner) supports UK filmmakers producing non-fiction feature films intended for theatrical release. Two strands: Directions (first- or second-time directors) and Momentum (established talent). AMOUNT: up to £150,000 non-recoupable grant (the total Doc Society contribution per project cannot exceed £150,000). ELIGIBILITY: UK-based filmmakers, both emerging and established directors; experienced and debut producers eligible; projects must meet at least three of six stated priorities: equity/diversity, audience impact, talent development, creative risk, UK-wide access, and environmental sustainability. PROCESS: two-stage — submit an Expression of Interest, then a Full Application if invited. Rolling intake year-round. Opened May 2026. APPLY via BFI Doc Society website. Contact: hellobfi@docsociety.org.

  6. Arts Council England: National Lottery Project Grants (rolling)

    Arts Council England · England, UK. For individual practitioners and organisations; funded activity must primarily benefit audiences in England. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 1,000 to GBP 100,000 per project. Decisions in about 8 weeks for requests up to GBP 30,000, about 12 weeks above that. No application fee.

    England's main always-open public funding route for project work: individual artists, freelancers, collectives and cultural organisations can apply at any time for GBP 1,000 to 100,000. Supported disciplines span visual arts, combined arts (including festivals), dance, theatre, music, literature, museums and libraries, and the fund regularly backs digital and interdisciplinary work within those. WHAT YOU GET: project funding covering artist fees, production, R&D within a project, touring or presentation, with decisions in roughly 8 weeks under GBP 30,000 and 12 weeks above. ELIGIBILITY: applicants based in England (others can apply if the activity substantially takes place in England and benefits English audiences); individuals must be 18+. DEADLINE: none, rolling applications via Grantium on artscouncil.org.uk.

  7. Creative Scotland: Open Fund for Individuals (rolling)

    Creative Scotland · Scotland, UK. For artists and creative practitioners based in Scotland. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: GBP 500 to GBP 50,000 (two levels: up to GBP 20,000 with decisions in 10 weeks, GBP 20,001 to 50,000 in 14 weeks). National Lottery funded. No application fee.

    Scotland's main always-open fund for individual artists and freelance creative practitioners across all artforms, including visual art, moving image, sound, digital and cross-disciplinary practice. It supports the development of creative practice and new work: research, experimentation, creation, collaboration, professional development and small-scale presentation. WHAT YOU GET: GBP 500 to 50,000 covering artist time at fair pay rates, materials, access costs and project costs; decisions in 10 weeks for requests up to GBP 20,000 and 14 weeks up to GBP 50,000. ELIGIBILITY: individuals based in Scotland (not organisations; a separate Open Fund serves them). DEADLINE: none, rolling applications via the Creative Scotland portal, subject to available budget.
