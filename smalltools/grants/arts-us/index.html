Visual and Media Arts Grants in the US

Currently 13 active visual and media arts grants, fellowships and residencies open to applicants in the US. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. Gallery Route One: Fellowship Program 2027-2028 (Point Reyes Station, Bay Area artists)

    Gallery Route One · Gallery Route One, 11101 Highway One, Point Reyes Station, California, USA. Applicants must live or work in the greater Bay Area within commuting distance of Point Reyes Station. · Deadline: 30 Sep 2026 · Award: Two fellowships. Each fellow receives USD 1,500 to support the production and presentation of work, a minimum of ten exhibition opportunities over two years including one solo show and one two-person exhibition, mentoring, publicity and public engagement support, and eligibility to teach in the Artist in the Schools Program. No application fee.

    Gallery Route One, a nonprofit artist-run gallery in Point Reyes Station, offers a two-year fellowship for emerging artists from the greater Bay Area. WHAT YOU GET: USD 1,500 toward the production and presentation of work; a minimum of ten exhibition opportunities across the 24 months, including one solo show and one two-person exhibition; mentoring from GRO artists; publicity, receptions, artist talks, radio appearances and studio visits; website and social media exposure; professional development; networking with artists, donors and the community; and eligibility to teach in GRO's Artist in the Schools Program. ELIGIBILITY: artists aged 21 to 40 who live or work in the greater Bay Area within commuting distance of Point Reyes Station, with a serious fine arts practice built on training, education or experience, a portfolio of work from the last five years, and clear goals for artistic development and public exposure. Two fellows are selected each cycle. Applications are accepted 1 to 30 September 2026 via the form on the fellowship page. DEADLINE: 30 September 2026.

  2. Frameline Completion Fund 2027

    Frameline · Administered from San Francisco, United States. Open to filmmakers of all nationalities. · Deadline: 09 Oct 2026 · Award: Grants of up to USD 5,000, with a standard range of USD 1,000 to 5,000; the jury can recommend amounts above or below that range. No application fee.

    Long-running post-production grant from Frameline, the San Francisco organisation behind the world's longest-running LGBTQ+ film festival, for films that represent and reflect LGBTQ+ life in all its complexity and richness. Grants run up to USD 5,000, typically USD 1,000 to 5,000, and the jury may recommend more or less than that range. WHAT IT COVERS: any post-production cost, explicitly including editing, special effects and graphics, music licensing, photo licensing, and, notably, closed captioning and audio description, so accessibility work is fundable rather than an afterthought. STAGE GATE, the thing that disqualifies most applicants: your project must be at least 90 percent complete with a rough cut available, and in or about to enter post-production. Work still in development or mid-production is not eligible. WHO AND WHAT: all formats, lengths, genres and nationalities are welcome, spanning documentary, narrative, experimental, animated and episodic work, so long as the film is about LGBTQ+ people and their communities. Directors must have creative and financial control of the project. NO APPLICATION FEE, which is worth flagging given how many completion funds charge one. TIMING: the submission window opens 31 August 2026 and closes 9 October 2026, with decisions in January 2027. The 2026 cycle's grantees have already been announced ahead of Frameline's 50th festival, so this is the next round.

  3. Pedantic Arts Residency and Research Grant 2027 (Pittsburgh)

    Pedantic Arts Residency · Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, USA. Open to non-Pittsburgh residents aged 18 or older; travel is self-funded. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: Residency: free furnished housing in a shared apartment plus a USD 2,000 living stipend (half on arrival, half on completion). Research Grant: a free private bedroom at the Pedantic Apartment plus a USD 500 or USD 1,000 honorarium at the grantee's discretion. Application free until 28 September 2026, then USD 20.

    Pedantic Arts Residency opens applications for its Summer 2027 and Fall 2027 programmes in Pittsburgh: two four-week residency cohorts and two research grants. THE RESIDENCY houses three creatives at a time (a visual artist, an organizer and a writer) in a collective four-week programme that emphasizes exploration, exchange and community connection over production. Residents get free furnished housing in a shared apartment, a USD 2,000 stipend to cover living expenses (half on arrival, half on completion), a Pittsburgh-based guide working in their field, tickets and institutional passes, and introductions across the city's creative community. THE RESEARCH GRANT invites an individual visual artist, curator or writer for a one- to two-week independent stay to conduct Pittsburgh-related research, with a free private bedroom at the Pedantic Apartment, a USD 500 or USD 1,000 honorarium usable at the grantee's discretion, and help establishing relevant connections. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be 18 or older and not Pittsburgh residents at the time of application; travel costs are the participant's own. Residency attendance and staying in the provided housing are required. APPLICATION: applications are free until 28 September 2026 at 23:59 EST; a USD 20 fee applies from then until the final deadline of 15 October 2026 at 23:59 EST.

  4. AAPI Fund: Creative Catalyst Fellowship 2027 (theme Belonging)

    AAPI Civic Engagement Fund · United States; US residency required, no citizenship requirement (W-9 or W-8BEN needed). In-person retreat 16 to 18 February 2027, travel and accommodation covered. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: Up to eight artists each receive USD 30,000 to produce new work. Also covered: the two-day Fellows retreat (travel and accommodation paid by the AAPI Fund), an accomplished communications consultant, digital advertising, community introductions, exhibition placement support and replica development (posters, books, calendars) promoting the finished work. No application fee.

    The Creative Catalyst Fellowship from the AAPI Civic Engagement Fund supports individual artists whose work engages the public on critical issues facing Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders and can serve as a catalyst for civic participation. Up to eight artists each receive USD 30,000 to produce work with the potential to break through to broader audiences and spark conversation around the 2027 theme, Belonging: a sense of community, home, agency and the essential right for AAPI communities to flourish, as a counterweight to racism and violence. Fellows join a yearlong cohort (January to December 2027) convened for peer sharing and learning. REQUIRED: a virtual orientation, the two-day in-person Fellows retreat on 16 to 18 February 2027 (location to be finalised; the AAPI Fund covers travel and accommodation), virtual check-ins through the year, and a narrative report; showcasing at AAPI Fund events is optional. OFFERINGS beyond the grant: the annual retreat connecting fellows with community work and the wider social justice movement, a communications consultant to place local and national news stories, digital advertising promoting the final products, introductions to AAPI organisations, exhibition placement support (priority to venues experienced with social justice themes), and replica development such as posters, books, calendars and stickers. ELIGIBILITY: individual artists only (no organisations or collectives), 18 or older, US residency required; there is no citizenship requirement, but selected fellows provide a W-9 or W-8BEN. Work must connect with AAPI communities. Eligible forms include visual art (printmaking, illustration, photography, modular installations), digital media, written forms and hybrid work; feature-length film, fully staged theater, dance works and large-scale fixed installations are ineligible, though such practices can be adapted into more concise formats. Previous fellows may reapply three years after completing their fellowship. APPLICATION: via the online form with up to five examples of work relating to the proposed project as a single PDF (portfolio or website links allowed). Deadline 15 October 2026 at 11:59pm PT; fellowship announcement 14 January 2027. Artwork must be completed by 15 August 2027. Contact: creative@aapifund.org with subject CC inquiry.

  5. Culture Push: Fellowship for Utopian Practice 2027 (New York City)

    Culture Push · New York City, US. Project work must occur within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of NYC. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 2,250 stipend, plus fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, feedback and mentoring. No application fee.

    Culture Push, the New York nonprofit founded by artists to support hybrid art and civic experimental practice, selects 6 to 8 fellows per cycle for its yearlong Fellowship for Utopian Practice. The fellowship backs boundary-pushing, socially engaged projects that are new and without prior institutional support. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 2,250 stipend, fiscal sponsorship for fundraising, and structured feedback and mentoring across the year. ELIGIBILITY: minimum of three years of experience; currently enrolled undergraduates cannot apply; group and collaborative proposals are welcome; project work must take place within the five boroughs and fellows must live within commuting distance of New York City (not strictly NYC residents). The same deadline also covers the 2027 Black Utopian Fellowship, Disability Arts + Dreaming Fellowship, and Climate Justice Fellowship. Info sessions were held 24 September (virtual) and 26 September (in person). DEADLINE: 15 October 2026 at 11:59 pm ET. Fellowship runs January to December 2027.

  6. Humanity AI: Open Call for Proposals 2026

    Humanity AI (coalition of 10 philanthropies) · United States. Lead applicants must be US-based 501(c)(3) organizations (or Tribal governments); all proposals must serve US-based communities. Individuals can apply through a fiscal sponsor or as part of a collaboration. · Deadline: 21 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 10 million total pool, grants of USD 75,000 to 1,000,000 across three tiers: Tier 1 USD 75,000-150,000; Tier 2 USD 150,000-500,000; Tier 3 USD 500,000-1,000,000. Grants run up to three years. No application fee.

    Humanity AI, an initiative of ten leading philanthropies, opens a USD 10 million call for community-led initiatives that shape how AI affects people's lives. ISSUE AREAS: Education (student and educator-centered work shaping AI's educational impact); Labor and Economy (worker-led efforts to ensure AI enhances rather than replaces employment); and Humanities, Arts, and Culture (artist and creator-focused work protecting cultural contributions). ELIGIBLE APPROACHES: shaping the rules for AI governance and deployment; building AI controlled by communities rather than corporations; and challenging misaligned AI deployments. Community-led means the affected community defines the goals and shapes the approach. Priority goes to open-weight and open-source AI models over proprietary ones. OUT OF SCOPE: AI literacy training, AI adoption promotion, and research-only work. GRANTS: three tiers from USD 75,000 to 1,000,000, up to three years. ELIGIBILITY: US-based 501(c)(3) organizations as lead applicants; individuals via fiscal sponsors or collaborations; Tribal governments treated as States under IRC Section 7871; for-profit entities only as collaborators, not leads; all proposals must serve US-based communities. TIMELINE: launched 10 September 2026; applicant webinar 14 September 2026; initial applications due 21 October 2026 at 4:59pm PT; finalists notified December 2026; awards announced early spring 2027. Apply via Submittable; an organizational readiness tool on the site helps assess eligibility first.

  7. American-Scandinavian Foundation: Fellowships for Americans in the Nordic Countries 2027-28

    American-Scandinavian Foundation · Nordic region (Denmark, Faroe Islands, Greenland, Iceland, Finland, Norway, Sweden, and Sapmi). For American graduate students, academic professionals, and creative artists; project must take place in the Nordic countries. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Long-term fellowships of up to USD 23,000 for 4 to 12 months; short-term grants of up to USD 5,000 for 1 to 3 months. In 2026, 31 American Fellows received over USD 277,390 in total funding. Awards are given in all fields, including creative arts projects.

    The American-Scandinavian Foundation's award program for study and research abroad is its longest-standing commitment to educational exchange: over the past 100 years, more than 4,000 fellowships and grants have gone to Americans working in the Nordic region and Scandinavians in the US engaged in study, research, or creative arts projects. Fellowships for Americans in the Nordic Countries support projects in Denmark, the Faroe Islands, Greenland, Iceland, Finland, Norway, Sweden, and Sapmi. AMOUNTS: long-term fellowships of up to USD 23,000 for 4 to 12 months, and short-term grants of up to USD 5,000 for 1 to 3 months; awards are given in all fields, with graduate students (preferably dissertation-related), academic professionals, and creative artists eligible. In 2026, 31 American Fellows were granted over USD 277,390 in total. REPORTING: fellows abroad more than six months submit an interim report mid-project, and all fellows submit a final narrative report within 60 days of the fellowship period's end. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026 for the 2027-28 cycle. See ASF's Program Guidelines for full requirements and apply via the ASF application portal.

  8. MIT Museum: BRAIN Digital Content Open Call

    MIT Museum · Cambridge, Massachusetts, US (content shown on-site; remote creation possible) · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: Flat honorarium of USD 2,500 to 5,000 per selected proposal, at the Museum's discretion

    The MIT Museum seeks proposals for compelling digital content exploring humanity's relationship with the BRAIN, the museum's thematic focus from April to September 2027 bringing together art, science, technology and design on cognition, neurotechnology, brain-computer interfaces, mental health and the boundaries of ability. Selected proposals run as ambient content on the two-story digital screen (3840x3024, 80:63 aspect ratio, 20'3" x 16'2") in the museum's central Exchange amphitheater. The museum wants immersive, non-linear, experimental, non-didactic work, such as artful visualizations of data or scientific phenomena; interactivity (touchscreens, QR codes) is out of scope and content-critical audio is discouraged. Content must run on the museum's existing hardware (Windows PC with RTX 3090, NEC LED wall with Analog Way 4K system). WHO CAN APPLY: artists, scientists, engineers, designers and other creative practitioners, from MIT or the broader community. SUPPORT: flat honorarium of USD 2,500-5,000 per selected proposal; no additional development funding. Applications reviewed from 2 November, accepted list finalized January 2027. Deadline: 1 November 2026.

  9. Harry Ransom Center Research Fellowships 2027-2028 (Austin, Texas)

    Harry Ransom Center, The University of Texas at Austin · Austin, Texas, USA. On-site research in the Ransom Center's collections; international applicants welcome and receive an additional one-time USD 500 stipend for visa and travel costs. · Deadline: 06 Nov 2026 · Award: Postdoctoral Fellowships: USD 3,500 per month for one or two months. Dissertation Fellowships: USD 2,500. Travel Stipends: USD 2,000 (open to graduate students, faculty and independent researchers including artists and journalists). International recipients get an extra USD 500. No application fee.

    The Harry Ransom Center at The University of Texas at Austin awards research fellowships for on-site work in its collections spanning literature, photography, film, art, performing arts, music and cultural history, supporting scholarly articles, dissertations, creative works, digital humanities projects and exhibition proposals. FELLOWSHIP TYPES: Postdoctoral Fellowships of USD 3,500 per month for one or two months, open to holders of a PhD or terminal degree (MFA, MLIS), current or former faculty, and similarly qualified researchers without institutional affiliation; Dissertation Fellowships of USD 2,500 for graduate students with doctoral dissertations in progress; and Travel Stipends of USD 2,000 open to graduate students, current or former faculty and independent researchers including journalists and artists (not combinable with other Ransom Center fellowships). International recipients in all categories receive an additional one-time USD 500 stipend toward visa and travel costs. Fellows spend most days in the Reading Room consulting the collections, with access to a shared office, during a residency period between 1 June 2027 and 31 August 2028. APPLY with a three-page proposal PDF and letters of recommendation; recommendation letters are also due by the deadline. DEADLINE: 6 November 2026, 5:00 PM CST.

  10. A.I.R. Gallery: Fellowship Program 2027-2028 (NYC)

    A.I.R. Gallery · New York City. Applicants must be residents of the five boroughs for the full fellowship duration. · Deadline: 10 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 3,000 stipend per fellow, plus a solo exhibition at A.I.R. Gallery, 4 to 5 professional development workshops, an assigned artist-mentor, a studio visit with a review panelist and ongoing staff support. Six fellows selected. No application fee stated.

    The A.I.R. Fellowship Program for Emerging and Underrepresented Women and Non-Binary Artists, established in 1993 by artist Stephanie Bernheim, annually awards six emerging artists based in New York City their first solo exhibition in NYC with the support of the A.I.R. community; more than 130 artists have come through the program. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 3,000 stipend, a solo exhibition at A.I.R. Gallery in spring/summer 2028, 4 to 5 professional development workshops, an assigned artist-mentor, a one-on-one studio visit with a review panelist, and curatorial and staff support across the 12-month fellowship (September 2027 to August 2028). WHO IS ELIGIBLE: women and non-binary visual artists working in any media, 18 or older, resident in the five boroughs of New York City for the full fellowship; you must not have had a solo exhibition at a permanent NYC venue in the last 10 years, must not be enrolled in a degree-granting program during the fellowship, must not be in a comparable development program, and must not be an A.I.R. staff/board member or fellowship alum. SELECTION PANEL for this cycle: Ada Potter, Jo-ey Tang and Mindy Seu. DEADLINE: 10 November 2026, 11:59pm ET. TO APPLY: via Submittable, linked from the program page.

  11. Beam Camp 2027 Project Open Call (large-scale collaborative artwork)

    Beam Center · Strafford, New Hampshire, USA. Proposals accepted worldwide; design work is remote, and reasonable travel expenses are reimbursed if the proposer joins camp during the July 2027 session. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 award for the selected designer(s), plus a fabrication budget of roughly USD 15,000 for the project and reimbursement of reasonable travel expenses if the proposer attends camp. No application fee.

    Beam Center seeks design proposals for an ambitious public artwork to be built by a community of more than 100 young people (ages 9-17) and 20+ staff at Beam Camp in Strafford, New Hampshire in July 2027. Every year the winning Big Project becomes the centerpiece of the four-week camp session: projects are always ambitious and technically challenging large-scale constructions, and the themes they introduce circulate through all of camp life. The Project Team works with the winning designer(s) to translate the vision into a real construction with a high level of craftsmanship. WHO CAN APPLY: creative individuals or teams with big ideas, including artists, engineers, architects, builders, filmmakers, musicians, designers and technologists, from anywhere in the world and of any age. Winning proposals are selected by a committee of Beam Center staff, youth, past project designers and community members. The call notes that while AI is recognised as useful, it should be used sparingly; the committee wants to hear the idea from its human designers. TO APPLY: submit a single PDF with project title, detailed description, visuals (sketches, diagrams, renderings, video links) and information on all co-designers to contact@beamcenter.org. Info sessions on Zoom 22 October and 14 November 2026. DEADLINE: 30 November 2026, 11:59 PM EST.

  12. Wave Farm: 2027 Research and Production Program (Transmission Art Residencies and Radio Art Fellowships, NY)

    Wave Farm · Wave Farm, Acra, NY, US (Catskill Mountain Park). International open call. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 1,000 stipend for each 10-day residency; USD 2,000 for each two-month Radio Art Fellowship. No application fee.

    Wave Farm, the Warhol Foundation-backed transmission arts center in Acra, NY, opens one application covering all its 2027 opportunities: the Transmission Art Residency, the Spatial Sound Residency, and two Radio Art Fellowship tracks (Research, and Community Engagement) connected to Wave Farm's WGXC radio station. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 1,000 stipend for each 10-day residency and USD 2,000 for each two-month fellowship, with access to Wave Farm's research library, transmitter and receiver equipment, and on-site staff support to develop a new transmission artwork. ELIGIBILITY: international; collaborations allowed; full-time students are ineligible except rare post-graduate cases; past residents and fellows may reapply; applicants may apply to as many opportunities as they wish but will be offered at most one; DJ sets are explicitly ineligible. A strong fit for signal-based, broadcast, spatial sound and radio-adjacent work. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026 at 11:59 pm EST. Finalists are interviewed by Zoom, with notifications in late February 2027 and residencies during 2027.

  13. apexart Open Calls: Curatorial Proposals for NYC and International Exhibitions

    apexart · Exhibitions in apexart's New York City space (October call) or anywhere in the world except NYC (February call). Anyone, from anywhere, can submit. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 10,000 per selected exhibition: USD 8,000 for expenses (curator travel, artwork shipping, artist and participant honorariums, equipment, installation) plus an honorarium of up to USD 2,000 for the curator/organizer. For exhibitions outside NYC, apexart matches up to USD 1,000 for venue rental if needed. apexart additionally covers insurance, advertising, publicity, brochure design/printing/mailing, the opening reception and (for NYC) documentation, and handles most logistics. No application fee.

    apexart's Open Calls turn a curatorial idea into a funded apexart exhibition, selected by a crowdsourced international jury of more than 600 people in 70 countries. WHO CAN SUBMIT: anyone, from anywhere, with no fees at any level: trained and student curators, artists, writers and creative people regardless of experience, background or location. HOW: a 500-word proposal for a focused, idea-driven, original group exhibition, submitted through the online form during the proposal window. TWO ANNUAL WINDOWS: every October for exhibitions in apexart's NYC space, and every February (to early March) for exhibitions taking place anywhere in the world except NYC; for the international call you must propose a location you have actually visited, with the specific venue settled after selection. SELECTION: jurors vote anonymously online (each proposal gets an equal number of views, submitter names hidden); proposals are judged on the strength of the idea and how well it is communicated, not on connections or CV. WHAT WINNERS GET: a USD 10,000 budget (USD 8,000 for expenses, up to USD 2,000 curator honorarium, plus up to USD 1,000 venue-rental match outside NYC), with apexart covering insurance, publicity, the printed brochure, the opening reception and most logistics including loan agreements and shipping, and staff working closely with the guest curator. Winning proposals become part of apexart's next exhibition season. Check the site for the current window's exact dates; submission forms only go live during the proposal periods.
