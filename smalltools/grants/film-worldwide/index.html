  1. Day Job: Short Film Fund 2026

    Day Job Films · Worldwide applicants; all production must take place in the UK · Deadline: 10 Aug 2026 · Award: Production budget of up to GBP 7,000, with full creative control and no producer or external interference. No application fee.

    Short film competition from Day Job Films created to give auteur directors full creative freedom, awarding a production budget of up to GBP 7,000 to make a short film with no producer interference or commercial agenda. Open to animation, live-action and documentary projects. Runs in three rounds: (1) a pitch deck outlining concept, story, key characters and visual style; (2) a full script for those who progress; and (3) an interview to discuss the vision and execution. A panel of industry professionals judges the pitch deck, script and interview, with at least one team member reading each pitch deck and script in full. The winner receives full creative control over the project. ELIGIBILITY: applicants can be from anywhere worldwide, but all production must take place in the UK; applicants must have a producer on board who is separate from the director/writer. Applicants may optionally include a plan for adapting the script into a feature-length film (this does not affect the outcome). Submissions open February 10, 2026 and close August 10, 2026. Enquiries: hello@dayjobfilms.com.

  7. Sundance Collab x Merrell: Brand-Supported Storytelling Program 2026

    Sundance Collab (Sundance Institute) in collaboration with Merrell · Online program, open to filmmakers worldwide. Production takes place wherever the filmmaker is based. One participant receives a covered hotel stay at the Sundance Film Festival in Park City, Utah, USA in January 2027. · Deadline: 18 Aug 2026 · Award: USD 20,000 production budget per selected filmmaker to make and deliver the greenlit concept, cutdowns (:06, :15, :30) and stills. Includes an online brand-supported storytelling program, and a covered hotel stay for one participant to attend the Sundance Film Festival in January 2027. No application fee.

    Sundance Collab and Merrell are selecting five filmmakers from around the world to each make an original 60 to 90 second vertical film for social platforms, built around Merrell's 'It Starts Outside' brand platform. The brief is not a commercial: it asks for a complete, emotionally resonant human story in which the outside acts as a catalyst for change, exploring how stepping outside is an antidote to increasingly screen-locked, indoor lives. Stories should reflect transformation (a moment of reset, reconnection or personal shift), connection (belonging, community, renewed relationship) and human truth (an observed, emotionally grounded moment). The strongest entries show what changed in a person because they went outside, emotionally, physically, mentally, creatively, spiritually or psychologically, rather than a final achievement. Selected filmmakers enter a three-day online intensive in September on collaborating with a brand, then receive USD 20,000 to produce and deliver their greenlit concept, cutdowns (:06, :15, :30) and stills between October and December, with scheduled check-ins. One participant also receives a covered hotel stay to join the team at the Sundance Film Festival in January 2027. APPLICATION: one entry per person; all submitted materials must be created by the submitter; all answers and materials must be in English or carry English subtitles. The core submission is a written response of up to 500 words on how you would approach the brief. CRITICAL GUIDELINES: design for vertical with a strong hook in the first five seconds; prioritise human, observational storytelling over polished commercial aesthetics; feature real people and authentic moments; treat the outside as context rather than the hero; favour everyday, intimate moments over epic outcomes; avoid overt brand narration; no competitor products (Merrell product is provided but must not be the focus); no AI-generated content in consumer-facing materials. Selection criteria are originality, emotional arc, a distinct point of view and cultural relevance. TIMELINE: submissions opened 28 July 2026; deadline 11:59 p.m. PT on Tuesday 18 August 2026 (08:59 CEST on 19 August); selected artists notified by 16 September 2026. An Insider Session with Merrell Creative Director Alex Ho and Sundance Collab Director Patty West runs on 11 August. Sundance Collab provides accommodations and support services for participants with disabilities; email accessibility@sundance.org to arrange services.

  10. AIxDESIGN: Grain, Open Call for Critical AI Literacy Recipes

    AIxDESIGN, co-produced with the Netherlands Film Academy · Remote/online; open to educators and practitioners based anywhere · Deadline: 31 Aug 2026 · Award: Need-based fee of EUR 150 per recipe for freelance and independent practitioners: say so in the proposal form if making the time would be hard without it, and it is settled in the same reply. Launch contributors also get their recipe in the first edition of Grain in print and online, credited to them, a print copy when it lands, an invite to the launch event in Amsterdam in spring 2027, and support turning the exercise into a recipe. Published under CC BY-SA 4.0; you keep authorship. No application fee.

    Grain is a cookbook of critical and creative AI literacy exercises for educators in film and the wider audiovisual and creative field, co-produced by AIxDESIGN and the Netherlands Film Academy and led by Nadia Piet. It publishes in March/April 2027, digital and print, under CC BY-SA 4.0. The current open call is to propose a recipe by the end of August 2026 and join as a launch contributor. WHAT THEY WANT: tried-and-tested group exercises you have actually run with people in teaching or workshops, specifically in the context of film, audiovisual work, time-based media and storytelling. Hands-on or analog, 15 minutes or a full day, beginner or advanced; the one hard requirement is that another educator can read your recipe and run it themselves. What that might look like: hands-on experiments with specific AI tools (image generators, chatbots, code assistants, video models); analog activities that reveal computational logic through embodied practice; critical readings, watchings or listenings with structured discussion; provocations, games or role-plays that get at how AI sees, thinks or makes; and exercises that help people work out how they want to relate to AI in their own practice. WHAT MAKES A GOOD RECIPE: it helps a room of people think together and trusts the room to be smart given the right container; it emerges from real practice rather than an untested idea; it is specific about what participants do, step by step, so a stranger could pick it up and adapt it to their group, time, tools or context; it has at least one designed social moment where the thinking happens between people rather than privately; it is about something larger than technique (how a machine sees an image, where your values bump up against what the tools want from you, what a film's rhythm looks like laid out in space instead of time); it takes a position without telling people what to think; and it points beyond itself with references, reading and reference projects. Published example recipes include Image Forgery: Archival Mimesis with AI (Artefacto), Machine Ways of Seeing (Nadia Piet), AI Film Now: A Close Watching Exercise (Nadia Piet) and Outside the Timeline: Spatial Grid + Visual Clustering (Artefacto), alongside a root recipe template. WHAT LAUNCH CONTRIBUTORS GET: your recipe in the first edition, print and online, credited to you; a print copy when it lands and an invite to the launch event in Amsterdam in spring 2027; support turning your exercise into a recipe, via Recipe Press (their LLM helper), the cook-along, or directly with the team; and a need-based fee of EUR 150 per recipe for freelance and independent practitioners, which you request in the proposal form. After this round contributions stay open but web-only: no fee, no dedicated support, no print. COOK-ALONG: one live 30-minute online session on Wednesday 12 August 2026 at 16:00 CEST, where you bring a rough exercise and turn it into a recipe on the spot with Recipe Press (sign up at luma.com/84w148in). HOW IT WORKS: (1) propose your recipe through the proposal form (who you are, what the exercise is, where you have run it, and whether you are requesting the fee); a short pitch is enough, and if it is already written you can just link your draft; (2) they reply on a rolling basis, with one reply covering both whether it is a match for the first edition and the fee if you requested it, so the earlier you send the earlier you hear back; (3) once it is a yes, write it up using the structure on the Contribute a Recipe page, with Recipe Press or at the cook-along. TIMELINE: proposals open until the end of August 2026, reviewed as they come in; accepted recipes due in full by mid-September 2026; Grain publishes March/April 2027. Questions to hello@aixdesign.co.

  15. IDA Emergency Assistance

    International Documentary Association · Remote (worldwide applicants) · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: One-time emergency grant of USD 500 to USD 5,000

    The International Documentary Association runs an Emergency Assistance program that provides direct financial support to documentary filmmakers whose work puts them in imminent and extreme physical danger. Eligible filmmakers may request a one-time emergency grant between USD 500 and USD 5,000. IDA responds to all applications within 24 hours and handles them with strict confidentiality, with approved funds transferred in as few as five business days. Applicants need an existing risk assessment plan, a concrete safety plan, two references confirming the emergency, and a secure way to receive funds. The program launched as a pilot on March 9, 2026 and accepts applications on a rolling basis. For sensitive cases, applicants can contact emergency@documentary.org to share information over an end-to-end encrypted app such as Signal.
