Cross-Disciplinary and Social Impact Grants in Canada

Open calls

  1. Video Pool: Media Art + Technology Residency (MATR) 2027

    Video Pool Media Arts Centre · In person at Video Pool Media Arts Centre and its Poolside Gallery, 221-100 Arthur Street, Winnipeg, Manitoba R3B 1H3, Canada. · Deadline: 30 Aug 2026 · Award: CAD 2,250 artist fee, covering both the residency period and the subsequent Poolside Gallery exhibition. Residents also get full access to VP's equipment, facilities and technical resources; the Poolside Gallery itself is equipped with two projectors and four speakers. Applicants must be VP members, so a membership is purchased separately, but there is no separate application fee.

    Video Pool's Media Art + Technology Residency supports experimental, independent, technology-based work. The selected artist or artists develop a new project (or adapt and transform an existing one) in direct response to the intimate scale and specific character of the Poolside Gallery, culminating in an exhibition there. The call is deliberately open on medium: video, sound, installation, digital media, performance, interactive technologies, expanded cinema and interdisciplinary practices are all welcome. VP asks proposals to speak clearly to your practice, the questions, materials or ideas that drive the work, and how that work might grow, shift or take new form inside the gallery. It is explicitly interested in expansive proposals, work that pushes past the gallery's four walls to engage its surrounding spaces (hallways, windows, projection wall) or that reimagines what the gallery itself can hold, and in projects that engage technology as a creative, critical, aesthetic or socio-political tool. Priority is given to artists from equity-seeking communities whose practices contribute to the advancement of experimental media arts. Residents get full access to VP's available equipment, facilities and technical resources throughout the residency, and applicants are encouraged to review the equipment and facilities list before proposing. ELIGIBILITY: open to VP members (this is a member submission call); no nationality restriction is stated, but the residency and exhibition are in person in Winnipeg. APPLICATION: artist bio (1,500 characters), headshot, description of your artistic practice (3,000 characters), project proposal for MATR (3,000 characters), equipment and facility needs (750 characters), description of how you would use the space (750 characters), CV, up to five images (optional) and up to two video or documentation links. Submit via the online form at videopool.org/matr-2027-application/ by 30 August 2026; alternative submission formats can be arranged by contacting Emma (emma@videopool.org) or Iyunade (iyunade@videopool.org). General enquiries: info@videopool.org, 204-949-9134.

  2. Guggenheim Fellowship 2027

    John Simon Guggenheim Memorial Foundation · Anywhere; the fellowship funds the fellow's own work rather than residence at a host institution. Open to citizens and permanent residents of the United States and Canada. · Deadline: 15 Sep 2026 · Award: Variable cash grant sized to the applicant's proposed budget and needs; the Foundation does not publish a fixed figure or range, and recent awards have typically run into the tens of thousands of US dollars. Roughly 175 to 200 fellowships are awarded each year across more than 50 fields. The Foundation charges no application fee and has no affiliated application consultants.

    The Guggenheim Fellowship is awarded to mid-career individuals who have already demonstrated exceptional capacity for productive scholarship or exceptional creative ability in the arts, giving them six to twelve months of funded time to work with as much creative freedom as possible. Around 175 to 200 fellows are chosen each year from a field of several thousand applicants (a success rate of roughly five percent), across more than 50 fields spanning the creative arts, the humanities, the natural sciences and the social sciences, including architecture, film, music, photography, fine arts, computer science and interdisciplinary work. ELIGIBILITY: citizens and permanent residents of the United States and Canada; applicants must be mid-career with a significant record of published or exhibited work, and current students and previous Guggenheim Fellows are not eligible. APPLICATION: through the Foundation's own portal, which opens in late summer 2026, so check the site if it is not yet live. The application deadline is 15 September 2026 with no late submissions accepted; work samples are due 29 September 2026 and reference letters 13 October 2026. Results are announced in late April 2027. No fee.

  3. Artists in the Library 2027 (Toronto Arts Council)

    Toronto Arts Council, in partnership with Toronto Public Library · Toronto, Canada. Programming takes place at a Toronto Public Library branch outside the downtown core, in Etobicoke York, North York, Toronto and East York, or Scarborough. Applicants must be City of Toronto residents. · Deadline: 06 Oct 2026 · Award: Maximum grant of CAD 20,000, which may cover up to 100 percent of project costs. Payment of professional artist fees is a requirement of the programme. Recipients also get access to space and facilities at the assigned library branch for arts programming and presentation, and support from the branch team. Funded by the City of Toronto. No application fee is mentioned.

    Created in 2014 as a partnership between Toronto Arts Council and Toronto Public Library, Artists in the Library gives Toronto artists paid opportunities and space while widening access to the arts in Etobicoke, York, North York, East York and Scarborough. It funds professional artists, arts organisations and collectives in any artistic discipline to create and deliver free community-engaged arts programming at a Toronto Public Library branch. TAC encourages applications from its equity priority groups and from artists working in their local communities. WHAT RECIPIENTS GET OUT OF IT: a chance to explore their practice in a community setting, access to branch space and facilities, meaningful engagement with the branch and its community, close support from the branch team, experience navigating a multi-stakeholder environment, and the delivery of a community-engaged arts project from start to finish. WHAT COUNTS: community engagement is required but its format can vary, covering exhibitions, installations, performances, concerts, screenings, readings, filmmaking, storytelling, workshops, artist talks, participatory programmes, arts education and mentorship. Permanent exhibitions and installations are not eligible. Projects are produced by the recipient, hosted and supported by the assigned branch, and must be delivered on site within branch operating hours. SCALE: because host slots at TPL branches are limited, this is not for one-off workshops or small-scale events; the offering must be substantial with a budget to match. LOCATION: there is no list of eligible branches for 2026. Applicants indicate a preferred area or neighbourhood outside the downtown core and TPL staff assign the branch based on suitability and availability, so a different branch than the one requested may be assigned. For Toronto and East York, downtown is excluded, defined as south of St Clair, east of Jane and west of the Don Valley. Only one branch may be proposed. ELIGIBILITY: individuals must be Canadian citizens, permanent residents, have a pending PR application, or be a protected person, must hold a Social Insurance Number, and must have lived in the City of Toronto for at least one year before the deadline and live and work there at least eight months a year (no PO boxes). Artists in graduate study may not apply for work related to their programme without a letter from the programme director. Organisations must be incorporated non-profit arts organisations, and collectives must operate on a non-profit basis, be located in Toronto, and have both members (for a pair) or a majority of members resident in Toronto; successful collectives must supply bank account details in the collective's name. IMPORTANT: proposals must align with TPL's programming policy, and proposals that TPL staff judge unsuitable for a branch or impossible in its context will not be funded, so read the 'Working With Toronto Public Library' section of the guidelines. First-time applicants are encouraged to consult the programme manager and to attend the Zoom office hours on eligibility and project fit (17 September) and on budgets (29 September). Contact: Lena Golubtsova, lena@torontoartscouncil.org. Deadline 6 October 2026.

  4. SAT: Research-Creation Residency Program in Arts and Technologies 2026-28 (Montreal)

    Societe des arts technologiques (SAT) · Societe des arts technologiques, 1201 Boulevard Saint-Laurent, Montreal, QC, Canada. Open to professional artists and collectives from anywhere in the world; no nationality restriction stated. · Deadline: 18 Oct 2026 · Award: CAD 10,000 grant per selected project, for individual artists and collectives alike. Also included: access to SAT workspaces during the residency periods, access to equipment and software resources subject to availability, mentoring and training activities (on site and remote), creative and technical support from the SAT teams, and documentation and archiving of the work. Travel, meals and accommodation beyond the grant are NOT covered. No application fee.

    The Societe des arts technologiques (SAT) in Montreal runs a research-creation residency program in arts and technologies for artists and collectives working in the performing arts and/or media arts. The residency funds a research-creation stage of a project in technological arts rather than the finalisation of a finished work, so the call weighs the exploration process and the innovation in it above a polished outcome. Each selected project receives a CAD 10,000 grant, whether the applicant is an individual artist or a collective, plus access to SAT spaces and equipment, technical and creative support from the SAT teams, mentoring and training activities, and documentation and archiving. Access to spaces and equipment is assessed against the project's needs and granted subject to availability. Travel, meal and accommodation costs beyond the grant are not covered. Open to professional artists and artistic collectives worldwide, at any level of experience and from any primary discipline, provided the profile and project suit immersive technologies; collectives must submit a joint portfolio showing they can carry out the project independently, particularly the visual and sound creation inside a dome. Each application must include a form and supporting documents setting out the concept, the technical solutions considered, and what will be explored during the residency. One application per project and per person; incomplete applications are not evaluated; use permanent, downloadable links rather than WeTransfer or similar temporary services. Accommodations and alternative support documents (large print and so on) are available on request at residences@sat.qc.ca. Deadline 18 October 2026. Apply through the Google Form linked from the SAT open-call page.
