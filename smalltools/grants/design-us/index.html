Open calls

  1. Beam Center: 2027 Beam Camp Project Open Call (USD 5,000 commission, remote-friendly)

    Beam Center · Brooklyn, New York, USA (organisation); the work is built in Strafford, New Hampshire by 100+ youth. Designer collaborates remotely; optional site visit with travel reimbursed. Open worldwide, any age, individuals or teams. · Deadline: 30 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 award to the selected proposer, plus a fabrication budget of approximately USD 15,000. No application fee.

    Annual design commission for an ambitious, technically challenging large-scale public artwork or structure, built over summer 2027 by more than 100 young people working with Beam staff. The selected designer collaborates through bi-weekly remote planning meetings and may visit the build with travel reimbursed, making this genuinely remote-friendly for a physical commission. A strong fit for creative-technology and installation practices. AWARD: USD 5,000 to the proposer plus roughly USD 15,000 in fabrication budget. ELIGIBILITY: open worldwide, any age, individuals or teams. DEADLINE: 30 November 2026, 11:59pm EST.

  2. Loeb Fellowship 2027-28 at Harvard Graduate School of Design

    Harvard Graduate School of Design · Harvard Graduate School of Design, Cambridge, Massachusetts, US. Open internationally; fellows must remain in residence in the Cambridge area while classes are in session (J-1 visa for international fellows). · Deadline: 05 Jan 2027 · Award: Stipend of USD 57,500 for the academic year (taxable), plus housing for fellows and their families coming from outside the Boston/Cambridge area through 30 June, and a travel grant covering approximately one economy round trip home per semester. No application fee.

    The Loeb Fellowship at Harvard Graduate School of Design is a year-long residential fellowship for accomplished mid-career practitioners shaping the built and natural environment: urban designers, public artists, architects, landscape architects, urban planners, developers, journalists, civic leaders, policymakers and community development leaders (past fellows have included a theater director, public artists and a documentary filmmaker). Fellows audit courses at the GSD and across Harvard (minimum one GSD course per semester), engage with the school community, and join a lifelong global alumni network. SUPPORT: a stipend of USD 57,500 for the academic year (taxable income), housing through 30 June for fellows and families of modest size coming from outside the Boston/Cambridge area, and a travel grant covering roughly one economy round trip home per semester. ELIGIBILITY: minimum 8 years of experience in the field (a typical class averages about 42 years of age); international candidates are encouraged to apply and must be eligible for a J-1 visa at selection; fellows must remain in residence in the Cambridge area while classes are in session. HOW TO APPLY: via SlideRoom with a resume, three recommendation letters (due 7 days after the deadline) and optional supplemental materials. No application fee. DEADLINE: 5 January 2027 at 11:59 PM ET for the 2027-28 class.

  3. GLEAM 2027: Request for Light-Based Artwork Proposals (Olbrich Botanical Gardens; honorariums USD 5,000 to 30,000)

    Olbrich Botanical Gardens (City of Madison Parks Division and Olbrich Botanical Society) · Madison, Wisconsin, USA. Open to local, regional and international artists; works can be installed by local preparators if the artist cannot travel. · Deadline: 06 Jan 2027 · Award: All-inclusive honorarium of USD 5,000 to 30,000 per artwork, covering artist fee, materials, fabrication, travel, lodging, install and maintenance. Usually only one USD 30,000 project is selected; proposals under USD 25,000 get the most consideration. Free host lodging and loan projectors and speakers are available. No application fee stated.

    GLEAM is an annual night-time light art exhibition across a 16-acre botanical garden and a 10,000 sq ft tropical conservatory. The 2027 edition will show 9 to 11 temporary, large-scale, light-based works that are immersive, interactive or kinetic; 3 to 5 interactive works are wanted, and traditional object-based sculpture is not considered. WHO: local, regional and international artists, collectives, architects and lighting designers with previous experience in illuminated art, projection or lighting design, preferably outdoors. Up to three proposals per artist or collective. PRIORITIES FOR 2027: the Bolz Conservatory (closing for restoration from 2028), the Great Lawn, the 150 ft Thai Bridge, tree-lined paths, and a projection mapping work on the Royal Thai Sala for its 25th anniversary. Projection on landscape and water is encouraged. AI: any use of AI must be disclosed and will be credited as a material; projection content made without generative AI is preferred. PRACTICAL: works must be weatherproof for two months outdoors (rain, irrigation, 30 to 81 F) and interactive parts must survive 35,000+ visitors; works over 10 ft need a structural assessment paid from the budget. Olbrich provides site prep, power, a forklift and basic install support. International applicants must budget for local preparators (USD 50 per person per hour) in case they cannot travel. BUDGET: USD 5,000 to 30,000 all-inclusive; scaling options at different price points are encouraged, and there is little room to raise an honorarium after invitation. APPLICATION: Airtable form with one combined PDF (max 25 MB) on the required proposal template, with sketches or renderings, resume and up to five images of comparable work. Optional 30-minute curator consultations run 19 November to 17 December 2026. DEADLINE: 6 January 2027, 11:59 pm CST. Finalists notified 15 February 2027; final proposals due 19 April 2027.

  4. Roman J. Witt Residency 2027-28 (University of Michigan Stamps School and UMMA; USD 20,000 honorarium, housing, studio and a museum exhibition)

    University of Michigan Stamps School of Art & Design and University of Michigan Museum of Art (UMMA) · Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA · Deadline: 25 Jan 2027 · Award: USD 20,000 honorarium for up to twelve weeks in residence, plus housing, studio space and up to USD 5,000 for materials, research and development. Travel support is limited. SlideRoom charges a USD 15 processing fee for media uploads.

    One residency a year for a visiting artist or designer to make new work at the Stamps School while engaging the university community, ending in an exhibition at UMMA's Irving Stenn, Jr. Family Gallery, a 1,200-square-foot, glass-walled, street-facing space beside a main campus route. WHO: established artists and designers. Proposals may address any subject and take any artistic or design approach; recent residents include Conrad Egyir, Holly Bass, Stacey Kirby and Machine Dazzle. No citizenship or residency rule is stated. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 20,000 honorarium for up to twelve weeks in residence, housing, studio space and up to USD 5,000 for materials, research and development. UMMA gives curatorial support and handles installation, de-installation and shipping; travel support and other resources are limited. The work stays the property of the artist. WHAT IS EXPECTED: a project that responds to the gallery's setting and scale and draws on campus resources, plus one student engagement and one public programme; part of the time on campus goes to critiques, talks, workshops or open studio hours. APPLICATION: through SlideRoom (um.slideroom.com) with a CV, references and portfolio media (video, audio or PDF), a project description, an explanation of how university resources could contribute, the proposed student engagement and public programme, a preliminary schedule and work plan, and a budget. SlideRoom charges a USD 15 processing fee. Review runs February to March 2027, finalist presentations and interviews April to May, notification 15 June 2027. DEADLINE: 25 January 2027 (time not stated).
