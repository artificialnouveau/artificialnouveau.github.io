Funded Artist Residencies in the US

Currently 10 active funded artist residencies open to applicants in the US, across AI, arts, film, journalism, research, tech and cross-disciplinary practice. Hand-curated and updated weekly. Almost every entry is funded; a few notable unpaid open calls and festival submissions are included as clearly flagged exceptions. Open calls

  1. MoHA: CATS+ Residency 2026 (Austin)

    The Museum of Human Achievement (MoHA) · Austin, Texas. Local residency: applicants must be based in the Austin area. · Deadline: 01 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 1,500 honorarium, plus access to MoHA equipment and workspaces.

    CATS+ (Collaborative Art + Technology Situation) is a local residency program at The Museum of Human Achievement in Austin for artists and tech wizards, grounded in hands-on experimentation, critical thought and community-building in media art and live AV production. Cohorts take part in weekly gatherings to learn and share on a broad range of resident-driven topics, build troubleshooting and collaboration skills, co-create projects, and contribute work to MoHA's media art exhibition during the Austin Studio Tour in November and/or MoHA's Holiday Show in December. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 1,500 honorarium and access to MoHA equipment and workspaces. WHO IS ELIGIBLE: applicants of all skill levels and fields are welcome, especially those experiencing barriers to media and tech community, knowledge and tools; must be in the Austin area, 18 or older, not a full-time student and not a CATS+ alum. DEADLINE: 1 October 2026. Info session at MoHA on 28 September 2026, 7 to 9pm (RSVP via the application page). TO APPLY: via the application and info page on the MoHA wiki.

  2. Eyebeam AGENCY Virtual Residency, Fall 2026 (US-based artists)

    Eyebeam · Online. Applicants must be based in the United States, OUTSIDE New York City, and remain US-based for the full residency term (4 November 2026 to 12 February 2027). · Deadline: 11 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 2,000 stipend for each of six selected artists, plus weekly residency programming, mentorship with Eyebeam alumni and visiting professionals, technical consultation, and access to tools and expertise through Eyebeam's network. No application fee (via Submittable).

    Eyebeam's 2026-27 program year runs under the theme AGENCY: preserving human agency as AI systems increasingly act, decide and carry out tasks on our behalf, asserting agency in imagining and building the societies we inhabit, and rethinking agency as collective, relational, entangled and more-than-human. The Virtual Residency (Fall/Winter) supports US-based artists working outside New York City with time, community and structure to develop new work around the theme; it is well-suited to early-stage projects where shared research, discussion groups and feedback on nascent ideas would advance concept development and prototyping. Projects might engage AI directly but do not have to; Eyebeam welcomes critical and creative approaches to power, autonomy, technological systems, collective action, human and nonhuman relationships, and the structures through which agency is exercised, including reimagining AI systems through collective governance, repair, rehearsal or mischief, and repurposing AI toward tools for the public good. STRUCTURE: six artists, 12 weeks, minimum 3-4 hours per week, with weekly cohort sessions, independent project development, work-in-progress shares with peers and guest critics, a closing virtual process presentation, and a contribution to an end-of-year publication. ELIGIBILITY: individuals only (no collectives this cycle); not enrolled in a full-time degree program; not committed to another residency during the term; US-based outside NYC for the full term. TIMELINE: deadline Sunday 11 October 2026, 11:59 PM ET (no late applications); interviews 26-28 October; notifications 29 October; residency 4 November 2026 to 12 February 2027. Info session Monday 28 September 2026, 1 PM ET. Apply via Submittable, free.

  3. Pedantic Arts Residency and Research Grant 2027 (Pittsburgh)

    Pedantic Arts Residency · Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, USA. Open to non-Pittsburgh residents aged 18 or older; travel is self-funded. · Deadline: 15 Oct 2026 · Award: Residency: free furnished housing in a shared apartment plus a USD 2,000 living stipend (half on arrival, half on completion). Research Grant: a free private bedroom at the Pedantic Apartment plus a USD 500 or USD 1,000 honorarium at the grantee's discretion. Application free until 28 September 2026, then USD 20.

    Pedantic Arts Residency opens applications for its Summer 2027 and Fall 2027 programmes in Pittsburgh: two four-week residency cohorts and two research grants. THE RESIDENCY houses three creatives at a time (a visual artist, an organizer and a writer) in a collective four-week programme that emphasizes exploration, exchange and community connection over production. Residents get free furnished housing in a shared apartment, a USD 2,000 stipend to cover living expenses (half on arrival, half on completion), a Pittsburgh-based guide working in their field, tickets and institutional passes, and introductions across the city's creative community. THE RESEARCH GRANT invites an individual visual artist, curator or writer for a one- to two-week independent stay to conduct Pittsburgh-related research, with a free private bedroom at the Pedantic Apartment, a USD 500 or USD 1,000 honorarium usable at the grantee's discretion, and help establishing relevant connections. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be 18 or older and not Pittsburgh residents at the time of application; travel costs are the participant's own. Residency attendance and staying in the provided housing are required. APPLICATION: applications are free until 28 September 2026 at 23:59 EST; a USD 20 fee applies from then until the final deadline of 15 October 2026 at 23:59 EST.

  4. Open Interval: An Artist-Scientist Exploration of Turbulence

    Simons Foundation · United States (trio must be regionally co-located) · Deadline: 21 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 30,000 per awarded trio

    Open Interval is a Simons Foundation program that supports trios consisting of an artist, a scientist, and a representative from a 501(c)(3) arts organization to collaborate around a shared scientific theme. The current cycle centers on the theme of turbulence, a phenomenon at the edge of chaos and beauty. Awarded trios receive USD 30,000 to support a sustained period of thought partnership and exploration rather than the production of finished works or exhibitions. The program runs from April to December 2027 with a touchstone meeting in May 2027 and possible cohort convenings. Trio members must be regionally co-located anywhere in the United States so they can collaborate in person. The scientist should hold a Ph.D. and be conducting basic research, with priority given to fields such as astrophysics, neuroscience, quantum physics, biology, or mathematics. The application portal opens September 9, 2026, and all three trio members must submit for the application to be complete.

  5. MATS Residency Program: 6-24 Month Funded AI Safety Research (Winter 2027 intake)

    MATS (ML Alignment and Theory Scholars) · In-person at MATS offices in Berkeley, London, or Washington DC; visa support provided where required. For experienced researchers with at least one substantial piece of research-caliber work. · Deadline: 31 Oct 2026 · Award: USD 155,000 to 285,000 per year gross (set by location, seniority, and experience), plus medical/dental/vision/life insurance, retirement plan, paid time off, conference funding, catered meals, and up to USD 25,000 relocation support. No application fee.

    The MATS Residency, announced in August 2026 as a new track alongside the seasonal MATS fellowship, funds 6 to 24 months of high-independence AI safety research for experienced researchers, whether already in AI safety or moving in from adjacent fields. COMPENSATION: USD 155,000 to 285,000 per year gross depending on location, seniority and experience, plus insurance, retirement plan, PTO (17 days US / 20 days UK), conference funding, catered meals, and up to USD 25,000 in relocation support. ELIGIBILITY: at least one substantial piece of research-caliber work; credentials are not required. Full-time (part-time case by case for academics on sabbatical), in person at MATS offices in Berkeley, London, or Washington DC, with visa support where required. TIMELINE: the Winter 2027 intake window runs 25 August to 31 October 2026 (AoE), with decisions by end of November and interviews plus a paid work test in the first week of December; three intake windows per year.

  6. Sanford Underground Research Facility: Artist-in-Residence Program 2027 (Lead, South Dakota)

    Sanford Underground Research Facility (SURF) · Sanford Underground Research Facility, Lead, South Dakota, USA, in the Black Hills. The call does not state a residency or nationality restriction; travel, housing and per diem for both visits are covered. · Deadline: 01 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend paid in two installments, plus up to USD 5,000 in additional funds the artist may apply for to create the artwork. Transportation (airfare, rental car or mileage), housing and per diem are covered for both visits; a studio space and guided surface and underground tours are provided; return shipping of the exhibition is covered up to USD 7,000.

    The SURF Artist-in-Residence program invites artists to create work inspired by the Sanford Underground Research Facility in Lead, South Dakota. At 4,850 feet deep, SURF is the deepest underground laboratory in the United States, housed in the former Homestake Gold Mine, and hosts experiments in physics, biology, geology and engineering. The residency is modeled after programs run by the National Park Service, CERN and Fermilab, and aims to create awareness of the science and encourage interdisciplinary work. STRUCTURE: a four-week on-site visit in summer 2027 (between 22 May and 31 July, timed to the artist's schedule) with studio time, guided underground visits, tours of Lead and the surrounding Black Hills sites, and dinners with scientists; then a one-week visit in October 2027 to install an art exhibition, give up to two lectures based on the proposal, and run promotional or outreach events in the local community (which may include schools, universities, interviews or documentation of the residency). FUNDING: a USD 5,000 stipend paid in two installments; the artist may apply for up to USD 5,000 in additional funds for creating the artwork; transportation, housing and per diem for both visits; and return shipping covered up to USD 7,000. CONDITIONS: each artist donates at least one artwork, selected by a SURF representative, to SURF's permanent art collection, and is responsible for installation and de-installation in coordination with SURF and the exhibition space. A timeline and expectations are agreed in an MOU before the residency begins. PHYSICAL REQUIREMENTS: working underground requires being comfortable in dark spaces, following all safety protocols, walking on uneven surfaces for at least a mile at a time, and negotiating wet, slippery conditions and an elevator ride in the dark; safety training and loaned personal protective equipment are mandatory, and a SURF guide accompanies the artist at all times. DEADLINE: 1 November 2026, via the SURF AiR application portal.

  7. Eyebeam AGENCY NYC Residency, Spring 2027

    Eyebeam · New York City, USA. Applicants must live within the five boroughs and be able to work on site at Eyebeam at least one day a week for the full residency term (15 March to 11 June 2027). · Deadline: 15 Nov 2026 · Award: USD 5,000 stipend per selected artist, plus mentorship and access to tools and technical expertise through Eyebeam's network. No application fee (via Submittable).

    The NYC track of Eyebeam's AGENCY residency cycle supports New York City-based artists exploring questions of agency at a moment when AI systems increasingly act, decide and carry out tasks on our behalf: how to preserve human agency as these systems shape human behavior, how to assert agency in imagining and building the societies we inhabit, and what agency becomes when understood as collective, relational, entangled and more-than-human. Projects might engage AI directly but do not have to; critical and creative approaches to power, autonomy, technological systems, collective action, human and nonhuman relationships, and the social and material structures of agency are all welcome, including reimagining AI through collective governance, repair, rehearsal or mischief, and repurposing AI systems toward new tools, methods and models for the public good. Residents receive a USD 5,000 stipend, mentorship, and access to tools and technical expertise through Eyebeam's network. ELIGIBILITY: individuals only (no collectives this cycle); not enrolled in a full-time degree program; not committed to another residency during the term; must live in New York City within the five boroughs and be able to work on site at Eyebeam at least one day a week for the full term. TIMELINE: deadline Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:59 PM ET; interviews 7-10 December; notifications 14 December; residency 15 March to 11 June 2027. Info session Monday 19 October 2026, 1 PM ET. Apply via Submittable, free.

  8. Wave Farm: 2027 Research and Production Program (Transmission Art Residencies and Radio Art Fellowships, NY)

    Wave Farm · Wave Farm, Acra, NY, US (Catskill Mountain Park). International open call. · Deadline: 01 Dec 2026 · Award: USD 1,000 stipend for each 10-day residency; USD 2,000 for each two-month Radio Art Fellowship. No application fee.

    Wave Farm, the Warhol Foundation-backed transmission arts center in Acra, NY, opens one application covering all its 2027 opportunities: the Transmission Art Residency, the Spatial Sound Residency, and two Radio Art Fellowship tracks (Research, and Community Engagement) connected to Wave Farm's WGXC radio station. WHAT YOU GET: a USD 1,000 stipend for each 10-day residency and USD 2,000 for each two-month fellowship, with access to Wave Farm's research library, transmitter and receiver equipment, and on-site staff support to develop a new transmission artwork. ELIGIBILITY: international; collaborations allowed; full-time students are ineligible except rare post-graduate cases; past residents and fellows may reapply; applicants may apply to as many opportunities as they wish but will be offered at most one; DJ sets are explicitly ineligible. A strong fit for signal-based, broadcast, spatial sound and radio-adjacent work. DEADLINE: 1 December 2026 at 11:59 pm EST. Finalists are interviewed by Zoom, with notifications in late February 2027 and residencies during 2027.

  9. Kaatsbaan Subsidized Residencies

    Kaatsbaan Cultural Park · Tivoli, New York, USA · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Generously subsidized residency; includes hotel-style on-campus rooms, locally sourced meals, and access to studios, a 160-seat black box theater, and a 153-acre Hudson Valley campus. No artist stipend.

    Kaatsbaan Cultural Park offers subsidized residencies for artists of all genres to create, live, and collaborate on its 153-acre campus in the Hudson Valley near Tivoli, New York. Residencies are individually designed between the artist, Kaatsbaan, and partners to fit the needs of the artist or company, welcoming disciplines from dance, theater, music, and visual arts to writing, composition, design, and film. The program is generously subsidized and includes individual hotel-style rooms, healthy locally sourced meals, and access to professional studios with sprung floors and pianos, a 160-seat black box theater, and exhibition space. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis through an inquiry form, with early inquiries encouraged to secure preferred dates. Artists at any career stage worldwide may apply, individually or as collaborative teams. There is no application fee and no artist stipend.

  10. Good Life Residents Program: AI and Finance

    Roosevelt Institute, in partnership with Data & Society · Remote. Travel may be requested for select convenings and for the culminating public event. US economic policy focus. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,000 stipend, plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event if needed. No application fee.

    The Good Life Residents Program is a six-month Roosevelt Institute initiative that develops new leaders and elevates bold ideas across six economic policy priorities under its Good Life Agenda. Each topic area hosts a cohort of 3 to 4 residents plus 1 to 2 senior Topic Advisors (academics, policy leaders, organizers or government practitioners) who provide strategic and intellectual mentorship and moderate the closing event. Residents write a roughly 1,500-word original policy essay advancing a people-centered policy vision against a topic-specific prompt, take part in structured dialogue with peers, Topic Advisors and Roosevelt Think Tank directors, help prepare and appear at a culminating public event, contribute to press opportunities such as op-eds and reporter calls where applicable, and act as ambassadors for the Good Life Agenda in their professional and public networks. The current round, the AI and Finance Residents Program, is run in partnership with Data & Society and asks for bold ideas on how to shape, redirect, interrupt or make more visible and contestable the flows of capital into generative AI. Framing: breakneck investment in generative AI technology and infrastructure is distorting the stock market, profit measures, bond issuance, the pace and composition of growth, job creation and trade policy, with seven tech companies accounting for more than a third of S&P 500 market capitalisation and complex, sometimes circular financial arrangements obscuring risk. Suggested lines of inquiry include what government interventions could improve public oversight, risk allocation and direction-setting in private markets; how other investments could be coordinated and scaled if AI spending is crowding them out; what role public spending or new institutions could play in catalysing technology R&D designed to support workers, sustainability and supply chains; what democratic input mechanisms would keep state influence over capital flows less vulnerable to corruption; and how federal AI policy should address public hostility to data centre investments alongside national security and competitiveness. Compensation is USD 3,000 plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event. FLAG: Roosevelt does not publish an application deadline for the AI and Finance round on the programme page, and earlier rounds (Social Security financing, Federal Reserve powers, housing) each ran their own short application window. Check the Formstack application form linked from the programme page for the current closing date before applying.
