    Surel's Place · In person in Garden City, Idaho, USA (Boise area). Residents must live at the home and studio; no studio-only residencies. · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: Free rent, utilities and wi-fi, a living stipend of USD 100 per week, a USD 300 travel stipend, use of well-maintained bicycles, free or discounted tickets to art events and movies, marketing through Surel's Place channels, and event hosting including logistics and installation help. Each resident also gets a local host to orient them and host their public events. No application fee. NOTE: Surel's Place takes a commission on sales arising from the residency, 35% of retail price on artwork, 45% on ticketed events that are offsite or need contracted services (calculated after administrative expenses), and 15% on books, CDs or other merchandise tangential to the artist's medium. A surcharge may apply to cover additional utility costs if a partner joins.

    Surel's Place is a residency in Garden City, Idaho with a two-fold mission: to give artists the time, shelter and professional support their work deserves, and to give the public accessible art experiences including performances, readings, exhibits and workshops. In exchange for the residency, artists offer art experiences back to the community. OPEN TO professional visual, literary and performance artists: painters, writers, musicians, architects, filmmakers, choreographers, any artist who needs a place to focus. LIMITS: because of the property's constraints and proximity to neighbours, it cannot accommodate loud or very messy processes such as welding or blacksmithing. The organisers also note that predominantly conceptual work, work that must be explained to attract a viewer, may not be met with as much enthusiasm by the jurors, and that they welcome artists who want to connect with an audience. Each cycle, one of the five residencies includes outreach to local schools through the PARTNER Schools Program. REQUIREMENTS: maintain residence at the home and studio (no studio-use-only residencies); use the residency in a focused way towards a stated goal with no other employment or work happening simultaneously; visual artists give a show of and/or lecture about their residency work, writers give a reading and discussion session; all artists offer at least one public workshop (2 to 3 hours, minimal lecturing); visual artists donate one piece of art to Surel's Place for future fundraising; writers allow Surel's Place to publish a free-standing excerpt of their residency work in books, broadsides, posters or similar. SALES COMMISSION applies as set out in the funding notes. HOUSE RULES: the non-smoking requirement is non-negotiable and any evidence of smoking is grounds for early termination. Partners are welcome but children and pets cannot be accommodated, no more than two adults may live there during a residency, and partners wanting concurrent residencies must apply and be accepted separately. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be 25 or older and non-smokers. APPLY by email to info@surelsplace.org with the online Artist in Residence application plus, for VISUAL ARTISTS, a cover letter introducing yourself and saying why you want to be at Surel's Place specifically, a proposal including a workshop outline, a CV, electronic images of 4 to 6 pieces of work and an image list (title, size, medium, year); for LITERARY applicants, cover letter, proposal with workshop outline, CV and PDF work samples with your name and page number in the top right corner (fiction, prose or script no more than 10 typed double-spaced pages; poetry no more than six poems and no more than 10 pages); for OTHER MEDIUMS, cover letter, proposal with workshop outline, CV and electronic samples (contact Surel's Place to discuss sample requirements for your medium). Applications are reviewed by juries of accomplished artists, writers and fine arts professionals, and all applicants are notified by email.

The 2027 residencies are dedicated to projects that reflect upon the Canal's physical, environmental and social condition and history, its materiality and its context, and the project must directly address the Canal. Applicants propose a specific creative visual project that must be exhibitable in a gallery setting, identify their target audiences and explain how the work will be shared with them and with the public at large. Applications are reviewed by a panel of Erie Canal Museum and Canal Corporation representatives plus invited arts professionals (the 2026 panel included Elizabeth Dubben of Collar Works, artist and educator Dennis RedMoon Darkeem, and Garth Johnson, Curator of Ceramics at the Everson Museum of Art); finalists interview with the panel. SUBMIT application packages as PDFs by email to Natalie Stetson, Executive Director, at employment@eriecanalmuseum.org with 'Erie Canal Artist-in-Residence' in the subject line, by 11:59 pm on Sunday 6 September 2026. The RFP is at eriecanalmuseum.org/about/careers/.

  9. MAD Artist Studios Residency, Cycles 46 and 47

    Museum of Arts and Design (MAD) · Sixth floor of the Museum of Arts and Design, New York City, USA. Open to residents of the New York City metro area only. · Deadline: 11 Sep 2026 · Award: Stipend based on the on-site public schedule: USD 15,000 for three public-facing days per week, or USD 10,000 for two days. APPLICATION FEE: USD 5 per application, with fee waivers available on request by emailing education@madmuseum.org before submitting.

    Established in 2008 and the first of its kind, the Artist Studios at MAD is a public-facing community engagement and professional development programme on the sixth floor of the Museum, where visitors meet artists-in-residence at work. Two artists are selected per six-month session (the application text elsewhere says three, so confirm with MAD). More than 200 artists and designers have come through the programme, which MAD describes as an innovative model for museum education, with a sustained focus on artists from historically underrepresented communities whose work pushes the boundaries of contemporary craft; alumni have exhibited at the Queens Museum, Whitney, Brooklyn Museum and Studio Museum. WHAT MAD MEANS BY CRAFT: both a discipline and a way of thinking, covering traditional studio craft materials such as clay, fiber, wood, glass, metal and stone, and craft-based methods used in new ways, including handwork combined with digital tools, performance or experimental materials. Craft here is defined by deep engagement with process, materiality and the hand rather than by medium. RESIDENCY EXPECTATIONS: this is a substantial public commitment, not quiet studio time. Residents commit to 14 or 21 public open studio hours a week on Thursdays, Fridays and/or Saturdays 10:30am to 5:30pm and/or Wednesdays 11:00am to 7:00pm, on a schedule fixed with MAD's Education Department for the whole six months. During those hours residents host K-12 school groups (in person and virtual) and public tour groups. Because of the contact with schoolchildren, all selected artists must pass a background check. ELIGIBILITY: an artist or designer with an active studio practice in the applied arts; a resident of the New York City metro area, defined as the five boroughs plus nearby New York, New Jersey and Connecticut counties within reasonable commuting distance, typically on MTA, PATH or NJ Transit; at least 21 at the time of application; and a US citizen or legal US resident with a valid SSN or EIN. The Museum does not sponsor visas and states this is not subject to exception. Programme alumni may not reapply, though unsuccessful past applicants may. SELECTION: the committee looks for a mature body of work engaging critically with the transdisciplinary fields of contemporary craft, design, art and technology, a strong conceptual framework, deep engagement with process and materials, a clear direction or question for the residency, and genuine readiness for the open studios format. Top applications go to interview with the Deputy Director of Education and Manager of Public Programs and Studios, and finalists' references are contacted. Applicants are strongly encouraged to visit the studios in person first. NOT AN EXHIBITION PROGRAMME: residents may show work in progress in their studio and finished work in the sixth-floor vitrines, but the residency does not include a gallery exhibition or a proposal for MAD's galleries. STUDIO RESTRICTIONS: no open flames, all hot work approved by the Director of Facilities, no large or unsafe power tools such as saws, and no toxic materials including turpentine, certain resins, glazes, lusters and lead-based paints. Applicants submit one application indicating their preferred schedule and which session or sessions they want, and are selected for one. Initial notifications October 2026, finalist interviews November 2026, offers December 2026. Questions: education@madmuseum.org. Deadline 11 September 2026.
