  1. AMR: 2026 Paper of the Future Prize

    Association for Mathematical Research (AMR) · Worldwide (digital submission) · Deadline: 01 Sep 2026 · Award: USD 10,000 prize to the winner; runner-up recognitions at committee discretion. The committee reserves the right to withhold the prize if no submissions meet the standards.

    USD 10,000 prize from the Association for Mathematical Research for serious experimentation in how mathematicians communicate with one another, beyond the static PDF. Submissions should demonstrate communicative capabilities fundamentally unavailable in a linear paper: interactive exploration of parameter spaces in differential equations, dynamic visualisation of group actions or geometric structures, multi-perspective representations of algebraic or number-theoretic objects, nonlinear navigation of proof architectures or dependency graphs, embedded computation as part of exposition, or similar. ELIGIBILITY: no nationality, institutional or career restriction stated; the focus is mathematical depth and communicative innovation. This is NOT for popularisation, production polish or short-form video content - it is about novel research-to-research communication. EVALUATION CRITERIA: mathematical depth and rigor; conceptual insight enabled by the medium; communicative innovation beyond static exposition; scalability and reproducibility; transformative potential for research communication. PROCESS: initial submissions are a short public YouTube concept demonstration (with the hashtag #AMRPotF and a concise written explanation of the mathematical substance and communicative innovation in the video description), with the YouTube link emailed to PotFPrize@amathr.org. Finalists are asked to provide a fully accessible prototype suitable for hosting or linking within AMR Reviews; the winning submission will be published in AMR Reviews as an interactive exposition. Selection committee: Mohammed Abouzaid, Benson Farb, Alex Kontorovich, Akshay Venkatesh, Maryna Viazovska.

  3. Arcane Grant 2026 for Artist Books

    ADAGP and SGDL (Societe des Gens de Lettres) · France. Open to a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or of French nationality living abroad) paired with a French-language author. · Deadline: 07 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 9,000 to the single winning project, supporting the conception and production of an artist's book. NOTE ON TAX: ADAGP grants count as primary artistic income under the French Intellectual Property Code (art. L112-2), so the sum falls under the artist-author social security and tax regime, subject to URSSAF contributions and income tax declared as non-commercial profits (micro-BNC or controlled declaration). VAT is not due. No application fee.

    Artists' books get little support, financially or in distribution, despite their place in art history and in the careers of the people who make them. ADAGP, the French collecting society for copyright in the visual arts, and SGDL, the French society of authors, created the Arcane grant in response: EUR 9,000 to one winning project for the conception and production of an artist's book made collaboratively by a visual artist and a writer. WHO CAN APPLY: a pair consisting of a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or a French national living abroad) and a French-language author working in fiction, non-fiction, poetry, children's literature or theatre who has already been published by a publisher. A trio is also possible where a publishing structure joins the project. WHAT COUNTS AS AN ARTIST'S BOOK: a creation, a work of art in book form, in a limited print run of between 1 and 500 copies, which may be signed or numbered. Explicitly not eligible: magazines, fanzines, limited editions of an ordinary book, and any publication that simply presents an artist's work, such as monographs or illustrated books. APPLICATION: the file must be in French and includes a jointly written project presentation of up to three pages covering the artistic and editorial vision, constituent elements, methods of realisation and desired final form; a one-page technical sheet giving title, print run, format, page count, paper quality, printing techniques and materials; a provisional timetable; and a detailed balanced provisional budget of expenses and financing. Artistic elements are a CV of exhibitions and publications (2 pages) and a portfolio with website link (10 pages) for the artist, a CV and website link for the author (2 pages), and a one-page editorial line for the publishing structure if there is one; contact details must be omitted from these. Administrative elements are sworn declarations that the project is a new creation with copyright held solely by the pair, that the artist is from the French scene, and, if applicable, that the publishing structure undertakes to publish the work. Templates are downloadable from the Required Documents page. TIMELINE: call launched 7 July 2026, applications close 7 September 2026 at 2pm, jury meets November 2026, winner announced end of November 2026, and the work must be published within two years of that announcement. The regulations must be read before applying, and submitting an application constitutes acceptance of them. Contact: arcane@adagp.fr.

  8. American Academy in Berlin: Berlin Prize Fellowship 2027-28

    American Academy in Berlin · Berlin, Germany, at the Hans Arnhold Center. RESTRICTED: fellowships are open only to citizens and permanent residents of the United States. · Deadline: 25 Sep 2026 · Award: Monthly stipend of USD 5,000, plus roundtrip airfare, accommodation at or near the Hans Arnhold Center, partial board, in-house library services and professional networking opportunities. Roughly two dozen fellowships awarded each year. No application fee.

    The American Academy in Berlin is an independent, nonprofit institute for advanced study in the arts, humanities, social sciences, law, journalism and public policy, whose primary mission is to strengthen the cultural and intellectual ties between the United States and Germany. Each year, after a rigorous selection process, the Academy awards roughly two dozen semester-long Berlin Prize fellowships to individuals who demonstrate excellence in their fields. DISCIPLINES: applications are welcome across the humanities, social sciences, law, journalism and various areas of public policy. The Academy generally encourages projects addressing pressing issues of the day, specifically naming democracy, security, technology and society (especially AI), energy including climate, and global order. Dedicated fellowships also support projects in Jewish studies, German studies, and public health and biotechnology. Project proposals need not focus on Germany, but candidates should explain how a residency in Berlin would enhance their work. NOT ACCEPTED: proposals in mathematics or the natural sciences. NOTE FOR ARTISTS: visual artists and composers are invited through separate, nomination-based competitions, so there is no open application route for them here. ELIGIBILITY: fellowships are restricted to citizens and permanent residents of the United States. Both emerging and established figures are eligible. PhD holders qualify as emerging scholars with a strong record of peer-reviewed work beyond the dissertation; candidates in non-academic fields such as journalism, filmmaking and policy need a significant record of publication or production; fiction and nonfiction writers must have published at least one book with a reputable press. WHAT FELLOWS DO: fellows present their work to audiences in and around Berlin through lectures, readings, public discussions, performances and film screenings, and should be willing to engage with relevant German institutions, organizations and the media; these activities are integral to the Academy's mission of fostering transatlantic dialogue. APPLICATION MATERIALS: a project proposal of 1,500 to 1,750 words plus a one-page bibliography; a curriculum vitae of up to 10 pages; two writing samples totalling no more than 60 pages; and three letters of recommendation, which are due 2 October 2026. DEADLINE: Friday 25 September 2026 at 12:00 noon Eastern Time (18:00 Central European Time). TIMELINE: all candidates are notified of the competition outcome by email in late March 2027; the 2027-28 Berlin Prize recipients are announced in May 2027.
