  1. Sundance Institute: Episodic Program and Lab 2027

    Sundance Institute · Open to international and U.S. applicants. In-person Lab takes place in the Atlanta, Georgia area, USA in Spring 2027; the Intensive track is fully online. · Deadline: 25 Aug 2026 · Award: No stipend or honorarium for participation. For the in-person Lab, Sundance covers round-trip basic economy airfare from the airport nearest the fellow's residence, shared housing with a private room and bath, and ground transport to and from the airport (fellows within 100 miles of the Lab get ground transport only). Sloan Foundation for a series with an illuminating emphasis on science, including medicine, energy, technology or invention. APPLICATION FEE: USD 50 nonrefundable processing fee per project submitted.

    The Sundance Institute Episodic Program is an entry point for emerging to mid-career writers and creators working across all scripted series platforms, combining a lab experience with customised support to move projects and careers forward. The Episodic Lab lets writers workshop an original pilot and develop their writing and pitching skills: working with showrunners, producers and executives, fellows take part in one-on-one story meetings, pitching sessions, craft workshops and writers rooms. Up to ten applicants are selected, either for the in-person Lab (preceded by virtual workshops) or for a fully virtual Intensive with a creative advisor. ELIGIBILITY: applicants must be 18 or older; the script must be the applicant's original creation or adapted from optioned source material and written in English; the draft must be revised or new, not already produced, not sold to a third party and not distributed online; and applicants cannot have previously had an episodic project (pilot or pitch) produced by a studio or network. Open to international and U.S. participants, subject to travel guidelines. Up to two writers per project may participate, with one named as primary applicant. APPLICATION: submitted via FilmFreeway, including writer bios (250 words each), a series logline (50 words), pilot synopsis (500 words), one-page series overview (500 words), personal statement (250 words), community connection, thematic statement (250 words), characters and core relationships (250 words), episodic vision, optional video samples (up to three links, 10 minutes total), and the full pilot script as a PDF in standard screenplay format. Demographic questions are optional and do not affect consideration. Candidates may submit up to three applications, with a separate application and USD 50 fee for each project. Submissions opened 13 July 2026 and close 25 August 2026 at 6:00 p.m. PT. Finalists are notified by email through FilmFreeway around mid to late March 2027 (FilmFreeway lists 31 March 2027), and the list of selected writers and projects is published at sundance.org/epi after 15 April 2027. Finalists must return a Fellow Agreement, a Participation Agreement and any other required documents within five days. Questions: episodicprogram@sundance.org.

  2. University of Twente: Postdoc, Ethics and Epistemology of LLMs4EU (17 months)

    University of Twente, Philosophy section / 4TU.Center for Ethics and Technology · Enschede, Netherlands · Deadline: 25 Aug 2026 · Award: EUR 4,241 to 5,538 gross per month (scale 10, steps 4 to 12) at 1.0 FTE, plus 8 percent holiday allowance and an 8.3 percent year-end bonus.

    A postdoctoral position in the Philosophy section at the University of Twente, part of the 4TU.Center for Ethics and Technology, working on the ethics and epistemology strand (Work Package 6) of LLMs4EU. LLMs4EU is a European Commission funded initiative led by the Alliance for Language Technologies, spanning more than 20 countries, to build large language models across all EU languages and preserve European linguistic and cultural diversity in the digital age while keeping the resulting tools compliant with European values and legislation including the AI Act and the GDPR. TASKS: establish benchmarking frameworks that address legal and ethical concerns; develop guidelines for fine-tuning processes with an emphasis on preserving cultural heritage; oversee ethics compliance and advise the project ethics board; and build frameworks that integrate ethical considerations into LLM design. This is the tech-communication and language-politics end of AI research rather than a modelling job: the questions are whose language, whose culture and whose norms get encoded when a continent builds its own models. REQUIREMENTS: a doctorate in ethics, philosophy, science and technology studies, AI ethics, computer science with an ethics focus, or a related field; demonstrated expertise in AI ethics and ethics compliance; understanding of AI tool design and LLM development; appreciation for cultural diversity; strong communication and organisational skills; and professional English. Apply through the AcademicTransfer posting. Closes 25 August 2026.

APPLY by email to info@surelsplace.org with the online Artist in Residence application plus, for VISUAL ARTISTS, a cover letter introducing yourself and saying why you want to be at Surel's Place specifically, a proposal including a workshop outline, a CV, electronic images of 4 to 6 pieces of work and an image list (title, size, medium, year); for LITERARY applicants, cover letter, proposal with workshop outline, CV and PDF work samples with your name and page number in the top right corner (fiction, prose or script no more than 10 typed double-spaced pages; poetry no more than six poems and no more than 10 pages); for OTHER MEDIUMS, cover letter, proposal with workshop outline, CV and electronic samples (contact Surel's Place to discuss sample requirements for your medium). Applications are reviewed by juries of accomplished artists, writers and fine arts professionals, and all applicants are notified by email.

  18. Arcane Grant 2026 for Artist Books

    ADAGP and SGDL (Societe des Gens de Lettres) · France. Open to a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or of French nationality living abroad) paired with a French-language author. · Deadline: 07 Sep 2026 · Award: EUR 9,000 to the single winning project, supporting the conception and production of an artist's book. NOTE ON TAX: ADAGP grants count as primary artistic income under the French Intellectual Property Code (art. L112-2), so the sum falls under the artist-author social security and tax regime, subject to URSSAF contributions and income tax declared as non-commercial profits (micro-BNC or controlled declaration).     Artists' books get little support, financially or in distribution, despite their place in art history and in the careers of the people who make them. ADAGP, the French collecting society for copyright in the visual arts, and SGDL, the French society of authors, created the Arcane grant in response: EUR 9,000 to one winning project for the conception and production of an artist's book made collaboratively by a visual artist and a writer. WHO CAN APPLY: a pair consisting of a professional visual artist from the French scene (working or residing in France for at least five years, or a French national living abroad) and a French-language author working in fiction, non-fiction, poetry, children's literature or theatre who has already been published by a publisher. A trio is also possible where a publishing structure joins the project. WHAT COUNTS AS AN ARTIST'S BOOK: a creation, a work of art in book form, in a limited print run of between 1 and 500 copies, which may be signed or numbered. Explicitly not eligible: magazines, fanzines, limited editions of an ordinary book, and any publication that simply presents an artist's work, such as monographs or illustrated books. APPLICATION: the file must be in French and includes a jointly written project presentation of up to three pages covering the artistic and editorial vision, constituent elements, methods of realisation and desired final form; a one-page technical sheet giving title, print run, format, page count, paper quality, printing techniques and materials; a provisional timetable; and a detailed balanced provisional budget of expenses and financing. Administrative elements are sworn declarations that the project is a new creation with copyright held solely by the pair, that the artist is from the French scene, and, if applicable, that the publishing structure undertakes to publish the work. Templates are downloadable from the Required Documents page. TIMELINE: call launched 7 July 2026, applications close 7 September 2026 at 2pm, jury meets November 2026, winner announced end of November 2026, and the work must be published within two years of that announcement. US economic policy focus. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 3,000 stipend, plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event if needed. No application fee.

    The Good Life Residents Program is a six-month Roosevelt Institute initiative that develops new leaders and elevates bold ideas across six economic policy priorities under its Good Life Agenda. Each topic area hosts a cohort of 3 to 4 residents plus 1 to 2 senior Topic Advisors (academics, policy leaders, organizers or government practitioners) who provide strategic and intellectual mentorship and moderate the closing event. Residents write a roughly 1,500-word original policy essay advancing a people-centered policy vision against a topic-specific prompt, take part in structured dialogue with peers, Topic Advisors and Roosevelt Think Tank directors, help prepare and appear at a culminating public event, contribute to press opportunities such as op-eds and reporter calls where applicable, and act as ambassadors for the Good Life Agenda in their professional and public networks. The current round, the AI and Finance Residents Program, is run in partnership with Data & Society and asks for bold ideas on how to shape, redirect, interrupt or make more visible and contestable the flows of capital into generative AI. Framing: breakneck investment in generative AI technology and infrastructure is distorting the stock market, profit measures, bond issuance, the pace and composition of growth, job creation and trade policy, with seven tech companies accounting for more than a third of S&P 500 market capitalisation and complex, sometimes circular financial arrangements obscuring risk. Suggested lines of inquiry include what government interventions could improve public oversight, risk allocation and direction-setting in private markets; how other investments could be coordinated and scaled if AI spending is crowding them out; what role public spending or new institutions could play in catalysing technology R&D designed to support workers, sustainability and supply chains; what democratic input mechanisms would keep state influence over capital flows less vulnerable to corruption; and how federal AI policy should address public hostility to data centre investments alongside national security and competitiveness. Compensation is USD 3,000 plus travel and accommodation for the culminating event. FLAG: Roosevelt does not publish an application deadline for the AI and Finance round on the programme page, and earlier rounds (Social Security financing, Federal Reserve powers, housing) each ran their own short application window. Check the Formstack application form linked from the programme page for the current closing date before applying.

  44. Rory Peck Trust: Crisis Fund for Freelance Journalists

    Rory Peck Trust · Remote (worldwide); London-based organisation supporting freelancers anywhere · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: Individual emergency grants; amounts are set case by case from an assessment of circumstances and need. No application fee.

    The Rory Peck Trust's Crisis Fund gives direct financial assistance to freelance news journalists whose work has put them in crisis: physical attack, urgent medical costs, replacement of confiscated or destroyed equipment, forced relocation, or support for the family of a journalist who has been injured, imprisoned, kidnapped or killed. This is one of the few funds built specifically for freelancers working in conflict and high-risk environments, who are usually outside any staff safety net. ELIGIBILITY: professional freelance journalists in news and current affairs whose sole income comes from journalism, who have been working freelance for at least the last 12 months and can provide reference letters from editors, who have no access to other financial or government support and no contractual benefits such as sick pay or social security. The crisis must have happened within the last six months and must be evidenced (invoices, bank statements). Anyone who received Rory Peck Trust support in the last three years is ineligible. The Trust also runs a separate Therapy Fund and a HEFAT safety-training fund. Applications are rolling, in English, French, Arabic, Spanish, Ukrainian or Russian. Contact assistance@rorypecktrust.org or info@rorypecktrust.org.

THE ROLE: turn the lab's research into clear writing and visuals for funders, partners, policymakers and the public. You read technical and policy work on AI and explain it in plain language without losing the substance. NOTE THAT A LARGE PART OF THIS ROLE IS VISUAL: building slide decks and visual maps that show what the lab works on and how the parts fit together, used for fundraising and partner updates. You work closely with the research and leadership team. WHAT THEY WANT: ideally 2 to 5 years of relevant experience, though the posting says a strong body of work can stand in for years. You do not need to be a known voice in the field; you do need to write clearly, learn their research quickly and care about getting it right. WHAT YOU WOULD BE EXPLAINING: technical AI governance and policy (evaluation standards, incident reporting, logging and provenance, attribution, liability), AI security and alignment research including the security of India's digital public infrastructure, and the societal and economic impacts of AI on labour, growth and public finances in India and other developing economies. HONEST WARNING FROM THE POSTING: 'The work is demanding, and we mean that plainly.'   46. Rest of World: Labor x Tech Reporting Commissions (Year 3)

    Rest of World, underwritten by the Ford Foundation · Remote. Reporting from anywhere in the world except India; writers based in India are not eligible for this series. · Deadline: Rolling / undated · Award: USD 1,000 per commissioned story of 800 to 1,000 words. No application fee.

    Rest of World is in the third year of its Labor x Tech program, underwritten by the Ford Foundation, commissioning reported stories on the changing nature of work at a moment of upheaval: AI tools and robotics reshaping professions at every rung of society, from self-driving cars replacing drivers to humanoid robots working alongside nurses; lay-offs across the tech industry; and the gig work model continuing to reshape traditional employment. The programme's premise is that this moment needs documenting now, in stories that help prepare society for what comes next. WHAT THEY COMMISSION: reported stories of 800 to 1,000 words from around the world on this beat, at a fee of USD 1,000 per story. WHAT THEY ARE LOOKING FOR, based on previous years: how new technology is transforming existing industries in unique ways and creating unexpected new pockets of employment; how technology is leading to significant job gain, job loss, and government responses to those trends; whether large demographic shifts are following the adoption of new technology or the arrival of new industries; how workplace technology is changing workplace dynamics; how new industries and tools are changing workers' incomes; and the expansion of the gig work model alongside the threats of automation. EXAMPLES of the kind of story they hope to commission, all previously published by Rest of World: workers in Chinese data factories teaching humanoid robots boring tasks; entry-level tech workers describing the AI-fuelled jobpocalypse; Microsoft and Google claiming their data centres create thousands of jobs while their permit filings say otherwise; AI helping judges close cases quickly and lawyers open them quickly; tech workers fuelling a mini baby boom in Taiwan's Silicon Valley; Dubai's robotaxi ambitions coming for human drivers; and the job brokers who run the lives of the migrant workers staffing Taiwan's chip plants. ELIGIBILITY NOTE: the programme is unable to commission reporting in India or from writers based in India for this series. HOW TO PITCH: send 2 to 3 paragraphs laying out the nutgraf of the proposal and explaining how the pitch fits the editorial mission of the programme. Indicate what sources will be used, whether any pre-reporting has been completed, what reporting has already been done on the topic at other outlets, and what the story contributes to the discourse. Pitches go to laborxtech@restofworld.org. Pitching is open on a rolling basis; no closing date is stated.
